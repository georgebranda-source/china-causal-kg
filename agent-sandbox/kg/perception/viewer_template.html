How it was built

A local model (Qwen 3.5 9B, temperature 0, frozen prompt) read each article and listed the cause/effect relationships stated explicitly, each with a word-for-word quote. Denied links were skipped. A second local pass recorded who makes each claim and what kind of speaker they are.

Each cause and effect was then compared with fixed anchor sentences for each category (embedding model qwen3-embedding:8b). A claim gets the nearest category if the similarity is at least the threshold and beats the runner-up by 0.02; otherwise it is "other". No category was assigned by hand. Similar descriptions were grouped into events (cosine distance 0.35), which make up the event chains.

How to read it

British voices are the paper itself, British officials and other British speakers. Chinese voices are Chinese individuals and bodies, and the Canton government or Strike Committee.

In the event chains, a solid arrow is asserted (the linked claims come from the same article) and a dashed arrow is composed (it joins claims from different articles). Composed chains can show a story nobody actually told.

A missing arrow means no explicit causal sentence was found. It does not mean the paper denied it.

Limits

Speakers and categories are machine-assigned and have not yet been checked against a hand-coded sample. Counts are small, so read them as presence or absence rather than proportions. "Red agitators" versus "the strikers are Red" is the least reliable distinction. Months after July 1925 cover only pages that were already OCRed, a thinner sample than May to July.
