**NOT CROSSING THE BORDEER**

An excuse that he was going in search of an employee who abandoned with $1,600 belonging to a foundry at Yaumuti was tendered at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning when a Chinese was charged with attempling to export $15 from the Colony and thus contravening a strike emergency enactment which is still in force.

## [13] THE BOYCOTT.

**RESUMEES, EXCEPT ERITISH.**

**LATEST UP-COUNTRY NEWS.**

From Our Own Correspondent.)

Pakkai, Kongmoon, Oct. 8.
All firms here, with the exception of British, have resumed trade. The Marion Barclay Hospital (Caneolian) and schools are still closed.

The situation generally is normal. Business is dull. The harvest has been good.

H.M.S. "Mourhen" and H.M.'s armed launch "Kwong Lee" were here during the greater part of the strike.

There is a shortage of kerosine and' liquid fuel. Merchants have attempted to smuggle the commodity in but their junks have seldom got through the strike pickets' cordons.. All seized cargoes are confiscated.

The s.s. "Leung Kwong" (formerly on the Hongkong-Kong-moon run) is now plying between Canton and Wuchow. The s.s. "Kung On" was removed from Pakkai to Kong Mei, the owners' village, for fear of being "requisitioned" by strikers or the military.

## [17] CANTON ITEMS.

**STRIKE PICKETS ASSIST PASSENGERS.**

**FURTHER WEAKENING**

The action of the strike pickets in actually supervising the landing arrangements at Canton for passengers carried by the s.s. "Honam" on her last trip illustrates the general weakening of the strike directed against Hongkong. The prices charged by the junk and samparas operating under their supervision are considerable and it is doubtless their need of such revenue that has led to the change of front, but the fact remains that no difficulty was experienced by passengers desirous of landing.

The "Honam" left again this morning with 400 passengers, nearly all of whom were Chinese. The "Fatshan" will leave tomorrow. No arrangements have to be made by the Steamboat Company for the "special tugs" which were deemed necessary to navigate that portion of the river which the Canton Government has stated is mined; what actually occurs is that the signal to stop is given from the hillside off Bocca Tigris forts, whereupon a boat puts out, questions about cargo and passengers carried are asked and a pilot boat proceeds ahead, deviating but slightly from the usual route.

## [18] TO DISARM STRIKERS?

The strikers' executive have acquired considerable power in the absence of troops in the neighbourhood of the East River. The seizure of Annamite clerks in the employ of the Banque de l'Indo Chine is typical of their high-handed actions. These clerks had spiked through the French gate and presumably had the passes from the French Consul without which they are not allowed to pass through into Cantor.

The disarming of the strikers upon the return of the troops from Whichow is looked upon as a certainty, as the Canton authorities are stated to have regarded the strikers' growing power with considerable uneasiness. but they have been powerless as yet to act in the matter.

## [19] TRADE LOAN.

**WEDNESDAY NOT FINAL DATE.**

**CAN APPLY LATER.**

Application for the provisions of the Trade loan were asked to be made by Wednesday, October 22, but the “China Mail” understands that this date was not looked upon as final, the idea in fixing a day being to have indicated the measure of the demand.

Many applications have been made, some of which are not thought to come under the category of needy relief and will therefore have to stand down. The sums applied for do not amount in aggregate to anything like £3,000,000.

The credit placed with the Colony in this manner will, however, be kept for the purpose for which it was asked and firms wishing to avail themselves of a portion of it may make application at any time.

## [24] A TURNING POINT IN HISTORY.

**GERMANY'S SINCERITY.**

*Berlin, October 18.*

Lord D'Abernon, the British Ambassador, in a-greeting to the German Delegation on their return from Locarno, congratulated them on behalf of Mr. Austen Chamberlain who would always remember their spirit of sincerity and frankness, and added that the honour in taking the initial course of action resulting in the Treaty belonged to the German Government.

The Locarno agreements mark a turning point in the history of Europe.

## [25] A PREMIER'S TRIBUTE

In a speech at the closing banquet of the Radical Congress, M. Painleve, the Premier, declared the Locarno agreement were the outcome of Carle's policy and inaugurated a new era.

At the conclusion of the Radical Congress, Mr. Painlevo promised that he and Mr. Herrick would discuss the most efficacious means of dealing with the formidable difficulties confronting the country.

He pointed to Locarno as evidence of the Castel's policy of international peace and added that all danger in Morocco was now past. France desired neither consquent nor oppression, and was only less doing for peace.
