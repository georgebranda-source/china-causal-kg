The appointment of a special envoy, a man of high prestige and standing, to discuss with Peking the grievances the Chinese, have against the British, is a proposal which has found favour with a considerable section of the London press.

Proceedings continued at the Central Magistracy yesterday afternoon in respect of a charge against the nephew of a deceased man, that he forged documents purporting to bear the signature of the dead man and relating to the transfer of shares in two Companies, the China Light and Power Company and the Hongkong and Kowloon Wharf and Godown Company.

Another charge, not coming under the heading of felony, is that, he forged a letter requesting outstanding dividend warrants to be forwarded to him (defendant).
Mr. H. Birkett, of Messrs. Moxon & Taylor, continuing his evidence, said that the defendant was a regular customer of the firm. The transfer of 50 Wharves represented a transaction carried through in his office. They were handed in by the defendant and according to the books of the firm had been sold to Lammert Brothers.

The transfer in respect of the second charge was also carried through by his office. The shares were received from the defendant. Cross-examined by Mr. F. O. Jenkin, who represented defendant, witness said all his evidence with regard to the two transactions of shares had been taken from the books. None of the transactions had been made by him personally and he had nothing personally to do with any of them. After further evidence, His Worship adjourned the case until to-morrow afternoon.

## [5] SNUB FOR REDS.

*(Reuter's Service.)*

The members of the left wing made an attack on Mr. Ramsay MacDonald in connection with the Zinovieff letter which they say proved a flasco, at the Labour Conference this morning. A composite resolution on the subject was introduced demanding inter alia that an apology be sent to the Soviet Government and Zinovieff, but it was overwhelmingly rejected on a show of hands.

## [6] COMMUNIST LEADERS CANNOT THROW STONES.

*London, Sept. 30.*

The Labour Conference overwhelmingly defeated a Communist motion to refer back the section of the Executive Board's report dealing with unemployment. Mr. Gallagher, of Paisley, submitting the motion, asserted that the movement was being handed over to the middle classes. Mr. MacDonald, replying, said that it was a libel to say that the labour leaders had blank cheques from Moscow and that it was time that such slanders were faced. He declared that nobody was less entitled to point the finger of scorn at himself or anyone else from the viewpoint of vulgar display of wealth or riotous living than the present British Communist leaders. (Cheers).

Shanghai, September 30.—When nearing the upper Yangtze port of Tuchow on the 27th the American steamer “Chichuen” was fired on by a large contingent of soldiers ensonced on the heights overlooking the gorges. The firing was kept up for an hour when steamer was struck at least two hundred times. A Chinese member of the crew was slightly wounded. The American gunboat “Palce” was ordered to escort the vessel through the soldier ridden area to Chungqing. The miscreants are believed to be fleeing soldiers of General Yangsen. The “Chichuen,” which was flying the American flag, belongs to the Yangtze Rapids S.S. Co. The skipper was the only foreigner aboard.—Reuter.

## [7] CRIMINAL LAW.

**LORD DARLING ON A PRISON CONFERENCE.**

Lord Darling, in an article in the London "Evening News," headed "Criminal Law in Ruggles-Brials," deals with a resolution recently passed by the International Prison Conference, "presided over by so good a man as Sir E. Ruggles-Briels."

It was solemnly resolved that after conviction of the accused, and before passing sentence upon him, the magistrate must make himself acquainted with all the home life of the convict—his family, his pleasures, his pursuits—since all of these combined have made him what he is, a thief, an assassin, a bigamist, or a burglar, and the prescription which is to cure him can only be composed or compounded by one with this fullest knowledge of the case.
—"Who they are who passed this resolution I know not," comments Lord Darling, and adds:—

But be they whom they may, and dwell they where they must, they have before dispersing with the swallows left to us here in England their overpowering decision that whoever would be a magistrate, high or low, must be compelled to attend lectures on those branches of queer learning known as psychology, penology, sociology, and, above all, forensic psychiatry. This is to begin; but one easily foresees that to these sciences must soon be added second-sight, prophecy in all its branches, murder as one of the fine arts, alchemy, conjuring, and all the learning of the Egyptians.

Because it is well established that diet affects the mind as well as the body; that, for example, a surfeit of tinned salmon induces to libel, slander, and petty larceny, whereas roast, beef, Yorkshire pudding, with horse-radish sauce, and strong beer fill the veins with the essence of that British brutality from which lotus eaters, such as the Irish, are notoriously free; meat, drink, cookery, pills and purgatives must all be carefully studied by him who would be a judge in the Commonwealth of Ruggles-Brisa.

Were I writing these reflections for publication in the "Quarterly Review" I would here permit myself the spacious luxury of an imaginary trial in the High Court of Ruggles-Brisia. A yerdict of guilty pro forma having been returned, the judge would begin his investigation of the past of the prisoner—who meanwhile would probably be accommodated with a seat on the Bench or allowed the solace of a cigarette.

Obviously it would be unsafe to rely on the reports of the police concerning so delicate a matter. The nearest relations of the presumed wrongdoer must therefore be brought to testify concerning his infancy and his adolescence. His mother must be sought, and his father, or his “putative father” as the law has it in but too many instances. His mother's “marriage lines” would be put in —provided there were any. Her decrees, nisi and absolute, would naturally be exhibited.

Lord Darling also asks what would be more necessarily than to know the nursery rhymes which "the patient absorbed with his mother's—or with Nestlé's—milk," and comments, "Sing a Song of Sixpence" may well stimulate a child to stand on the path of acquisitive evil," and that "a pocketful of rye is in itself a suggestion of capitalistic hoarding."
