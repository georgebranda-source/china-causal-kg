## [11] QUEER FISH.

**STRANGE YALMATI CATCH.**

**A MERMAID'S CHILD!**

Of the size and appearance of a baby's head, a strange sea-animal was caught by a Shuntung police-man at the ferry wharf. Youmati on Sunday.
HONGKONG, TUESDAY, NOVEMBER 10, 1925.

The description is borne out by a European of about twenty years' standing in the Colony.

Those who saw the "fish" maintain that it must have been a fish as it came out of the water.

It is not correct to say that there was a body as it was all "head," not unlike the head of an infant. The fish had eyes, mouth, nose, ear-holes and fins above the eyes. Another description is that the "fish had a round body, like a California orange."

A close inspection of the bowl in which the "fish" was kept revealed the fact that the specimen was very much alive. As it breathed and opened its mouth, two tiny teeth could be seen.
Nobody has yet fixed the identity of this extraordinary "fish" but it will be sent to the Museum.
According to the Chinese classics there is a "Kwai" (not Kwei") fish, also known as "Wa Wa," which amounts to a "merbaby" and is able to cry. Possession of such was supposed to bring immense luck.

## [12] POPPY DAY.

**THE GREAT DRIVE TO-MORROW**

Arrangements are now complete for the great Poppy Day drive to-morrow and having regard to the promises of help received and the amount of keen interest being taken the organisers have every hope of making the day a great success.

It cannot be expected that the tremendous figure collected all over China will be reached this year. That total was almost £1,000 and Hongkong's share was £2,645. It only because little or nothing can be expected from the many towns in China that contributed last year the local organisers are anxious that Hongkong should again do well. If the interest shown bears proportionate results then a successful day can be anticipated for it must be believed that Hongkong is not unconscious of the "debt which still is owing to the survivors of the Great War and their dependants. They are in a sense their country's creditors and it is plainly the duty of all who can to help to pay off a portion of the huge debt that is owing to them.

It is obvious from Earl Haig's cable received a few days ago and published in these columns that the G.H.Q. of the Legion is conscious of the Colony's efforts and grateful for all that has been done. His appeal has been received again and everyone will hope that help will be readily forthcoming.

## [16] SENTENCE QUASHED.

**PASSENGER SHIPS AND HONGKONG**

**MASTERS' LIABILITIES**

A passenger ship which brings passengers into the waters of the Colony but does not discharge them and which is not making Hongkong the terminal point of call is not considered to be liable under the provision of the Ordinance which states that the master of a ship should have a certificate if his vessel is "carrying passengers from, to or between places in the waters of the Colony."

This is the effect of a Full Court judgment given yesterday by His Honour Mr. Justice H. H. J. Gompertz and Mr. Justice Wood, the case being an appeal by the Japanese master of the U.S. Kia Lee's from the decision of the Hongkong Harbour Master that the master was liable to a fine of $100 in respect of each passenger carried (as the passengers were 52 a fine of $5,200 was imposed). The fine in question was therefore quashed.
