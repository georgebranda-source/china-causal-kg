## [6] WAR OF REVENGE.

**YUNNAN PROVINCE IN DANGER.**

**CANTON'S EX-STRONG MAN.**

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

*Yunnanfu, Octobər 2.*

General Fan Shek-sang (the strong man of Canton last year who left on a long trek, to his native province before his colleagues—the Yunnanese generals — were expelled by the Kuomintang) was reported yesterday to be on the move again. This time he seems to be in real earnest to secure revenge on Tuchun Tang Chi-yao who is alleged to have put Fan's father to death. Fan has 15,000 men, new equipment and both large and small guns. It is said that he will be satisfied with nothing less than Tang's head.

On a previous occasion Fan was thought to have been repulsed. It now appears that he only withdrew to renew provisions, his vanguard having made this necessary by denuding the country in which he was to operate.

Fan is also mentioned as having telegraphed many of the leading merchants asking them not to let Tang leave before he (Fan) arrives, which will be in a few days, he says.

In this connection, Fan's ally from Kweichow is advancing from that direction with Yunnanfu as his objective; also Yang Sen is marching on Yunnanfu from Szechuan. If these reports are correct—and there is no reason to disbelieve them—Tang will find it difficult to repel the invadera.

Brigands have been creating trouble at Chaotung up north and the city gates were closed for a fortnight. When the soldiers did arrive to drive them off, the trouble was intensified.

Missionaries living outside the city were requested to move inside. Subsequently they were given permission to fetch their property with the stipulation that they return by a certain hour. They failed to comply and found the gates closed. Nothing could induce those inside to let them in. Failing at another gate, the foreigners had to leave their horses and climb the city wall by ropes. Firing had been going on all the time and was thought to be directed at the brigands. It transpired that the fire was directed by the soldiers at their own men retiring before the outlaws. Several men were killed.
About 3,000 soldiers have been sent to Chaotung and district to

Inside the capital there does not seem to be much excitement but there is an under-current of feeling which is difficult to probe.

## [7] WATER SUPPLIES

With a full supply in all rider main districts during September, the consumption of water per head per day for the month is less than last year (21.5 gallons in Victoria as compared with 24.3 last year and 16 in Kowloon as compared with 18.1 last year).

More water is in storage in Kowloon in consequence of Shek Lai Pui reservoir functioning but slightly less in Hongkong, although Tytam Tuk has considerably more water in store.

The Government Analyst's reports show that the water is satisfactory.

## [9] China" Jitil

**HONGKONG, SATURDAY, OCTOBER 10, 1925.**

**"DOUBLE TEN."**

**ANNIVERSARY OF CHINESE**

**TO-DAY'S OBSERVANCES:**

Known commonly as the "Double Ten" festival—falling, as it does, on the tenth day of October—to day is the (14th) anniversary of the Chinese Republic.

In the last two or three years the observances in Hongkong were on a moderate scale. This year there does not seem to have been any big preparation. Some of the business houses and all the department stores close while a very few guilds also observe the holiday. The private Chinese schools, in the past, used to assemble to hear speeches.

His Majesty's ships dressed ship this morning in honour of the presence here of the Chinese men-of-war "Hai Chow" and "Ying Chi" whose arrival was reported in yesterday's "China Mail." At noon a salute will be fired.

## [16] TODAY'S MEETING.

At a meeting this morning of the shareholders and directors of the China Provident Loan and Mortgage Co., Ltd, Mr. R. G. Shewan made reference to a letter appearing in the morning newspapers. It did not give proof that it was signed by shareholders, he said, and he was not sure whether notice should be taken of it. Abraham Lincoln, who in the speaker's opinion was one of the wisest men who lived, used to say one should never "swop" horses when crossing a stream. "We are in full mid-stream now," said Mr. Shewan, "and the sooner we reach the other side the better."

"To attempt to stop these proceedings now and commence new negotiations with the Tug and Lighter Co. continued Mr. Shewan," would be the height of folly and would result disastrously. It was quite true that the Tug and Lighter Co, made a loss last year but they had since been able to get an insight into the business with the result that better days had been experienced. The Tug and Lighter Company were not pressing the sale. The Provident, Loan and Mortgage Company were using the lighters to advantage to-day and could not do without them and it would be nine or twelve months for the Provident, Loan and Mortgage Company to get other steamers. They would also cost a good deal more than the price the Tug and Lighter Company were asking. If those who had signed the letter would think more deeply into the matter they would appreciate the time it would take and the money it would cost to acquire a fleet which was now being got at once.

## [17] MALAYA'S FUTURE

**HELP FOR TRADE AND COMMERCE.**

**TRIBUTE TO MALAYS.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, October 9.*

Sir Laurence Guillemard, Governor of the Straits Settlements, speaking at a luncheon of the Overseas League, presided over by Sir Ernest Birch, declared the Malayan Government's first problem was to see the individuality of their separate parts scrupulously preserved and there was no question of fusion. Subject to it, the administration would make it as easy as possible for trade and commerce to go their way undisturbed.

He eulogised the Malays as gentlemen sportsmen very loyal to the Empire as testified by war time gifts.

The British Malays were united in bonds of complete understanding and deep affection.

Referring to the advance of the last half century, he said Malaya had had a most efficient Press, the tone generally being moderate, although there was no fear of the Governor lacking advice and criticism.

He expressed the ordination that in certain respects there was much still to be done in references to sanitation whilst the housing problem was particularly difficult owing to the partiality of the Malays to live in a crowded condition. ## [18] COLLISION INQUIRY

Claiming that he did carry lights, the owner of a fishing junk which collided with the Blue Funnel steamer "Talthyblus" at 3 a.m. on August 23, stated in the Marine Court yesterday that his boat was struck by the steamer's bow, cutting his steam cleanly and that three children were missed, presumably drowned.

Lieut.-Comdr. G. F. Hele, R.N., (Harbour Master), presided over a Court of inquiry to investigate the circumstances. Other members of the Court were Lieut.-Comdr. C. R. H. Harvey (Naval Chair de Depôt), Captain T. Croft (s.s. "Namsang"), Captain F. Gill (s.s. "Haifong"), and Captain Shearer (s.s. "Halvard").

Addressing the solicitors at a stage of the proceedings, the President said the point on which the enquiry turned was whether or not the junk carried lights. If it was shown that the junk had lights then the Court had to decide whether or not the master of the steamer was to blame for the loss of life.

Mr. G. G. N. Tinson represented the owners of the "Talthyblue" and Mr. C. A. S. Rusp appeared for the junk master.

Captain Lloyd described how his ship passed two fishing junks near Waglan Light and lights were not seen. Mr. J. Watson (chief officer), Mr. L. F. Marland (2nd officer) and the captain all stated that it appeared as if the junk had cleared the steamer. Captain Lloyd also stated that at times the junk on his starboard touched his vessel's side, but only the boom.

One of the lost junk's crew maintained that he himself had trimmed the lamp.

Mr. Tinson, cross-examining, submitted that not more than 50 per cent. of fishing junkes carried nights.

Later.

The verdict of the Court was that the junk carried no light and that no blame is attached to the master and officers of the "Talthy-bius" for the collision.

## [20] STRIKE DOOMED.

**COLLAPSE EXPECTED SHORTLY.**

**FREMANTLE TRUCULENCE.**

*(Router's Service.)*

*London, October 9.*

The National Sailors' and Firemen's Union headquarters report that the strike is expected to collapse in the near future. A definite decision is to be taken at a meeting in London on Sunday.

A similar meeting will be held at Bristol and a ballot will be taken at Southampton while it is believed the strike has collapsed at Liverpool.

In consequence of the violent form, assumed by the seamen's strike at Fremantle, the Australian Steamship lines have decided to discontinue the call there until the restoration of normal conditions.

## [22] "SUI YTK" OFFICERS

**THE LAST OF THEM.**

**CHOICE OF DESTINATION**

A decision has been arrived at by the Police in conjunction with other officials which will finally dispose of the complement of the s.s. "Sui Yick," the ship seized by the strikers at Shannai, transferred to the Canton Government. The ship was not allowed to troops and returned now to the owners owing to the coxswain having brought it into British waters.

Most of the men were put across the border last Monday but a few officers and others were detained for further enquiries. It has now been decided to send these away from the Colony to places of their own choosing and the last will be seen of them in the course of the last few days.

In spite of the strong and eloquent British opposition the Tariffs Committee overwhelmingly voted a recommendation to be submitted to a plenary meeting of the Conference to increase terminal and transit charges on all telegrams over the world.
It is estimated the increase will average about 20 per cent.
The Committee also unanimously agreed to a recommendation that the Press rate be applicable day and night throughout the 24 hours all over the world.
It is regarded as certain that the plenary meeting will adopt this measure which is an important concession to News Agencies and newspapers, going far to counter-balance the increased charges.

## [25] EARLIER NEWS.

It is expected that the International Telegraph Conference which opens in plenary session on the 10th will increase the terminal transit charges on all telegrams. It is understood that the increase will not be a flat rate but will vary from ten to thirty per cent., countries belonging to the telegraph union being divided into a number of categories en-titling them to greater or lesser increase according to their financial necessities.

The British representatives, in committee, steadily opposed the increase and had the moral support of the American observers, but were overruled by the majority of other countries which emphasised the necessity to offset increased working costs. The increase will apply to Press, besides ordinary and urgent messages. —Reuter.
