**HONGKONG, TUESDAY**

**BARE ARMS.**

**CANTON'S AMAZON DETACHMENT.**

**BORBED HAIR FIGHTERS.**

With their hair bobbed and their arms bared, about twenty women bodyguards have arrived at Canton. The complete description of them in the "Wah Tsz Yat Po" is that they are also barefooted, they carry rifles, appear to be ferocious, and roll their eyes about when looking ahead!

This body of Amazons marched into Canton with the commandant of a detachment of Farmers' Volunteers, the last-named having been defeated and disbanded by General Chan Kwing-ming's troops.

The Volunteers once held sway in the Hoi Luk Fung area, reports the special correspondent of the "Wah Tsz Yac Po."

It is presumed that when the Farmers found that the anti-Reds had expelled the Reds from that territory, the leader mustered his awe-inspiring Amazons and made his way to Canton to offer his services to the Reds.

Reds move to Base.
Trainloads of Red troops have been leaving Canton by rail for Shektan, the railway station on the Canton side and next to Shekhang (40 miles or so from the southern metropolis).

The 1st division of the Red Army has arrived at Shektan and encropped. General Cheung Kai-shek has made Shektan his army headquarters and his main strength is massed there.

Having gone to Whampoa and there obtained an issue of a large lot of arms. Wong Kam-guen, commander of the Strike Pickets, is reported to be equipping the pickets with a view to their volunteering for active military service against the anti-Rede on the East River.

## [22] CONFUCIUS DAY.

**TO-MORROWS CELEBRATION IN TOWN.**

This year has seen a marked tendency towards observing Confucius Day, which falls on the 27th day of the 8th moon (tomorrow) according to the Chinese Calendar) as a general holiday. It seems as if the time is not far distant when the anniversary of the birth of China's most famous sage—although not ranking as one of the four big festivals—will take second place to New Year in the round of Chinese festivities in Hongkong.

It is estimated that more than half the shops in the Colony will be closed to-morrow whereas in previous years only a small percentage observed the day.
At the Tai Ping Theatre, the local Confucius Society will hold a mass meeting with speeches from the society's officials, followed by a dramatic show. Nearly all the schools attended by Chinese students will have a day off and some of the schools have arranged their own celebration.
The amisture of the Ching Shing Harvest Society are assisting the Confucius Society by presenting old-style Chinese items on the stage.

## [23] BOYCOTT OVER?

**SWATOW FREE TO TRADING VESSELS.**

**ALL NATIONS WANTED.**

Merchant vessels of all nations can now proceed freely to Swatow, says the Foreign Commissioner of the port. Mr. Wong Keung.
In a letter to the Hongkong Chinese General Chamber of Commerce, Mr. Wong explains that since the troops of his party retook Swatow, steps have been taken to restore commerce. Accordingly all mercantile marine ships may put in there for commercial purposes, he concludes.
