## [8] HIGH HOPES.

**GERMANY AND LEAGUE.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Locanno, October 12.*

There is extraordinary optimism in Conference circles. The consensus of opinion is that the main difficulty in regard to Germany's entry into the League of Nations has been overcome.

A communiqué on the Conference says there is continued general discussion on questions raised by participation in the covenant of the League of eventual signatories to the Security Pact.

A request for supplementary explanations formulated by the German Delegation gave rise to an exchange of views of various Delegations which caused the work of the Conference to progress towards the adoption of a satisfactory solution of the point under examination.

The Conference adjourned until Tuesday.

HOW IT HAS BEEN DONE?
Paris, October 12.

A French source at Locarno states that the German Delegation asked for 24 hours in which to consult Berlin before finally assenting to Article 16 of the Covenant.

It is stated an agreement is probable on the following basis:—

Germany to enter the League on ordinary conditions, but to allay German anxieties the Allies will make a declaration investing the Assembly of the League with the right of interpreting the Covenant and recognising that the contribution of any State called to enforce the decisions of the League in the event of war shall be proportionate to its means.

## [9] THE DISCORDANT NOTE.

While the European delegates are doing their utmost in the interests of peace at Locarno, the German monarchists are striking a discordant note.

General Von Arninn, unveiling a regimental monument, referred to the "loyalty to our military oath which we swore to our war lord" and alluded to Germans' gratitude and unswerving loyalty towards the Kaiser.

Later, President von Hindenburg in field marshal's uniform drove up to the monument and took the salute of the Reichswehr Company of the nationalist organisations, regarding which the Republican Press is most indignant.

Meanwhile the German National extremist organs deplore the meagre results of the Locarno Conference and express the fear that the German delegation may be satisfied with vague promises from the Allies regarding Germany's vital demands.

## [12] STILL TO STAND.

**RUBBER RESTRICTION POSITION**

**THE NEW PERCENTAGE.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, October 12.*

In connection with the Colombo cable of the 10 inst. in regard to rubber exportation, the Colonial Office emphasises that there is no ground whatever for any suggestion that decisions have been reached in the sense of that message.

As the Secretary of State stated in Parliament, he is working on the Stevenson scheme constantly, and confidential proposals of all sorts are frequently being exchanged between the Government in London and the East in regard to the operation of the scheme, but no decision has been reached for changing the scheme.

With regard to Rubber Restriction, it is understood the present rate of percentage output will expire on the 31st inst. when a new percentage will be fixed.

No decision has yet been made, but it is possible an official announcement will be issued within the next fortnight.
