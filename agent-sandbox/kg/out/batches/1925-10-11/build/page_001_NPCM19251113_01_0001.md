## [9] FENG WAITS.

**STILL THE MASTER OF PEKING.**

**FENCHMEN REVERSE?**

*(Router'la Service.)*

*Peking, Nov. 13.*

Foreigners visiting Fungchow this morning report that Feng Yu-hsiang and Mukden troops are both quartering there and are fraternising.

Several pro-Mukden officials have fled from Peking.
There is no sign yet of Feng. Yu-hsiang evacuating Peking as has been reported he suggested doing.

A communiqué issued quotes a telegram dated November 8 from Chang Tyung-chang stating that Fengtian troops had suffered a loss of nearly 2,000 killed in heavy fighting at Huchow, Yinchsien and Kitchen.
The enemy's losses are estimated at two brigades.
The telegram goes on: "In compliance with your orders to honour General Wang Shih-chen's and Chao Keh-houn's peace suggestions, and pending further instructions, we have assumed a defensive position."

## [12] STREET ACCIDENTS.

**TWO CASES REPORTED YESTERDAY.**

A Chinese boy in alighting from a tram car at Pi'an'y East, near the Soldiers' Club, yesterday afternoon, was knocked down by a motorcar, and was injured in his legs.

The driver of the car, Mr. Harry Kong, of Causeway Bay, took the injured boy to the hospital, and in his report of the accident he also stated that he lost at the time his pocket book containing a sum of $10, his driving licence and some papers.

The fifteen-year old daughter of a boutman was slightly injured by running across the road at Praya East, near Percival Street, and being knocked down by a motor car driven by a Chinese yesterday.

For not giving correct particulars when "clearing" his junk, he owner was fined $5 at the Marine Court this morning.

## [13] REDS EXECUTED.

**NAVAL OFFICERS SHOT AT KONGMOON.**

**CANTON FLAGSHIP STIR**

Obscurity shrouds the motive behind the execution of three Red naval officers at Kongmoon.
It is believed that the three officers were attached to the "Chang Shan," (formerly, the "Wing, Fung" and renamed in honour of Dr. Sun Yat-sen), flagship of the Canton Navy.

When the Soviet influence reached its zenith a Russian commander, was appointed to the "Chang Shai." Since then several vague reports—not all substantiated—have been given in connection with her. One was a supposed mutiny in which the Russian chief was supposed to have walked the plank. A subsequent story was that the cruiser had disappeared!

Capt. Au-Yeung is the Chinese commander and it is thought that the executions were under his authority. "The officers are stated to be suspected of disloyalty but there is also a hint that a scramble for power behind the scenes may have some connection with the matter. Captain Au-Yeung is at Pickland, the port of Kongmoon, write the "China Mail" correspondence, is command of the Center River Defence Field.

## [17] RED SCHOOL ROW.

**AGITATOR STUDENTS AID POLITICIANS**

**BIG SUMS COVETED.**

By stirring up agitation among the younger students attending colleges and universities Canton politicians are alleged to be making a determined move to secure control of the very large sums tied up in endowment funds.

Several months ago the Kung Yee Hospital control was transferred to the Cukton University of which Chew Loo is the principal Dr. S. F. Lee of Hongkong was said to have given up his post at the Kung Yee because he did not concur with the decision.

Now organisations of junior students are said to be actively working on Red political initiative to get the properties standing to the name of the educational institutions out of the present holders' hands.

A telegram to the "Wah Tsa Yat Po" says that educationalists have been refused payment of 40 per cent expenses which they asked for. A general meeting is to be held which may decide to hold a strike in all schools, etc., in Canton on Monday if the request is not accepted to.

ANT-NORTH OFFERSIVE?
Canton is also reported by the same paper to be forming bases on Kwanglung's northern friendlies for an offensive against the provinces of Fukken and Kiangsi.

## [18] A SAD TIME

**MAGISTRATE'S SYMPATHY WITH FORESTERS.**

**AN ABUSED PERMIT.**

During the hearing of a forestry case at the Central Ministry in which Mr. H. Green gave evidence, Mr. R. E. Lindseil struck a sympathetic note by remarking that he understood the department had been having a sad time. Mr. Green agreed that it had indeed been a very sad time.

The case was against ten women who were charged with unlawful possession of wood cut from the plantations in Wong Nei Chong village. It was stated that the first defendant held a permit from a contractor and the department to remove wood from a certain area. Under cover of this permit the others entered the plantations and were engaged in wholesale removal of wood until they came under the notice of an Indian constable.

His Worship was surprised to hear that all the defendants had readily furnished bail money of $25 each. This was explained by the fact that a crowd of friends and relatives followed the arrested women to the Police Station and bailed them out. They were stated to be fairly well to do and not of the ordinary coolie class.
The woman who held the permit was discharged with a warning, and the others were fined $10 each.

## [20] NO SHORTAGE OF FOOD.

Reports that there was any shortage of food on Pratas and that this was responsible for the deaths amongst the coolies engaged on work there are entirely without foundation according to the definite statement of a high Chinese official staying in the Colony in whose hands has been the Chinese Admiralty's responsibility for the erection of the station.

There were 180 bags of rice untouched when the coolies amongst whom had spread the beri-beri were taken off by the "Haishing," he informed a "China Mail" representative. Wild reports that the men in question had lived on grass and seaweed for a week were absolutely false—in fact, reliefs which had been despatched from Shanghai some time before the beri-beri had spread and had proceeded to Frutas by the "Haishing" on the occasion of her last visit took no supplies with them, relying for the time being on the stocks left behind.

Asked whether...if there seemed a possibility that heri-beri had spread through the agency of the rice in question it was advisable that this rice should be used, the "China Mail" informant replied that this had been put to the contractors...who were responsible. They had since supplemented their supplies. The Chinese Government had also despatched to Pratas a very large supply of tinned stuffs from Hongkong, primarily for the Admiralty men manning the station, but for the others if needed.

With the last reliefs had also gone a certain number of planters with seeds in order that fresh vegetables might be grown. There were great possibilities on the island in this latter respect and Indian rice was already being grown. A large open space used previously for the laying out of fish and seaweed was a most fertile spot and the island was not the barren desolate spot it was made out to be, stated our informant. A few indigenous coconut trees were bearing fruit, also newly planted ones.

The men who had gone to Pratas for relief work were conversant with the position there and some of them had previously worked there, being part of a detachment which had been taken away as the work was nearing completion and their work, it was thought, would no longer be required. There had been two cases of beri-beri at that time and they were informed, together with the others, that it had'sprend. They had suitable medicines with them.

The number of deaths had been very much over-reported, stated the official in question. There had originally been 92 men engaged on the work of building, over 30 had been taken off on a previous occasion when there were two cases of beri-beri and as the work was nearing completion it was thought their services would no longer be required and a message he had received stated that over 30 were taken off by the "Hai-shing" when the disease had spread so rapidly. Presuming that all those not taken off were dead they only amounted to some 24 and not 62 as had been reported.

**SUBMARINE BELIEVED LOST**

**SIXTY MEN ABOARD.**

*(Reuterr's Service.)*

*London, Nov. 12.*

The Admiralty announces that Submarine M1 during exercises this morning was seen to dive 15 miles south of Star Point.

The submarine has not been seen since.

Every effort is being made to locate her and establish communication.

The missing submarine is a Portsmouth vessel with a normal complement of $60$.

## [24] CHRISTMAS MAIL.

**PARCELS COUNTER SCENES**

The parents' counter at the General Post Office was literally besieged from morning by those anxious to deliver their packages before the last minute rush for the Christmas and New Year mail closing for Home at 5 p.m. to day. This mail is due in London on December 17.

Over 1,450 parcels had been received for despatch by the s.s. "Mantua" up till yesterday afternoon, and it is expected that last year's total of 3,050 parcels will be exceeded.

Although faced with an impossible task owing to shortage of hands and the calls on the parcels' staff to attend to inward deliveries, they are determined to get away all the parcels offering to-day even by working well into the night.

The Christmas letter will close on November 24.

Mrs. B. Thompson is in the French Hospital with malaria. Her friends will be pleased to hear she is making good progress.

Moscow, November 12.
