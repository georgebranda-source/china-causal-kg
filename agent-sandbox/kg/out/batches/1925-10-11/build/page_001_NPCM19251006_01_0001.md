**SINGAPORE TO FIND A THIRD.**

**INTEREST THAT RISES.**

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

*Singapore, October 5:*

At the urgent request of the Secretary of State, the Government has agreed that the Colony should advance the Hongkong Government sums not exceeding one million pounds, these advances to come out of the currency guarantee fund.

Upon enquiry locally the “China Mail” was informed that owing to the delay which would be involved, it had not been anticipated that the loan would be raised on the London market, and some Colonies had signified willingness to co-operate in providing the money. Such Colonies, in common with Hongkong, conducted the negotiations for the employment of their reserves through the Crown agents and such a loan as that to Hongkong would provide suitable employment.

It can be readily understood that a loan for a short period with suitable interest would serve the interests of such Colonies much better than a long term loan and it is understood that the Hongkong Government looks upon the loan to them as a short one, in fact the interest to be charged them will be on a rising scale so it is obvious that local borrowers will be expected to pay back bbrrowed sums comparatively soon.

## [8] MOTORS COLLIDE.

**DRIVERS' CONFLICTING VERSIONS.**

**ROBINSON ROAD REPAIRS**

The repairs which are being effected to Robinson Road were indirectly the cause of an accident which had a court sequel this morning.

The owners of two motor cars which collided at the junction of Robinson Road with Mosque Street were plaintiff and defendant respectively, the former being Noor Ahmed of the Wanchai garage and the latter, Leung Yuen-cheung.

The plaintiff's driver claimed that the defendant's driver drove on the part of the road which was not being repaired at a speed of 20 miles an hour and gave plaintiff, who was turning the corner, no chance of avoiding him. The plaintiff had the right of way on that portion of the road.

The defendant said that he was going at 10 miles an hour. He turned into the portion of the road which was up when he saw the other car coming and at the time of the collision was stationary. He did not hear plaintiff sound a horn.

Cross-examined by Mr. H. S. Fitzroy, who appeared for the plaintiff, defendant admitted that the portion of the road which was not up was not much wider than the width of his car. There was a big hole where the road was up. He denied that he had admitted that he was in the wrong by asking the other driver to get repairs effected and charge him (defendant) with them.

His Honour found that defendant had been guilty of dangerous driving and dismissed the counterclaim which he had brought forward. His Honour also adjourned the matter of damages. (Plaintiff claimed $463 for actual damages and loss of use of car).

## [10] HONGKONG, TUESDAY, OCTOBER 6, 1925.

**THIRD COUP.**

**CANTON RED'S LATEST MOVE.**

**GENERAL DETAINED?**

Yet another coup—the third of the present régime—has been brought off by General Cheung Kai-shek, the commander of the Canton Cadets. This coup has resulted in the disarming of several hundreds of non-Cantonese mercenaries in Canton, the rounding-up of about sixty officials and the reported detention of General Hung Hak-mu of Szechuan.

The net result is the further strengthening of the hands of the Cadet commandant. General Hung has a strength estimated at 10,000 bayonets on the North River, and the morale of his rank and file is bound to be badly shaken.

It was only a few weeks ago that General Hung, together with another non-Cantonese adventurer, brought his following into Canton province. Inspired reports from Canton hinted that he was welcomed by General Cheung who had a view to a non-Cantonese alliance to overcome the Cantonese armies should they ever become united.

General Hung once held a command in his native province. In the earlier days of the Republic he was an ardent admirer of Choy Ngok (Chai Yo) who died after inflicting the first check on Yuan Shi-kai's monarchical plans. Hung is said to be allied to Tuchun Tang Chi-yao of Yunnan.

## [11] CANTON'S ADMIRAL

**RESIGNATION OF RUSSIAN CHIEF**

**ALLEGED MONEY DISPUTE:**

一

The Russian officer who has been appointed as admiral of the Canton Navy is reported to be contemplating early resignation on account of financial trouble.

It was expected, says the "Wah Tsz Yat Po" that $500,000 would be paid for refitting the Canton Navy when the appointment was made. Keen disappointment is felt at non-payment. The admiral is believed to be asking, shortly, for leave to return to Russia, where he will then hand in his resignation to Canton.

## [20] “SUI YIK” SCENES.

**DISARMED TROOPS SENT TO CANTON.**

**MORE ARMS SEIZED.**

The large number of Cantonese troops from the s.s. "Sui Yik" were safely placed over the border on Thursday. The Chinese authorities at Shumchun later conveyed the soldiers to Canton by train.

Prior to the men being placed on the train at Kowloon, they were again searched and several loaded revolvers were found on them. The weapons had apparently been secreted on the vessel and the attempt to take them away proved unsuccessful.

The vessel was handed back to her local owners, the Fuk Hoi Company, but not before a thorough search had again been made when 30,000 rounds of ammunition in addition to the seizures already made were found concealed in unlikely places on the boat.
