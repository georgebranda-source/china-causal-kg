## [6] CANTON DELTA.

**MINED OFF BOGUE FORTS.**

**VESSELS WARNED.**

**MOIST EMOGATE SPECIAL FLOTS.**

A notice to mariners posted on the Harbour Office notification board contains the information that the Canton river channel has been mined off the Bogue Forts. Vessels proceeding to Canton are warned that they should take special pilots for proceeding through the mine field.

This information was imparted to the British Consul at Shamcen by the Canton authorities. the "China Mail" was informed upon enquiries. The pilots which will be required were not specified but it is presumed that they will be pilots engaged by the Canton authorities with the knowledge of the whereabouts of the mines.

The presence of the two Peking gunboats in the Hongkong harbour is thought to have a bearing on this, presumably, hasty action on the part of the Canton Government.

A rumoured consular message regarding responsibility in the event of the mines taking effect is denied by the naval authorities.

The s.s. "Honam" was escorted by the river gunboat "Cicala" upon her voyage to Canton yesterday.

## [7] JUNK LOST.

**STRUCK BY RIVER STEAMER.**

**ALL HANDS SAVED.**

A fishing junk carrying a crew of 17 was struck amidships on the starboard side by the s.s. "Honam" at 8.40 a.m. yesterday in Capsuimun Channel near the south-west point of Wa Nam Island. The junk was on the way to Hongkong, while the "Honam" was on a trip to Canton.

The muster of the junk made an effort to gain harbour, but the vessel became water-logged and sank near Ma Wah lighthouse. All the members of the crew were saved by another fishing boat which happened to be in the vicinity.

The lost junk contained an armoury of three cannons, 60 bs. shot, three rifles, 30 rounds ammunition, three muskets and 10 lbs. gunpowder.

## [8] SUNK BY PIRATES.

Another junk, sunk by pirates in the Kongmoon district on October 2, constitutes a danger to navigation and mariners are warned that it is about three miles above Wang Moon Customs station with its hull submerged at high water and 30 feet of its mast showing.

Owing to local conditions, the authorities announce that it is not possible to maintain wrecking marks and masters are warned that they should navigate the district with caution.

## [9] PASSING THROUGH.

**MACAO GOVERNOR ENTERTAINED.**

His Excellency the Governor of Macao, Colonel Maia de Magalhues, is passing through the Colony on his way to take up his appointment, accompanied by his wife; and in honour of their arrival the Portuguese community have arranged a function to take place at the Club Lusitano at 5.80 to day.

The Club de Recreio and the Socorros Mutuos are also cooperating in the welcome.

## [10] China's First

**HONGKONG, THURSDAY, OCTOBER 15, 1925.**

**SHIP'S OFFICERS.**

**LOCAL VIEWS ON NOTICE CLAUSES:**

**A GOVERNMENT MATTER?**

There is nothing besides consideration for his own and his fellow-officers' interests to prevent a ship's officer from signing an agreement under which he can be dismissed at 24 hours' notice, whereas he himself has to give a month's notice, said a local Guild official to a "China Mail" representative, but he thought such cases were rare. In proof of his contention that a few China coast smaller shipping firms draw up such one-sided agreements the Guild official showed the reporter certain such documents.

The interview, granted yesterday afternoon, arose out of an article in "Shipping and Engineering" in which such agreements were termed as iniquitous and the desirability was also stressed of following the advice of the "Suian" Piracy Commission and of the Senior Naval Officer in the matter of making illegal the 24 hours' mutual notice clause contained in many Hongkong agreements.

Three months have passed by since the fatal riot in the international settlement at Shanghai provoked the Chinese boycott of British residents which has done untold harm. Delays are doubtless inevitable in diplomacy, especially when so many Powers have to be consulted and brought into line. But for Great Britain, whose commercial interests in China are older and still more important, relatively, than those of any other Power, this long postponement of action of any kind has been peculiarly unfortunate. As it is, though the representatives of the British ratepayers predominate in the Municipal Council administering the settlement, the Council itself is responsible to the whole diplomatic corps at Peking. Whether the Foreign Office could have done more by independent diplomacy without violating the principle that the Powers should deal in concert with Chinese questions is a matter on which our Government and the British firms interested in the trade of the Far East seem to differ. ## [17] MILITARY STORES.

**EXPORT AND CARRIAGE PROHIBITED.**

**TO-DAY'S ORDER.**

The exportation from the Colony or the carrying through the waters of the Colony of arms, ammunition, gunpowder or military stores without the permission of the Hongkong authorities is prohibited.

A proclamation to this effect was issued by the Hongkong Government this morning, the provisions extending to "any articles which the Governor might judge capable of, being converted into or made useful in increasing the quantity of military or naval stores."

The powers to issue the proclamation are contained in the Military Stores (Exportation) Ordinance, 1862.

## [19] $4,000 FIRE:

**WESTERN DISTRICT BLAZE.**

**SHARK FIN STOCKS LOS**

The Central Fire Station received a call at 1.50 this morning through the fire alarm post at Bonham Strand. Appliances were despatched both from here and Kennedy Town, and the Wanchai equipment was also summoned later.

The building involved. was a three-storeyed one bearing No. 28; Queen's Street, and it was found to be all ablaze on the arrival of the fire appliances soon after the call.

A number of hoses connected with two street hydrants and two motor pumps drawing water from the sea gave very effective results when directed on the flames from the building opposite, and the stop signal was given at 2.55 a.m.

The origin of the fire has been traced to a cockloft on the ground floor, used as a truck godown and a bicycle shop. The trucks and other stock were slightly damaged, the amount being estimated at $300, which was covered with the Fuk On Insurance Company.

The first floor was occupied by an import and export firm, and the damage to contents amounted to about $2,000, also covered with the same Company for $1,500.
The second floor was vacant, and the top floor was occupied by a shark fin dealer, who estimated his loss at $1,500, the stock being insured with the Tung On Company for $2,000.

The building, although not gutted, also suffered considerable damage by fire.
