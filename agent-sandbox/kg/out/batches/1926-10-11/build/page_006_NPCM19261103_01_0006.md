## [9] NAVAL TRADITIONS UPHELD.

### Series of Squalls Beggar Description.

A stirring account of the sinking of the British Naval sloop "Valerian" was given by Commander Usher at the court-martial to enquire into the loss of the ship which honourably acquitted all the nineteen survivors. It found that every one was at his post until the first, and that all had maintained British Naval traditions by exemplary conduct.

## [10] Störm.Warning.

<sub>section under [9] NAVAL TRADITIONS UPHELD.</sub>

Commander Usher reported that economical steaming was necessary on sailing from the Bahamas, owing to the scarcity of bunkers. He received warnings when 200 miles from Bermuda, so he increased his speed to a maximum of 9½ knots with one boiler. Further warnings led him to start a second boiler, and increase the steaming rate to 11½ knots, but he slowed down to 10 knots owing to the bearings becoming heated. He was only five miles from Gibbs Hill at eight o'clock in the morning; but, contrary to anticipation, was unable to make land and headed for the south-east.

The wind increased and, thundis capped with the maximum engine power at her disposal, the ship was seaworthy until one afternoon when a series of squalls, begging description, ensued. The "Valerian" healed over 70 degrees, but righted herself again. More squalls followed and the engines stopped. The ship was apparently aground, though no breakers were visible. She turned over and slowly her funnels became submerged; her boilers exploded; and she sank.

## [16] PLOT IN SPAIN.

**NUPPED IN BUDD BY THE POLICE**

**SIXTY ARRESTS.**

Perpignan; Nov. 2

What is believed to be an extensive revolutionary plot directed against Spain was nipped in the bud by the prompt action of the Police in arresting about 60 persons at various points of the frontier who, it is alleged, furnished field telephones and military equipment to Spanish Italian, and British subjects disguised as tourists.
The suspicions of the French authorities were aroused by the despatch of numerous cipher telegrams.

The frontier trains were closely scrutinised, resulting in widespread arrests unearthing the conspiracy whereby the revolutionaries were supposed to have arranged to assemble at Estagell, Bataille, and Milhas, and thence proceed across the frontier and launch a revolution.—Reuter.

The newspapers deny that there is any foundation for the recent "disquieting rumours." The "Nacion" says that the Government cannot be blamed for taking any necessary measures for the security of the State.—Reuter.
