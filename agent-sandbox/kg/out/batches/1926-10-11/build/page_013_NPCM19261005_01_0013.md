## [16] China the Retail

**HONGKONG, TUESDAY, OCTOBER 5, 1926.**

**AFRICA'S POLICY.**

**HELPFUL ASSOCIATE OF**

**A NOTABLE STATEMENT:**

*Rugby, October 4.*

General Hortzog, Nationalist Premier of South Africa, arrived in London to-day to attend the Imperial Conference.

To representatives of the Press he made a notable statement. He wished to assure the people of Great Britain that at the Conference the Union of South Africa had no wish to be anything but a helpful associate in advancing the interests of the British community of nations.

"We intend to apply ourselves to the task of the Conference in a spirit of co-operation, seeking by united action to promote the common interests of us all. One of the main tasks—if not the main task—with which the Conference will be confronted will be to have co-operation between the members of the States of the British Empire placed on a more certain and satisfactory basis. ## [19] RUM-RUNNERS.

**SURPRISE FOR A TRAWLER'S CREW.**

**AN OLD OFFENDER.**

The sudden appearance of coast-guards at Bayway, New Jersey, caused a scuttle of a crew unloading the cargo of a large British steam trawler, which, the officials describe as an old rum-running offender.

It apparently sailed through the coastguard fleet, eluded the police boats, and had unloaded 1,000 out 2,000 cases of liquor on board before the coastguard arrived and took possession.—Reuter's American Service.

## [20] BANDITS BUSY.

**CAPTURE OF AMERICAN MISSIONARIES.**

A delayed despatch from Changsha, dated September 29, which has just reached the American Legation, reports the capture of three American missionaries by bandits at Maliwan in West Hunan, namely, Cal. Beck and Minerva Wall, both of the Reformed Church, and Lewis Koebbe, of the Evangelist Church.
The State Department has been informed, and steps are being taken to secure a release—Ruter

## [22] DOCK STRIKE.

**MEN REFUSE TO OBEY LEADERS**

**2,200 MĂN INVOLVED.**

London: Oct. 4.

Work has been brought "to a standstill" at Tilbury Docks in consequence of a lightning strike of 2,200 dockers. "Thirteen steamers, including food ships, are idle."

The dispute is due to the Port of London Authorities refusing a permit to Mr. Attwood, the Trade Union Branch Secretary, to enter the docks.

Resentment in the connection has been smouldering since the ban was imposed after the general strike in May. The Union did not ask the men to strike and the men are not willing to obey Mr. Attwood's repeated appeals to resume work.

The principal ships in dock are the Orient Line's "Osterley" and the P. & O. "Kashmir", "Cathay" and "China." — Reuter

The dockers leader, Mr. Thompson, at the conclusion of an interview with the shipowners at Tilbury declared that he had received an understanding that the Port of London Authority would call a meeting to discuss the grievance. Meantime he had recommended the men to return, but was unable to guarantee that the men would obey.—Reuter.

## [23] BACK TO WORK.

**NINE THOUSAND MORE MINERS RESUME**

**NOTTS AND DERBY MEN.**

Nearly 9,000 more miners resumed work to-day, 7,000 of them being in the counties of Nottingham and Derbyshire, where the Districts Executives have recommended the acceptance of the Government's proposals.

It is authentically stated that the total number of persons employed at the pits producing coal was to-day 171,419.—British Wireless Service.

## [29] FIVE-DAYS' WEEK.

**DEMAND OF AMERICAN WORKERS**

**"DEFINITE GOAL"**

*Detroit, Oct. 4.*

A five-days week for all workers is declared to be the definite goal of the Federation of Labour at the final meeting of the Executive Council prior to the 46th annual Convention to be opened to day.
This policy will be recommended to the affiliated organisations as the basis of negotiation for renewal of contracts with employers.

Reuter's American Service.

## [30] SOVIET CHEEK.

**BRITISH AND AMERICAN FLAGS REMOVED.**

*Tokyo, October 4.*

Hakodate messages state that a Soviet steamer arrived recently from Wrangel Island where she landed fifty emigrants, whilst three aviators surveyed the island from the air.

It is alleged that the members of the expedition removed the British and American flags, which had been planted there, substituting their own.—Reuter.
