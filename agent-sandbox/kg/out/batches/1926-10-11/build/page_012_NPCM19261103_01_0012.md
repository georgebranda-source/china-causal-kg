## [8] STARTLING CANTON REPORT.

On Responsibility for Preserving

Coming in the nature of a big surprise is a report received last night from Canton that the Strike Committee has forbidden members of a certain labour union to search shops for British goods!
The Chinese merchants responsible for this piece of news say that the Strike Committee feels that it is answerable to the authorities for the preservation of order in labour disputes arising immediately out of the boycott settlement.

"Volunteering" for the work of taking stock at all shops where foreign merchandise is carried, this Union of Fokis actually did start to make the pounds until the order was made by the Strike Committee, pointing out that such duties were the work of the strikers (in conjunction with the Committee for the Severance of Economic Relations).

Trattle has improved and increased but the under-tone of uneasiness has not been dispelled. The principal obstacle is the fear of running foul of those who, until the week-end maintained the common around the Steamboat Co.'s wharves, and of being found out by the andonymous publishers of the street posters enjoining no intercourse with "a certain nation."

Although news has come down from Kongmoon that the Surtax Bureau is now in operation and that cargoes can now be shipped up from Hong Kong, another report says that difficulties still exist. Such, however, are not attributed to the boycott, but to a demand by the cargo coolies' guild of prohibitive wages.

That confidence is felt in the situation at Kongmoon is evident in the announcement of the s.s. "On Lee" to resume the trip this week.

A New Steamboat

A new steamboat will join the Hong Kong-Canton run in a few days under the Chinese flag. This is the s.s. "Yuet On," built locally, for the Fook Yuet Co. Yesterday she proceeded from her anchorage at Cheung-sha-wan to the Po Tak wharf at the Praya.

A representative of the owners is having stated that the boat cost about $200,000 to build. A German will be in command when she sails.

The following details are also given: —length 192 feet, speed 13½ knots, can take 1,000 passengers and 300 tons of cargo.
Yet another "outside" steamer is joining the Hong Kong-Canton run. This is the s.s. "Kwong Foon". (formerly on the Canton-Wuchow), owned by the Sai Hing S.S. Co. of Hong Kong.
In spite of protection by the Canton Police, there is no increase in the traffic carried by the Steamboat Co. but intimidation on the West Bund had considerably decreased.

**DEAL IN IRON.**

**DO ALL FIRMS HAVE A LICENCE?**

**REFERENCE TO JARDINE'S**

Eleven well-dressed Chinese, each the proprietor of a marine store-keeper's establishment, appeared before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning to answer charges of infringement of the Marine Storekeep's Ordinance relative to the keeping of books.

Mr. M. K. Lo appeared on behalf of five of the defendants whilst Mr. S. H. Looseby represented the remaining six.

Opening the case on behalf of his clients, Mr. Lo said that he pleaded guilty to a purely technical offence. His clients carried on other business than that of purely marine stores. Hundreds of shops dealt in old iron without holding licences. It could not be within contemplation of the Ordinance for a licence did people no good, in fact, in many cases, it did a great deal of harm.

He did not think that Messrs. Jardine, Matheson and Co., Ltd., held a licence. He would like to ask for an adjournment for one week in order that he might have an opportunity for consulting Mr. C. G. Pertdue, the Assistant Director of Criminal Investigation.
Mr. Perdue raising no objection to the proposal, His Worship adjourned the case against Mr. Lo's clients for one week.

Speaking on behalf of the remaining defendants, Mr. Loseby said that he would plead guilty to a technical offence but would ask that His Worship dealt with the defendants as leniently as possible.

After hearing Mr. Perdue with regard to the number of entries that had been omitted by each of the defendants, His Worship imposed various fines ranging from $20 to $50.

## [12] STEAMER RAMMED.

**BROUGHT TO TAIKOO FOR REPAIRS.**

The United States Shipping Board steamer "Cross Keys" was rammed in Iloilo harbour, Philippine Islands by the s.s. "Consuelo" of Madrigal and Company, the latter being a regular caller at Amoy.

On inquiry from Mr. Victor Smith (Oriental Manager of the Shipping Board), the “China Mail” learns that the “Cross Keys” was tied in mid-stream at Iloilo when the s.s. “Consuelo” (in charge of the pilot who took in the “Cross Keys”) entered port. The “Cross Keys” was rammed on her port side midship.

A representative of the Taikoo Dock was sent to Manila for a general survey of the vessel. The approximate damage is estimated at $40,000: Minor repairs were effected in Manila to make the "Cross Keys" seaworthy for her trip to Hong Kong.

The “Cross Keys” went into dry dock at Taikoo yesterday afternoon and it will probably take a fortnight to repair her.
No damage was done to the cargo of the vessel when she was rammed.

Investigations are now being made by the harbour authorities of Iloilo.

## [15] H.K. BANK SUED

"Pulpy Mass" Salved From, Washed Garment.

"Is a Bank liable to proceedings if it declines (to pay out on a note which has been so mutilated that the numbers and date are missing although its denomination and signatures are decipherable? This is the point which Mr. Justice Wood is called upon to decide in the Summary Court this morning, the parties being Leo Lee-shi, a Chinese woman (who is the plaintiff) and the Hong Kong and Shanghai Banking Corporation whom she is suing for $500. What was left of the note in question was tendered in a state of pulp after having been washed, ironed and wrung whilst in the pocket of an under-garment."

Mr. H. S. Fitzroy (instructed by Mr. W. B. Hind) represented the plaintiff and Mr. Eldon Potter, K.C., (instructed by Messrs. Johnston, Stokes and Master) appears for the Hong Kong and Shanghai Banking Corporation.

Mr. Fitzroy said that the action was brought in respect of a Shanghai note for $500 which the plaintiff had obtained, together with another of the same denomination, from her husband's time in June, 1925. The plaintiff, upon receiving the notes, placed them in the catchpocket of an under jacket where they remained for some time. On washing day, the woman proceeded to wash this jacket, unfortunately not removing the notes when so doing. Having dried the jacket, the woman proceeded to iron it and it was not until this stage in the proceedings that she became aware of the presence of the notes. The iron coming against something hard, she examined the pocket and removed from it a lump of pulpy material.

## [18] SERIOUS DANGER.

**CANTON TO RETIRE FROM NORTH?**

**ALLIES ATTACKING:**

Canton is now seriously faced with more than a possibility of having to give up a material part of her conquered territory along the Yangtse valley.

Fighting broke out again near Hwangchow (which had not yet been retaken by Marshal Sun Chuan-fang's Allies) and the Southerners fell back after several hours, during which they did not attempt to counter-attack nor to hold their ground.
In south-west Kiangsi, the Allies are still moving out slowly to take up positions vacated by the Nationalists.

Wu Pei-fu is still making spasmodic attacks on the end of the Cantonese line, extending northwards from Hankow and claims the capture of Shinyang, which he lost in the last offensive against him.

Mass Campaign By Canton in Figures.

## [22] LUCK OF TWO INSPECTORS.

First in $2 Draw, Fanling Steeplechase.

Two senior officers of the Hong Kong Police are the exceptionally fortunate joint holders of the winning ticket (for a prize of nearly $10,000) in the special $2 cash sweep of the Fanling Hunt. decided at last Saturday's steeplechase meeting.

They are acting Chief Inspector C. F. Aris and Inspector J... Moss;
The number of the "winning ticket" was 5,457, which was purchased by Mr. Aris and Mr. Moss together. The exact amount of the prize was $9,537.15.

A Chinese lady living in the central district bought ticket No. 10,353 which drew the second prize of $2,724.90.

It is curious to note that the race on which this sweep was decided (the third of the day) was the only event in which an outsider won.

"Patrol," the "China Mail" turf correspondent expressed the opinion that Jambu (the favourite which ran second) was not fit. He also expected Wahkeena to beat the winner, but the former finished third through "refusing" a fence. The way was thus paved for The Golden Pheasant to beat his more fancied rivals. Congratulations will go out to these two popular officers in their great fortune on drawing the winner.

Both prizes of $454.15 (for un-placed starters) went to Chinese. One of these tickets was purchased on the course and the other before the meeting.
