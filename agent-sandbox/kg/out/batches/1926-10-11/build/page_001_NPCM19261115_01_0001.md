## [10] CARGO TO MOVE AGAIN.

Confirmation has been received in Hong Kong of the partial withdrawal of Canton's new boycott.
On Saturday it was reported in the "China Mail" that the Transport Union which had refused to work cargo on British ships as from last Monday, had now reseined their decision.

Further details to hand indicate that the president of that Union of cargo-boat workers "was mainly instrumental in this change of attitude.

The first resolution for a boycott was passed unanimously. Later, in the face of strong opposition, the president is said to have put through another resolution canceling the first.

## [13] A VILLAGE FEUD.

**MAN'S LIFE FOR PIG FODDER.**

**MANSLAUGHTER CHARGE.**

The growth of a quarrel between parties from two New Territory villages (in the Yunlong district) was described at the Criminal Sessions this morning during the trial of three men on a charge of manslaughter.

It was stated that the quarrel arose out of the use of a certain field for the purpose of dragging for a water weed used as pig fodder. The understanding was that the field, which adjoined the two villages, should only be used for such purposes when not containing paddy.

The quarrel arose out of the action of certain women from one village who went to the field and commenced to drag for the weed while paddy was growing in the field. They were chased away by men from the other village (the three prisoners, it is alleged, among them).

The prisoners then alleged that men from the adjoining village came out to start a fight at the instance of the women who had been driven away. The Crown witnesses allege that after scaring the women away, the three prisoners attacked certain other of the men from the adjoining village who were working in the fields.

According to the wife and daughter of the deceased, who died as a result of a blow from a spear, the deceased was merely tending his paddy field when he was

assaulted by prisoners. According to prisoners, the deceased took part in an organised attack against them and the deceased himself held the weapon which had been seized from him and later used for their self-protection.

The case is being heard before Mr. Justice Wood and a jury, the Attorney-General, the Hon. Mr. J. H. Kemp, K.C., is conducting the case for the Crown.
Mr. C. G. Alabaster, K.C., is defending.

## [17] London Views.

<sub>section under [14] WAR MOVEMENTS.</sub>

*London, Nov: 14.*

Showers of protests against the “illegal” tariff exactions are worthless, says the “Observer,” commenting on affairs in China. The West must make up its mind that the old treaty days in China have gone, never to return. The paper points out that India under British rule has tariff autonomy, will not be denied the same, and the only policy for the West is to deal in this spirit with the real authorities in China, wherever it finds them, and whatever politics they profess.

The journal declares that talk of Bolshevism confuses the problem in China as it confused the Anglo-Russian relations. China even in chaos has bulwarks countries old against communism or policies wearing the aspect of communism. Both countries are fundamentally nationalist, and the West, while paying lip service to the idea, has not truly convinced itself that nationalism in the East is growing and the old order has gone. It concludes that Russia understands this, and uses her knowledge.

The worst possible policy, not only for the defence of British interests but for China's own good, is further to acquiesce in fictions and meekly to accept any part of the blame for the present happenings in China, says a "Times" leader, emphasising the patience and lack of aggressiveness on the part of the British Government in spite of the campaign of persistent vilification and misrepresentation against Great Britain.

## [23] TALAMBA" MURDER.

**DEPORTEE PRISONER FOUND GUILTY**

**JURY RECOMMENDS MERCY.**

The trial was concluded on Saturday evening of the Chinese deportee from Singapore, charged with the murder of a fellow deportee on board the船. "Talambu."
Concluding evidence provided somewhat of a surprise in that two deportees called by Mr. H. G. Sheldon for the defence denied in the witness box that they had made certain statements to him at the police station concerning a flight which had taken place among the deportees on board prior to the stubbing on deck.

The jury retired to consider their verdict at 6-20 and did not return until 7-40. The foreman announced that they unanimously found the prisoner guilty but strongly recommended him to mercy in view of the fact that they were of opinion that he had strong provocation.

His Lordship said that the sentence would be forwarded to the proper authorities.

Sentence of death was then passed on prisoner.

Was Captain Barrett Given a "Life."

An argument has started as to whether Captain Barrett's innings of Saturday was entirely clear.

From the Press box he did not appear to give a chance. But he faced Bowker with his back to us and an incident may have been overlooked.

A privileged spectator who was at the Naval Yard end, whose word is not open to doubt, informs me that the Shanghai skipper had a life in Bowker's second over, when he had only made a very few runs.

That was the occasion on which Hong Kong conceded the only bye thus far—a boundary (4 runs) as the ball rolled on to the screen.
Tam Pearce, who was keeping wicket, did get his hands to the ball, or the ball struck him. Fielding at first slip, Ramsay also shot out his left hand and barely reached the leather without even being able to deflect it.
My informant maintains that Captain Barrett touched the ball with his bat. If that was so, he certainly had a "life" early on and Shanghai might not have made those 2nd and 3rd wicket stands which put them in a commanding position.

I noticed that Tam Pearce, in the first over, stood well back to Bowker. In the second over, Captain Barrett faced the Hong Kong speed merchant and Tam fielded close up. When that bye was given he immediately retreated. The "chance," if such it was, was not observed in the Press Box, where that particular view was "obscured." I am therefore open to correction, but if the spectator referred to is correct, then it is hard lines on Hong Kong.

## [27] Cork Civic Guard's Fate.

**OUTBURST OF DISORDER.**

*London, Nov. 14:*

An outburst of disorder analogous to the old anti-British riots occurred at Cork to night. Raiders armed with revolvers shot dead Sergeant Fitzsimmons, the officer in charge of the Civic Guard Barracks, at St. Luke's, a populous suburbs of Cork City.
It transpired later that three other barracks in the city and several barracks in the County of Cork were visited by armed men who overpowered the guards, searched the premises and removed documents.
The police and military are at present active throughout the city, holding up motor cars and interrogating pedestrians.—Heuter.
