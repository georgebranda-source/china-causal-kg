The 12th report of the Shanghai Mission to Rikisha men states:—
If in some former years the happy monotony of the peaceful routine of the Mission's work has tended to a measure of repetition in the annual report, this year, with its attendant political and social upheavals and unrest, has provided a sufficiency of alarms and menaces to the peace and safety of the work to provide quite sensational writing if considered desirable, but having weathered all successfully, it seems more fitting to dwell gratefully on what has been avoided.

## [5] *(no headline: column 1)*

A day memorable in the history of Shanghai is the beginning of the general strike which most seriously affected all the industrial sections of the city for over three months except the rikisha pullers who alone stood untouched by the strike movement, although most determined efforts were made to induce the men to stop work.
Strike promoters tried all methods at their command to attain their aim, intimidation, actual violence, inflammatory literature, promises of money and support, etc., but all failed. The rikisha man refused to be threatened pra cajoled.

## [7] Purchase of Building Site.

The long-talked-of and urgently needed new building has assumed a more solid aspect in-as-much as that May 19, 1925, saw the completion of the purchase of Lob No. R.C. 11252, as a site for the new Mission premises.

Situated in Sing Ming Road, Chapel, a densely populated coolist district, the position is considered most suitable.

In consequence of local disturbances building operations had to be postponed, but plans and estimates are now completed with the kind co-operation of Mr. A. C. Wheeler, of the Municipal Council's Public Works Department, who has also most generously consented to supervise the construction of the building, which will be in simple Chinese style and will accommodate in the hall about 700. Completion will depend upon conditions prevailing in Shanghai.

For the seventh year in succession the "North-China Daily News" made its appeal to the community on behalf of the rikisha men and, as on all former occasions, the appeal met with a sympathetic and generous response.
