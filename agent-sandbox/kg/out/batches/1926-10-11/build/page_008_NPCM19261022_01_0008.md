## [8] LEE FONG

Peking, October 12.—The fact that Mr. Fan Fu has gone to Mukden to consult Marshal Tsoglin as regards financial and other matters before coming to Peking to take up the finance portfolio is regarded by some quarters as inciting the decline in importance of Peking as a political centre.

The one hundred and twenty-sixth daily newspaper has started in Peking. It has assumed the title of "Chen Yen Pro" which roughly translated, means the "Right-Speaking newspaper." It says its purpose is to give accurate news, and a plea for public support its broadcast on this account.

At the age of 69, Mr. Robert William Drane, a shrimper, who lives in a cottage on the river side at Yarmouth, has made his 112th rescue from drowning by jumping into the River Yare, and saving Thomas Waters, aged six. Mr. Drane holds 16 medals, with four bronze clasps and one silver clasp, and nine diplomas of the Royal Humane Society.

Philadelphia, October 14.—The annual convention of the American Legion to-day selected San Antonio, Texas, as the meeting place for 1128. The convention meets next year in Paris. The convention defeated a resolution renfirming its position in favour of the world court. The opposition argued that the matter was politicak and that the American Legion is not political.

It is rumoured in naval circle that H.M.S. "Malaya" is likely to act as an escort to H.M.S. "Renown" when the latter all takes the Duke and Duchess of York to Australia, says a home paper. It is significant that both vessels are now being refitted at Portsmouth Dockyard. The only objection to the plan is that of expense, but the "Malaya" may accompany the royal tourists as far as the Malay States.

Peking, October 8.—All the Suit Gabelle funds under the control of Sun, Chun-fang will cease to be sent to the Central Government from now on owing to the financial pressure caused by the present war against the Cantonese. The funds of Marshal Sun's collection have actually been sent up to Peking for the last few months. But a telegram which reached Peking yesterday informed the Government that this will be discontinued from the month of September.

## [9] *(no headline: column 2)*

Berlin, October 5.—The United States aviation department have offered the French aviator Tarascon $25,000 to cross the Atlantic Ocean. Tarascon is starting the attempt on October 21.

Kansas City, Mo., October 10.
—Ralph Stout, managing editor of the Kansas City "Star," died at his home here to-day. Early this year he made a trip to the Far East in an effort to regain his health. After his return home he wrote a series of articles on the Philippines that attracted wide comment.

A special dinner dance will be held at the roof garden and ball room of the Hong Kong Hotel to-morrow night, on the occasion of the ball room being re-opened after the damage by fire on New Year's Day. The charge is $4 per head. Dancing is from 8 p.m. to midnight (fancy or evening dress optional).

Those who have received invitations are reminded of the dance arranged by the ship's company of H.M.S. "Hermes," to be held in the R.E. Theatre, Wellington Barracks, from 8.30 to 1.30 a.m. (to-night). The dance at the Seamen's Institute fixed for to-night by H.M.S. "Vindictive" has been cancelled.

Hong Kong racegoers will regret the impending departure from the Colony of Captain R. G. Sturges, R.M. A very fine type of sportsman, he has ridden both "on the flat" at Happy Valley and over the country at Fanling. Reynolds has, perhaps, been his most successful mount, from the public's point of view. If anything, Captain Sturges's first love was with the Fanling Hunt rather than as a gentleman rider under Jockey Club rules. His many friends will wish him many more years of fun in the saddle. Tonight the H.K. Royal Marines' Old Comrades Association are giving Captain Sturges a farewell dinner and a presentation to Captain Sturges, at the Cafe Restaurant Parisien. The "China Mail" joins with others in wishing him good luck and thanks him for highly appreciated help in the past.

## [10] LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS.

Official figures show that 63,673 tons of potatoes, valued at £377,968, were exported from Jersey this season the tonnage being the beaviest recorded, but the amount realised was one of the lowest.

Messrs. Gande, Price and Co., Ltd. have in stock the Birkenhead milk stout, which is claimed to be an invigorating and stimulating beverage, ideal for rheumatic invalids.

At 2 p.m. to-morrow the Bank of Canton, Ltd., will hold an extraordinary general meeting to pass resolutions to convert the capital of the company from sterling into Hong Kong dollars.

A fresh case of enteric fever (typhoid) was reported on Thursday. The patient is a Chinese from the Kowloon district. This was the only case of notifiable disease during the last 24 hours.

Nauen, October 2.—The Archbishop of Bordeaux has penned a sharp attack upon the French Nationalist orgian. "Action Franquise," declining that it represented a revival of paganism. It has been announced that the Pope's supporting the Archbishop in his stand.

No luck has attended the attempid salvage of the "Hindeng" the.27,000 tons ex-German cruiser sunk at Scapa Flow. Having partially raised the vessel several times, Messrs. Cox and Denks, Ltd., the salvage engineers, have runken again, and operations they be add to have been abandoned until after the winter.

This New Zealand Government for many months past has been preparing for full control of the marketing of its dairy produce and the new system has just been put into operation. In future all the dairy produce sent from New Zealand to England will be marketed through a Government Dairy Control Board, which will regulate the supply and also fix the selling price. The latter will be based upon actual transactions in the London markets, and it is declared that there is no intention of placing quotations at other than a fair level. That New Zealand will have much to gain by the development is obvious, for it means a very wide co-operative movement in the collection of the produce, while the goods will receive most careful supervision in their transit to England.

The first dance of the season at the Craigengower Cricket Club starts at 9.15 to-morrow night. These social gatherings have proved immensely popular in the past.

Berlin, October 5. Signor Mussolini was lauded by the Papal Delegate, Cardinal Merry Del Val, at the Assisi festivities on the occasion of the seventh centennial of Saint Francis. The Italian Minister for Public Instruction, Signor Fedele, pointed to the Duce's endeavours in the cause of religion in Italy.

Tokyo, October 14.—Admiral Heihachiro Togo, hero of the battle of the Sea of Japan, May 27, 1905, is bed-ridden due to nervous trouble. On account of his age, 79, his physicians are apprehensive. Admiral Togo practically destroyed the Russian fleet during the battle and virtually put an end to the Japanese-Russian war.

Peking, October 6.—Women students at the Sino-Russian University will be allowed, according to an order issued by the Ministry of Education quoted in the Chinese press "to-day, to enter the newly organised Peking National Academy for Women after passing entrance examinations. Men at the former Sino-Russian school will be allowed to continue their studies at the University of Law, according to the same order.

Soochow, October 6.—The 40th wedding anniversary of Dr. and Mrs. W. H. Park of the Southern Methodist Mission was celebrated this afternoon. Among the guests were many old friends who arrived in China in the '80s and '90s—those here in Soochow and some from Wusih. Dr. Park took charge of the Soochow Hospital in the early '80s. This work was founded by the late Rev. W. R. Lambuth, M.D., who was later made a bishop of the Southern Methodist Church.

Singapore, October 14.—Mrs. Briddon, a French lady, figured as the accused in a case of alleged cheating which was mentioned before the Second Magistrate yesterday morning. The matter involves a loan of $200 to the accused who was going to open a shop, in Orchard Road. The compliment, Mr. R. J. He Montigny alleges that he lent the $200 to the accused and that she signed a demand note for it. When he went to collect the money the accused asked for a receipt which he made out and gave to her. The allegation is that she thereupon destroyed the receipt.

A correspondent writing to the Editor of the "Daily Mail" says: — Sir, letters about "Young People's Manners" remind me of a recent experience. I was in an omnibus between Waterloo and Norwood. Three street urchins got in, very dirty and badly clothed; their ages would be about 11, 12, and 9. As only one seat was vacant the two bigger boys made the youngest one take it. They were bubbling over with fun and it was great pleasure to watch them as seats became vacant. The oldest one always waited to see if another passenger was entering; if not the second was allowed to take the seat, but was made to give it up immediately it was wanted. This continued until at last all the boys were able to have a seat just before it was time for them to get off. It was all done so quietly and nicely—just a look from bright blue eyes and a dirty liand pointing to a seat, but the finest aristocrats could not have been more polite.

News has been received from England that Mr. E.C. Peters, the taipan of Messrs. Jardine, Matheson and Co., Ltd., Tientsin, has severed his connection with the firm. It is understood that he intends to return to Tientsin.

Paris, October 12.—Forty officers including Admiral Yamamoto and one hundred and thirty young men of the Japanese training ship have arrived here. They placed a wreath on the grave of the unknown soldier and were received by Marshal Foch.

Mr. H. C. Steiner, who for some time past has been the Shanghai representative of the Goodyear Tyre and Rubber Company, has been transferred to Yokohama. Mr. Steiner is now in Yokohama and Mrs. Steiner expects to leave Shanghai for Japan some time before the end of October.

Professor Joseph Bayer of the Vienna Museum who in 1908 discovered the now famous "Venus of Willondorf," a small figure of a woman so named because it was found near Willendorf in Lower Austria, has excavated near the former place another sculpture of the same period, this one being made of ivory and also representing a woman, but with a height of nearly ten inches. It is much bigger than the first one, and all others of that period so far known. Both are estimated to be between 20,000 and 25,000 years old.

The first of the series of papers, arranged to be presented before the members of the Institution of Engineers and Shipbuilders of Hong Kong during the winter months, will be read this evening at six o'clock. The paper to be given is on the subject of "The Ship," and should prove of exceptional interest in view of Hong Kong's very life being bound up in shipping. The paper will be read by Mr. J. S. Gillingham, M.I.N.A., M.B.E., of the Royal Corps of Naval Constructors, and Chief Construction at the Hong Kong Royal Naval Dockyard. Mr. J. Ormiston, President of the Institution of Engineers and Shipbuilders of Hong Kong, will preside. Members, their friends and also ladies are invited to attend.
