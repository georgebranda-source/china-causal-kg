## [16] Acrimonious Discussion.

<sub>section under [14] China</sub>

An acrimonious discussion at the Miners' Delegate Conference preceded the vote on the South Wales proposal to "intensify the struggle, prevent outcrop work, endeavour to place an embargo on foreign coal, and to secure a Trade Union levy to support the

The rejection by the districts of the Government's plan left the Federation the only alternative of continuing the struggle during the Winter and striving to maintain the status quo and secure a more favourable compromise.
It is generally agreed that the Conference must immediately decide the Federation's policy.
The President, Mr. Herbert Smith, criticised the South Wales proposal amid protests, Mr. Smith threatening to leave the chair.
No amendment was submitted to the South Wales drastic scheme.

Henceforth the Executive's policy will be noteworthy. The Federation controls 85 per cent. of the safety men in South Wales, although the proportion in the rest of the country is smaller. The levy proposal will require a special Congress of Trade Unions.

To-day 3,300 more miners returned to the pits, making a total of 197,000.—Reuter.

## [17] Chance for Orators.

<sub>section under [14] China</sub>

*Rugby, Oct. 7*

The coal fields meetings rejected the Government's scheme for settling the coal dispute by 737,000 to 42,000 votes, whereupon the Miners' Delegate Conference to-day decided that the offers should be rejected officially.
A resolution was passed by a majority calling for the withdrawal of safety men from the pits and the placing of an embargo on coal imports, ordering miners to cease working outcrops, urging the Trades Union Congress to impose a levy on members to aid the miners, and recommending a speech-making campaign in the areas where the men have returned to work.

The delegates are continuing the conference to-morrow.

Meanwhile the number of miners now working was to-day again increased by several thousands, and is approaching 200,000.—British Wireless Service.

## [18] TRADE UNION LAW.

**CONSERVATIVES: SEEK AMENDMENTS.**

**SECRET BALLOT IMPERATIVE**

*Rugby, Oct. 7.*

For first time the annual conference of the Conservative Party, which was opened at Scarborough to-day, was presided over by a woman, Dame Caroline Bridgeman; wife of the First Lord of the Admiralty, occupying the chair.

One of the first subjects discussed was a proposal to amend the law relating to Trade Unions, and a resolution, which was carried unanimously, urged the Government to make illegal any strike without secret ballot; to increase the security of the individual worker against victimisation and intimidation on account of this political belief; to make mass picketing and picketing of a man's private residence illegal; and to require national accounts of Trade Unions to be audited by certified accountants. — British Wireless Service.

## [20] HONGKONG, FRIDAY, OCTOBER 8, 1926.

**RUM RUNNING.**

**SERIOUS CHARGE AGAINST A SCHOONER.**

**MASTER COMMITTED.**

Bridgewater (Nova Scotia),

The Master of the "auxiliary schooner "Grape Fisherman" has been committed for trial on a charge of attempting to ram, 75 miles from the American coast, two United States coastguard vessels, the officers of which gave evidence that they took up a position on either side of the schooner which suddenly repeatedly swerved, narrowly missing a collision. As a result of the confusion the schooner escaped in a fog bank.

The master of the schooner admitted carrying alcohol. He denied the other allegation, saying that he swerved in order to prevent the coastguards fouling the dog line.

He was released on bail of $3,000.

The trial opens on Tuesday.—
Reuter's American Service.

## [22] Mishap to Cobham.

<sub>section under [21] AVIATION.</sub>

"A slight mishap to-day befell Sir Alan Cobham whilst flying to Manchester, where Civic Week is being celebrated."

Experiencing minor engine trouble with his small Moth aeroplane he found it necessary to make a forced landing at Nuneaton. This was safely accomplished and, after some delay, he completed the journey on another machine, which was despatched from the London Aerodrome.
