## [13] MINERS TO DECIDE

Negotiations for the settlement of the coal dispute have been taking place to-day at Downing Street, and there have been rapid developments.

The Mediation Committee of the Trade Union Congress was received this morning by Mr. S. Baldwin, Prime Minister and the full Cabinet Coal Committee. The mediators stated that the Miners' Executive had authorised them to accept district agreements as a basis of negotiation, providing that there were safeguards that the same national principles, which were embodied in the last national agreements should run through all district agreements.

The Prime Minister, after leaving the Mediation Committee, consented to receive the Miners' Executive. Accordingly the "miners' leaders arrived at Downing Street and the Mediation Committee left, one of them saying: "Negotiations have definitely been opened and we are now leaving it to the miners."
The discussion between the members of the Government and the miners' leaders was adjourned for lunch and was resumed later in the day.

Meanwhile, it is understood that the Government has been in touch with the Coal Owners' Association. When the miner's leaders left Downing Street this evening it was announced that the discussion would be resumed tomorrow afternoon.

It is expected that the coal owners will visit Downing Street between now and then.—British Wireless Service.

## [14] BOLOGNA SEQUEL.

**DRASTIC MEASURES ENFORCED IN ITALY.**

**ANTI-FASCIST CAMPAIGN**

Indignation throughout Italy in consequence of the attempt to assassinate Signor Mussolini in Bologna is the subject of a report by the Minister of the Interior to the Cabinet which approved of his drastic measures for public safety us follows:

The revision of all passports for abroad and the cancellation from November 9 of all passports issued except those possessed by Italians abroad.

Severe "penalties, including the use of armed, force, for anyone attempting to leave or abetting attempts to leave Italy without a passport."

The suppression sine die of all publications opposed to the present regime.

The dissolution of all parties, societies, and organisations having anti-Fascist aims.

The strictest Police supervision of those who have committed or who have shown an intention of committing acts likely violently to subvert the social and economic institutions of the country and endanger the safety of the State.

Severe penalties for anyone illegally wearing a uniform or insignia of any institutions especially Fuscist organisations.

## [15] Attempts On High Persons.

<sub>section under [14] BOLOGNA SEQUEL.</sub>

The Minister of Justice explained and the Cabinet approved of a Bill for the defence of the State establishing the death penalty for anyone committing an act against the life, health, or personal liberty of the King, Regent, Queen, Crown Prince, or chief of the Government, also any act calculated to lead to the subjection of the State to a foreign Power, affect the independence of the State, or compromise its unity, and anyone revealing or procuring political or military secrets or committing acts calculated to cause armed risings or participating therein, or on anyone inciting civil war or pillage.
Complicity in some of the above-mentioned crimes is punishable by 15 to 30 years' solitary confinement. The instigating of such crimes by means of the Press or their condonation is punishable by solitary confinement of five to 15 years.

## [19] EMPIRE ECONOMICS.

**INDUSTRIAL STANDARDIATION REPORT.**

**ADVANTAGE TO COMMERCE.**

Several reports submitted by the Economic Committee of the Imperial Conference were approved by the meeting of the conference at a brief session this morning.
Those include a report on industrial standardisation within the Empire which states that the Committee was much impressed with the advantages that would accrue also to manufacturers and to buyers of goods to which the principle of standardisation was applied. It is recognised, however, not only that the principle of standardisation can be less advantageously applied in some industries than in others, but also that it is not one to be applied indiscriminately.

In particular the report points out that it is important to beware of the risk of impeding technical progress, and to see that suitable provisions for adequate revision are not overlooked.

A considerable amount of progress has been made within the Empire both in the extension of standardisation and in securing common standards, but the belief is expressed that much more could be done to the advantage of trade within the Empire and to the trade with the world at large. It is, therefore, recommended to the favourable consideration of the several Governments of the Empire that they should take steps to promote the further development of standardisation; should arrange for the exchange of information with other parts of the Empire; and, when common standards are possible and mutually advantageous, should co-operate, with other parts of the Empire in regard thereto.

## [20] Wool Statistic

<sub>section under [19] EMPIRE ECONOMICS.</sub>

The report on wpol statistics approved by the Conference contained the following resolution:—

“The Imperial Conference, impressed by the need for more complete, more uniform, and more prompt statistics regarding production, stocks, and consumption of wool throughout the world commends that such steps as are found to be most suitable should be taken to secure the co-operation of foreign countries in this matter; and, further, that the Governments of the Empire should consider the adoption of such measures as are necessary and feasible to secure the provision and publication of such statistics, provided that the foreign countries materially concerned either as producers or consumers are prepared to take similar action.”
