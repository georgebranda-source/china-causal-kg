## [12] CONSUL KILLED.

**OUTRAGE ON "FRENCH OFFICER"**

**TROOPS RUSHED UP.**

*Peking, Nov. 2.*

Bandits killed the French Consul M. Robert, of Bungchew, on October 31, while he was motoring from there to Bangson, in Indo-China. — Reuter.

Lungchow is in Kwangsi province, near the boundary separating China and India-China. It should not be confused with a minor town or the same name in Kwangtuhr province.

## [13] Body Found in Grove. Lutei

<sub>section under [12] CONSUL KILLED.</sub>

M. Robert was accompanied by Monsieur Cadet, an administrator in the French Colonial service. The gang of bandits or pirates attacked them 25 kilometres from Lungchow in Kwangsi province whence they had started early in the morning.

The Chinese authorities, on hearing the news, sent a battalion of troops who arrived on the spot at two of the afternoon and found Mr. Roberts's corpse in a grove, pierced by two bullets in the head and stomach.
M. Cadet and the Annamite ahuffear were dragged away by the bandits but managed to escape and after 24 hours' exhausting walking arrived safely at the French Consulate at Lungchow on the night of Nov. 1.

## [14] BOLOGNA CRIME

**ASSAILANT'S FATHER'S BAD RECORD.**

*Bologna, November 2.*

An official communique states that Mussolini's assailant's father is an ex-anarchist who compelled his son to leave the Young Fascist organisation of which he was a

member, but apparently the father has not participated in subversive activities for many years.

Enquiries are proceeding with a view to discovering others responsible for the crime.—Reuter.

A huge demonstration of thousands of Fasciats and representatives of ex-service organisations thunderously cheered Mussolini.

The Secretary-General of the Fascist party of Turati in a speech declared that Fascists demanded the death penalty on those behind the dastardly attempt.—Reuter.

## [16] Fatal Disturbances. Roma Nov

<sub>section under [14] BOLOGNA CRIME</sub>

Disturbances have occurred in various parts of Italy in consequence of the attempt on Signor Mussolini's life, two people being killed at Genoa.

A Fascist at Cagliari climbed to the balcony of a house belonging to an anti-Fascist named Lussu and hiranged the crowd, when he was killed. Lussu was arrested on a charge of causing his death.

The entire Press demands now measures to protect Signor Musselin's life. The "Impero" asks whether the Opposition wishes to have a new St. Bartholomew Night (referring to the historic massacre of the Huguenots). The "Impero" describes the death of Mussolini's assailant as "holy "Lynching'—Kautan

## [23] EMBARQU

**OVERWHELMING MAJORITY.**

At a conference held this afternoon between representatives of the various transport Unions and the Minders' Executive the question of an embargo on foreign coal was discussed.

It is officially announced that the conference decided to adjourn to enable a report to be made to the General Council of the Trades Union Congress.

Nothing is said in the official statement as to the views expressed at the conference, but this silence is regarded as confirming anticipations that the transport workers did not approve an embargo, which had already been declared impracticable by their leaders.

The admission is made that the Associated Society of Locomotive Engineers and Firemen, which is one of the two railwaymen's Unions, declined even to send representatives.

It is also learned unofficially that a ballot of the National Union of Seamen was announced. Only 908 voted in favour of an embargo and 10,060 were against it.

The Levy Proposal.
To-morrow representatives of all the Trades Unions will meet to consider the miners' proposal for a levy. It is considered that negative decisions both in regard to an embargo and in regard to a levy are foregone conclusions, and it is expected that when the miners' delegates assemble in London later in the week these negative decisions will have an important effect. British Wireless Service.
