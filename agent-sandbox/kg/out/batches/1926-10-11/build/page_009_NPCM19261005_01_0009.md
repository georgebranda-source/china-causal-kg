The Canton Insurance Office, Ltd. issues a notice concerning loss of scrip.

Dick Norton, and his "Globe Trotters", announce a complete change of programme for this evening's performance at the Star Theatre.

An official announcement has now been made about the showing of the famous Zeobrugg film at Queen's Theatre, on October 21 at 9.30 p.m.

Mr. W. L. Hall, the new Attorney-General for Noya Scotia, has been elected unopposed for the constituency of Queen's County, Prince Edward Island.

As the result of a survey a Government expert recommends the stocking of the inland waters of South Africa with edible fish. He believes it is possible to establish salmon in the western streams.

Two children from Victoria, Alberta, Canada, named Patsy and Billy Agnew, aged respectively 10 and 12, have climbed Mount Edith Cavell, one of the highest peaks of the Rocky Mountains. They were accompanied by two Swise guides.

Melbourne.—The Defence Department is arranging a 14,000-miles surveying flight from Melbourne to Samoa, by way of the Eastern Australian coast and New Guinea, touching Fiji and several intermediate islands. It will be the most ambitious flight ever attempted in Australasia and also the most risky, as the extent of aviation difficulties in the greater portion of the Pacific region is unknown. Group Captain Williams, R.A.F., will be the pilot. He will be accompanied by a mechanic and a wireless operator. The flight will start in a fortnight and will occupy about two months.

Calcutta, September 3.—Undertakers have notified the Reverend R. C. Biswas of "Holy' Trinity Church, Calcutta, and the Government that in future they will be unable to supply coffins at as cheap rates as formerly. Business, they state, has been carried on at a loss and they have been obliged to double and in some cases treble their prices. After much discussion, states the "Calcutta Diocesan Record," it was resolved at a general meeting of the congregation of Holy Trinity that a "cofin fund be started at once, with a membership fee of at least one anna per month per family.

Miss Aileen Woods, the popular teacher of dancing is due to arrive back in Hong Kong on October 15, after an absence of over three years. "She will resume her tuition at her studio and hopes to popularise the French tango (her favourite dance), also the Foxtrot-Charleston.

Simla, Sept. 18.—The Viceroy has telegraphed the Hon. Mr. Beyers on board the “Tukhliwa” a most hearty welcome on behalf of India to the South African Delegation on their arrival in this country. The Viceroy adds: “I earnestly hope that your visit, although shorter than we should have wished, will be throughout full of sources of interest and that it will please you all.”

The terrible cyclone experienced at Karachi on Sept 7, was the marks of prolonging the stay of Mr. R. B. Salisbury's "Quaints" who went there and were prevented from playing for three nights. Miss Doris Woods wrote a vivid description of the unprecedented and unusual occurrence as Karachi will go for years without rain! One conclusion the company had was that it would make their journey of 25 hours cooler across the Sind Desert to Quetta. The tour is certainly a remarkable success and will not terminate till December. Miss Doris writes that the air route from London having its destination at Karachi will bring that city into great prominence.

## [7] LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS

One seventh of the human race are Moslems and every one of them should make a pilgrimage to Mecca at least once.

Washington, September 28.—Five thousand special agents of the Treasury Department began the task of checking income tax dodger in all parts of the country. A drive was also launched for the purpose of collecting about $400,000,000 in overdue taxes.

Long Island City, New York, Sept. 27.—An unidentified man ran amuck during a local baseball contest and murdered the umpire Patrick McTavey, before a large crowd of spectators. Several persons had just yelled "kill the umpire" to emphasise their disapproval of his last decision. The man ran from his seat to the centre of the field and struck McTavey, where after he escaped leaving no trace.

Tokyo, Sept. 20.—A "schooner seaplane," equally at home on the waves or in the air, is being tested by the Japanese Navy. The machine carries a light must and sails in its fuselage and in event of a forced landing and a disabled motor can sail into shore just like a windjammer. The hydroplane is of the "R" series of the Naval Aviation Corps. It is equipped with radio and propelled by two Rolls Royce motors of 360 horse power.

Washington, September 28.—The White House spokesman denied reports recently circulating in Washington to the effect that the United States government was about to enter into treaty negotiations with Soviet Russia for the purpose of reaching terms for recognition. It was stated that there was no prospect of immediate change in the relations between the American government and Russia and that certain fundamental obstacles had first to be removed before such negotiations could be undertaken.

Hankow, Sept. 25.—The native press states that over 70 wounded soldiers of Fan Chung-sheu arrived at Liu Kia-miao yesterday, afternoon. These soldiers had been engaged in the severe fighting which had taken place the day before yesterday northward of Sinyang, with the troops of Tien Weiching for a successive day and night. The casualties of Tien resulting from the fighting are stated to be 1,000 odd soldiers killed, and several hundred wounded. The loss was estimated over 10,000 rifles and bombs were in large quantities.

## [8] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS.

Mr. M. J. Quist, Netherland Consul-General in Hong Kong, will be away on a month's holiday from to-day (5th instant); during his absence Mr. A. Methofer, Vice Consul, will be in charge of the Consulate-General.

Washington, September 28.— Major General John L. Hines will be relieved as chief of staff of the army and assigned to command the Ninth Corps Area at San Francisco on November 20. He will be succeeded as chief of staff by Major General Charles P. Summerall.

Syracuse, N. Y., Sept. 28.—The Democrats in state convention today unanimously and for the fifth time nominated Governor Alfred E. Smith for governor of New York. There was tremendous enthusiasm after the nomination. Modification of the Volstead act and American adherence to the world court were among the chief recommendations in the declaration of the party principles adopted by the convention.

Kuala Lumpur, Sept 23.—The Methodist Boys' School, Kuala Lumpur, launched an appeal for funds to enable them to enlarge their present school building in order to meet the increased demand for admission. Besides this appeal to the public, the staff and pupils of the school took it upon themselves to explore all possible avenues which would bring them financial help. The result of this united and determined effort was seen, when Mrs. H. W. Thomson laid the foundation stone of the new building.

Formerly, secretary to the Baroness Burdett-Coutta, and to Sir James Brooke, Rajah of Sarawak, the late Mr. Henry Wagner (85), of Belvedere Terrace, Brighton, son of a former Vicar of Brighton, whose will was published recently, left unsettled property valued at £196,824, with net personalty £184,880. Mr. Wagner, who was a barrister, and one of the oldest members of the Alpine Club, left over £38,000 for charity and his numerous legacies included sums of £2,000 each to his cousins, Dean Tage, of St. Paul's Cathedral, and the Rev. Charles Culbertson of St. Giles, Oxford.
Penang, Sept. 27.—The death of Mr. J. L. Woodford is announced and the funeral took place at the Western Road Cemetery on Saturday. There was a large attendance and many wreaths were sent. The Rev. Father A. Devals, of the Church of the Assumption, officiated

Geneva, Sept. 29, — Major General Dennis E. Nolan told the missionary disarmament commission to-day that America was unable to accept the national defence budgets and measures of the commission. He said the pay of one American soldier covers 58 French, 41 Italian, and eleven Japanese soldiers.

Hankow, Sept. 25.—The Chinese paper publishes the news that the piece of land located near the cemetery of the six countries near the Japanese Concession purchased by Chao Yuen-hwa, late Chief Executive of the front line under Marshall Wu, at the cost of several million dollars, had been confiscated by the Revolutionaries on the 22nd instant.

Amsterdam, "Sept. 27.—16 is learned in financial circles that the Laras Rubber Estates are making important additions to their holdings in Sumatra and have just purchased a plantation of 2,395 acres, in full coffee and rubber production for the reported price of $4,500,000. The former owners were a Dutch Indian land syndicate. The new concern is preparing to offer 2,000,000 shares, also 7 per cent. first mortgage debentures totalling 625,000 pounds sterling.

Ichang, September 10.—Yesterday the French steamer "Yung-An" arrived from Chungking, bringing the Chinese staff and crew from "Butterfield and Swire's steamer "Wanhsen," which has been held captive at Wanhsen by General Yang Sen. Later in the day another French steamer, the "Yung-fung, arrived from Chungking, bringing Mr. D. H. Davies of the Customs service.

Washington, Sept. 27.—As a result of the resignation to-day of Allan Dulles, chief of the Near East Division of the State Department, several more resignations of persons occupying high positions are expected shortly in protest against the low salaries paid by the government. The resignation of Dulles is the culmination of many months of agitation by assistant secretaries and heads of special bureaus against the scale of salaries which, they declare, are entirely inadequate to meet the present high cost of living and maintain a standing in the community commensurate with their positions. Dulles was recently appointed counsellor to the legation in Peking.

Los Angeles, Sept. 27.—The national convention of the Women's Christian Temperance Union, meeting here to-day, will consider several programmes "for a strong campaign against the activities of wet organisations throughout the country." Delegates are unanimous in declaring that any compromise with the anti-prohibitionists at this time would mean defeat for the cause of prohibition. As one leader said, it would be a "national calamity and a world disaster."

London. Sept. 28.—A royal mystery that had endured since Queen Alexandra's death has been cleared up by the revelations that she left no will, and that her property was divided among her four children—King George, the Princess Royal, Princess Victoria, and Queen Maud of Norway. It is understood, however, that the Prince of Wales is to receive Queen Alexandra's famous pearls, so that they may be worn by his Princess of Wales when he marries.

His many friends and associates will be pleased to learn that Mr. Eric B. Cumine, son of Mr. H. M. Cumine, has just passed his final examination of the Architectural Association. He left Shanghai in the spring of 1922, up to which time he made himself well-known in various lines of sport, particularly in cricket, making his century and playing in the Shanghai Foot-Ball League matches for the final Skottows Cup, and for the Shanghai Public School in 1921. While in England during his studies he found time to captain his school cricket team and to serve on the selection committee for "rugger." He will return to Shanghai at the end of next year to join his father's firm. He is now employed in the architectural section of the P.W.U. of the London County Council, and is also taking a course for the Surveyors Institute examination.
