Washington, October 9.—The enlisted strength of the United States army has fallen to 110,345, which is 8,405. Below pay strength, and 14,655. under the authorised strength. it was announced 10-day. The reduction is attributed to the order of September 15 which stopped recruiting. The order was issued to avoid a threatened deficiency in funds.

Realistically accurate in every detail is a new group, typical of the oil menace to sea birds, placed in the Central Hall of the Natural History Museum, South Kensington. It consists of a portion of sea beach on the south coast showing the effect on marine bird life of oil refuse discharged by ships at sea. The birds exhibited include red-throated divers, guillemots, and a razor bill. They were picked up from time to time on the beach in a dead or dying condition, their feathers clogged with oil. This oil floats on the surface of the water and congeals into the consistency of tar. When the birds dive the oil clogs their feathers. They can neither swim nor fly, and drift ashore and die of starvation.

## [9] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS.

Madras, Sept. 27.—It is understood that the wedding of Captain Portal, A.D.C. to the Governor of Madras, with the Hon. Cecily Goschen, daughter of Lord and Lady Goschen, will take place in the third week in November at Madras.

Los Angeles, Calif., Oct. 8.—Jackie Coogan's parents decided to day to acquiesce in his wishes and to permit him to have his hair trimmed into a boyish bob tomorrow. The youthful movie star has been pleading for this for five years. He is now 12 years old, and has long flowing curls.

White Sulphur Springs, Virginia, October, 9.—Tex Rickard, world famous fight promoter, "eloped" with Miss Maxine Hodges of New York and they were quietly married in Lewisburg. They arrived here yesterday on a brief honeymoon. Rickard's first wife died about one year ago.

Two prominent Red Cross workers will visit Manila in December. The Honourable John Barton Payne, chairman of the American Red Cross, will be accompanied by Dr. Frederick Humbert of the Secretariat of the League of Red Cross Societies. Dr. Humbert is chief of the health division, and one of the most prominent public health experts in Europe.

Rangoon, Oct. 1.—At the inquest into the circumstances of the Hanthawaddy District Police Superintendent, Mr. J. T. M. Cowan's death, the Magistrate found that the deceased had committed suicide. He looked normal on Wednesday evening. It is understood that the deceased occasionally suffered from neurasthenia and insomnia. The remains were buried yesterday at Kungyangam.

## [12] 1926-27. BY H. G. W. WOODHEAD.

The Singapore Municipal health statement for the week ending September 25 gives the total number of deaths as 256, representing a death rate of 32.60, compared with 29.04 the preceding week and 80.70 in the corresponding week of last year. Among the nationalities' mortality occurred as follows: Chinese 212, Indians 22, Malays 15, and Europeans 2. The chief causes of death were malaria, 37, pneumonia 38, phthisis 27, dysentery 22, convulsions 22 and beri-beri 12. There were six deaths from enteric fever. Fourteen persons died who had been less than three months resident in Singapore. The infantile death rate was 179.9, compared with 229.5 in the previous week and 201.4 in the corresponding week of last year.
