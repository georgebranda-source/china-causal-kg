## [8] IMPROVEMENT AT CHUNGKING.

### Hankow Processions Diverted By Chinese Troops.

There were large processions in Hankow yesterday (Sunday) but these were diverted by Chinese troops and were prevented from entering the Concession without difficulty. There was no disturbance of any kind.
H.M.S. "Cockechafers!" the s.s. "Wantung" and s.s.s. "Wanhsien" have arrived in Hankow.
They have come from up the five following the negotiations which followed the taking possession of the two B. and S. boats by General Yang Sen.

Chungking is, quieter. Supplies have been obtainable during the last two days and local officials have promised to afford protection. The outlook in Szechuan province is, however, uncertain at present.

Chungking is the last British trenty port up the Yangtse., in Szechuan province, and has suffered much as the result of intense feeling among the Chinese following the Wanhien incident, demonstrations having been held outside foreign residences, which were threatened, and hillside bungalows had to be hurriedly evacuated by foreign residents.

There have been a few cases of desultory firing at passing ships at Shasi and Hwangchow.—Naval Descatch.

[Shasi is further up than Hawkow and Hwangchow below. "Both ports are believed to be held by the southerners.]

The "Wanhsen, " arrived back in port a few days ago, Her Chief Officer Mr. Johnstone, and Chief Engineer Mr. Kerr went up to Kueifu for her and brought her down.

General Yang's Sen made use of her as he did of the "Th Ling" an American ship, and of three flying the French flag, to bring down what looks more like a horde of roughs than what they claim to be—soldiers. They were from Kuéifu and Wushan only.

The main army, following Yang Sen are said to be coming down from "Wanhsien," but just to-day news has come through that the troops favouring the south have started to attack Yang Sen, so they may be kept nearer home.

"The 'Wanhsien' shows much evidence of the attack made on her, and like, the 'Wantung' much sign of wanton destruction."

The British gunbouts "Mantis," "Teal" and "Widgeon" accompanied by the "Kiawo" have left for up river. A rise in the river made it possible for the larger of the gunbouts to go up. The "Bee," with Rear Admiral Cameron on board, the "Cricket," and the "Cockchafer" are still here. Mr. A. P. Slunt; the British Consul for Teang, has gone up river on the "Mantis" to continue the negotiations over the "Wanhsien" attack. There is much fear amongst the Chinese that, if Yang Sen arrives here with his army, he will go no farther.

The Chamber of Commerce has already raised $100,000; and now a similar sum is demanded.—N. C. Daily News.

## [9] Slow Improvement.

*Shanghai, October*

Conditions at ports on the upper Yangtse, recently swept by serious anti-British demonstrations which led to the evacuation of Chungking by the British community are reported to be slowly improving, according to reports received in Shanghai yesterday.

Anti-British agitation is reported to be still proceeding but no serious incident is reported.—“Shanghai Times.”

*Peking, Oct. 11.*

The position of the foreigners in Szechuan has become worse. ## [11] CANTON'S MANIFESTO

**BOYCOTT SETTLED: BUT STRIKE NOT OVER!**

**PRESENT KUOMINTANG POLICY DEFINED.**

Blockade of Hong Kong Given Up—Intensified Struggle Continues.
NATIONALIST GOVERNMENT SILENT: PICKET & COMMITTEE TO REMAIN.

A long official announcement by the Canton Government makes two statements of fact which are of the utmost importance from the point of view of “settlement of the boycott.” These are:—

A bureau of the government (the Canton Information Bureau) says categorically:—the Strike Committee called off the boycott.

In a declaration, the Strike Committee emphasises:—Therefore, we cannot say that the Canton and Hong Kong strike is settled.

The Nationalist Government has issued no proclamation.

The Kuomintang declaration lays stress on:—the giving up of the policy of direct boycott and the blockade of Hong Kong. But this same declaration, in a mass of political and other utterances, gives prominence to "intensification of the struggle against Imperialism." It is noteworthy that the Strikers specifically refer to Hong Kong and the Kuomintang does not.

Latest reports on the situation to-day appear below, revealing the effect of Sunday's decision.

## [12] OFFICIAL MANIFESTO.

The following despatch has been sent by the Canton Information Bureau (a Government institution), via Reuter:—

"At a great gathering at the East Párade ground yesterday at noon the Strike Committee called off the boycott."

In this connection two Declarations have been issued:—one by the Strike Committee and the other by the Kuomintang addressed to the general body of Hong Kong and Canton strikers.

The Nationalist Government has issued no statement, the boycott being a patriotic popular movement under the leadership of the Strike Committee as the elected executive of the general body of the strikers.

The general sense of the Declarations is that though the specific struggle which has found expression in the boycott was directly caused by the Shanghai massacre of May 30 and the Shameen-Shakee massacre of June 23, the same is but a part of the general nationalist struggle against imperialism. Owing to fundamental change in the national situation brought about by an extension of Nationalist power and influence to the Yangtse, there is necessity to change the technique of the specific struggle and to resort to a new policy as well as to propaganda in order to make the people of the whole country realise the semi-colonial status of China and lead them to join the active anti-imperialist movement whose nim and purpose is the liberation of Nationalist China from unequal Treaties and the redress of wrongs.

After a review of the national revolutionary movement in China the Kuomintang Declaration to the strikers emphasises that "the giving up of the policy of direct boycott and the blockade of Hong Kong" (which is a specific struggle) does not mean the cessation of the general struggle of Chinese Nationalism against Imperialism but its intensification "on a national scale until the independence of China is completely secured." ## [16] MUCH IMPROVEMENT.

### Chinese Returning to Former Employers.

The boycott has been publicly announced at an and the Strike Committee have issued a proclamation to this effect.

Chinese are returning to duty with their former employers and the situation is much improved.

Arrangements are now being made for a general resumption of trade and business, but movements and berthing of steamboats are handicapped due to the sifting up of wharves during the strike.

Processions and demonstrations on Sunday, October 10, passed off peacefully.—Naval Despatch.

Strike pickets have been withdrawn but, pending the return of two delegates sent to Canton to ascertain the position. the boycott is being continued, and no employees of British are yet allowed to return.—Naval Despatch.

## [17] CONDITIONS TO-DAY.

### River Steamers Resume Trips To Canton.

Hundreds of Chinese stood on the Praya this morning to see if it was true that communications had been resumed with Canton.

In addition to British shipping movements recorded below, the Tung On S.S. Co.'s "Sai On" left this morning.

There was no flourish of trumpets when the long-missed prodigal set off—after a year's exile on the Hong Kong-Macao run.

She carried between 200 and 300 passengers but no cargo. So soon as the "Tung On" comes out of dock, she will join her sister ship.

On inquiry from the management the “China Mail” was informed the “Sri On” would act on instructions from the British Consul General in Canton as to whether they should go alongside their own wharf or anchor in the stream.

They had also heard that the river near the wharves had silted in the interval. (Note: This is confirmed by a despatch published by courtesy of the Naval authorities).
As yet no other "outside" boat has resumed the run. The "Kwong Tung's" owners wish to take up the night trip but are waiting for information as to the examination regulations—during the night—on account of martial law below Whampoa.

## [18] WAR MYSTERY.

### Southerners Defeated Report.

**KIUKIANG IN THE BALANCE**

Struggle Being Extended to Two New Zones.

[By Our Special Correspondont.]

The fate of Kiukiang is in the balance.

Marshal Sun Chuan-fang claims to have repulsed each one of a series of southern drives on his main camp but the position is still obscure.

Details are given in latest despatches of small towns re-occupied by Allied troops who say that they have pushed the Cantonese away from the northern half of the railway between Kiukiang and Te-an.

Further inland from Te-an to Nanchang, less is known for want of communication.

The 7th Canton Army appears to have been used as "shock troops in the offensive of Kiukiang and is being replaced by the two famous divisions from the 4th Army.

## [19] Allies on Defence.

Indications are that the south-erners have not lost their grip on the situation and that Sun Chuanfang is still concentrating on defence of his base at Kiukiang.

On the Fukien-Kwangtung frontier, the opposing troops are in the field but fighting is only on a small scale.

Chinese reports state that General Yang Sen is still anti-Canton and that he is marching victoriously eastwards to attack the southern bases (on their line of communication) between Yochow and Changsha.
