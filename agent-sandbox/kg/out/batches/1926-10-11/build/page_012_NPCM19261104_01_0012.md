## [10] A J. P.S SON: FINED FOR A "ROW" LN A BUS.

**HEAD OVER HEELS.**

The son of Mr. Tong Yat-cheun, J.P., appeared this morning before Mr. J. H. B. Nihill at the Kowloon Magistracy charged with assaulting a bus conductor, and refusing to alight from a bus when ordered.
The complainant conductor, alleged that defendant boarded his bus while it was in motion at Yaumati, despite his refusal to admit him because there was no room. Complainant also alleged that he was kicked by defendant who on jumping from the "bus" fell "head over heels."
His Worship imposed a fine of $10 on the first charge and dismissed the second.

## [19] "STEAM-ROLLER."

### Canton Rolling Back on Own Track.

The war situation remains clothed in as much uncertainty as it ever did, due to a number of reasons.

On the face of things it would seem that the greatest Cantonese "steam-roller," which sallied forth from Canton little more than two months ago upon such an unprecedented and ambitious campaign, had at last begun to roll back along its own tracks.

The tide of Bolsheviks unto which the great march northwards has been likened so often, has apparently been atemmed and the ebb has commenced in real earnest.
It may be a little early yet to venture into predictions of this sort, for in Chinese warfars the unexpected always happens, but unless some really sensational coup data or betrayal takes place in the ranks of the Allied armies, the Red army may be expected to sue for the best

peace terms possible under the cir
cumstances.

Fighting Single-Handed. Such will depend upon Marshal Sun Chuan-fang, upon whom has rested the task of repulsing the Red rush almost single-handed and who at present appears to have accomplished this in worthy fashion.
Marshal Sun on at least two occasions recently was reported to have offered to cease hostilities providing that the Reds withdrew from his five south-eastern provinces of Kiangsu, Chekiang, Anhui, Hunan and Fukien.

## [20] BURGLARS' HAUL.

**660 THEFT FROM MR. GRIMBLE.**

**MYSTERY MAN IN WHITE.**

Mr. George Grimble (the well-known shipbroker), of No. 10, Branksome Towers, May Road, has reported to the Police that shortly after 2 o'clock this morning, an unknown Chinese dressed in white, was seen in the neighbourhood of his house.

Investigations proved that Mr. Grimble's house had been visited by thieves and a quantity of jewellery which Mr. Grimble estimates to be worth at least $660 together with two pairs of gold spectacles were found to have been stolen.

The Police are busy investigating the case, but up to the time of going to press were unable to make any statement on the matter.

## [24] ST. ANDREWS DAY

**CONTINUOUS PRAYER IN CATHEDRAL.**

**HELPERS WANTED:**

On St. Andrewstide, the November number of "St. John's"

"The eve of St. Andrew's day has been for many years the day appointed as a day of special prayer for the extension of the Kingdom of God amongst non-Christian people." It has long been observed as a day of continuous prayer in the Cathedral, different people taking a quarter of an hour and spending the time in the Cathedral in prayer. The Chaplain will be very glad to hear from any who like to take part in the work. It is, noticeable how the missionary work of the Church has spread and increased since the initiation of the Day of Intercession in the year 1872. At the present time the four reports issued by the Missionary Council of the National Assembly of the Church of England have shown how pressing is the need of a great increase in the number of workers and in the amount contributed by our Church to the evangelisation of the world. Empire involves responsibility, and we of the British Empire are bound to see that we do our part in spreading the Christian message which has been the foundation of our own national life."
