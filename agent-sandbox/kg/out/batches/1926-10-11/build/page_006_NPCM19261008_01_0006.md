## [10] CORRESPONDENCE

[To the Editor of the "China Mail"] Sir,—As an old resident, I wish to tender you my personal thanks for your spirited and “thrilling” account of the rescue of 35 Chinese fishermen, which appeared in your respected paper on Friday, October 1. One can easily recall the weather that commenced with the typhoon on Monday, September 27, and continued during the remainder of the week and so realise the conditions under which the gallant crew of the s.s. “Hydrangea” saved the lives of 35 survivors of the Yeung Kong fishing fleet. It was as you soably described. “An Epic of the Sea” and well worthy of your remarks on British tenacity.
You also mentioned the instance of the saving of the crew of the British vessel “Antenor” by the s.s. “President Roosevelt,” and that “The rafters of the world rang with praise of the heroism” of those on board the great liner. I am sure every British man, woman, boy and girl felt grateful for what the captain and crew of the “President Roosevelt” had done for our shipwrecked sailors. It was therefore easy for us in Hong Kong to picture the great welcome which was given the American liner on her arrival in Southampton. It must have been a great event and in listening to the muffled cheers of a crowd of human beings whose hearts were full of gratitude to those who had so nobly saved our own people one would realise it was an occasion which could hardly ever be forgotten either by those for whom the cheers were given or by those who gave them. I feel sure that the gallant action by the crew of the s.s. “Hydrangea,” will be duly honoured and that arrangements will be made whereby the public of Hong Kong may have the privilege of expressing their feelings on this the Latest Epic of the Sea.

Which once again recalls the expression used by the Captain of the “President Rossevelt” who in answer to the thanks given him said, “It is nothing, it is only what we sailors do for one another.”

*Hong Kong, Oct. 7.*

## [11] MOTOR DRIVERS CLAIM.

In the Summary Court this morning, a Chinese driver claimed a month's wages in lieu of notice from Mr. J. H. Mitchell by whom he alleged he had been engaged as a chauffeur.

Mr. Mitchell said that the man had only been engaged on trial and that when, being dissatisfied with his driving, he (Mr. Mitchell) asked the driver for his licence he refused to produce it, became insolent and the next morning did not put in an appearance.
Holding that as a driver on trial plaintiff was not entitled to pay in lieu of notice, His Honour made an order for payment of amount outstanding on his wages only. This amount Mr. Mitchell had previously indicated his willingness to pay.

## [14] U. S. BUSY SESSION

**NO TIME TO CONSIDER THOMPSON REPORT.**

Wnahington, September 30.—A jammed calendar of farm relief, French debt, prohibition, railroad, coal and Muscle Shoals legislation is being whipped into shape here for the coming short session of Congress and there will be no room for the Thompson report, the Kiess bills or any legislation affecting the Philippines.

This is the opinion of members of the steering committee who have virtually absolute control over the programme. It is their opinion that it will be a year at least before Philippine legislation actually reaches the floor of either house.

## [15] Too Much Business.

<sub>section under [14] U. S. BUSY SESSION</sub>

When the national legislative body reconvenes for its second session on December 6 it will find 140 bills and 33 resolutions on the Senate calendar awaiting action and 230 bills and 15 resolutions on the House calendar. Administration leaders agree that it will be impossible to dispose of this volume of business in a session lasting only seven weeks.
The belief is gaining ground that President Coolidge will be compelled to summon a special session beginning March 5, as a continuation of the session which closes March 4, in order that long delayed matters of legislation may be considered.

The Senate will reconvene November 10 for the impenchment trial of Federal Judge George W. English, eastern district of Illinois, who was charged in the House with misuse of his official position. Under a special rule adopted at the close of the last session the rivers and harbours bill will be taken up December 14 and will probably be out of the way before the Christmas holidays.

Five Railway Bills.
The steering committee already has agreed on five railroad bills for consideration before March 4, including the Cummins measure for compulsory consolidation; also a bill to lease Muscle Shoals to a fertilizer company; the Copeland bill to prevent future coal strikes; several farm relief bills; 13 bills to amend and strengthen national prohibition enforcement; and the complex problem of the French debt.

"We expect loud growls from those who have been demanding action on the Philippines," said one prominent figure on Capitol Hill, "but we will toss them the Thompson report and let them fight over it in committee."—"United Press."

## [17] THE KUOMINTANG.

**GOVERNMENT EXTENSION**

### —

The joint conference of the Central Executive Committee of the Central Kuomintang and of the various provincial branches will be held on October 10 at Cahton.
Its importance is parallel to that of the All-China Delegates Congress, because the delegates from different provinces to this conference are similar to those for the Congress. Moreover, among the attendants of the joint conference the members of the Executive Committee of the highest party branch will be present.
This Conference will not only determine the most important problems for the extension of the Party in connection with present developments, but also the welfare of the people and consolidation of the foundation of Revolution.

## [18] Peace & Diplomacy.

<sub>section under [17] THE KUOMINTANG.</sub>

The problems for discussion at this Joint Conference, such as, Extension of the Nationalist Government, Nationalist Government and Provincial Government, Provincial Kuomintang, and Provincial Government; hastening of peace; Diplomatic Policies, etc., are of great importance and are closely related to the future of the National Revolution and the welfare of the people. Therefore all persons, and especially the revolutionary members, should devote their entire attention to this Joint Conference, remarks the: "Canton Gazette."

Prior to the session of the conference, the C.E.C. of the Central Kuomintang organised a committee to prepare the various proposals for the Conference. As previously stated, this committee met several times and the various proposals have been concretely fixed. Since this Joint Conference is more important than ordinary meetings, it is necessary to discuss the various problems in detail, formulating our opinion and submitting them to the Conference in order to obtain satisfactory results.

The members of the Standing Committee are to bring up the proposals at the conference and on each proposal a report will be submitted. Afterwards they are to be discussed and resolutions will be prepared for submission to the C.E.C. of Central Kuomintang for execution.

## [21] ABDEL KRIM'S EXILE

Casablanca, August 28.—As soon as the ordinary passengers had left the station, the special coach in which Abdel Krim and his family left Fez yesterday was shunted opposite the exit, and the Riff leader's servants helped his women folk to descend. Last of all came Abdel Krim himself, accompanied by his father and uncle and an interpreter.

The party walked quickly through the station, at the entrance to which a curious crowd was kept back by a cordon of police, and took their places in several motor-cars, which immediately left for the quay, where the once formidable guerilla leader went on board the steamer "Abda," which is carrying him to Marseilles on his way to exile in Madagascar.
According to an Exchange telegram from Madelae, a decree has been issued in Morocco ordering the confiscation of all Abdel Krim's wealth. All his legitimate property will be acquired by the civil Power, while the remainder is to be returned to the former owners.

Maria Henkina's intensely dramatic rendering combined with a voice of wide range and exquisite purity to a brilliant interpretation at last night's opera of the well known part of the Paris courtesan who, forsaking a gay life of revelry for her lover, sacrifices the happiness she finds by so doing for the sake of his family.

In few of Verdi's operas does the success or failure of the rendering depend so exclusively on one performer as in "La Travista" and it is not exaggerating the measure of Sign. Henkina's personal triumph to state that had the whole of the rest of the characters been in the hands of mediocre performers her interpretation would have redeemed the performance from mediocrity.

Sig. A. Balliotti, who took the part of her lover, possesses a tenor voice of pleasing quality but, is too much lacking in the art of dramatic gesture to make the part completely convincing.

More successful in this particular was Sig. Bigardi, the action in the scenes between the fond father and desolate lover rising to heights of dramatic intensity.

The chorus was strong, the great finale to the second act being a fine example of harmony and unison.

The Orchestra — the Italian Opera Company's own—comprises some talented musicians exceptionally well led and providing the company with excellent support.

## [23] A FREE RIDE.

**PASSENGER WITH AN OLD TICKET.**

**A TRAMWAY CASE.**

With an old tramway ticket stuck in his hat, a Chinese had a free ride in a car last night until an inspector with keener sight than the conductor discovered the fraud.
Evidence of this was given when the man was charged before Mr. E. W. Hamilton at the Central Magistracy this morning with attempting to evade payment of fare.

## [26] FALSE TEETH TAX

New York.—False teeth may be assessed as taxable personal property, according to the ruling of the tax assessors of Atlantic City. They assessed a set of teeth worn by Mr. William B. Riddle at £250 and levied a tax of £6.

Mr. Riddle paid £420 for the set which he took from his mouth during a debate on the accuracy of the assessment of his personal property. A dentist member of the tax board noted the value of the teeth and commented on it. The board decided that they were subject to tax.

After a debate whether they should be taxed at Mr. Riddle's home or at his place of business the board decided that he used the tooth at business as much as at home and Ievied the tax in Atlantic City.
