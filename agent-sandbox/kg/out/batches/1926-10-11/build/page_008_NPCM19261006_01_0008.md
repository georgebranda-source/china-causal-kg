Warsaw, September 28.—Despite two votes of suspicion and lack of confidence against the minister of the interior and the minister of education, the Bartels cabinet has been reconstituted without the least change. The ministry will again be presented to the Diętoon Thursday. If the Diet again refuses to accept the Bartels cabinet, Marshal Pilsudki will dissolve the diet.

Greece is now willing to liquidate the debt to Great Britain within 60 years by annual payments averaging from £250,000 to £300,000. For the first ten years she desires that the instalments should fall below this average, and it is proposed to limit the first two years' payments to £150,000. M. Cacamanos has been supplied with a schedule of monthly payments for submission to the British Government.

Two Home mails arrived yesterday! The R.M.S. "Empress of Asia" brought letters from the United Kingdom via Canada (London, September 4) and the U.S. "Calchas" brought mail from Home and Europe via Siberia. The total number of bags landed from the "Empress of Asia" was 299, of which 81 were for Hong Kong, the remainder being for Canton and outports. There were 15 bags from London. The "Calchas" brought 278 bags, 58 for Hong Kong, with 28 bags from the United Kingdom via Siberia and 21 from the Continent via the same route.

An unusual case was heard at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday, a Japanese residing at Kowloon City being charged with cruelty to a peacock belonging to Acting Sub-Inspector Smith and with throwing stones to the danger of the public. Sub-Inspector Stimson said that the bird, which was invariably on the walls of the Kowloon City Police Station, appeared to have attracted the attention of the defendant who threw a number of stones at the bird. The defendant told His Worship that he had no intention of harming the bird and a fine of $10 was inflicted on the second charge only.

In the development of fighting aeroplanes driven by powerful engines and capable of very high speeds the United States authorities, in conjunction with medical experts, have been investigating the physical effect upon pilots of swift dives and sudden accelerations. In some trial movements carried out at extreme speeds, pilots reported a sudden feeling of blindness. This, in the doctors' opinion, was due to the fact that the blood had been driven temporarily from the head.

Mr. Verhomal Shewaram, the proprietor of Pioneer Silk Store, who has recently been in Japan for a pleasure trip is returning by the French Mail s.s. "D'Artagnan," on or about the 12th inst. During his stay in Japan, he was entertained by all his friends in Yokohama and Kobe.

Admiral Sir Robert Harris, whose death was recorded recently, was a great sportsman, and hunted game in all parts of the world. In China, one of his largest "bags" was secured on the banks of the Yangtze. In 14 broken days shooting a party secured 794 head of game, Sir Robert's contribution being 109 wild pheasants, nine hares, 22 anipe, 17 quail, 13 pigeons, five ducks, and five various, 180 in all.

Singapore, September 28—Mr. Tessensohn, who died at Singapore on Sept. 26, was admitted to the General Hospital about six weeks ago and although his illness took on a serious complexion and necessitated an operation being performed, it was not expected, in spite of his ripe age (71 years) that there would be a fatal termination. His mother, who survives him, is 93 years of age. He leaves a widow and several children, grandchildren and great grandchildren, with whom much sympathy is felt. The funeral of the honourable gentleman took place at Bidadari Cemetery on Sept. 27 and was very largely attended. The deceased gentleman's relatives, including his sons, daughter, and many grandchildren, were amongst those who were present at the cemetery.
Fourteen grouse from the Scottish moors were despatched from Southampton recently by steamer for New Zealand, where efforts will be made to rear them.

Rome, September 28.— Benito Mussolini to-day emphatically denied that Italy will have any part in the naval manoeuvres off Tangiers. The report was generally that Spain and Italy would merge their navies for the training period and engage in a sham battle. This was done a year ago but Mussolini now considers the Tangicrs issue too momentous to have such a joint manoeuvre.

Ottawa.—Sir George Perley, the acting Postmaster-General, referring to the statement made at Victoria, British Columbia, by Sir James Allen, late New Zealand High Commissioner in London, that the misunderstanding between Canada and the other members of the Pacific Cable Board had been cleared up, said no action had been taken and the correspondence was continuing. Canada has threatened to withdraw from membership of the Pacific Cable Board owing to the proposed duplication of the All-Red cable route from Fiji to Canada.

## [11] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS.

Berlin.—The Duke of Hohenberg, eldest son of the murdered Archduke Francis Ferdinand of Austria, has become engaged to the Countess Elizabeth Wolfegg-Waldburg. The duke was a boy of 12 when the assassination of his parents at Serajevo led to the world war.
Of the total number of 1,318 opium smokers and eaters who underwent the treatment by the Singapore medical authorities for curing them of the habit 580 absconded without completing the treatment. It is in the early stages of the treatment, during which the patients suffer from great discomfort, that patients abscend, states the annual medical report for last year. The mental effect is very pronounced and in bad cases when the craving is at its height they literally roll about and groan in agony. The reasons for admission for treatment are stated to be financial in practically every instance and not to be rid of the supposed ill-effects of the drug. As regards the starting of smoking, the reason appears to be equally divided between pleasure and sickness.

Winnipeg.— The Duke of Leuchtenberg, a relative of the late Czar of Russia, who fought in the Russian Imperial Army during the war, intends to settle in Canada and is selling his estate in Bavaria. He is at present touring with a party in Western Canada.

Mr. T. H. Woo, who has served for 30 years as Senior Accountant Clerk, was the recipient last week of a presentation from the staff of the Civil Engineer in Chief's Department of the Naval Yard. Mr. Carothers, in presenting him with a token of esteem from the European staff, expressed regret at the loss of an able and efficient officer.

The Hong Kong A.D.C. are exceedingly busy these days; rehearsing their productions for "Cricket Wcek." Full houses and a record success are confidently anticipated.

The many friends of Mr. E. L. Matteson, late general agent of the Dollar Line in Hong Kong, will regret to hear that his 16 months' old daughter died after a brief illness, from bronchial pneumonia in San Francisco on September 6. Mr. Matteson and family left here on leave in April last and the bereavement occurred while on a visit to Mr. Matteson's mother, Dr. Susie Frasior. The funeral took place on September 9.

Marshall Sun-Chuan-fang has issued a proclamation addressed to the people of Shanghai reiterating his sympathy for the sufferings of the nation in general and the local Chinese in particular. He adds that he will do his utmost to keep affairs running smoothly and peaceably in the provinces under his control, and to that end he has given instructions to his troops to come down on trouble makers with the utmost severity. Also, all undesirables will be expelled from his territory.
The South Australia House of Representatives has passed a vote of appreciation of the services of Mr. Gunn on his resignation of the Premiership. Mr. Gunn resigned to become a member of the Migration Commission.

## [13] 1926-27. BY H. G. W. WOODHEAD.

Cupetown. — The discovery and proclamation of an ever-increasing number of alluvial diamond diggings is beginning to cause anxiety, not only to the directors of diamond mines (who fear the consequences of flooding the market with cheap stones) but also by the Government and social reformers. The opening up of a new diamond field attracts both the professional digger and the man who either is out of employment or is lured from his regular occupation by the hope of making a fortune at the diggings. The vast majority who try their luck are, as a rule, bitterly disappointed, and every diamond rush is invariably followed by a frantic endeavour by many of the disillusioned men — and women—to return to their former avocations.
