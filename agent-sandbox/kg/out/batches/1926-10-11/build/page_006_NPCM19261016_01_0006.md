## [11] SATURDAY CAUSERIE

**OF NEW YORK.**

Whilst anti-British feeling in Kwangtung has not, as H.E. the Governor remarked yesterday, completely disappeared, there are indications that the merchants there are becoming more confident that the calling off of the strike pickets indicates the complete removal of all hindrances to free and unfettered intercourse in all goods between the two ports. If the “indications” consisted merely of reported opinions without anything practical to bear them out, there would be logic in the arguments of the pessimists who still refuse to believe that the boycott of British goods is a thing of the past. But already ships are beginning to, berth at the Canton wharves and have been able to get labour for loading without interference.

## [19] WUCHANG TREACHERY.

### Northern General Seeks Safety of U.S. Flag.

Through treachery the southern forces at length have captured Wuchang after breakdown of plans for evacuation which brought about a brief artillery battle, final engagement of the siege.

At 4 a.m. to day, northern troops inside Wuchang's walls who secretly had been negotiating with the southerners suddenly opened three gates of the city admitting about 3,000 southern soldiers who after a brisk exchange of rifle fire advanced carrying everything before them.

In taking over the city, they placed in custody General Lu Yu, chun, the northern commander, and his staff, although on the appearance of the foe General Lu sought safety under the American flag fleeing into the home of Dr. A. M. Sherman, Principal of Central China University.

Evacuation of the city by the northern forces was to have taken place on October 9 as the besieged northerners had accepted terms laid down by their foes and were to have been incorporated, into the army of General Liu Tso-lung at Chingshan, seven miles down the river from the city.
Although the terms that the southerners laid down defined what disposition was to be made of small arms no mention was made of artillery. Therefore when the northerners began to leave the city they undertook to take with them their artillery and began loading it on lighters to be transported down the river.
Seeing this the artillery at Han-yang arsenal went into action and cannon from Wuchang answered frustrating the plans laid down for the evacuation. It was while negotiators were busy making new arrangements for the southerners to take possession of the city that the actual occupation took place at 4 a.m. when the soldiers inside threw open the gates.

## [21] MACAO'S GOOD NEWS.

The “China Mail” learns from Macao that the Chinese-owned steamer “Shing Cheong,” (Capt. Francisco Marques), flying the Portuguese flag, completed her round trip to Canton and back without any incident.

The “Shing Cheong” took 200 Chinese passengers from Macao to Canton and was the first river steamer to berth at her wharf since the boycott.

On the same day (Wednesday) as the "Shing Cheong's" departure for Canton, a Chinese passenger tow-boat left Shekki also for"Canton. The tow carried on her initial trip over 200 passengers and a cargo of 1,400 picals of saltfish.

The passenger-boat service between Macao and Kongmoon was resumed on Thursday. The Chinese merchants and traders are in high spirits over the resumption of the services with Canton.

## [23] CHINA'S TREATIES.

**CANTON REQUEST FOR REPLACEMENT:**

**EQUALITY AGITATION**

Certain Chinese treaties with Great Britain, Japan, the U.S.A., Italy, Spain and Portugal expire this year (says a correspondent in the "Canton Gazette") also treaties with Annam (the foreign relations of which are directed by France,) and with Belgium.
The agitation for their replacement by agreements in which the sovereign rights of China shall be recognised is therefore most timely. It is significant that with the exception of groups bound by financial or political dependence on the foreign imperialists all classes of Chinese are united in urging the termination of such Unequal Treaties, continues the writer.

Other extracts from this article are:—

The nations involved are all, with the exception of Annam, signatories of the Washington Conference. France, which controls Annam, was also a signatory so that the good faith and the given word of all these countries are at stake in their attitude on this matter. Each of these nations at that gathering solemnly bound itself to recognise the sovereignty, independence, and territorial integrity of the Chinese Republic.

## [25] NOT "MISSING."

**"BORNEO" ARRIVES AFTER SHELTER.**

**ANXIETY OVER.**

Anxiety was allayed shortly before midnight as to the safety of the s.s. "Borneo" which, with a cargo of coal consigned to the Taikoo Sugar Refinery, was reported yesterday as overdue.

The vessel arrived off Taikoo at 11.45 p.m.' yesterday.

It appears, from the report of the master, that the ship, which sailed from Honggy at daylight on October 10, experienced a strong N.N.E. gale in Tongking Gulf and have to for eleven hours. Shelter was also taken in Kami Bay and Ponchin Bay for over four hours.

In the China Sea, nearing St. John's Island, a strong monsoon with heavy seas was experienced, this also contributing to the delay.

Having no wireless, the ship was unable to communicate its position.
