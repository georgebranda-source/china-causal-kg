## [9] LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS.

The case against a "former employee of the New Zealand Insurance Company who is charged with embezzlement, was again before Major Wilson at the Certifical Magistracy. The case was further adjourned.

The Resident of Pahang has appointed Mr. C. J. Dunn to be a member of the Vigilance Committee in and for the district of Temerloh, for the purpose of watching cinematograph films, posters and advertisements and to act as recipient of complaints and information arising from exhibition display or publication of such.

Another kidnapping case was brought before the Mixed Court, Shanghai, on November 23, when four elderly women were charged with kidnapping six "girls," the wages ranging from five to ten years. Owing to their being a number of witnesses the Court was unable to proceed with the case, which was adjourned for one week, the children to be held by the Door of Hope.

Since the end of August the silver dollar has come to be an article of merchandise in which there is a big import from Hong Kong, says a Bangkok paper. We know of one case in which a ship brought something approaching half a million dollars, and no doubt there were many other cases. The price of silver is down and so is the exchange value of the silver dollar. People with money buy dollars in the hope of a big profit when the value goes up again.

Dao Ching-sie, formerly a cotton mill worker and what, coolie, who was thrown out of employment as a result of the May 30 incident last year, was arrested in Pootung by the Chinese police and transferred to the military court for interrogation. He was eventually executed a few days ago. The Shanghai General Labour Union has issued a declaration stating that the execution of Dao Ching-sie by the Chinese authorities should convince the people of the absolute necessity of having self-government in Shanghai.

The strike of the Wei Tuns Cotton Mill, which began on November 21, still contipues, and U,481 hands are affected.

There will be a Public Lecture at the Helena May Institute on Monday, December 6, at 5:30 p.m. given by Mr. Lindseil on "Chinese Names." Admission free.

Observing that it was a cause of wanton cruelty, Major C. Wilson fined a Chinese $10 at the Central Magistracy yesterday for plucking the feathers off a live pigcon.

Mr. D. C. Weir employed by the Jardine Engineering Corporation, was fined $5 by Magistrate King in the British Police Court, Shanghai, on Nov. 19, for failing to obey a police traffic signal at the junction of Yutes and Bubbling Well Roads.

In view of the fact that native soldiery have been making a habit of searching Chinese in the Nantao and Chapei districts of Shanghai before the hour of the Curfew Colonel Yen Tsung-yang has issued an order that this practice must cease and that all pedestrians must be treated with the utmost civility and courtesy.

The proposal that the Japanese share of the Boxer indemnity be devoted to educational work in China has aroused the hostility of the Kiangsu Educational Association in Shanghai, on the ground that it will mean the training of Chinese students along Japanese lines. The Association has urged the teachers and students of local Chinese colleges to join in a strong protest on the subject to the Peking Government.

Inquiries made in Shanghai on Nov. 20, into the whereabouts of Mr. Harrendorf, German merchant, arrested by the Japanese authorities at Yokohama recently in the act of absconding from Shanghai after allegedly perpetrating huge frauds on several local banks, revealed the fact that Harrendorf is still in custody in Japan, though steps are being taken to have him removed to Shanghai for trial.

Senior Puisne Judge, Penang, has

Mr R. Scott has been appointed Resident Councillor, Penang, with effect from June 9, 1926.

Archdeacon and Mrs. Swindell have been granted six months' furlough and are due to leave Singapore on December 7.

Aafar Pasha Elaskari, the Diplomatic Agent of Iraq in London, has been recalled to Iraq to form a Cabinet, and is leaving immediately for Bagdad, says a London message of November 7. Atta Beymin, Secretary of the Agency, is carrying on in London provisionally.

Miss Hope Falkner, well-known Singapore artist and daughter of Lieut.-Col. and Mrs. Hope Falkner, who has been seriously ill in hospital for a month, is improving. The improvement is necessarily slow, however, and Miss Hope Falkner is likely to be in hospital for several weeks more.

Mr. B. C. Van Buren, Warehouseman of the Singapore Harbour Board, retires to-day after about 13 years service and proceeds to Australia early next month to join his wife and three children who preceded him some time ago. It is his intention to settle there instead of returning to his home in Ceylon.

Officially stated to be travelling for private reasons, General Li Lieh-chun came down from Canton to Hong Kong yesterday, it is believed, to go to Shanghai. From Shanghai, General Li is expected to return to his native province of Kiangsi, by way of Kiukiang. General Li was chief of staff to the late Dr. Sun Yat-sen several years ago, and recently returned to Canton to confer with the Nationalist Government.

The death of Mr. Maximian Joseph Gomes took place in Malacca on November 10. He was a school teacher, and was so engaged for over forty years. He was born in Malacca, and educated at the High School (then known as the Free School), leaving there to go to Raffles Institution, Singapore, as a teacher, where he remained for about four years. He then went back to Malacca, to join the teaching staff of the High School, from which he retired on pension.

## [11] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS.

Mrs. A. P. Scott has returned to Shanghai from England.

Dr. A. G. H. Smart has been appointed Health Officer for Penang.

Mr. Chung Wing-kwong, the Canton educationalist, returned to Canton from Hong Kong yesterday.

A farewell function to Rao Sahib D. Arulanandam, the Indian Government Agents, in Singapore was largely attended.

Mr. D. Beatty, Secretary for Chinese Affairs, S.S., has been granted leave of absence for two and a half months, before retirement.

Mr. B. Preedy, of Anderson School, Ipoh, goes on Home leave shortly. His place will be taken by Mr. Lewis, who arrived from Home recently.

A Reuter cable, from Tokyo states that the Bishop of London arrived there yesterday afternoon, and is stopping two or three days at the British Embassy before going to Korea. A very full programme has been arranged.

The health of the Emperor of Japan continues unimproved, according to court physicians, and His Majesty will spend the remainder of the winter in the Imperial Villa at Hayama, of which he is very fond. The health of the Empress this winter is described as excellent.

In connection with the taking over of the control of the International Mixed Court in Shanghai, Marshal Sun Chuan-fang, and Civil Governor Chen Dao-yi, have jointly appointed Mr. Zee Vei-tsing, former Chief Procuretor of the Shansi High Court, to take the change of the preparatory work for the formation of a provisional Court in Shanghai in place of the present International Mixed Court.

The death occurred at the General Hospital at Shanghai last week, of Miss E. Fletcher, the Mitron of the Hospital. Miss Fletcher was taken suddenly ill with internal trouble and although everything possible was done it was too late to save her life. The deceased lady assumed duty in Malaya in 1908 and, apart from furlough, had been continuously on duty at Pemang, Malacca and, for the last four years, Singapore.

## [13] BEST

In Washington on November 18, Gov.-Gen. Wood of the Philippine Islands was charged with “usurpation of Legislative and judicial functions” in abolishing the board of control of Manila in a statement issued by the Washington office of the Philippine Independence League.

A Peking message. of Nov. 18 states: —As a result of "to-day's meeting of the Cabinet, the Government has instructed the Ministry of Home Affairs to make preparations for the restoration of administrative power of the Belgian Confession in Tientsin, which is to be followed by the declaration of the invalidity of the Sino-Belgium Treaty of Commerce.

"It appears that many people in other provinces" (writes the Kueiyang correspondent of the "N.C. Daily News") "think that travelling in Kueichow is very dangerous on account of robbers. I am glad to say that since Gen. Chow Hsi-cheng took over the Governorship of the province, he has been steadily suppressing robbers and it is probably as safe to truvel in most parts of this province as elsewhere.

The Western conception of "the sinister Oriental" it seems is not yet dead. On November 17, says the "N.C. Daily News," a number of "Ryndum" students, anxious to get as much colour as possible in the short space of time at their disposal, but with no ambitions in the way of waking up in the middle of the night with "a fendish yellow devil" leaning over them with a knife, demanding their money or other life, asked a police officer if he thought it would be very dangerous to sleep in a Chinese hotel for the night. We suspect the officer of a sense of humour, for though his reply is not on record, we do know that the students dropped the idea.

## [14] *(no headline: column 4)*

Mr. J. A. Spiller, acting General Manager, F.M.S. Railways, is going Home.

Mr. Chin, Nyit Aun, an ex-pupil of the Anglo-Chinese School, ipoh, is now on his way back to Malaya. He has passed his Final Civil Engineers' Examination at the Battersea Polytechnic.

A rather sensational report published locally last week that Sun Chuan-fang's family had left Shanghai and taken refuge in Tientsin was evidently erroneous, says the "N.C.D. Mail." Seven delegates (tai-piao) from Shanghai Sun arrived last week, but the report that they were Sun's "family" caused much amusement.

A very successful Franco-British tiffin was held at the Royal Air Force Association of Shanghai headquarters, on Nov. 19, the guests being British and French officers. These included Lieutenant Bruyere, the new Deputy Chief in the French Police, Captain E. Fiori, Chief of French Police. Paymaster Commander J. M. L. Cusack, Resident Naval Naval Officer, Commander Harvey, and Captain M. Chapeaux, Chief of the French Fire Brigade of the French Fire Brigade.

News has been received that Mr. Tan Soo-hock, F.R.C.S. Edin., eldest son of Mr. Y. E. Tan, has left Genoa for Singapore and is due there on or about the 26th inst. Mr. Tan Soo-hock, after graduating in Edinburgh, proceeded to Vienna to take up post graduate work and completed a special course on eye diseases. On completing this course he went to Berlin, Leipzig, and Paris to study the methods adopted at the eye clinics in these places. He will start private practice in Singapore, says the "Free Press."
