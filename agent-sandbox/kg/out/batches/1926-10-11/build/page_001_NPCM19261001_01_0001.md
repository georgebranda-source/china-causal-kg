## [6] FOES ON FRONT AND REAR.

Sun Chuan-fang Advances on. Wuchang.

From Out Special Correspondent.
Continuing their retreat on the most northerly sector of the Klingai-Hunan front, the Nationalists give the impression of falling back in preparation for a determined stand, rather than through inferiority in the field.

Marshall Sun Chuan-fang is now estimated to have 80,000 in Hupeh province, on the march to Wuchang. The southerners opposed to him there do not number more than 50,000, some of whom have been fighting for two months.

On the centre and south of this front—where the southern troops are comparatively fresh—there has been little of account during the last two days except that the Cantonese are retiring to preserve their alignment with those further north.

In the absence to date of a big, deciding battle, it is difficult to predict whether Sun Chuan-fang will capture Wuchang, Hanyang and Hankow.

The southerners are now sopperilously near their line of communication with Canton that unless they stem the tide within a few days, they will be pushed out into the south-west of China, having then to make a detour home.
On Wednesday and Tuesday, the "5 province allies" were moving forward gradually, keeping in touch with the southerners, but trying to press back the whole of the northern sector.

Advanced, brigades of the southerners still hold strategic points, abutting into the allies' lines.

Chang Kai-shek's Position.

Sun Chuan-fang's main advance has been along the southern bank of the Yangtse, going up river close to the right bank, with the Wu-Han cities as his objective.

There is now an appreciable danger that the Cantonese will struck a dendly blow here.

Rather than face such a risk, it is surmised that General Chang Kai-shek will shorten his line by sacrificing Hankow, and concentrating on a stand between Yochow and Changsha.

Meanwhile General Yang, Sen's id portends an attack on the Nationalist rear, in Hupeh.

The Cantonese are facing Sun Chuan-fang from the east and Yang Sen is marching down on them from the west.

Race for the Spoils?

Further north, Marshal Wu Pei-fu still talks about a counterattack. Outposts of the Nationalist 8th army, stretched northwards from Hankow, are gradually coming back southwards, but Wu Pei-fu has yet to advance again. Should there be a southern debacle there may follow a unique race for control of Wu-Han between five contenders, viz. (1) Gen. Liu Tso-lung (who betrayed Wu Pei-fu and now occupies Hanyang as commander of 15th Nationalist Army); (2) Gen. Liu Yu-chen (defender of Wuchang and pro-Wu); (3) Sun Chuan-fang (going up the Yangtse); (4) Yang Sen (coming down the Yangtse); (5) Wu Pei-fu (from Honan province, in the north).

### Picket Chief Says He Will Leave on October 10.

. The "China Mail" learns from an authentic source that the pickets did not leave Samchun yesterday.

## [21] NOT USING WHARF YET.

"Not until the boycott is officially finished," was the reply of Mr. John Arnold to-day when asked as to when the Steamboat Co.'s vessels were going alongside the wharves at Canton again.

The “China Mail” asked, in view of Chinese expectations that the Hong Kong, Canton and Macao Steamboat Co., Ltd., will take the lead in “restoring” communications,” would the steamboats go to the wharf instead of anchoring in the stream at Canton.

Mr. Arnold, as secretary, said that the company did not intend to do so until the boycott is actually and officially over.

Nor is there any truth in rumours that the Company has been overhauling the wharves at Canton. "None of the company's officials have been near the wharves for over a year," stated Mr. Arnold.

### [11] MOTOR BOAT FIRE

People on the Queen's Pier this morning were treated to a little excitement by the motor boat "Lee Kuen" suddenly catching fire.

The blaze was caused by a leak-age in the petrol pipe, and was confined to the engine parts.

Boatmen in the vicinity assisted with buckets of water, and the incident was soon over.

A little damage was done to the engine of the boat.
