## [6] CHINESE DELEGATE'S SPECIAL STATEMENT.

Important recommendations are contained in part four of the Extra-Territoriality Commission's report which was issued by the Chinese Foreign Office yesterday.

The Commission recommends a progressive scheme for the abolition of extrality. China meanwhile taking certain steps to improve the administration of justice.

The fifth recommendation deals with the question of taxation as follows:—“Pending the abolition of extraft, the nationals of the Powers concerned should be required to pay such taxes as may be prescribed in the laws and regulations duly promulgated by the competent authorities of the Chinese Government and recognised by the Powers concerned as applicable to their nationals.”

The Chinese delegate qualified his assent to the report, and issued a declaration expressing disappointment that the Commission found it inexpedient to recommend the immediate abolition of extrality owing to the conditions of the country but hoping that once China has completed her judicial reform programme, the Powers will open negotiations for the final abolition of extrality.

The Walchiaopu this evening issued part four of the Extrality Commission's report consisting of the Commissioner's recommendations.

The Commissioners express the opinion that when these recommendations have been reasonably complied with the several Powers would be warranted in relinquishing their respective rights of extrality.
They declare that it is understood that upon the relinquishment of extrality, the nationals of the Powers concerned will enjoy freedom of residence, trade and civil rights in all parts of China, in accordance with the general practice in intercourse among nations, and upon a fair and equitable basis.

## [7] *(no headline: column 2)*

Judicial System Reform.
The first recommendation declares that the administration of justice with respect to the civilian population in China must be entrusted to a judiciary which will be effectively protected against any interference from the Executive or other branches of the Government, whether civil or military.

The second urges the adoption of a programme for the improvement of the existing legal, judicial and prison systems, and suggests that the Chinese Government should consider parts I and II of the report with a view to making such amendments and taking such action as may be necessary to meet the observations there made.

After enumerating a number of laws which it is recommended should be completed and enforced, the Commissioners urge the Government to extend the system of modern courts, prisons and detention houses with a view to elimination of the old-style magistrates' courts, prisons and detention houses, and to make adequate financial provision therefor.

A Progressive Scheme.
The third suggests that prior to reasonable compliance with all the recommendations above-mentioned, but after the principal items thereof have been carried out, the Powers concerned, if so desired by the Chinese Government, might consider the abolition of 'extraity' according to such a progressive scheme as may be agreed upon.

The fourth suggests that pending the abolition of Extrality, the Powers concerned, with the co-operation of the Chinese Government wherever necessary, should make certain modifications in the existing systems and practice of extrality.

Modifications Outlined.
Peking, Nov. 29.
These suggested modifications are:—

(1) The Powers concerned should administer as far as practicable in their extra-territorial or consular courts such Chinese laws and regulations as they deem proper to adopt.
(2) As a general rule Mixed Court cases between nationals of the Powers concerned as plaintiffs and persons under Chinese jurisdiction, as defendants, should be tried before the modern Chinese courts. Evidence of Goodwill.

The declaration appreciates as evidence of the Powers' goodwill the recommendations respecting immediate discontinuance of the practices which are not warranted by the treaties, and other improvements in the existing relations which will promote better understanding between the Chinese and foreigners.

The declaration emphasises that the earnest hope of the Chinese people is to see the extraterritorial regime abolished and replaced by a condition consonant with the sovereignty of their country, and this aspiration can be aided in its fulfillment by a sympathetic attitude on the part of the Powers.

The Chinese people confidently anticipate a further manifestation of that attitude and early realisation of their legitimate aspirations.

## [9] HANKOW TROUBLE.

Hankor has brought about a position similar to that in Canton a few months ago.
It is interesting to note that the official nominated by the Nationalist Government, ever since the capture of the Wu-Han cities, to be Superintendent of Customs at Hankow, is Mr. Chan Kung-pok (Cantonese pronunciation).

This post does not carry with it any administrative powers over the Chinese Maritime Customs, which, of course, is directed by the Inspector General at Peking. But at the major treaty port in each province, a Chinese official holds the post of Superintendent. Usually this official is also Commissioner for Foreign Affairs for the provincial government.

## [14] Who Mr. Chan Is.

<sub>section under [13] COUNSEL RAISES QUESTION OF HANDCUFFS</sub>

When Hankow was in Wu Pei-fu's hands, a Northerner held the position but Mr. Chan succeeded him on the Cantonese entering the city.

Prior to going North, Mr. Chan was head of the Government Labour Department (not Ministry). He was nominated as one of the official delegates to the Boycott Conference but did not serve, as he left for the front.

At Hankow now, some of the extremists are agitating for the native employees of the Customs to form their own union.

## [15] Trouble Anticipated.

<sub>section under [13] COUNSEL RAISES QUESTION OF HANDCUFFS</sub>

Such a union was started in Canton. Loyal employees were intimidated but Colonel F. Hayley-Bell (then Commissioner) was' adamant. A stage was reached when some of the loyalists had to be quartered" in the Customs House. Then the trouble blew over and Chinese in the Canton Customs remain without a union.

It is alleged that the formation of a Customs employees' union is contrary to good working.

Mr. Chan Kung-pok is reported to have made a speech in which he called upon the employees of the Hankow Customs to join the (proposed) union, if only from patriotism, as the Customs "should be run by Chinese and not by foreigners." Accordingly, a certain amount of trouble is anticipated.

A general anti-foreign strike and boycott is threatened for December 4, embracing servants, office workers and all Chinese in foreign employ. British residents have cabled the Foreign Office for additional protection. Arrangements are being made for the formation of a corps of special constables, since the agitators are intimidating the Chinese municipal police and threatening their families.

The French are taking up a very strong attitude with the result that after a complaint to the military a soldier and a civilian were executed this morning for creating a disturbance in the French concession last night. The various concessions are making plans for storing up food.—Reuter.
