## [1] YANGTSE COMPLEX.

**INSPECTING BRITISH GOODS SOCIETY:**

**SCHEME FOR BOYCOTT.**

A fortnight ago the promoters of an anti-British boycott were getting busier.

They have organised an anti-British preaching society and these were lecturing any who would listen to them as to the heinous crimes of the British.

Then an inspection society for the examining of British goods and enforcing a boycott has also been formed.

According to the published programme of the latter they are first to tabulate the number of British merchant ships flying on the Yangtse, the number of banks established by the British in China, the number of factories, the different type of British banknotes, the number of British companies, concessions and pieces of land leased, the raw material and Chinese manufactured goods which are imported and exported by the British and the Chinese in the employ of the British. When all this information is gained they intend to take steps for a complete shut down of all British tons living in China, says the "Central China Post."

## [6] ASSAULT CASE.

**STABBED A WAITER.**

When three Chinese were charged before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning, with assaulting a restaurant "waiter," Mr. A. J. O. Donoghue was not present to defend the first two men. The case was about to be adjourned until to-morrow when Divisional Inspector Grant asked for permission to withdraw the charge against the two, as he had no evidence against them. These men were discharged and the case against the third man was continued.

The complainant said that the defendant made a threat on October 8, to do him harm for meddling with his affairs. At 2 o'clock the following morning, while he was seated outside a house at West Point, defendant with several others rushed at him and stabbed him on the left arm with a dagger. They then made their escape.

A district watchman "in evidence said that he heard cries "of "save life" and saw the complainant bleedingly profusely from the stab wound. He searched the vicinity and found the defendant disabled in the back yard of a house. The man had been injured by falling on an iron spike in trying to evade arrest.

Defendant, who denied that he was the assailant, was sentenced to six weeks' hard labour.

## [7] SANK WITH SHIP.

**CHINESE STEAMERS COLLIDE AT SAMSUI**

**20 PASSENGERS DROWNED.**

Some 20/steerage passengers are reported to have been drowned as a result of a collision between two Chinese river steamers of the smaller type on the Canton—Wuchow run.

The collision occurred about ten o'clock at night on Tuesday about forty minutes run out of Samsung, the West River.

One of the vessels (the "Leung Kwong") was badly holed near the bows and sank in a few minutes. The upper deck passengers were saved but, the whole of the steerage passengers are reported to have gone down with the vessel.

The "Tin Sing," the other vessel, was badly damaged but was able to keep afloat.

Both vessels, which run under the Chinese flag, were callers at Hong Kong in pre-strike times; the "Leung Kwong" being once on the Hong Kong—Canton run.

## [8] CAFE THEFT.

**ABSCONDING EMPLOYEE CAUGHT.**

**RECOGNISED BY POLICE**

A Chinese boy who absconded from the Cafe Albert in May last with a sum of money was recognised by a European police officer and arrested at Kowloon yesterday. This morning the boy was charged before Major C. Willson at the Central Magistracy with larceny by bailee of $290 and a bank paying-in book.

M. Albert Guetat, the proprietor of the Cafe, said that he sent the defendant to the bank to pay in two cheques and $20 in cash (total $290) which had been entered in the book, but he disappeared without carrying out his errand. Witness visited Kowloon yesterday and identified the defendant when he was pointed out by the police.

Defendant, said he lost the docubank and was afraid to return. He offered to repay the money in installments if complainant would take him back.

The Magistrate sentenced him to two months' hard labour.
