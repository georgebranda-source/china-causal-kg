Groton, Mass., October 2,—A revolver and rifle battle between the Kn Klux Klan and the Anti-klan forces numbering more than 500 in which more than 100 shots were fired after midnight was under investigation by the authorities today.

Mrs. K. G. Robinson, of Kingsclere Hotel, Kowloon, has reported to the police that she lost a pair of field glasses, valued at $30, on Wednesday afternoon. The article, which bears her initials, was left on a seat at the "Star" ferry wharf on the Hong Kong side.

A Chinese who was recently arrested in Kowloon City with a dagger and two pieces of wire in his possession was to have appeared before two magistrates at Kowloon yesterday afternoon, but owing to the inability of Major C. Wilson to sit together with Mr. J. H. B. Nihill, the case was adjourned until next Tuesday.

Rangoon.—The Burma ruby mines are being offered for sale as a going concern, subject to the approval of the Government of India. The mines, which are at Magot, have been operated by a London company for many years, and have produced from surface working gems to the value of three crores of rupees (about £2,000,000).

"There has been no legislation and no attempt at legislation in regard to flannelette," said Dr. W. Aylmer Lewis, holding an inquest on Anne's Beddow, aged E4, wife of a market gardener at Balmer-grove. Welshampton, Shropshire, who died from burns. Nearly all the coroners in England, said Dr. Lewis, were tired of talking about the dangers of flannelette. The number of inquests he had held on deaths from this material was appalling, but to protest was like the voice of one crying in the wilderness.

Washington, October 2.—President Coolidge gave assurances to day that there would be no material reduction in national defence appropriations for the next fiscal year. This synchronised with the disclosure that the War Department already has taken steps to loop nearly 1,000 enlisted men off the regular army this year to avoid a deficit under the current appropriation in connection with the stop order on recruiting issued September 15. It was explained, however, that the cut in the enlisted strength is temporary and does not mean a permanent reduction of the army.

## [7] *(no headline: column 2)*

The Warrant Officers and Sergeants of the East Surrey Regiment are giving a farewell dance in the R.E. Theatre, Wellington Barracks to-morrow evening.
The first experiment of broadcasting a film was made from the Manchester station. A film called "The Greater Glory," a story of Vienna during the war, was chosen, and a dramatised version of it was given from the studio, with an announcer relating the story as the scenes changed. An orchestra supplied the appropriate martial or peaceful atmosphere, and a medley of cheering supplied the crowd effects.

The forthcoming weddings are announced of Albert George Martin, engineer in the employ of Messrs. Jardine Matheson Co., Ltd.; to Isabel Emma Louisa Holloway, residing at the Army Ordnance Quarters; and Hilmar Flrenz Sommers, of the Union Building, to Ethel Mary Saffold, of Repulse Bay Hotel.

Buenos Aires, September 28.—The Argentine Supreme Court today ruled that foreign women may acquire Argentine citizenship by complying with the usual formalities. This opens the door for rich American women to obtain easy divorces for the Argentine law will now be more attractive than that of France.

The Bishop of Truro has received from an anonymous donor the sum of £7,000 towards the provision of a new church at Carbis Bay. This help comes most opportunely when an attempt at church extension is being made in the many new places around the coast of Cornwall that are becoming increasingly popular as holiday resorts. The gift was received through the post in seven brand new £1,000 Bank of England notes enclosed in a registered envelope, with a tiny scrap of paper bearing in little characters the words, "For Carbis Bay Church."

In memory of the late Mr. James Oxberry, an inscribed tablet was unveiled at the R.A.O.B. Institute last night in the presence of a large attendance of members. Feeling reference to the work of the "Father of Buffaloism in the Far East" and the loss the Order had sustained in his death was made by Primo Jefferys, P.G.P. of China. Among those present at the unveiling ceremony was Mrs. Oxberry, the widow, who was presented with a framed set of "in memoriam" verses, composed by Bro. Douglas Gibb, Secretary of the R.A.O.B. Institute. An "Open Night" concert followed the programme having been arranged by Mr. Jack Grenham.
Cologne.—Train-wrecking attempts continue in the Rhineland and Westphalia. Recently a train from Mayence to Coblenz was heavily stoned and two travellers were seriously injured by glass splinters. Near Plettenberg an electric train was derailed and it was found that the line had been tampered with. Similar reports have been received from other parts of Germany and the State Railway authorities are offering large rewards for the apprehension of the perpetrators.

No fewer than twenty-three countries, including Great Britain, are represented at the conference of the Air Traffic Association which was opened in the Federal Council Hall of the Foreign Office at Berlin. After official greetings by representatives of the German Government, Heir Wronsky, director of the German Air Line Hansa, was voted to the chair, and the German draft for a universal aerial bill of lading was discussed. A winter International air service and insurance questions were considered.

## [10] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS.

Bob Harrod the last of the original Sandringham staff appointed by King Edward VII, when, as Prince of Wales, he took over the estate in 1862, has just retired with a pension at the age of 76. The retiring age is 70, but King George waived the rule in this case because Harrod was hale, an exceptionally good man in the daffy, and wanted to continue his occupation. He is still in good health, and, having no dairy work to do, is pursuing his favourite hobby of gardening.

Vice-Admiral Sir H. W. Richmond, K.C.B., has assumed the duties of Commandant of the Imperial Defence College. As announced by the Prime Minister on June 22, last, the function of the college will be the training of a body of officers and civilian officials in the broadest aspects of Imperial strategy. Vice-Admiral Richmond commanded the East Indies station from October 1923, to October last year, when he was succeeded in the post by Rear-Admiral W. M. Ellerton, C.B.

Amsterdam, September 22.—Serious disorders are reported from Ede, in the Province of Gelderland, but no fatalities have occurred. The War Office statement confirms the reports and says that complete order has now been re-established.

Copenhagen.—A Danish doctor named Vendel believes that he has discovered the cause of foot-and-mouth disease in a fungus of the Monilla family. His theory is that this fungus acts as the primary agent of infection and creates favourable conditions for other bacteria to become malignant. Iodine proved effective against the action of the fungus at an early stage of the disease.

New York.—According to a Billings (Montana) message, Thomas D. Campbell, "known in America as the 'World's Wheat King," is in the midst of what is believed to be the largest thrashing enterprise in the history of agriculture. On the 48,000 acres he has under cultivation on the Crow Indian Reservation he is thrashing, a wheat crop which he estimates "will reach 500,000 bushels." Upwards of 200 men are employed in the actual thrashing operations.

Concord New Hampshire, September, 30.—The Democratic state, convention to-day went on record as favouring the enforcement of all laws "including the Eighteenth amendment," and held the Republican party responsible for the failure to enforce the dry laws. Ex-Senator Thomas P. Gore of Oklahoma in the keynote address to the convention that the farmers of the west were displeased, with the administration which he said promised everything and gave nothing, assailed the Republican claims of prosperity and economy as fictitious.

A unique display of Chinese fireworks will be given at "Lee Garden", East Point, tomorrow and on Sunday, beginning at 3.30 each night. The feature of the show is a "human bonfire dance," not unlike the war gestures of the Red Indians. As far as is known, the performer is decorated with cartwheels and other explosives and he moves around in such a way as to give an impression that a lighted set was going round automatically. The necessary articles for the displays have been brought down from Canton at considerable risk and expense—owing to interference by "strike pickets."

## [13] *(no headline: column 4)*

Prince Alexandre Murat died in Paris from pneumonia. He was the son of Prince Joachim Murat and Princess Murat (nec Elchingen), and was born in 1889. He married, in 1920, Mlle. Gillois, daughter of Colonel Gillois, and leaves three children—two girls and a boy. By profession he was an engineer. He served in the artillery during the war, was mentioned several times in despatches, and was awarded the Croix de Guerre.

Mr. Robert C. Coudray, of the American Foreign Service, attached to the Consulate General, Hankow, has received a well earned promotion from a Secretaryship to that of Vice-Consul, taking his oath of office at 12:30 o'clock September 27 at the Consulate General. Consul General Frank P. Lockhart administered the oath in the presence of Admiral Henry H. Hough, United States Navy, and the Admiral's Aide, Lieutenant-Commander L. J. Wilthe, U.S.N.

Field-Marshal von Mackensen's visit to the ex-Kaiser at Doorn has revived certain unconfirmed but persistent rumours as to the latter's alleged falling health. It should be remembered, however, that, unlike Field-Marshal von Hindenburg, for instance, von Mackensen, before the war, was very much seen at Court and was, in his capacity as the erstwhile military mentor of the Crown Prince, naturally on a fairly intimate footing with Wilhelm II.

Athens.—Owing to the discovery of a plot to liberate General Pangulos and permit his flight abroad he has been removed from the island of Egina and is to be interned in an old Turkish fortress in Crete. Apparently the ringleaders of the plot were Captain Roll Alexis and M. Bouldies, the Director of Military Justice, both of whom have been arrested and cast into prison together with General Keroulis and M. Maeris, the inte Dictator's right hand man.
