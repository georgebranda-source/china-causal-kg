Another sensation in the art world of Moscow has been caused by the suicide of one of the most famous violinists of Russia, David Krein, Professor of the Moscow Conservatoire and soloist at the Opera (says a "Central News" message from Moscow).

With the entry of the troops into Hankow, says a North China contemporary, counterfeit notes of the Bank of China (so finely forged that one "cannot distinguish them") are being circulated in "Hankow, with the result that the markets there are panic-stricken.

Feking, October 7. — The Watchlaop is expected to declare the expiration of the term of the Siro-Japanese Commercial Treaty on the 19th instant. The Ministry is now making necessary preparations for the negotiations with Japan on the revision of the Treaty.

New York.—A chess team. In London will play a team in Chicago by cable "on November 8 for the British-American Chess Trophy," presented by Mr. Samuel Insull of Chicago. It is understood that the Lord Mayor of London and the Mayor of Chicago are to make the first moves, after which "play will" be continued by the respective teams.

Peking, October 2.—According to an article in the “Chen Pao” today, the Chinese government's investment of 5,000,000 tacks in Russo-Asiatic Bank share was made in 1896, the interest being meant for educational purposes and being placed at the disposal of the Education Ministry. Interest was paid up to 1917, since which year no interest has been paid, according to the paper. This information was given in a report sent to the Foreign Ministry by the Ministry of Education.

Shanghai, October 8.—That no employees over 40 years of age be engaged by the Shanghai Municipal Council is the recommendation made to the Council by members of the Staff Committee, according to the Municipal Gazette. At a recent meeting, it was submitted that it was desirable that a definite ruling be made as to the age limit at which employees should be engaged for the Council's service. After discussion, it was recommended that heads of departments be directed not to engage any employees, over 40 years of age without special reference to the Council.

Mr. T. K. Tseng, the Chinese Minister to Sweden, arrived in London on September 8 and is staying at Kensington Palace Mansions, De Vere Gardens.

Mr. T. Y. Lo, formerly Chinese Charge d'Affaires of the Legation in Portland-place, has arrived in London with Mrs. Lo, his wife, Mr. Lo is now Minister in Denmark.

Dr. Mello "Franco, Brazilian Minister to China" was among the passengers in the R.M.S.P. Company's "Almanzora," which arrived at Southampton on September 13 from South America.

Lord Willingdon, the new Governor-General of Canada, who was head of the Boxer indemnity Mission to China, was sixty years of age on September 12. It is a strange coincidence that Lord Byng, his immediate predecessor, celebrated his birthday on September 11.

The late Mrs. Frances Middleton Darbishire, Elms Cross, Bradford on-Avon, widow of Mr. Charles William Darbishire, formerly of Singapore and Liberal M.P. for the Westbury Division of Wilts, left estate in her own disposition of the gross value of £53,317, with net personality £52,947.

Rear-Admiral Count Tsuncha Sano, of the Imperial Japanese Reserve, sailed from Liverpool on September 11 on the White Star liner "Celtic" for New York's en route for home. He has been attending the International Boy Scout Conference at Kandersteg, Switzerland, as the representative of Japan.

An excellent concert arranged at the Military Hospital, Bowen Road, last night by Mrs. Robert Lewis was in the nature of a farewell to departing members of the Hong Kong Garrison Women's Fellowship. The following took part in the programme: the Rev. G. E. Arrowsmith, Mrs. W. R. Fleming, Lieut. A. S. Bedelia, Mr. R. M. Keown and Miss Audrey Steele. The Lyric Orchestra played several selections while Tirohito Takada and Gonuske Kawasaki, the famous Japanese dancing conjurers made a special appearance.

Minehead.—The semi-final of the Junior Championship in the West Somerset Polo Tournament was being played between the 17/21 Lancers and the Zawatis when a big stag appeared on the ground with, close on its heels, the Devon and Somerset Staghounds in full cry. Leaping a flight of hurdles in its course, the stag went to water in the Avill river, which skirts the polo ground. Polo players and spectators promptly left one-diversion for another and ran to the bank of the river, where, in the midst of the excited crowd, the "mort" (death of a stag) was sounded.

Mrs. Shackleton, the mother of Sir Ernest Shackleton, the well-known explorer, has died at her home at Sydenham, in her eighty-first year. Early in August Mrs. Shackleton, upon rising from her chair, slipped and fell heavily to the floor. She was discovered lying in considerable pain, and a doctor who was summoned found that her right hip was fractured. She was later reported to be making progress, but gradually became weaker, and her death was not unexpected.

Manila, October 7.—Vice-Governor Gilmore yesterday announced that the bureau of health is soon to launch an extensive and vigorous campaign for the control of malaria diseases in the island. Such a campaign has been decided upon following the discovery of paris green as a cheap larvaeial, the result of four years of experimental work by the Rockefeller Foundation in co-operation with the bureau of health.

Basle.—The World Power Conference, which is meeting here, has requested the International Executive to take the initiative in creating an organisation for the purpose of examining all the methods employed, and the experiments made, in the use of electricity in agriculture. A representative from the Côte d'Or has presented a report on the use of electricity in such things as drying coconuts and destroying insects on trees.

Berlin.—The Communist Party in the Prussian Diet has repeated its demand for the dismantling of the well-known "Alley of Victory," which runs across the Tiergarten in Berlin, and is lined with statues of former Prussian rulers—on the plea that the Prussian Finance Ministry is spending too much for renovation work. This Communist motion has hitherto always been rejected by the Landing, but the party is now strong enough to assure Parliamentary treatment of the question, and a discussion of the fate of the "Alley of Victory" may be expected in the session.
