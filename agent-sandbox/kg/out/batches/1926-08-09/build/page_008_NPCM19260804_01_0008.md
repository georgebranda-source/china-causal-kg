## [7] LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS.

A vehicle of the China Motor Bus Company, travelling empty on the Hunghorn Road, overturned yesterday evening while going round a bend. The driver was badly hurt.

Parcels will not be forwarded by the General Post Office, Hongkong, to either Canton or Swatow till further notice. This decision is due to the Postal atrike in those two ports. The Canton Commissioner of Posts has asked Hongkong to send only "light" mail at present.

Posing as an official of the Seamen's Guild, a Chinese was alleged to have obtained $5 each from fourteen persons on an offer to find work for them on ships. He was charged before the Kowloon Magistrate yesturday with "obtaining money by false pretences and remanded.

Those interested are reminded of the second demonstration in occult science to be given at the Theatre Royal to-morrow start at 5.30 p.m., by Professor Maximilian Langsner (Kara-iki). The Professor has consented to give this matinee, in response to so many requests. Booking is with Messrs. Moutrie's.

Miss Mura Shipoff (the baby Pavlová) and her talented art ballet company are to give a show at the Seamen's Institute tomorrow evening, " start at 9.15 p.m. This popular company needs no introduction to the Hongkong public. It is hoped that good support will be forthcoming as the members have met with ill luck recently.

At the Auctioneering and Brokering Company's Sales Rooms yesterday, property comprising what was formerly known as the "Kung Wo" Hotel, and numbered 461, 463, 465, 467A and 469A Queen's Road West was disposed of. The property, which was disposed of by order of the mortgagee, consisted of three sections of the following areas: 9223 square feet, 263 square feet and 79 square feet. They were all sold as one lot, Mr. Tam Wing becoming the owner at $60,500, an increase of $500 on the upset price.

Hankow, July 21.—According to a message from the Honan Governor, all the missionaries at Sianfu are safe.

Chinese newspapers state that the head office of the Bank of Communications will shortly be removed from Peking to the French Concession in Tientsin under direction of Mr. Liang Shih-yi.

Nos. 155 and 157. Queen's Road West, with respective areas of 750 and 744 square feet were to have sold yesterday at the auction rooms of Mr. A. G. da Rocha but the sale was postponed, no date of auction being fixed.

Peking, July 29.—Generals, Chafr Chung-chang and Chang Hsueh-Hiang have been made responsible for the Nunkow warfare, so Marshall Wu Pei-fu intends to return to Hankow to take personal command of a renewed attack against the southern inviders in Hunan.

Peking, July 23.—The price of telegrams is to be raised because of the need of more funds, according to the Peking. "Yi Shih Pao," The new regulations have been drafted and sent to the Ministry of Communications. If the ministry passes them, they will soon become effective.

A few dogs ago it was reported that Chapel residents had supplied Dr. V. K. Ting with bottled samples of the water provided by the Chapel Waterworks, for him to examine and report on Dr. Ting is stated to have described this water as not only filthy, but "evil smelling." He has ordered the company to make big reforms, in consequence.

Peking, July 23.—News from certain military headquarters here says that Kiangsi Province has definitely thrown in its lot with Wei Pei-iu and is sending troops into Hunan to assist in the campaign to wrest back Changsha from the grip of Tang Sheng-chi, Gen. Teng Ju-cho is preparing to send three divisions and two mixed brigades into Hunan by way of Ping Siang, which is close to the Hunan border and southeast of Changsha.

## [8] *(no headline: column 2)*

Rome, July 24.—Commander Francesco de Pinedo declared today that the accident to his second plane would not delay his world flight, as the damaged plane was not intended to be used on the tour.

With regard to the tragic death of Mr. F. Glass of the Hongkong Electric Co., Ltd. last Saturday at East Point, an advertisement appears to-day asking for claims against the estate of the deceased to be sent to Messrs. Johnson, Stokes and Master.

Estate in Hongkong amounting to $1,900 was left by Mr. R. H. B. Mitchell, formerly of the Hongkong, Whampoa Dock, who died whilst home on leave in December of last year. Probate of the will has been granted at the Supreme Court, Hongkong.

"Estate in Hongkong valued at $44,700 (comprising mainly interests in Jardine Matheson and Co., Ltd.) was left by the late Mr. Thomas Shaw Forrest whose will has been re-sealed at the Supreme Court. Mr. Forrest died at St. Andrew's, Fifeshire, in November of last year. His estate in England was £63,538.

Moscow, July 22.—The funeral of Felix Dzerhinsy, People's Commissar, took place to-day, the body being buried in Red Square, near the Kremlin Wall, behind the Lenin mausoleum. The coffin was followed to the grave by members of the Government, delegations from many cities of the Republic and hundreds of thousands of Moscow residents. As the remains were lowered into the grave, at 7 p.m. Moscow time, a gun-salute was fired in every town in Russia and the factory sirens wailed a mournful farewell.

The spring session of the London Diocesan Conference opened at Church House, Westminster, recently, the Bishop of London presiding. In his presidential address the Bishop said that there was a very grave change in the habits of the people, especially the well-to-do. He could not but view with really grave anxiety the great number of well-meaning Christian men all over London and the country who had given up Christian worship for golf. It was a bad example to young people.

Tokyo, July 30,—The Japanese authorities refused the landing of an Afghanistan delegate who was attending the Pun-Asiatic Conference on the ground that he had no passport in his possession, and detained him in an hotel at Moji. With the exception of the Chinese delegate no representatives from other countries are arriving.

Peking, July 23.—General Chay Yu-pu, for the purpose of getting funds, has sanctioned the petition of the government wine and tobacco bureau at Tientsin to levy fifteen per cent. on all sorts of foreign wine imported into Tientsin for public consumption. General Chu declares that foreign wine is now largely sold to wealthy Chinese so that it is quite proper to tax it.

Tokyo, "July" 29.—According to the "Nichi Nichi", the American Missionary, Dr. Heysmier, who was recently accused of branding the word "Thief" with acid on each of the cheeks, of a Korean boy whom he caught stealing apples from an orchard was to day publicly tried at Pienyang. The Procurator demanded, three months' imprisonment. Dr. Heysmier pleaded guilty.

Summer White House, July 28.
—It was stated on behalf of President Coolidge that the United States government had never entered any "gentlemen's agreement" with any European country relative to a settlement of the war debts. Reports that the administration had come to a secret understanding with certain debtors' nations were flatly denied.

Peking, July 23.—There is a great deal of poppy-growing in northern Anhui, under the regulation of the military, who, tax, it highly, extensively to prevent its cultivation, according to the Peking "Yi Shih Pao." The tax is four, five or six dollars a mow, according to the fertility of the fields, and the total receipts from taxation are over four million dollars. Up to date more than three million dollars have been collected. The inspectors, are asking for twenty percent, of the receipts, as they have not so far been paid for their services.

Manila, July 30.—The Past Grand Exalted Ruler of the Elks, Judge William Hawley Atwell, of Dallas, Texas, accompanied by his wife, Mrs. Atwell, will arrive in Manila on the President McKinley, August 14. Mr. and Mrs. Atwell will remain in the city only three days, as they plan to sail for the United States on the same steamer on August 17. While in the city they will be guests at the Manila Hotel. Judge Atwell was born at Sparta, Wisconsin, in 1869. He is a graduate of Southwestern University and of the University of Texas. He is a Republican and is editor and publisher of Atwell's Federal Criminal Law and Procedure.
