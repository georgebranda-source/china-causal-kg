## [6] LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS.

Another cause of enteric fever (typhoid) was reported on Wednesday. The patient was a Chinese from the village of Aberdeen.

Peking, July 30.—Replying to a letter from the Kuominchun forces denouning the Tariff Conference, the American Minister, Mr. McMurray, stated that as the Conference aims at the happiness of all China, it does not consider the interest of any particular party.

About a fortnight ago Holhow was plunged into complete darkness because the pickets seized a shipment of crude oil on route to the Holhow Electric Light Plant. Inasmuch as his plant furnishes lights to a big part of Holhow it was quite an inconvenience to the people. Some settlement was made the following day and the lights were on again.

Hitherto, convicts in Japan have not been allowed to take out life insurance policies, but the Department of Justice has decided to permit those whose behaviour in prison has been good and who show evident signs of amendment to take out State life insurance policies. The Governor's of all prisons were notified by the Department to that effect on July 22.

Berlin, July 26.—The Jugo-Slavin government has ordered wholesale reductions in the wages of state and civil service employees, according to a Belgrade dispatch received to-day.

"Officials with no income other than their salaries, who build their own houses are to be suspected, and officials, who have saved more than 10,000 are equally suspicious." This is the opinion of Mr. Kamiyama, the new Governor of Formosa.

Arrangements have been made by the Tokyo Association of School Teachers to send two women to America to study the educational system in use in the schools in the Western states. They are Miss Misa Yamakata and Miss Kiyo Takeda. They will leave Yokohama for San Francisco on August 26 aboard the N.Y.K. steamer Iyo Maru.

The five men against whom changes were brought of compliety in "a highway robbery on July 17 were committed for trial to the Criminal Sessions at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday." The robbery concerned three Chinese girls who were returning from Mongkok to Hunghom by way of the path leading along the railway when they were accosted by five men and robbed of jewellery.

Kobe, July 26.—The discovery on the seashore near Hiroshima of the corpses of a man, and a woman brought to a sad close an unusual and a not discreditable episode of the sea. The man was Shunko Shinowara, an artist, and Yashiko Murakami, his pupil. The artist was painting a picture of a mermaid, with the Hikoneshima rocks as a background, when a huge wave encompassed the girl who was standing in the water some distance out. He jumped in to rescue her, but both were drowned.

Berlin, July 26.—The chief of the French Commission for Occupation of the Rhineland has issued an order forbidding further processions and parades, especially those dealing in any way with the French occupation of this territory. The playing by the bands of German militaristic music is claimed to have incited the population, to act against the French. A similar decree has been issued for the Sear Basin district, and so, sweetening is the decree there that even parades by French troops with French bands playing are forbidden.

Tokyo.—There are 8,100,000 women and girls working for their living in Japan, including some 6,000,000 engaged in silkworn rearing, rice planning and other forms of agricultural labour, according to a report of the social bureau of the Home Office. House servants and restaurant waitresses number 514,000 and there are 78,000 women school teachers. Around a million girls are factory hands and there are 9,300 typists. The moving picture in Industry in Tokyo alone employs 620 actresses.

Peking, July 27.—Sun Pao-chi, appointed ambassador to Russia from China, is not able to journey to Moscow because of lack of funds. The Cabinet is said to have assured him they could raise transportation money but could give him no assurance of maintenance money for the embassy at Moscow. Dr. Koo, Finance Minister, is said to have been approached on the matter, but entered a polite refusal. Meanwhile Sun Pao-chi remains here against his will, as he is said to be anxious to leave the country.

According to the "San Francisco Chronicle" Major-General Sir Charles Herbert Powell, K.C.B., veteran of the British Army, who was Staff-Officer in China, 1900-1, was injured by a taxicab almost immediately upon his arrival at San Francisco. Travelling with the general from Shanghai, were Mr. and Mrs. E.F. Bothwell, local residents en route to England. Mr. Bothwell who is with the well-known local architects, Messrs. Palmer and Turner, left Shanghai, on May 27, with his wife on Home leave.

London, July 12.—The Military Correspondent of the "Daily Telegraph" understands that Colonel R. J. Collins has been chosen to command the Ninth Infantry Brigade, at Portsmouth, in succession to Sir Hugh Ellen. Probably Colonel Dill, commanding the Second Infantry Brigade at Aldershot, will succeed Col. Collins as Director of Military Training in India. It is reported that Vice-Admiral Richmond may be appointed Chief of the new Imperial Defence College.

Miss E. H. Mayhew, for 20 consecutive years headmistress of the Thomas Hanbury School for Girls, Shanghai, was a passenger on the Empress of Russia. She is returning to England by way of Canada, on retirement. Miss Mayhow ever since the formation of the Girl Guides has acted as Honorary Secretary and Treasurer. All her pupils present and former members of the tenching staff of the school and all who knew Miss Mayhew feel deep regret at losing her but felt that she well deserves to rest after such lengthy and continuous service.

## [9] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS.

The death occurred at the Fraser Home, Colombo, of Mrs. Margaret Doyle, aged 65 years, mother of Mr. Doyle, of the Colombo Stores, Ltd.

J. B. Hobbs writes as follows to Dr. John Rockwood:—"I greatly appreciate the charming souvenir which is so typical of your beautiful island, and it will always serve to remind me of the many pleasant hours I spent in Ceylon and of the excellent hospitality I have always received from the cricketers of your island." Will you, through your Press, please convey my hearty thanks and best wishes to all those who sent me the souvenir."

Miss Mura Shipoff and her art ballet company appeared at the Seamen's Institute last night under the patronage of the Rev. G. D. Waldegrave and gave a pleasing exhibition, prior to their intended departure for Manila next week. The Misses Andrews (of the Naval Yard) were very well received. Thanks are also due to Mr. G. H. Sellwood, manager of the Institute, for arranging the performance. The programme was published in yesterday's issue.

Mrs. Marie C. Haller, Republican, of Fort Thomas, is running for Congress from Kentucky on a "wet" platform.

Peking, July 31.—In reply to a request by the Peking Government urging him to send to the Government, the salt-revenue in Fukien Province, General Chou Yin-jen, Tupai of Fukien, has telegraphically replied that he cannot comply with the request unless the Government remits military funds to him.

Rumour is current that in succession to Dr. Sell, the German Ambassador in Tokyo, the German Minister in Portugal will be appointed to Tokyo. Neither the German Embassy nor the Foreign Office in Tokyo has yet heard anything about the allegation, and it is thought probable that even in case of the rumour providing to be true it will not be before the conclusion of the German and Japan commercial treaty.

In commemoration of the 40th anniversary of the promulgation of the Japanese Constitution, a Far Eastern Exposition will be held in Osaka for seven months, opening in March of 1929 at a cost of ¥26,000,000. All the countries in Asia, East Africa, Australia and the South Seas, and some of the nations in America and Europe, that have close economic relations with Japan, will be invited to participate in the exposition.

On Sunday, July 11, about six or seven men arrived late for Sunday. School in the church at Hoihow. They were not regular members of the congregation, and left during the closing prayer and were waiting outside when the congregation scattered. Just outside the church they attacked several of the Christian women, slapping them and striking them. They threatened them if they came to services next Sunday. They also attacked the hospital gatekeepers. No one was seriously wounded. The attack was quite unexpected.

A wedding in which a large circle throughout North China will be interested took place on July 26, at Peitaiho, when Gwendolen Mary Rees, daughter of the late Rev. Dr. W. Hopkins Rees was united in marriage with Jan Julius Ludovic Duijvendak, of Leyden. Miss Rees is well known and highly esteemed in Tientsin and North China both in Chinese and foreign circles. Mr. Duijvendak is Professor of Chinese in the noted University of Leyden, and has been spending some time in China in research work. The wedding took place at the home of Mrs. Hopkins Rees, Rocky Point.

Colonel J. Jarmer, Col. H. T. Trolle, Lt. Col. and Mrs. J. P. Andersen are leaving Siam. The latter, proceed to Rangoon and will stay in, Burma for some time before proceeding to Denmark.

Lieutenant-Commander C: M. Stack has taken up duty as Officer-Instructor to the Ulster Division of the R.N.V.R. at Belfast, in succession to Lieutenant-Commander E. J. J. Southby. Lieutenant-Commander Stack last served afloat as navigator of the Durban in China.

Major Witsell, who was a language officer in the American Embassy in Tokyo from 1920 to 1924, has returned to Japan to become assistant Military Attache of the Embassy, succeeding Major Pratton. Major Witsell arrived in Tokyo, July 21, on the President Jackson. Major Bratton is to be transferred to Manila, and will leave in September, with Mrs. Bratton, for his new post.

The death took place at the Fraser Home, Colombo, on July 18 of Mr. W. H. Goulstone, of Messrs. Gow, Somerville and Co., Colombo, in his 36th year. Mr. Goulstone had been ill for some time. He was connected with Eastern export and, after serving in Calcutta for some years, went to Ceylon and joined Messrs. Tarrant and Co. At the outbreak of the war he joined the Town Guard Artillery and volunteered for active service. He received a commission in the Indian Army Reserve of Officers and was gazetted to the Corps of Guides, with which he saw service in Mesopotamia.

Kandy, July 17.—The marriage took place to day, at St. Anthony's Cathedral, Kandy, of Hugh Noel. Wood, Superintendent of Police, Ratnapura, son of the Rev. H. Wood, M.A., Rector of Whitechurch, Bucks, with Stella Mary Leetham, the only daughter of Mr. A. R. P. Leetham, Divisional Inspector of Schools, Central Division, Kandy, and Ms. Leetham. The bride was given away by her father. The marriage ceremony was performed by His Lordship the Bishop of Kandy, assisted by the Rev. Father Charles Matthews, O.M.I., Rector of St. Patrick's College, Jiffua.
