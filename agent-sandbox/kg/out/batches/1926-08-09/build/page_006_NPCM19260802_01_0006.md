## [10] CLEAR THEM OUT!

If the recent general strike and the present coal crisis at Home have to be deplored, both are to be thanked for directing attention to the machinations of the "Reds." Several reputable newspapers have given prominence to their activities, and, incidentally, rebuked the Labourites for belitting the tell-tale documents exposed in the House of Commons by the Government. As one paper puts it, too many of the members of the Labour Parliamentary Party are always ready to place the foreigner invariably in the right when any question arises between aliens and their own countrymen. The way that they try to gloss over every unfriendly act of the Soviet Government, which is the mildest term we can use, toward bringing about a revolution and the overthrow of the British Empire, is a scandal to their patriotism—that is, if they have the slightest particle of such a virtue. The disclosure of direct communication between Communists at Home and the blood-stained Soviet Executive at Moscow has been most conclusively proved by the mass of documents published in the Blue Book. As a nation we are not only being attacked from Moscow. The plotters are on our own sole, free to carry out the

orders of the ruffians who have reduced Russia to unspeakable depths of degradation.

One does not generally look to a paper like the Police News, which usually confines itself to reports and pictures of sensational crimes, to indulge in outspoken language on such a subject. It is, therefore, gratifying to find in a recent issue a smart rebuke to those who would bolster up the "Reds." It points out that there can be no longer any doubt that immense sums of money have been remitted from the Moscow Government for the sole purpose of creating anarchy and revolution in Great Britain. Their idea is that if they can only smash up the British Empire they will find little or no difficulty in smashing up the capitalistic portion of the rest of the world. If our own people are ready to take Soviet money for such a purpose they are traitors of the worst type and should meet with the punishment that such miscreants deserve. If they are so enamoured of the methods of Moscow, and believe—as they profess to do—that Russia is such a paradise to live in, why in Heaven's name don't they go and live there? Britain could well spare them! But they know in their hearts that if they did so they would quickly lose every vestige of liberty and would soon sink to the degraded level of the poor Russian serfs who groan beneath the sanguinary cruelty of their rulers. But while they live in Britain, in a free land, they must be taught that if they conspire against the welfare and liberty they will in the future be met with sterner measures than in the past, and that, as traitors to their own kith and kin, they will find every hand against them. As for the alien intriguers in Britain many of whom are there under exceptional privileges, there is only one thing to be done with them. Clear the lot out bag and baggage, and take such steps as will prevent their polluting Britain's shores again.

Bucharest, Romania, July 25.
—Owing to continued raids by Bulgarian border troops, martial law has been proclaimed in the district of Dobrudja, and all movements of men in groups of more than four are forbidden by the Military Commander of the district. Additional troops are being sent to the district.

Malolos, Bulacan, July 26.—The passenger train from San Fernando, Pampanga, which left Malolos, at 9 o'clock this morning for Manila, while passing over the bridge under repair almost in front of the Guiginto station, fell into the river, as a result of the collapse of the bridge. The locomotive and one freight coach fell into the river. The freight coach was thrown straight on the river bed and served to stop the passenger coaches from falling. This prevented deaths or serious damage. The engineer was miraculously saved. He was bruised on the face.

## [14] LEAP TO DEATH.

**EAST POINT SENSATION.**

We regret to have to record the death of Mr. Frank Glass, mains engineer of the Hongkong Electric Company, which took place on Saturday afternoon in tragic circumstances.

During the past week Mr. Glass had been considerably depressed owing to private worries, but the news of his sudden death came as a shock to his friends and equally so to the writer who came to know Mr. Glass last week-end, having visited the Protestant Cemetery with him to inspect the damage done by the flood.

At about 2 o'clock on Saturday afternoon, he jumped out of the bathroom window of his residence. No. 12, East Point Terrace, second floor (opposite the Dairy Farm Company's premises at Causeway Day) into Pennington Street, a drop of about 30 feet. He was found to be suffering from multiple injuries and died shortly after his removal to the Government Civil Hospital.

Before throwing himself out of the window, Mr. Glass had locked the bathroom door from the inside and placed a chair to enable him to reach the window sill.

When a visit was paid late in the afternoon, a number of Chinese were still on the scene. It was apparent from a casual inspection of blood stains on the road that Mr. Glass dived out of the window over the footpath which runs along the side of the house.

## [18] PHILIPPINES.

**SENATE PRESIDENT BACK FROM AMERICA.**

**INTERVIEWED IN HONGKONG**

### -

The Hon. Sergio Osmena, President pro-tempore and majority floor leader of the Philippines "Sonate, who was sent by the Philippines Legislature as its special representative to attend the past session of the American Congress, and ask for the immediate independence of the Philippines, arrived on Saturday morning on board the President Jackson. After staying nine months in the United States, he is now on his way back to Manila. In an interview with a "China Mail" representative, the Filipino leader made the following statement:

"It has always been the aspiration of the Filipino people to become a free and independent nation. Having always had the generous encouragement of the American people, they have always been united in the pursuit of their aim."

The non-approval of any of the Bills, for granting the complete independence of the Philippines, during the last session of Congress, shows a backward step from their promise and policy of making Philippine Independence an accomplished fact.

"In my opinion, nothing will increase the good feeling of both the people of the United States and the Philippines, better than the prompt and complete execution of such policies."

Accompanying the Hon. Sergio Osmena is Dr. Jose Reyes, who is a Professor of the University of the Philippines, and who is traveling with Mr. Osmena as technical adviser.

## [23] CHOLERA.

**SERIOUS EPIDEMIC IN SHANGHAI.**

### 一

**MANY CHINESE DEATHS.**

In the last weeks Shanghai has been hearing much of the cholera situation here, and the two pathologists in the Laboratory of the Public Health Department of the Municipal Council have been working ten hours a day in an endeavour to put the final laboratory authenticity on the clinical or hospital diagnosis of the disease.

According to the health authorities, Shanghai is at the present moment in the state of a cholera epidemic. The fact does not imply, however, that the mortality rate from cholera is high. On the contrary compared with last year the death rate is considerably lower and during the week ending Saturday, July 24, a table prepared by the Health Department shows that one foreign death through cholera, that of a Russian girl resident in the French Concession, who tried to throw herself into the Soochow Creek not long ago in an attempt to commit suicide, 23 Chinese deaths in the Settlement, 8 Chinese in the French Concession, 42 Chinese in Chinese territory and 3 Chinese deaths of unknown residence have occurred. The lower death rate is attributed by the health authorities to a less virulent type of the disease, though a counteracting statement is made in the fact that the cholera season is just beginning here and that the peak of the epidemic is not expected to be reached before the end of August.
—"N. C. Daily News."

## [24] WAX EFFIGIES.

**INSECTS THAT WAR ON MANKIND.**

A new form of chamber of horrors, stocked with the wax effigies of criminals against the human race, has just been opened at the National History Museum, South Kensington. The exhibits are models of some of the more virulent disease-carrying insects, enlarged to from 100 to 200 times life-size, and the awful records of misdeeds which accompany them suggest that, in comparison, the common murderer is an angel of light. Strange as it may be, these ugly-looking parasites, some of them no bigger than a pin's head in their natural state, have been responsible for the most formidable maladies, which have not only caused millions of deaths, but vitally affected the histories of countries and peoples. Conspicuous amongst these exhibits are the malarial, yellow fever, and other mosquitoes, relatively less harmful, and these greatly magnified models show exactly the nature of the lancet-like probosces, with which their mischief is usually wrought. They are as valuable to the scientific investigator as they are interesting to the popular sight-seer. Then there is the tiny, midge-like sand fly, shown here as a fluffy creature, with wickedly piercing coal-black eyes. In the Eastern theatres of war its cruelly painful bite, though not fatal in its effects, put whole battalions out of action, and the worst offenders were apparently the females of the species, which usually do their infamous work by night.

The models, the work of three gifted artists, have been prepared under expert supervision, and it is claimed that there is nothing of the kind elsewhere so comprehensive and complete. In all there are about a dozen cases, and the explanatory labels, which give the life stories of the insects, with maps to show the parts of the world where they exact their greatest toll of man and animals, have been kept as free from technical terms as possible.
