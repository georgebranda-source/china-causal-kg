Cairo, September. — The Finance Commission decided not to recommend the Chamber to authorise the payment of interest on the Ottoman 4 per cent. loan of 1855, the guaranteed Egyptian tribute, on the ground that payments hitherto were made at the express demand of the Ottoman Government which no longer was suzgraim. The Coingh had a clann bill health on Friday; no new case notifiable disease being reported.

Toronto, August 1.—A collide dog crawled out of a railway wagon laden with grain at Port Arthur, Ontario, recently, having been carried all the way from Kindersley, Saskatchewan, and having been a week on the journey. It is surmised that while the wagon was being loaded at a country elevator the dog jumped in and that the grain poured in too fast to permit its escape. It was imprisoned under the weight of grain for a week.

Kobe.—Sleeping sickness has now caught Hyogo Prefecture in its grip and the Health Department is more panic-stricken than it was over cholera. The epidemic first broke out in Akashi-gun; since then more than 100 cases have been reported and 50 have died. All the gases are in Mihara-gun (Awa) Akashi-gun, Kako-gun, Iunnan-gun and Shikawa-gun. On the 4th inst., Prof. Nakano and other doctors held a meeting at the Prefectural Assembly hall and discussed prevention measures. A number of soldiers in the Himeji Garrison are affected.

During last year the increase in the price of salt and dried fish was less than half that of wet fish. The Asiatic much prefers wet fish. In times of prosperity he can afford more of it and so leaves out a proportional amount of salt and dried fish. The marked cheapness in Singapore of salt and dried fish is due to Singapore being the great collecting centre for the salt and dried fish trade of the archipelago. The high prices obtaining in Malacca are thought to be due to purchase on a small scale. The many Malacca dealers mostly come personally to Singapore and buy the small quantities they require.

San Francisco, September 10.—The cause of prohibition enforcement received a serious blow today when the circuit court of appeals ruled that prohibition agents were not empowered to seize liquor without research warrant. Hitherto, agents have searched automobiles for suspected liquor without a warrant, under the general provision that officers of the law could enter private property where it was thought a crime was being committed. It is understood that the government will institute an appeal in the San Francisco case to the supreme court at Washington, to obtain a final decision on this question.

Mr. G. G. Seth, Solicitor-General of the Straits Settlements, returned from leave by the "Morea."

His estate in Hong Kong amounted to $94,400 Among his bequests were the following, to the Shanghai St. Andrew's Society Tls. 1,000, and to the Scottish Company, Shanghai Volunteers Tls. 1,000.

Pin-money as a factor in restricting industrial output was mentioned by Mr. A. Angles of the National Institute of Industrial Psychology. Among reasons for restricted output he said was satisfaction with present earnings. This was generally the case with pin-money girls, who kept most of their earnings for dress allowance. That type, he said, had a harmful effect on the real worker and in one particular instance a well-known firm decided not to employ such girls.

Edmonton, August 1, — Statistics recently published show that for several years past November and December are the real marriage months in the prairie provinces of Canada. The reasons for this desertion of June in favour of November and December are purely economic, not sentimental. Wheat in Canada does not ripen until late in the autumn and sales off the grain are not made in most cases until the early winter. Therefore the cautious young farmer waits until nature has assured him a grain crop.

Johannesburg, South Africa, September 1.—A paradise for wives and mothers-in-law has been found in Northern Rhodesia by an expedition (ed) by Prof. Robert N. Doke, professor of ethnology at the Rand University. Prof. Doke studied the little known Lamba tribe, and found that in their extensive territory, succession to titles and property rights passes from mother, to daughter, not father to son. Fathers have no authority over their children, even officially. Women arrange all marriages, Husbands, upon marriage, move to their wives' villages. The mother-in-law as well as the wife, is entitled to order the husband about.

## [16] MAKING NAVAL HISTORY.

Officers and men of the China Squadron have been making history on the Yangtse. All the despatches published daily, with copious footnotes to tell strangers what the names of persons, places, &c. stand for, are reproduced.

Our reports give prominence to the rescue, the names of those concerned (also the mercantile marine officers), the effect of the bombardment and reprisals, and the steps towards negotiation.

Side issues at Hankow where warships and steamers have been fired upon, and in some instances retaliated, combine to make an impressive pen picture.

"Actions" in which American destroyers took part are also described.
