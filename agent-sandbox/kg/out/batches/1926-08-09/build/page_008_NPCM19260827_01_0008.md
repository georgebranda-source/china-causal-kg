Nairobi, August 12. — The lengthy trial of a native, charged with outraging an elderly European lady, aged 70 years, twice in a single night, and also with brutally assaulting her, concluded to day when the accused was found guilty and sentenced to 14 years' imprisonment and 24 lashes. This case, which occurred in Kijube on May 17, was one of the chief causes of anxiety regarding the safety of white women, which resulted in Government action and the revision of the law recently "any unced.

London, August 12. — Mr. Havelock Wilson, addressing seamen at Middlesbrough, said that one of the greatest misfortunes of the Labour movements was its entry into politics. The Trades Unions were now in a very serious condition, nearly everyone of them being bankrupt; worse still, hundreds of thousands of men had declared that they would never pay anything more into the Unions. Labour and Capital were mutually indispensable. "Let us try and foster a better understanding with the employers," added Mr. Wilson. "This is the one way in which the Unions can recover. I am glad to say that we have arrived at something like an intelligent position in the shipping industry."

Further evidence was taken at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday afternoon in the case in which four men are charged with committing an armed robbery at the residence of in Chinese merchant, his wife and daughter, from whom they are alleged to have stolen valuables and money to a total of $1,500. The case will be continued this afternoon, when it is expected it will be concluded.

In the Cooperative Hall, Methil, Fifeshire, five miners who were sent to prison under the Emergency Regulations during the general strike for holding up traffic and throwing stones at the police, were acclaimed as heroes and presented with medals provided through the International Class-War Prisoner's Aid. There was a large gathering, and the speeches were interspersed with the singing of the Red Flag.

Dairen, August 16.—Through the Japanese Consul-General at Mukden, the Kwangtung Government tendered a formal protest to-day to the Mukden authorities against "the drastic measures taken by them to check the steady decline of the Mukden notes. The protest, pointing out the arrest of the clerks of the Japanese exchange brokers and the temporary closing of the Japanese exchanges, demanded that the authorities take more moderate steps in future.

Sentence of six months rigorous imprisonment was on August 19 passed on E. D. Sykes who was convicted by the Singapore District Judge, Mr. P. F. David on three charges of cheating by tendering cheques in payment of debts when he had no funds in the bank to meet the cheques. Sykes had a sum of $1,700 in the bank, it was said, and during history in Singapore, doing business as an import and export broker, he had distributed cheques amounting to over $18,000.

## [15] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS.

Peking, August 16.—Mr. F. Hussey-Freke, formerly secretary of the Hailo Conservancy Commission, Tientsin, signed a contract yesterday evening appointing him Associate Chief Inspector of the Salt Gabelle. He assumed office this morning.

Nauen, August 4.—A Carnegie professorial chair will be inaugurated at Berlin's University of Politics, “Dentsche Honnschule fuer Politik” following the recent visit of President of the Carnegie Fund, Nicholas Murray Butler. Each year an internationally known authority on ethnology will lecture at Berlin, as was done before the war.

Cairo. — Considerable feeling has been created in Egyptian circles by personal attacks on ping Fuad in connection with the exchange of a Crown building (for the purposes of the Egyptian University) for State lands alleged to be worth six times the value of the building. The question was raised in the Chamber and this has been followed by a violent Press war around the person of the King.

By 187 votes to 173, the Wesleyan Methodist Conference at York recently adopted a recommendation that women should be made eligible for the ministry of the Wesleyan Church as far as possible on the same terms as men. The recommendation contained the qualification that the marriage of a woman minister should be regarded as equivalent to resignation or withdrawal from active ministry.

Excavators working on the Roman amphitheatre at Caerleon—the site of which was purchased for the nation by "The Daily Mail"—found an inscribed stone on the original road surface in the middle of the recently opened entrance to the arena. The stone is inscribed with a man's name, reading "U. Hentulium." In the opinion of those in charge of the excavation, the stone was probably carved not as part of an official work but just as a signature. Another interesting find was a Bin-long bronze pin, with a decorated end.

London, August 12.—There has been an unusual sequel to the sale of “Pilgrim's Progress,” mentioned in the message of July 26, the buyer having returned it to the auctioneers on the ground that it was not a first edition, but a second issue of the first edition. The existence of this issue, which is distinguished by a five-line errata at the end, was hitherto not generally known to bibliographers. The book is still worth a thousand sterling, but is scarcely as valuable as the original first edition.

A reference to a mounted white tiger being on "show in India," called forth a letter to the "Statesman," Calcutta, from a correspondent who says that recently two such white tigers have been shot in the Pasan Zemindary, Central Provinces, Bilaspore District. The skin is practically white chocolate (not black) stripes very prettily marked, a most handsome specimen, all else is normal including the eyes. Information frequently comes in of other tigers of the same colour having been seen, in the same forests. The correspondent raises the question of whether this is a distinct species.
