## [1] OLIVE BRANCH

**APPEAL TO PRESIDENT CALLES**

**TO END CONTROVERSY.**

Mexico City, August 19.
The Archbishop of Mexico, on behalf of the entire Catholic denomination, has memorallised President Calles asking for freedom of conscience, instruction, and Press, declaring that the Episcopate is yet at the same time patriotism and desire for peace and requesting President Calles to use his influence to persuade Congress to change the religious clauses of the Constitution and the penalties for disobedience of the new regulations.

Simultaneously a group of twenty Mexico City businessmen sent a letter to President Calles and the Episcopate proposing that they should confer to settle the controversy.

## [12] ANTI-TOBACCO.

**MOVE TO PREVENT WOMEN SMOKING.**

W. M. Mahoney, secretary of the anti-cigarette league, announced to-day that a convention of the organisation had been called for August 25, at Winona Lake, Indiana. At the meeting, a national campaign will be launched to seek legislation to prohibit women smoking and will attempt to prohibit all use of tobacco in any form. This is the first concerted anti-tobacco drive since the war, when the consumption of cigarettes increased fivefold owing to the demand from troops overseas and in training camps. The American consumption of cigarettes last year was about 75 billion. Fifteen years ago it was but seven billion.—“United Press.”

## [16] BACON'S BILL.

**NEW YORK "EVENING POST"**

**A NEW FRIEND**

Colonel Thompson's attitude toward the Bacon Bill, giving the Moros a separate government under American protection, will decide the fate of that measure, in the opinion of the New York "Evening Post", which publishes a long editorial to-day favouring the bill. It expresses considerable doubts to the value of the Thompson mission but is willing to wait until it is known whether the Colonel will favour or oppose the Bacon Bill. If he favours it, says the editorial, the mission will have been worth while; if he opposes or ignores it, he will have missed an opportunity. The writer says that with Colonel Thompson's recommendation, President Coolidge could come out openly in favour of the bill, which would fail without presidential support.

It says that the purposes and results of the mission are still uncertain and it is well to await the complete report before indulging in criticism. However, it continues, it would have been better if President Coolidge had sent ex-Governor Cameron Forbes of Newton Gilbert, who are more familiar with the Philippines and the Filipinos than Colonel Thompson.

"Commenting directly on the Bacon Bill it asks, 'why shouldn't the United States take advantage of this opportunity to break the British and Dutch rubber monopoly?'". It says that the bill although ostensibly for the purpose of giving the Moros freedom has for its chief interest, the production of rubber "but there is room for both Moros and rubber."—"United Press."

**MAY DISPLACE TASTY STRINGS IN ITALY.**

*Rome, August 11.*

Perhaps Spaghetti and macaroni will face serious competition by the potato. If the plans of the Fascist Government work out successfully.
