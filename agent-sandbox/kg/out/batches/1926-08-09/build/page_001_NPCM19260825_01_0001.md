## [6] GAS STOLEN?

**SENIOUS CHARGES IN KOWEOON.**

**CHINESE IN COURT.**

The never computations between the figures recorded by the meter of the Hongkong Gas Company at Kowloon, and the total consumption of the company's patrons there, led the company's officials to make investigations as to what caused the irregularity.
As a sequel to these investigations, the master of a cook-house at 90%, Canton Road, appeared this morning before Mr. J. H. B. Nihill at the Kowloon Magistracy charged with herceny of gas.
Mr. H. C. Mucnamare, prosecuting represented the Gas Company, and Mr. J. London Easick appeared for the Defence.

Sergeant Smith of the Faumati Police Station said that Mr. Blackburn, Manager of the Gas Company, went yesterday to the Faumati Station and reported that a house at Canton Road was suspected of "consuming" gas without a "meter. A search warrant was granted, and witness, together with the manager, went to the above-mentioned house, and found seven burners, burning supplied by a pipe coming from underneath the ground and without any meter. Although the search warrant was not in the defendant's name, he was arrested, as he was alleged to be the master of the cook-house.

To determine what bad should be fixed for the temporary release of the defendant, and to determine the seriousness of the case, the prosecution called Mr. Blackburn, who said that each burner could consume 40 cubic ft. of gas per hour, and that the seven burners if used 12 hours daily would consume gas worth about $250 or $300 per month.

Counsel for the defence asked the Court to fix a bail of about $300 for the temporary release of the defendant. He argued that as the search warrant was not in the defendant's name, there was a doubt as to whether the defendant was the person that ought to be charged. Accused denied being the master of the cook-house.

His Worship fixed a bail at $500; $250 to be given in cash; and the balance in bond.

The case was remanded until Saturday.

**CHINESE REPORT.**

## [11] “FILAME” ROBBERS.

**CAUGHT DURING A FIRE IN CANTON**

**THREE HOUSES BURNED.**

Taking advantage of the temporary confusion during a fire in Canton city on Monday evening, a robber gang made a haul, only to be rounded up by police cordons.

The blaze was in the eastern part of Wai Oi Road. On the first alarm, shopkeepers opened their doors in readiness to remove their stocks should the conflagra-

tion spread. When the robbers got on the move, the gates were shut again, irrespective of being isolated by the fire.

Three houses were involved before the fire brigade put the blaze out.

In attempting to pass out through police cordons, the robbers are stated to have been captured.

On the news of arrest getting through, the people at once reopened their doors.

## [15] KOWLOON FACILITIES.

### Opening Up New Beaches On Peninsular.

The question of bathing beach facilities in Hongkong and Kowloon is dealt with in the report of the Bathing Benches' Committee, which is to be laid before Council to-morrow.

This Committee was appointed to report upon existing facilities, sanitation and scavenging at the bathing beaches generally and in particular at Repulse Bay and at Castle Peak; upon the feasibility of inaugurating additional public

batking beaches; and to make recommendations as to the lines on which the future policy of the Government with respect to bathing beaches should be framed.

## [16] Kowloon.

<sub>section under [15] KOWLOON FACILITIES.</sub>

Regarding Kowloon, the Committee is of opinion that it is very desirable that similar bathing facilities should be provided there as are provided Hongkong.

Hung Hom is considered unsuitable as deep water comes up to the sea wall and bathing here would be dangerous for non-swimers, and also the sea water here is apt to be fouled by oil.

Tai Wan beach, the report states, is undoubtedly capable of development for bathing purposes, and we gather that the Kowloon Residents' Association is formulating a scheme for presentation to Government on the subject.

## [17] Matshede:

<sub>section under [15] KOWLOON FACILITIES.</sub>

Regarding the use of matsheds as a principle, the Committee considers that there is no call whatever to abolish bathing matsheds or to forbid their erection in future. It is of opinion that they could not be replaced adequately by any form of bathing boxes, tents or machines. In view of the fact that Repulse Bay area is congested, however, the Committee recommends that no further licences be issued there.

## [27] MORRISON. HILL.

**ALI. TO COME DOWN?**

**TOO MUCH EARTH**

The effect of heavy floods on reclamation, work would, on the face of it, be one of "assistance, in that the water carries down earth and other refuse, which would otherwise have to be placed on the reclamation from elsewhere.

In the case of the Prayn East scheme, however, the amount of silt and refuse which has found its way to the reclamation from places other than Morrison Hill has somewhat disturbed the original plans which provided for the removal of the Hill to the sea and calculated that the one would balance the other.

In other words, it was anticipated that it would be possible for all the earth from Morrison Hill to be removed to the reclamation and that when the Hill was levelled the reclamation would be complete.

But the reclamation has proceeded apace—some 20,000 "cubic yards being dumped a month there—and there is a greater proportion of the hill standing than the original calculations provided for, owing largely to other factors which have led to the deposit of considerable quantities of dirt.
It would appear probable that a section of the hill will still be standing when the reclamation is complete and that in view of the high cost of carting earth away by junk, which would be the only other alternative, no attempt will be made to complete the demolition.

Extra Police Resented.
London, August 24.

Hundreds of policemen are patrolling the road leading to the Mansfield Colliery, but there have been few incidents, although the miners are most resentful at the presence of extra Police.
The pits at Mansfield, in the afternoon, were deserted by almost all the men who worked during the week-end. Only 257 men were working at the Bolsover Colliery according to the coal owners and only 40 according to the men's leaders.

The miners' officials in Midlothian deny that there has been any breakaway there.—Reuter.

## [30] Pickets Active.

<sub>section under [28] COAL TROUBLES.</sub>

Whilst, generally speaking the number of miners working continues to increase, there has been a decrease in the Mansfield district of the Notts coalfield where picketing and intimidation is reported have been very active and large numbers of police are being drafted to protect those men who are willing work.

Acts of open violence are few, and so far the Police have detained only one man.

Mr. Cook is said to have a great deal of solid support in this district, where thousands of miners are attending meetings addressed by the leaders.
