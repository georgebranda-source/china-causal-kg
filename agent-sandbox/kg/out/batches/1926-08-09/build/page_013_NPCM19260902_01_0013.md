## [17] Permanent Senta.

### Geneva. Sept. 1.

The Committee on the composition of the League Council commenced the discussion on the question of permanent seats.

Senor Palacios (Spain) upheld Spain's claim for a permanent seat.

M. Fromagcot, (France) hoped that Spain would reconsider her decision.

Herr von Hoesch (Germany) expressed the sincere desire of Germany for continued co-operation with Spain in the League. He was of opinion that the matter should be left to the decision of the assembly.

M. Brouckere (Belgium) strongly opposed an increase in the number of permanent seats. He ardently hoped for the continuation of Spain in the work of the League, and regarded that this would be attained by the scheme now adopted.

## [26] SPAIN STANDS OUT.

### Others Approve of Draft Decisions.

The Special Commission of the League of Nations appointed to consider the composition of the League Council to-day completed its examination of the question of distribution of non-permanent seats on the Council.

The final draft decisions, which were approved by the Commission—the Spanish representative alone abstaining—incorporate in "the "Cecil" plan the modifications which have been closely discussed by the Sub-Committee for the past two days. In the form in which they will be presented to the League Council for approval the main features of Lord Cecil's plan are retained.

The Council will be increased in size by the addition of three non-permanent members. All the non-permanent members will normally be elected for three years, and one-third will be elected annually. The scheme provides that the Assembly may endow three of the occupants of these seats with semi-permanence by declaring them re-eligible for election for a further period of three years, after having already served for that length of time.

## [27] Method of Election

<sub>section under [26] SPAIN STANDS OUT.</sub>

The discussion has for the most part ranged around the immediate method of election that should be adopted. The proposals now drafted, which it is taken for granted will be approved by the Council, provide that the Assembly shall decide which of the nine members elected for this year shall sit for one, which for two, and which for three years. The Assembly may also decide at the 1926 election, by special two-thirds majorities vote in each case that not more than three of the members elected shall be re-eligible. The designation of these three members, however, would not deprive the Assembly of its right, which is asserted in an earlier article of the draft, to designate as re-eligible any member or members retiring from a non-permanent seat next year or in 1928, but it would be understood that only in exceptional circumstances would this right be exercised.

## [28] Good Compromise

<sub>section under [26] SPAIN STANDS OUT.</sub>

The proposals are regarded here as a good compromise between the conflicting views and at the same time as calculated to meet the aspiration of those members who have claimed permanent seats.
The special Commission is engaged in considering what changes, if any, should be made in regard to permanent seats on the Council—British Wireless Service.

## [29] TRAIN DERAILED.

**RESULT OF A BIG LANDSLIP.**

**FATAL FLOODS.**

Seventeen persons were killed and 36 wounded as the result of the derailment of a mail train running from Barcelona to Valencia, near Tortosa, in consequence of a landslip following heavy rain. A third class carriage was telescoped. Several villages in the environs of Barcelona are infundated by rains. Thirty-six persons have perished in the floods, and great material damage has been caused.
—Reuter.

## [31] OPTIMISTIC

**REPORTS OF CHINESE FIGHTING.**

Optimistic communiques from Hankow continue to arrive, claiming the recovery of Puchi and the advance of the Northerners towards the Hupeh-Hunan border.

Independent reports do not confirm the Northern successes, but none of the reports mentions any further advance of the Southerners. There seems no doubt that heavy fighting is proceeding and that Wu Pei-fu is receiving large reinforcements from the North.—Reuter.

*Shanghai, September 1.*

According to Japanese reports from Hankow Wu Pei-fu's forces are demoralised and have suffered several reverses. The Southerners are advancing rapidly against Wuchang, which is across the river from Hankow. The reports add that Wu Pei-fu's forces are preparing to check the "Red" advance at Chihfangshih, twelve miles from Wuchang, and are also constructing trenches at the suburbs of Wuchang.

The Northern reinforcements continue to arrive in large numbers at Hankow Refugees from Wuchang and the surrounding districts are pouring in to the Hankow Concessions.

Sun Chuan-fang, so far, has sent merely a handful of troops to assist Wu Pei-fu, and it is believed that Sun intends to maintain armei neutrality unless his five provinces are invaded; but he is enrolling recruits and taking all precautions. —Reuter.

## [32] FOREMAN SLAIN.

**MORE LABOUR TROUBLE IN SHANGHAI.**

*Shanghai, September*

This morning a Chinese foreman, employed at the Ewo Cotton Mills, was waylaid at a wayside road in the eastern district of the city by a gang of armed men, who mortally injured him with six bullets. The foreman, before his death, was unable to make a statement identifying the assailants, who, so far, have not been arrested.
Meantime, a meeting of the Strike Pickets' Committee has authorised the pickets to resort to the use of axes, iron bars and arms in the case of a clash with mill operatives desiring to go to work or with the police, promising compensation in the event of injury or of the death of picketers.
—Reuter.

## [33] 400 HOUSES RAZED IN AZORES.

The Governor of the Island of Horta, in the Azores, now cables that eight persons were killed and 200 injured in the Fayal earthquake. Four hundred houses were razed at Flamangas, in a continuous series of short shocks.

The Government has ordered the immediate departure of two cruisers and a transport vessel with medical units and a detachment of engineers.—Reuter.

The Governor of Horta cabled that there were fifty casualties (killed and injured combined) as a result of the earthquake.—Rutur.
