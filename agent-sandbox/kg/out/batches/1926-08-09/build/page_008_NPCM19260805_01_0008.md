## [8] LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS.

The "United States CRIOT of Engineers has directed the District Engineer in Buffalo to change the official name of the harbour, which is Rochester (N.Y.), from Charlotte Harbour to Rochester Harbour. This change is being made at the request of the Common Council of Rochester and with the concurrence of the United States Geographic Board.

According to a Mukden wireless message, the wireless telephone between Mukden and Harbin has been opened to traffic and that it works smoothly. Marshal Chang Tso-lin, who returned from Dairen to Mukden recently spoke personally to General Chang Huan-hsiang at Harbin on the wireless telephone and he has ordered its gradual extension to other important cities in Manchurip.

By way of propagating the jury system about to be carried into effect in Japan, the Kobe Propaganda Association, Kobe, assisted by many Kobe lawyers and leading people, held a sham trial dealing with a murder case at the Shurakkan. Such a sham trial being the first of its kind and there being no charge for admittance, there was a tremendous rush for seats and the occasion proved a great success.

The Finance Ministry has decided that hereafter the provincial authorities or various administrative areas...when ordering revenue stamps from the Central Government, must accompany their orders with cash in advance to pay for printing them, otherwise no stamps will be sent them, it is stated. This is the reason no stamps were recently sent when the Jehol authorities ordered some.

Peking: July 24. — Report from Kansu state that of late the American missionaries of the Christian and Missionary Alliance at Alashan have been ill-treated by the Mongol tribes there, who have refused to sell to them food and other daily necessities, trying to starve them, especially at Ting-yuanying. At the request of the American Minister, the Government has sent a telegram to the Kansu Authorities instructing them to take immediate measures to stop the anti-foreign movement among the officials of the Alashan Mongol tribes.

The theft of a sum of money from the cabin of the chief officer of the s.a. "Tjikini" on Monday has been reported to the police.

Twenty-nine out of a possible 160 miscellaneous cars have been licensed as public vehicles during the last two months. A large number still have to be re-licensed, having been sent back for various defects to be repaired, etc.

On Friday, August 6, the general committee of the Hongkong Chinese General Chamber of Commerce will hold a meeting at 2.30 p.m., in the Chamber's premises at No. 65, Connaught Road Central.

As from August 1 all 'buses' in Kowloon have to be examined by the Traffic Department of the Police once a month, in order to ensure that their steering gear and engines are all that they should be and that the 'bus' is generally mechanically sound.

Up to the present about 1,700 worries and motor-cars, public and private, have been weighed at the new weigh-bridge at the Centrall Police Station compound since the new system was brought into force. Cars of standard weight it will be remembered, are not bound to be weighed.

A Chinese was recently charged with causing grievous injuries to three persons by pouring boiling starch over them while they were asleep. One of the injured men having died: the alleged, assailant will be again brought before the Magistrate this week on a charge of murder.

The Hongkong Postmaster General notifies that the mail service to Swatow, Samshui, Wuchow and Kongmoon is entirely suspended until further notice. This is due to the Canton postal strike, which has spread over the province of Kwangtung and to Kwangsi, Wuchow being in that province.

The Government has received a communication from the British Legation claiming indemnity for the death of Mrs. Silbey, British subject, who was murdered by soldiers at Chengtu. The Chinese Government is asked to give a compensation equivalent to 15 years' salary of the deceased, besides the payment of $5,000 for funeral expenditure.

## [11] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL

Mr. Ben H. Stearns, of the Canadian Pacific passenger office, left Shanghai on the "Empress of Russia" on July 26 to spend his home leave in Canada. Mr. Stearns will return to Shanghai at the end of the year.

London, July 15.—Mr. Bernard Shaw will assist in teaching broadcast announcers how to pronounce difficult words. Mr. Shaw is a member of the B. B. C.'s Advisory Committee for this purposes, whose Chairman is Dr. Robert Bridges.—Times of Ceylon."

London, July 14.—Sir Squire Bancroft left £174,535. He bequeathed portraits of himself and his wife to the National Portrait Gallery. He regretted that Millais had refused to accept the large sum he offered him to paint the portrait of his wife at the height of her fame. He bequeathed £7,000 to various theatrical charities and an annuity of £200 to his wife's former maid.

London, July 14.—The “Westminster Gazette,” in a leader headed “A Great Englishwoman,” says that none of the most daring writers of fiction have created a character so remarkable as Gertrude Bell, who is mourned even more perhaps by the Arabs than by her own countrymen, for the Arabs knew her best and realised her greatness. She could do things which Burton would have found almost impossible, and even in General Lawrence would have been venturesome.

The marriage was solemnised on July 21, of Miss Holland, who recently returned to Japan after a lengthy stay in America, and Mr. J. T. Royo, Consul for Panama. The civil service was performed at the Kobe City Office in the morning in the presence of Mr. E. R. Dickover, American Consul, and the religious ceremony in the afternoon at All Saints Church. The bride and bridegroom will make their home in Kobe until November, when Mr. Royo expects to be transferred from Kobe.

## [12] *(no headline: column 4)*

Japanese Rourdes state that Sir Francis Aglen has agreed to increase the Japanese membership of the Maritime Customs Service. Of the present 1,262 foreigners in the Customs Service, 212 only are Japanese.

Farmington, Conn., July 26.
—The prisoners in the city jail, excepting those charged with murder and other grave offences, were released to day on their own recognisances owing to the intense heat.

San Antonio, Texas, July 28.—Representative W. A. Williamson stated to-day that he would move to have the office of governor declared vacant when the special session of the legislature convenes September 13, unless Governor Ferguson resigns before that time, in accordance with her agreement with Daniel Moody, her successful rival for the nomination for governor.

New York, July 1.—The Carnegie Endowment for International Peace has arranged to have a party of fifty professors of subjects relating to international affairs to visit Paris, The Hague and Geneva this summer as guests of the Endowment for the purpose of studying International Organisation. The party will sail from New York on July 28, returning on September 20.

Berlin, July 1.—The world's first "flying sleeping cars" are being built at the works of the Deutsche Lufthansa, a big aircraft manufacturing concern. By a patented device, the ordinary seats of passenger aeroplanes are converted into comfortable berths. The sleeping cars will be used on long-distance air routes, a porter converting them from day coaches to sleeping cars at night.

The gravity with which the Shanghai and provincial Chinese authorities regard the labour question in and around Shanghai is shown by a special summons made to Gen. Yen Chun-yang to visit Marshal Sun Chuan-fang in Nanking and there report to him on the labour situation generally. Gen. Yen left Shanghai for Nanking by a special train and it is expected that he will be away for some days. He expects to receive detailed instructions on how to deal with future industrial troubles in Shanghai.

## [13] NEWS.

Kobe, July 27.—Mr. Hioki, the chief Japanese delegate to the Tariff Conference, arrived here this morning from Peking and immediately left for Tokyo by train.

New York, June 26.—Permission has now been given for Charlie Chaplin's mother, who is of British birth, to remain in the United States in charge of her son, and her case will remain in suspense for an indefinite period.

Tientsin, July 25.—Mr. Yoshizawa, the Japanese Minister to Peking, left Tientsin to day by the str. “Chosei Maru” for Dairen, en route to his home in Japan where he will spend the summer vacation.

London, July 16.—Mr. G. G. Sim, until recently Financial Commissioner of the Indian Railway, has been appointed Secretary to Vickers, Limited. Mr. Ascoli has retired from the Indian Civil Service and has joined Dunlop's.

The Pope has appointed Canon Francis J. Vaughan, of Barry Dock, Bishop of Menevia, in succession to the Archbishop of Cardiff, Dr. Mostyn, who, since his elevation, has continued to supervise the diocese of Menevia. The archdiocese of Cardiff includes the counties of Glamorgan, Monmouth, and Hereford, and the rest of Wales is comprised in the diocese of Menevia.

San Francisco, July 28.—Crown Prince Gustav Adolf and Crown Princess Louis of Sweden, at present guests of the city of San Francisco, have decided to sail on August 3, aboard the "President Pierce" for Japan. They will make a brief stop at Honolulu continuing aboard the same ship to Yokohama. They plan to remain about ten days in Japan and then make an extensive tour of the Orient. Their exact itinerary will be announced from time to time as their tour progresses. They speak with enthusiasm of their journey across the United States, particularly of the time they spent in the region west of the Great Lakes which is populated largely by Swedish immigrants and their children.
