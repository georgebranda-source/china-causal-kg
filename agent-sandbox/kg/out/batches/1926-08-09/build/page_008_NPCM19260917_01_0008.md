One of the two Indian guards who was seriously wounded in the Salkung ferry "pirney alfair has" since died in hospital. A charge of murder will "now" be preferred against the five men now in custody and alleged to be the pirate gung.

Home mails and parcels arrived by the F. and O. "Morey" yesterday. The total mail amounted to 299 bags and there were 133 bags of London parcels of August 12. The "President Taft" brought 488 bags of letters and "papers" from the U.S.A., Honolulu, Japan and Shanghai.

A Chinese was charged at the Central Ministry yesterday with intimidating another man to join the Wai San Society. Det. Sergeant Carey stated that although they were not able to prove it, the police knew the Society to be an unlawful and dangerous one. Defentant was remanded for one week.

Rign. August 29.—According to the Soviet Press the measure hitherto adopted by the Soviet to meet the loss of output in factories owing to the enormous number of malingerers among the workers have entirely failed. Physical assaults on doctors for refusing to grant them opportunities for carousing instead of working are rapidly increasing. Many doctors have been killed or injured in carrying out their duties. The Council of Soviet Trade Unions is appealing to all members energetically to fight such hooigans.

Peking, September 3.—An in-starice of Chinese 'filial piety' is reported from Mengtuan Hutung, where Miss Huan Yi, the only daughter of Mr. Huang Fu-chow, aged 19, believes she has cured her sick mother with her own "flesh and blood. The mother has been in the bed for a long period. Re-calling acts of many dutiful children in past history, the girl took a knife and cut a piece of flesh from her left arm and mixed it as a medicinal dose for her mother to take." Strange to say, the woman soon felt comfortable and now is in good health. The girl, it is said, is now in the P.U.M.C. Hospital for medical treatment, and her recent action is highly respected in some circles.
—"N. C. Standard,"

Cologne.—Prince Joachim Albrecht of Prussia (a son of the ex-Kaiser), forsaking martial aspirations in order to woo the Muses, has attained some distinction as a musical composer. ## [8] *(no headline: column 3)*

The expenditure during 1925 on the Public Works Department exceeded that of any recent year and was, in fact, the highest ever recorded in the Colony, states the annual report of the Colonial Engineer. Taking into account variations in price it probably represents fully double the work done in 1921 and is 12 to 15 per cent in excess of that done in 1924.

Chinese deck passengers for the number of 69,610 have left Singapore for China this year to the end of August, compared with 46,893 for the same period last year. The number leaving last month was 9,662, against 6,336 in August, 1925. The total immigrants for the same periods were 227,445 and 16,257, compared with 140,889 and 6,333.

Peking, September 5. — The Chinese citizens residing in Formosa have addressed a petition to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs declaring their helpless situation in Formosa, on account of the lack of a Consul to look after their interests. For tens of years the Chinese people there have experienced all kinds of difficulties, and the Ministry is going to recommend the petition to the Cabinet for favourable consideration.

Salt Lake City, Sept. 11.—Senator Reed Smoot was re-nominated to-day as Republican candidate for the senate, by the state convention which is meeting here. Senator Smoot is now serving his fourth term in the Senate, having been elected for the first time in 1902. He is chairman of the committee on finance. Senator Smoot recently announced his intention of retiring from politics, but was persuaded to accept the nomination for a fifth term. He is 64 years old.
Berlin, August 31.—The late Fritz Ebert, who starting life as a saddle maker, became the first president of the German Republic, as a socialist, and then was thrown out of the radical saddle-makers' union because of the charge that he had lapsed into conservatism, "has postnumously been readmitted to membership in the union." The unique resolution, striking from the records all mention of Ebert's expulsion, was passed at the recent saddle-makers' convention in Hamburg. It declared among other things that "in the course of time the union's members have come to regard even this post-revolutionary period in a different light."

E. de Paula was the matron of honour. Mr. and Mrs. de Paula were the host and hostess at the large gathering in the Hotel.

It is estimated that in Singapore there are 25,774 children of school age, states, the annual medical report for last year. To deal with this large number of children there are in the Settlement 28 Government English and Vernacular Schools: 13 Government Aided Schools and 37 Private Schools Examinations of school children during the year shewed 78.7 per cent. to have good general condition; 13.3 per cent. had a generally fair condition, and 11.1 per cent. had distinctly poor condition.

Nairobi, August 28.—During the last five years a local syndicate has been exploring concessions for oil and coal in the coastal area of Kenya. It has now succeeded in interesting financial groups in Johannesburg, who have provided considerable capital for further investigation and have sent up experts and drillers, who are already working. Should coal be found in Kenya in payable quantities, it might revolutionise the whole future of the country.

A denial was given at the office of the Shell Company by one of the directors to a statement that the distribution of Soviet Petrol in England has been largely limited to supplies bought by the Shell group from the Russian Oil Products Ltd. The Royal Dutch Shell group, it was stated, have not handled Russian oil for more than a year, as the Customs would show, nor would the group be likely to facilitate the distribution of oil derived from properties which have been confiscated by the Soviet Government without compensation being paid to the former proprietors.

We regret to record the death of Mr. D. G. Robertson, the founder of the firm bearing his name in Kuala Lumpur, which occurred in Canada quite suddenly last month, says the "Malay Mail." Mr. Robertson retired from Malaya over ten years ago. He came out to Malaya to join Messrs. Riley Hargreaves and Co., Ltd., which he left to become the first manager of the Federated Engineering Co., Ltd., formed by the amalgamation of the two firms of Riley Hargreaves, "and Howarth Erskine. He left the Federated Engineering Co. in 1904 and started the firm of D. G. Robertson and Co. He was also a director of William Mackinnon and Co., Ltd., of Aberdeen.

A hundred years of bell-ringing at Southwark Cathedral, formerly St. Saviour's, Southwark, has just been completed by the Mash family, of Newcomen Street, in the Borough. Thomas Mash, the present head of the family, who has been keeper of the cathedral tower and verger of the Chapter House for thirty-three years, has been ringing since he was a youth. His father, the late George Mash, who was in charge of the tower before him for thirty years, helped to ring many famous peals. Before that his grandfather, James Mash, who held the same office, helped to ring the muffled peal for the death of George IV. in 1880. Six of the twelve bells were purchased from Henry VIII. The largest weighs 2 tons 10 cwt, and the smallest 7 cwt.

## [17] MAKING NAVAL HISTORY.

Officers and men of the China Squadron have been making history on the Yangtse. All the despatches published daily, with copious foot-notes to, tell strangers what the names of persons, places, &c. stand for, are reproduced.

Our reports give prominence to the rescue, the names of those concerned (also the mercantile marine officers), the effect of the bombardment and reprisals, and the steps towards negotiation.

Side issues at Hankow where warships and steamers have been fired upon, and in some instances retaliated, combine to make an impressive pen picture.

"Actions" in which American destroyers took part are also described.
