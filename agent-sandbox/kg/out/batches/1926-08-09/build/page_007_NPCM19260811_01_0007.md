## [2] SINKING FUND PROBLEM.

*Paris, August 10*

For only the third time history and the first time in 42 years the National Assembly met to-day to dead with the Constitutional question at the Palace of Versailles, namely, to embody the Sinking Fund statutes in the Constitution and thus remove the kernel of last week's legislation from the destructive hand of the legislator.

The Palace and gardens are closed to sightseers during the sitting of the Assembly, which the Government hopes will conclude in a single day provided it is able to forestall the anticipated action of the Socialists to profit by the occasion to seek a revision of the Constitution and curtail the Senate's veto over Bills passed by the Chamber.

The proceedings opened by the overwhelming defeat of a Communist motion designed to prevent the Government opposing amendments to the agenda.—Reuter

## [4] MIXED COURT.

**BRITISH ATTITUDE HONOURABLE**

**NO BARTER FOR TRADE.**

Mr. Alexander Fishman, the well-known Russian barrister, in the course of an interview with a Russian journalist regarding the Mixed Court, declared that rendition would give Russians an entirely new and different status. In spite of the fact that the Russians had lost their extrality, the Russians in Shanghai were at present under the authority of the Mixed Court, but after rendition he expected that they would be under purely Chinese Courts subordinate to the Supreme Court in Peking.

Mr. Fishman declared that the movement of the foreign Bar had been entirely misconstrued. They had no selfish motives, but desired to see Courts established such as would bring justice and order.
He utterly disagreed with the accusations made by the American Attorney that Great Britain has been bartering the Mixed Court for her trade with China. The British attitude was perfectly honourable and straightforward.—Feuter

New York, August 10.
Although the leader in the “New York Times” is tactful and conciliatory, the general opinion of the newspapers is that the United States will ignore M. Clemenceau's letter and that the opinions is formally expressed by President Coolidge more or less represent the general feeling of the country.
Even the Democratic paper, the "New York World," takes this view, its special correspondent in Washington declaring that officials contemplate that no reply will be addressed to the letter, which they describe as a bit of French politics. Sooner or later, the officials hope, the French people will learn that there is no hope that the United States will revise the debt compact.
The "New York World" adds that the letter is only calculated to enhance the bitterness of the French against the United States, whilst it cannot affect the question to which it is directed.—Reuter.

## [6] SUNK SUBMARINE.

**AN ADDITIONAL LIFE LOST.**

London" Auons

The bodies of the victims of the H20 are still unrecovered. Nightlong pumping operations ceased this morning, owing to the presence of other vessels in the basin. Two feet of the conning tower are now visible, but the submarine is fastened to a wall.

The Admiralty announces that an additional life was lost on the H29, namely, that of the engine fitter, who was aboard when the submarine sunk. He must have been killed.—Reuter.

Rugby, August 10:
The cause of the submarine disaster is unknown, and the Admiralty announces that an official enquiry will be held as soon as the vessel has been raised.

Salvage parties were at work throughout the night by the light of flares and "searchlights, and divers made many attempts to enter her, but in vain.

After ten feet of water had been pumped out of the Dock basin, it was decided to attempt to raise the sunken submarine bodily, and the vessel is being slung on wire hawers. Officials hope to raise her by a secret method, which is to be tried for the first time.—British Wireless Service.

## [9] OUTLINE COUNTRY'S MISERIES.

*Fekling, July 28.*

The Chinese National United Chambers of Commerce have sent out the following telegram, printed in the Shih Chieh Yi Pao:

"To the Cabinet, Tupana, Civil Governors, legal bodies, and newspaper offices of the various provinces." Previously we sent a telegram to Marshals Chang Tso-lin and Wu Pei-fu, Generals Sun Chuan-fang, Yen Hai-shan, the commanders at the front, Generals, Chiang Kai-shih, Tang Sheng-chih, Yeh Kai-shin, Chao Heng-ti, and the leaders of the Kuominchun, describing the war-stricken-condition of the merchants and the people. We are the centre for all the chambers of commerce of China, and we feel that we represent public opinion when we appeal for peace.
"In the south, the troops at Wuchang and Hankow are going out to fight. Tang Sheng-chih, and in the north the troops of the Fengtlen, Chihli and Shantung parties at Hankow, Shahsi at Tatung, and Jehol at Dolonor all are preparing to attack. Neither victory or defeat is in sight, but the people can no longer bear the daily suspense.
"In Cheklang and Hunan," there are floods, the worst in a hundred years. In Chihli the war has left empty nine houses out of ten. Taxes have been collected for years in advance. Coal cannot be taken from the coal-fields, for the military has taken over the railroads for its own purposes. Because fuel and food is all in Kulgan and Sulyuan, the people of the capital are facing famine. A great deal depends on the merchants, but the merchants also are hard-pressed. The military authorities tax them without mercy, little realising that it is as though they had drained a pond of water to more easily catch all the fish. And what happens to the money which is lost by the people and the merchants? It goes to foreign countries, in return for

"Military affairs are without doubt important. And it is true that they are not the affairs of the merchant class. But it is for the sake of the people as well as for ourselves that we speak, asking that we be allowed to suffer no more. The people are weary of death. Let the fighting stop at once. Give the country a few years rest, and then settle the military disputes, in such a manner as then seems most advisable."

## [10] FAILURE IN STRIKE

**COUNCIL SUED BY MANUFACTURERS.**

London, July 22.
Stratford Magistrates heard today an unusual charge arising out of the General Strike.

A number of manufacturing firms, which are in the electricity supply area of the Walthamtow Council summoned, the Council, for failing and neglecting to supply complainants with current and energy, which were cut off from May 6 to May 12, contrary to the Electric Light Act.

The Council pleaded not guilty. Their Engineer gave evidence that all the Council employees had given an ultimatum to the Council that they would go on strike unless power supplies for commercial purposes were discontinued. He admitted that kinemas were supplied.
The Court fined the Council, 20 shillings in respect of each day the supply was cut off and 25 gunners costs.—“Times of Ceylon.”

## [13] MURDER CHARGE.

**COILING WATER THROWN ON**

**MAN'S STRANGE CONDUCT**

A Chinese unlicensed hawker was "charged at the" Central Magistracy yesterday with the murder of another man and causing grievous bodily injuries to three other persons by pouring boiling water over them at 13 Rutter Street on July 13.

Mr. T. M. Hazlerigg, Assistant Crown Solicitor, in opening the case for the prosecution, said that the defendant and the victim with their families lived together. After a few days of his marriage in October, the defendant acted in a strange manner and beat his wife until in May this year the woman left him.

On the night of July 13, a mother and her two sons were awakened by a pan of boiling water thrown over them by the defendant, who was seen to be standing near the bed with a pan in his hand.

One of the injured persons died two days later. Defendant was then charged with murder, and in reply he denied the charge, adding that his wife had been taken away.

Mr. Hazlerigg said, the evidence would be to the effect that neither the deceased nor his family had anything to do with the wife going away and that there had been no quarrel between them. He did not propose to comment on the man's mental state; as medical evidence would be called later.

The case was adjourned.

## [14] OLD THEFTS.

**DISHONEST HOUSE COOLIE JAILED.**

**EUROPEANS' LOSSES.**

With the arrest of a Chinese who had previously worked as a house coolie at European residences, three old, larcenies were proved against the man when he was brought before Mr. J. H. B. Nihill at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday and charged with theft.

The charges against the man and in connection with which evidence was given by the Europeans concerned were (1) stealing $350 belonging to Mr A. Thomson, of the Tai Tam Tuk Reservoir; (2) stealing a silver cigarette case, the property of Mr. G. R. Haywood a local solicitor, with the alternative charge of receiving the case knowing the same to have been stolen or unlawfully obtained and (3) stealing a gold wristlet watch from Mr. Haywood's mother-in-law, there also being an alternative charge of receiving.

Defendant denied that he had been in the service of Mr. Thomson. Regarding the second and third charges, he said he picked up the cigarette case in one of the lodgers' rooms, while the watch was given to him by the owner (then Mrs. Searle) as a "cum-shaw" when she left.

Defendant was convicted on all three charges. On the first count he was sentenced to six months' hard labour, four months on the second and four months on the third. The second term to run consecutively with the first and the third concurrently, making a sentence of ten months' hard labour in all.

## [16] INDIANS JAILED.

**FIGHT AT THE VOLUNTEER**

**HEAD GROOM ASSAULTED.**

### 一 一 0

Mr. R. Kotewall, of 18 Ice House Street, and Mr. R. Young Hee, of 54 Kennedy Road, were said to have made a timely appearance to stop a desperate fight between Indian grooms at the Hongkong Volunteer Defence Corps stables at Causeway Bay, four of whom were charged before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning with assaulting their head man.

All the defendants pleaded guilty to the charge. The fourth defendant made a statement in which he alleged that threats of dismissal and abusive language used by the complainant led to the fight. The reason for this, he explained, was that all four of them had refused to pay a sum of $8 which the complainant was in the habit of receiving as "squeeze" from each man monthly.

The complainant attributed the attack on him to the fact that he had dismissed the second man for allowing a horse to get cut by barbed wire through neglect of his order to keep together while out on exercise on Monday morning.

C. S. M. Charles said that there had been a long standing grievance between the four men and the head man and they had done everything to make things unpleasant in an endeavour to get rid of him. He had not heard of the alleged "squeeze" and he thought the real reason for the attack was the dismissal of the second man by the complainant, who was responsible for the running of the stables and engagement of grooms.

His Worship disbelieved the story for the defence and sentenced each of the defendants to seven days' imprisonment. They were further ordered to sign bonds on $100 personal surety to keep the peace for twelve months.

## [17] RADE MARK CASE.

**CHINESE SUMMONED FOR INFRINGEMENT.**

**"AJINOMOTO" POWDER.**

Three Chinese firms were summoned before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning for an infringement of a trade mark by having in their possession for trade purposes a flavouring powder ("Ajinomoto") other than the genuine article.

Mr. L. R. Andrewes, representing the complainant, Hachiji Imura, of Praya East, said that the trade mark was registered in 1922. He submitted that the infringements extended to the outside wrapper on the tins, a label on the lid, and a chop. Further that the original tins had been used for refilling with a faked powder.

Mr. W. B. Hind appeared for the defence of one of the firms and Mr. D. McCallum for the other two. Possession was admitted by Mr. Hind, who said that his clients acted innocently in offering for sale the goods which had been bought from the Po. Wo compradore of Hing Loong Street. Mr. McCallum said he was in the same position.

Evidence of the purchase was given by the manager of the firm represented by Mr. Hind. Under cross-examination, witness admitted that the complaint paid several visits to his shop before he came on the last occasion with Sergeant Brittain and seized his stock consisting of three large and two small tins.

Mr. Andrewes proposed to call the evidence of Sergeant Brittain as to seizure of the goods.

Further hearing of the sum-mons was adjourned until Mon-day afternoon.

### [15] PEAK HOSPITAL: ESTIMATED LOSS TO GOVERNMENT.

**$13,640 FOR YEAR.**

When the Peak Hospital was taken over financially by the Government at the beginning of the year it was thought possible to make payment of salaries and other expenditure out of receipts. It has, however, been decided that gross receipts must be paid into revenue and expenditure must be charged against the votes for such expenditure passed by the Finance Committee of the Legislative Council.

At their meeting to-morrow the Finance Committee will be asked for a vote of $9,000. The estimated expenditure of the Peak Hospital for 1926 was $34,782, and it was anticipated that the revenue would amount to $25,000, this showing a deficit of $9,782. The result of the first six months working shows that there will probably be saving of $1,000 on this estimated expenditure. At the same time, however, he receipts have not reached expectations and these are now put down at $20,000. The revised estimate of expenditure for the year is $88,640 and with the revenue $20,000 the anticipated deficit at the end of the year is $18,640.
