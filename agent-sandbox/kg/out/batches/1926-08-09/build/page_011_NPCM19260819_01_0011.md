## [7] AT THE QUEEN'S.

Rafael Sabatini's story of "Scaramouche" will be screened at the Queen's Theatre from to-day till Saturday. Alice Terry, Ramon Novarro, and Lewis Stone are the stars and it is a Metro picture produced by Rex Ingram.

The action of "Scaramouche" takes place in France in 1789. 1793, beginning at the time the revolutionary spirit was first born in the people, and ending in the revolution itself and the attainment of their liberty after years of oppression. Historians agree that this is one of the most interesting and colourful periods in history. The picture proves the truth of such an assertion. It owes much to the fact that Rafael Sabatini, the author of the story, is an authority on history as well as the foremost writer of romantic-adventurous novels, and that Rex Ingram, the director, has gone to considerable trouble to ensure the historical accuracy of every detail of the production.
The fact that "Scaramouche" treats so illuminatingly and authentically one of the most vital events in the world's

## [10] KULANGSU:

**LITTLE ISLAND NOW WITHOUT GOVERNMENT.**

Amoy, Fuklen, August 2.—Mr. J. A. Bromfield, Manager of Tait and Company, and Chairman of the Kulangsu Municipal Council has resigned. His resignation has been immediately followed by like action on the part of all the other members of the Council. So that to-day Kulangsu is without a Council. The Consular Body is circulating an appeal to all ratcpuyers to come forward and serve on the Council. The response to this appeal is not so far encouraging.

Mr. Bromfield resigned because of threatened boycott of his business, owing to the fact that he was Chairman of the Council and had been rather active in promoting an electric light scheme which was to be accepted by the ratepayers. This was "an international syndicate" under the name of The Kulangsu Public Utility Company. A recent meeting of the ratepayers resulted in no tender getting the required two thirds vote, yet Mr. Bromfield's connection and activity was evidently not pleasing to the Chinese who as yet have no voice in the conduct of the Kulangsu government.

There is a good bit of history back of this. This island of Kulangsu became an International Settlement back in 1902. The Land Regulations by which it is governed are built on a village plan. At that time there were four or five thousand people on the Island. Now there are about 40,000. A government suited to a small town is naturally not fitted for a city.

Fourteen years ago the first definite move was made by the Council and the Consular Body to have a revision of these Land Regulations. The War came on and it was left-in abeyance. In 1918 and 1919 another definite and determined effort was made to have these revised. Up to this time the reports coming from Peking have been very brief and discouraging. They have run about like this, "will report later." "This is not a propitious time for consideration of these Land Regulations," "Will consult with colleagues and report as soon as possible," etc., etc.

And all this time this small Settlement has been left in the stew, with responsibility for its Government yet with an Instrument of government wholly inadequate to the situation.

The Land Regulations make no provision for a crisis. The Consular Body has no authority to take control. The Diplomatic Body in Peking have other matters to occupy their time and attention. What shall be done? No foreign man on the Island is willing to serve on the Council. Some time ago there was a motion before the ratepayers to turn the Island back to China. This motion was laid on the table, and is still there. The Advisory Committee was instituted and has served well but at the time it was first brought into existence. It was regarded as a temporary measure, and maybe the time of makeshift is about gone.

## [12] TOO MANY PIGEONS.

The pigeons which flock round the open spaces of London and form one of the sights of the Metropolis will be decreased in number if the L.C.C. is granted the powers it seeks to reduce them.

The chief grievance against the birds is that they cause stoppage of rain pipes and cutters.
