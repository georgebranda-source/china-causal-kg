The centre of gravity in the coal dispute lay to-day in the conference of the miners' delegates. Their deliberations were conducted behind closed doors.

With reference to the Regulation published in the “Gazette” of the 10th September, concerning the issue of receiving station licences under the Wireless Telegraphy Ordinance, 1926, it has been decided that the present arrangements will be allowed to continue until the 81st of December, 1926, and that receiving station licences will not be insisted upon until 1st January, 1927.

According to official statements the conference unanimously endorsed the actions of the Miners' Executive in their latest negotiations with the Government, and proceeded to discuss the Government's proposals.

Application for the grant of receiving station licences may be made to the Postmaster General on or after 1st November, 1926, and any licences issued between 1st November and 81st December, 1926, will take effect as from 1st January, 1927.

This discussion occupied the whole afternoon, and the conference adjourned until to-morrow, when the Government's proposals will be further considered.

From interviews with various delegates the impression was obtained that, "whilst there was a section in favour of a fight to finish, the majority of the delegates were anxious for some kind of way out. This feeling was evidently not lessened by the reports of an increasing resumption of work in the coal fields.

## [15] 125 MILES AN HOUR

**GREAT HURRICANE IN VERA**

*New York, Sept. 29.*

The town of Vern Cruz is reported as severely damaged, but owing to a break-down of communications it is imposed to determine the extent of the losses. — Reuter's American Service.

Lasted Several Hours.
Vera Cruz, Sept. '29:
The Hurricane lasted several hours, its velocity being 125 miles an hour.

The damage is estimated a $2,500,000 but no fatalities have so far been reported.

The crops in the vicinity of Vera Cruz were ruined and damage was also done at Boca del Rio, Cordoba, and Pasco del Macho.

Two persons were drowned in the floods at Jalapa.—Reuter.

The destruction of communications is responsible for very meagre details up to the present, but reports from Vera Cruz describe the hurricane of yesterday as "worst since the famous storm of 1888.

Torrential rain fell for 24 hours and overflowed the Santiago River flooding the surrounding country whilst driven by the terrific gale enormous waves swept toward the shore and smashed the sea front.

Most of the city streets are three feet in water, flooding the lower parts of the buildings in the business quarter, the windows of which were shattered by the wind.

Lightly constructed houses in the residential sections suffered severely.—Reuter.

## [18] MINERS TRAPPED.

**OVER 120 HOURS IN DIRE PERIL.**

**SPLENDID RESCUE WORK.**

Ironwood (Michigan), Sept. 19.
"We are alive" were the welcome words heard by the reacting which, after a day's strenuous tunneling, succeeded in reaching the 20th level of the Pabst Iron Mine, where 48 miners have been entombed for over 120 hours as the result of "a cave in," which killed three of their comrades.

Doctors and ambulances have been rushed to the pit head ready to attend the men when they are brought to the surface—Reuter's American Service.
