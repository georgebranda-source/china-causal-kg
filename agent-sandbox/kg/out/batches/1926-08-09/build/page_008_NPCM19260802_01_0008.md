## [12] *(no headline: column 2)*

Tientsin is now suffering severely from flood. A report from this centrepated the 14th inst. states that owing to the incessant rain and the rise of the river, all the South city is now several feet under water, while many houses have been completely washed away.

A fire broke out yesterday morning at Cheungsha Street, Mongkok, in a building used as an umbrella "store and dwellings. The Brigade extinguished the fire in fifteen minutes. The contents of the ground, floor were badly damaged by fire and water and the upper floors by smoke.

In the United States Court for China—before—Judge—Milton—Durpury, E. J. Beardsley was fined G. $500 or given the option of a jail sentence of four months for permitting gambling on his promise at 49 Avenue Edward VII, or December 11, 1925. It is understood that the fine was paid immediately after the decision was rendered.

Legaspi, Albay, July 26.— Four children died has a result of a landslide which occurred at a popular bathing place in the barrio of Masarauag, municipality of Guinobatan, last Sunday morning. The bathing place is a fountain of clear, cool water beneath a bluff, where 11 persons were bathing. Suddenly a large amount of earth fell from the bluff on to the people below; burying the four children. All the adults escaped injury.

In the course of an article on the recent heat wave in Shanghai, the "N.-C. Daily News," of July 27, says:—The heat of the past 11 days, together with the excessive humidity, "have been most oppressive and unusually discomforting, as most readers in Shanghai will have felt, the temperature exceeding the figures of last year by: very large degree. In 1926 the highest was, on the average, for the 11 days since July 16, 95.2 while in 1925 the average was 88. The minimum for the two year was 79.5 and 75.9, respectively. The rainfall was almost negligible during the period under review this year, although rain fell on seven days last year.

## [13] *(no headline: column 2)*

Mr. Y. Murakami, Consul-General for Japan, is leaving the "Colony on holiday.

Salt Lake City, Utah, July 26.— All Mormon missionaries who are American citizens to-day were ordered by the church heads to leave Mexico owing to the new religious laws. About 70 Mormons are affected.

"Madrid," July 23.—The Infanta Isabel assisted to-day in the laying of the cornerstone of the Infants' Home, the first institution of the kind ever established in Spain to feed the children of the poor while their parents are working."

Mr. C. A. Smith, London manager of the Commonwealth Bank of Australia, has joined the service of the Imperial Bank of India. He will be appointed a managing governor of the bank when the next vacancy occurs, probably in the autumn of 1927.

Bucharest, July. 23.—Crown Prince Michael of Rumania, five years old, was bitten by a mad dog to-day. He was rushed to Bucharest by airplane from the Royal summer palace, and was immediately given the Pasteur treatment: It is believed that he is in no danger, as the treatment was given within a few hours from the time of the attack.

Paris, July 24.—A demonstration of angry Parislans equalling that which attended the Herriot down-full greeted Andrew Mellon, Secretary of the Treasury, on his arrival this afternoon from Cherbourg. "Down with Mellon," "down with Borah," "down with debts," shouted the thousands who crowded around as he made his way through the jammed streets to his hotel. Several rioters have been arrested during the past few hours for striking Americans and other foreigners.

John Rogan, (the famous band master) as he called himself then, followed in 1867 his great-grandfather, his grandfather, and his father into the British army. He entered as a band-boy, and when he left in 1920 he was Senior Director of Music to the Brigade of Guards. He was thanked by the King for his prolonged and unique services, and was made a Commander of the Royal Victorian Order. Sir Alexander Mackenzie wrote to him on that occasion: "Any man who leaves his profession in a better condition than when he entered it merits special recognition."

Chicago, July 26.—Frank L Smith, successful candidate for the Republican nomination for United States senator in the April primary, testified to-day before the Senate campaign committee, that this campaign cost $250,000 of which Samuel Insull, president of the Commonwealth Electric Company, and chairman of the executive committee of the Chicago, Elevated Railways, contributed $100,000.

Dr. Stanton Youngberg, director of agriculture of Manila, says that rice pests this year are worse than in any other year that he remembers. Farmers and over the islands, particularly in the rice-producing regions, are complaining of the pest infestation, and fear that crop production will be lowered considerably. Dr. Youngberg is mobilising the bureau's agents and inspectors for a war on the pests, and has requested the co-operation of provincial and municipal officials.

The "Kobe Herald" of July 16 says that an American girl, 21 years of age, is shortly to be registered as a Geisha in the Shimbashi quarter. When 15 years old, this girl, by name Virginia Titale, saw performances by Tenkatsu, the Japanese woman juggler, and just before the troupe left America, she approached Madame Tenkatsu to allow her to join the troupe. Her parents' consent was obtained, so the Japanese papers say, and the girl was taken on. She was taught Japanese dancing and singing and judging by the performance which the Tenkatsu troupe put on at the Shochiku Theatre some time ago, she showed considerable talent, even in the eyes of the Japanese in the audience.

## [15] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL

Mr. Y. Murakami, Consul-General for Japan, is leaving Hong-kong on holiday.

The Governor Elect of British North Borneo is Mr. J. L. Humphreys, of the Malayan Civil Service. It is expected that Mr. Humphreys will arrive early in October.

His many friends will regret to learn that Mr. G.S.V. Bidwell is lying seriously ill in a London hospital. He left Shanghai, accompanied by Mrs. Bidwell and family, at the end of May.

Mr. H. H. Fox, C.M.G., Commercial Counsellor of the British Legation, "arrived in" Shanghai from Peking on July 26. He is proceeding to Hongkong for the purpose of meeting Mrs. Fox, who is on her way back to China from England.

Among forthcoming weddings are those of Eliseu Desio Botelho, clerk, residing at No. 19, Mosque Street. and Victoria Maria Mattos, residing at No. 32, Jordan Road, Kowloon; and Allister Pommerfelt, Chartered Accountant, Hill West, and Edith Dorothy Lillian, Birchall, residing at New Brighton, Cheshire England.

Among the passengers who passed through Hongkong on the U.S. President Jackson, during the week-end for Manila, were Vice-Governor A. Gilmore, of the Philippine Islands, returning there after a visit to the United States on Government business; Congressman W. D. Oliver, of Alabama, who is engaged on Government business; and Col. N. L. Stimson, formerly Secretary of War, making a tour of the Orient with his wife.

The many friends of Mr. A. McClure, of the firm of Seth, Mancell and McClure, will congratulate him on his narrow escape from what might have easily proved a fatal "accident" on Saturday night, says the "Shanghai Mercury" of July 26. Mr. McClure paid a visit to a local garage where a large German police dog is kept. In attempting to give a friendly pat to the dog the animal evidently mistook the intention of Mr. McClure and leapt at him, catching him by the throat. Mr. McClure required to have several surgical stitches applied to his wound which was within an inch of severing the jugular vein.

Honolulu, July 25. — The population of Honolulu in 1950 will be 160,000, in the opinion of Dr. Romanzo Adams, sociologist of the University of Hawaii. The present population is 118,000.

## [17] REST

Hankow, July 20.—It is reported that the Nisshin Kisers Kaisha tugboat "Ume Maru" for Changteh, Hunan Province, together with two lighters were detained by Chinese troops at Yunnanjiang on the 17th and that the "Ume Maru" was towed to Changteh.

Paul Smith's, New York, July 24.—President Coolidge and Secretary of the Navy Wilbur today discussed the advisability of building an airport for the navy which would have a capacity of 6,000,000 cubic feet. It was stated that no decision was reached. Secretary Wilbur spent the weekend as the guest of the President.

Henday, France, July 25.—It is reliably reported here that the Spanish government has ordered the release of Generals Weyler and Aguilera, who were being held for complicity in the recent plot against the dictatorship of Primo de Rivera. Weyler insisted at the time of his arrest that the idea of his being concerned in such a plot was ridiculous.

Baku, Azerbaijan. July 10.—Large numbers of lepers, who have arrived here from Persia, are becoming a serious problem, to government authorities. In the absence of an isolated colony, the immigrants afflicted with the deadly disease are walking the streets freely and are congregating in public places among the native inhabitants.

Rome, July 25.—The Fascists Syndicate and the Industrialists are arranging for the inauguration of special stores in the country's factories to enable the employees to obtain necessaries at cost prices. The innovation grows out of the government plan to provide stores for State employees in an endeavour to reduce the high cost of living.

## [18] NEWS.

The funeral took place on July 26, at Bubbling Well Cemetery, Shanghai, of Mr. Gordon E. Gray, of the Chinese Maritime Customs, who passed away at the Shanghai General Hospital. The Rev. E. F. Borst-Smith, M.A., chaplain of the Shanghai Volunteer Corps and minister of the Union Church, officiated.

On his arrival in Hongkong yesterday, H. E. Senhor Maia Magalhaes, the retiring Governor of Macao, was met by Mr. Albuquerque, the Portuguese Consul, Messrs. da Roza, de Sousa and others and in the evening was the guest at a dinner at the Hongkong Hotel given by Mr. Julio Silva, of Macao. Among others present at the dinner were Mr. and Mrs. Albuquerque, Mr. and Mrs. da Roza, Mr. and Mrs. de Souza, Mr. and Mrs. Silva Netto, Mr. Da Horta, and the Commander of the "Republic." To-day His Excellency will have tiffin with H.E. The Governor (Sir Cecil Clementi, K.C.M.G.) at Mountain Lodge and to-morrow he leaves for Europe by the s.s. President Van Buren.

It was thought that "the exodus of old residents for Home from Kowloon had ceased, but Saturday saw the departure of yet another in the person of Mr. Robert Nicholls, of the China Light and Power Company, Limited. "Bob" was both a power for the good of sport and a shining light of both the K.C.C. and the Kowloon Bowling Green Club. For six years he played in the Bowls League for the K.C.C. and for a time he more than held his own as a "No. 1"—one bowl on the jack and another just behind it. This season he has played in the League for the Bowling Green Club, the members of which gathered together at a pleasant little function on Wednesday evening, when the President was called upon to present Mr. Nicholls with a little parting souvenir as a token of the respect in which he is held. He expressed the thanks of the members to Mr. Nicholls for his loyal and unwavering support of the Club; trusted that he would have a pleasant voyage Home; and that he and his good lady would find the Homeland kindly disposed to them in the way of this world's goods and possessions. Finally, he assured their departing member that should he ever decide to return to this Colony he would receive a most cordial welcome from the members of that Club. Mr. Nicholls suitably responded.
