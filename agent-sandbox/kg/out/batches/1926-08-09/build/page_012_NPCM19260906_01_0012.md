**HONGKONG, MONDAY, SEPTEMBER 6, 1926.**

**WAR NEWS.**

**ENITISH GUNEOAT IN ACTION**

### 一

Hitherto there has been no confirmation of the persistent statements from Chinese and Japanese news agencies that Marshal Wu Pei-fu has been wounded or of the North China Daily News "official" wire from Peking stating that Wu Pei-fu is virtually a prisoner.
One report states that he was struck in the chest by a spent bullet but anyway he is still apparently directing the "defence of Hankow.

It now appears that the Southernerse penetrated" Wuchang on Wednesday and that fighting continued in and around Wuchang and the Wuchang Lakes.

The Southerners are hampered by the heavy rains and very boggy ground but Reuter's Peking correspondent wires that according to foreign official sources Wuchang tell on Friday. Wu Pei-fu, after retreating to Hankow, declared martial law and installed a new Civil Governor. The garrison commander at Hankow o: "Wu Pei-fu's forces is mounting artillery on Tortoise Hill. Hanyang, which commands the Wuchang side of the river and is making every preparation for a determined defence of Hanyang and Hankow. Reinforcements continue to arrive from the North. Reuter.

A report from Swallow says that Fukien forces are marching on Swatow, from which the "Reds" are fleeing.

A steamer is at present in the harbour waiting to carry the "Red" leaders southward.

The Wu Pei-fuite, Yang-sen, has commanded two British steamers at Wan-Hsien for the purpose of transporting troops to Chungking.

A report from Hankow says that he attack on Wuchang continued throughout Friday morning without appreciable result.

As an example of the heaviness of the bombardment the last telegram mentioned that the American Church and the General Hospital at Wuchang were hit twenty times.

A Chinese military report says that the fighting in Wuchang was most severe yesterday afternoon, shells constantly falling on the city.
The telegraph staff were removed to Hankow from where only gun fine was audible at nine o'clock, but the Northerners still held the city.

Another report says that the Southerners drew to the front all the Changsha garrison, whilst Sun Chuan-fang (Wu Peifu's ally) has gathered 50,000 troops on the border ready to occupy Changsha.

The excitement in Hankow was growing yesterday evening but there was no disorder.—Reuter.

A British official wireless message from Hankow states that Wuchang was still in the hands of the Northerners this (Saturday) morning.—Reuter.

According to a telegram received by a Japanese shipping firm from Hankow this morning, a Japanese vessel witnessed at 3.30 o'clock on Thursday afternoon the British river gunboat Scarab in action with the Southern troops near Huachingkung, 50 miles up from Hankow, exchanging fire for more than two hours.—Reuter.

The situation in China and particularly at Hankow, in which neighbourhood a struggle is proceeding between Northern Chihli, troops and Cantonese forces, is being closely watched in London.—British Wireless Service.

The newspapers report the foreign Concessions in Hankow have been placed in a state of defence as a precaution and that the Japanese have landed a force from gunboats to protect their Conces.

## [22] TRADE UNIONISM

**CL'AIMS OF CONGRESS PRESIDENT.**

*London, September 5.*

ence of the Trade Union Congress at Bournemouth Mr. Arthur Pugh, President, dwelt largely on the General Strike. He declared that the weapon would not be left unused when it was sought to enforce upon any section of the workers terms which had not been made the subject of negotiation and collective agreement.

The Congress had not met in a penitential mood nor with any consciousness of defeat. He claimed that the by-elections, especially of Hammersmith and Wallsend, had shown that Labour commanded increasing support and that it was not the Trade Unions but the Government that had lost the confidence of the people.
Referring to Labour's International policy, Mr. Pugh said that great and new problems were emerging in the change of direction of the main stream of emigration from Europe to undeveloped countries overseas, adding:—“We must bear in mind that our international organisation is mainly European in its structure and range. It is imperative that we should extend it to workers in these new countries whither the outflow of population from the older lands must inevitably tend in the future.” Practical consideration should be given to the question of obtaining International Trade Union agreements and understandings by which, combined with prevention of the importation of products of sweated labour, Trade Unionism in countries, where low economic standards prevailed, would be assisted by stronger countries and build up an effective organisation for the benefit of ill-paid and exploited workers.

## [25] COMMUNAL RIOT

**UROPEAN POLICE OFFICER INJURED**

**OVER 100 ARRESTS.**

Communal riot occurred in the Watgunge suburb, several persons being injured including one European and one Indian Police officer. Over 100 arrests have been made.

A Hindu procession with a band was nearing a Mosque when the Mohammedans summoned the Hindus to stop the music. The Hindus refused, whereupon fighting ensued, the Hindus marching past playing music.

Both Sides were reinforced and the police charged the rioters.—Reuter.

## [26] TYPHOON DAMAGE.

**PASSENGER TRAIN OVERTURNED.**

*Tokyo, September 4.*

As the result of a typhoon passenger train from Tokyo to Nagoya was overturned near Tamamatsu this morning. There were only a few minor casualties. The roof of a school at Hamamatsu was blown off.

The typhoon is expected to strike Tokyo this afternoon.

Incoming reports show that the damage was widespread. Communications were upset. At the military aerodrome at Akenogahara, near Nagoya, forty aeroplanes were wrecked, several completely.

At Toyohashi a school collapsed, pinning down children and killing twelve, seriously injuring twenty. Three hundred houses were inundated.

In the Tochigi prefecture of Yoshida, forty buildings were destroyed, including the police station. Three policemen and several others were seriously injured.

At Kawasaki, near Yokohama, two lighters capzized and fifteen persons were drowned.

Forty houses were blown down at Saitama, and five were killed this morning.

Yokohama sailings, including the Siberia Maru, have been held up, owing to the severity of the gale. — Reuter.

## [27] ILLEGAL PACT.

**STRIKE COMMITTEE SEIZE**

*Peking, September 5.*

Telegrams from Indo-China report that on the 2nd inst., the Cantonese Strike Committee at Tonghing, Kwangtung, seized the whole of the petroleum stocks of the French Compagnie Franco-Asiatique des Petroles of Haiphong, and announced their intention of selling the stocks at auction. The local authorities, having disregarded the French Consul's protests, the French Legation has now addressed a strong Note to the Waichiaopu asking that orders be given for the immediate restitution of the goods, and holding the Chinese authorities responsible for the consequences of this unlawful act and for reparation for all the losses sustained by the Company.—Reuter.
