Galesburg, Illinois, Sept. 2.—It is estimated that property redemption from a storm which swept over Central Illinois and town to-day will exceed $2,000,000. The light power and water works of this city were inundated and put out of commission. In many parts of this region the storm reached the proportions of a cloudburst and many streams overflowed their banks.

Owing the failure of its appeal for an increase in the Customs surplus appropriation for the maintenance of its monthly expenses, the Waichiaopu, like its sister ministries is enforcing a retrenchment policy. In a recent order, a number of members who seldom attend to their duties, including Mr. Fang Yao-liang, a Ruominchun, diplomat and Mr. Wan, Chuo-chi, were impaired of their salaries and given honorary posts.

Jerusalem, August 31,—Palestine is to have its own money. Word came today that the British colonial secretary has named a board to plan a Palestinian system of coinage based on the pound sterling. The cains will not be British but the designs will be made in England under the advice of an expert on the history of Palestine. Since the British government accepted a protectorate for Palestine, British money has been used.

Barcelona, Sept. 2.—It is estimated that 55 persons are dead and millions of dollars worth of crops and other property in ruins as a result of the most devastating storms and floods in many years which are sweeping the central and north-eastern parts of Spain. Twenty persons were killed yesterday when a train from Barcelona to Valencia was wrecked by a landslide near Ampollo. The Barcelona district suffered most heavily and relief supplies are being loaded on railroad trains and into automobiles for shipment to the stricken regions. Rescuers worked throughout the night and succeeded in saving hundreds of families marooned by the flood.

Mr. B. W. Fleisher, publisher of the "Japan Advertiser" of Tokyo and widely known throughout the Far East, will sail for the United States within a few days to undergo treatment for an arterial disease which has kept him bed-fast for nearly a year. Mr. Fleisher will enter the Mayor Brothers sanitarium Rochester, Minn. He plans to return to Japan as soon as corvalescence is complete.

Among the passengers who leave on the Dollar liner "President Pierce" for San Francisco tomorrow is Dr. R. W. Hart, the United States Public Health Officer in Manila. Archbishop M. J. O'Doherty of the diocese of Manila arrived in Manila on the "President Pierce." Mons. Jose Bustamante, vicar-general of the archdiocese of Manila, superiors of the different religious orders, rectors of Roman Catholic colleges, high government officials, laymen and students were members of the general reception committee that welcomed the returning prelate. At the close of the reception at the pier. a Te Deum was sung in the Roman Catholic cathedral in the Walled City. Archbishop M. J. O'Doherty during his stay in the United States attended the Eucharist congress at Chicago and later visited many of the larger cities in the middle west.

After nearly 30 years' service the clock in the tower of Southwark Cathedral has broken down, and the Chapter are to ask experts to examine the mechanism. The clock was the gift of Sir Frederick Wigman, and succeeded a clock which perdered service for 15 years.

The new director of the Revenue Stamp Bureau, under the Finance Ministry, is reported to have discovered that many Peking merchants have been purchasing counterfeit stamps at the rate of 40 per cent of their face value. The police and gendarmerie officers have been apprised of the facts and are attempting to find the forgors and to arrest the merchants who knowingly purchased the counterfeit stamps.

Hsincheng, a district, on the North of Shantung Province is reported to have been selected by General Chang Tsung-chang as the site in which a new arsenal will be built. With a view to facilitating transportation of various kinds of materials, necessary for the construction of the ammunition store, a light railway leading from the district to Tientkin will be soon laid.

Columbia, S. C. September 2.—A runoff primary will be necessary to determine whether Senator Ellison D. Smith or Speaker Edgar A. Brown of the South Carolina House of Representatives shall be the Democratic nominee for United States senator, neither having received a majority at the primary on Tuesday. Ex-senator Dial ran third in the primary. Senator Smith is an advocate of the world court.

Tenants of houses on the Duchy of Cornwall estate overlooking Kennington Oval have been notified that they must pay entertainment tax on money received as the result of letting windows and balconies to people to view the fifth Test match. Officials of the Excise and Customs Department called on the tenants and left two documents, one a type-written form drawing attention to Section II. of the Finance Act, 1922, and the other a leaflet setting out the scale of taxes.

Mr. Henry G. Anderson, whose death at the age of 79, has occurred on his farm at Scholtzfontein, was one of the early pioneers of the diamond fields. About 1869 he was one of a party from Port Elizabeth who joined in the first rush to the Vaal River. When the goldfields of the Witwatersrand were discovered Mr. Anderson occupied a farm which has since become a suburb of Johannesburg. He was one of the original claim holders at "New Rush," which afterwards became the Kimberley mine.

On the afternoon of the 2nd alt. two Chinese presumably students, threw a bomb from the top of Snake Hill, Wuchang. The bomb was thrown with the object of creating panic, and it succeeded in a certain extent as a large number of the populace mistook it for the sound of a southern gun. The two however were arrested by the military police and executed publicly.

Messrs. Colgate and Co., American perfume and soap manufacturers of New York, obtained judgment against the China Chemical Works of Shanghai on the ground that its "Vioris" trademark for soaps had been infringed By the defendants. The latter were fined $500, with the option of imprisonment at the rate of a dollar a day —(500 days). Assessor Spiker and Magistrate Kwan heard the case in the Mixed Court. Mr. A. Krisel appeared for the plaintiffs and Mr. A. M. Preston for the defendants.

Peking, August 25.—A “persecution of education” in China has progressed to such a point where any student or teacher displaying independence of thought is likely to be suggested. Like a criminal, carryon the Nationalist organ “People's Tribune” which is published in English in Peking. According to the “Tribune,” a regular grant is being conducted in educational institutions for the alleged purpose of cradicalizing bolshevik influence, but the term bolshevik subject to such wide interpretation that the ban on free thought has spread beyond all reason.

After a two months' tour in the United States, the Crown Prince and Princess of Sweden sailed on August 4 on board the liner "Shinyo Maru" for Hawaii, where they propose to stay for a fortnight before continuing their trip to Japan.

The Master of Stampill has been doing quite a lot of flying at his father's country home. Fintray House, Abercenshire. With his De Havilland Moth he has taken flights with a number of guests, going on one to John O'Greats. Both his parents went for flights with him, and his two sisters, Miss Margaret and Miss Elizabeth. Stampill, and his wife, were passengers as well.

The Prince of Wales, on learning that Police-Constable William Wakefield was doing his last duty at York House, St. James's before retiring on pension after 26 years' service with the force, sent for him and presented him with a silver watch bearing the Prince's monogram. Alluding to the fact that Constable Wakefield had kept a watchful eye upon his favourite dog, and had made a friend of the animal, the Prince said at parting, "I am very sorry to lose the services of so good an officer, and I am quite sure Cora will miss you, too." The constable has been on duty at York House since the Prince went into residence there seven years ago.

Word has been received of the death of Mrs. Laurence M. Sears, in the hospital at Fenchow, Shansi, following an operation for appendicitis. Mrs. Sears was spending the summer with her husband at the Yutaoho summer resort, near Fenchow. "A short time ago she developed appendicitis. When it became clear that an operation would be needed before she could be brought back to Peking, she was taken to the American Board Mission Hospital at Fenchow. Mrs. Sears was the wife of Mr. Laurence Sears, who is doing student work with the Y.M.C.A. in Peking. She also was the sister of Mrs. Sidney Gamble. During the two years that she had been in Peking, she had won many friends for herself, among the Chinese as well as among the foreigners. Mrs. Sears was President elect of the American College Women's Club for the coming year, and also teacher of French in the Peking American School. Her father, Mr. W. E. Lowe, is professor of History at Colgate University. Dr. Lowe and his wife were in Peking last autumn visiting their two daughters.
