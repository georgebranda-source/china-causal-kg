During the year ended June 30 last, 11,260,000 deposits were made in Yorkshire Penny Bank, representing a total amount of £35,648,000 and an average deposit of £3 3s. 2d.

Summoned by the P.W.D. for deviating from plans submitted and approved by the department, the owner of No. 16, Wing On Street, and of another building in Gilman Street, was fined $50 at the Central Magistracy yesterday.

One N.C.O. in the Polish Army was killed "and several privates were wounded when three "75" field guns of French make exploded during artillery target-practice near Nowy Targ (Neumarket) by troops of the Cracow garrison.

This year's conference of the International Law Association met in Vienna to the great satisfaction of the Austrians, who attach importance to the fact that this is the first meeting since the war outside what was Allied territory.

During Norwegian air manœuvres off Christiansand, two hydroplanes about to land collided, with the result that one sank immediately. The pilot, Lieutenant Troye, was seriously injured. The other machine was only slightly damaged.

The Council of the University of Paris has sanctioned the foundation at the Sorbonne of a wireless telephony institute, which will enable listeners in to follow lectures in their own homes. The new wireless institute will be opened in November.

The Recorder at Hull Quarter Sessions quashed the sentence of three months' imprisonment with hard labour passed by the Stipendiary on John William Dodsworth, a railway fireman, for throwing a missile, which the appellant contended was a ball of paper, at a volunteer during the general strike.
Paulsmiths, N.Y., September 2.—Will Hays, President of the Motion Picture Producers and Distributors of America, informed President Coolidge to-day that American movie fans annually spend approximately one thousand million dollars in admission fees at 20,233 theatres in the United States.

## [10] LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS.

The Yugoslav Prime Minister, M. Ouzounovith, has requested all the Ministers to revise their departmental estimates so as to secure a reduction in the Budget expenditure of 10 per cent. for the current year.

" Mr. Robert Mond and a family party of ten people, including nurses and several children engaged a giant super-muring Napier flying-boat of Imperial Airways to fly them from Southampton to Dinard, where they spent a holiday. This is the first time a family party has chartered a big flying-boat to take them on their holidays.

At Boston (Lincolnshire) three Wrangle youths were fined £2 each and ordered to pay costs for gross cruelty to a stray dog. They admitted taking the dog into a barn, fastening its jaws with a horse twitch, and pricking its hindquarters. They said it was done "for fun." The dog went mad, and had to be destroyed.

Four of the directors of the Jo-no Muslin Company in Tokyo have been prosecuted on the charge of having embezzled their employees' fund at the time of the dissolution of the company. About 2,000 workers who have been thrown out of employment are now appealing to the prefectural Governor for help.

Dr. Budberg died suddenly at his residence at Harbin on August 25, after a very short illness. He was born in the Baltic Provinces on January 22, 1867, and was graduated at Dorpat University in 1895. During the Russo-Japanese war he was in charge of a military hospital and thus became acquainted with the Far East. He paid a short visit to Russia in 1905 and then returned to Harbin, where he has lived ever since, as he received an appointment as police and prison doctor in 1906. He was one of the foremost doctors in the fight with the plague in 1910 and wrote a book in German on the subject. In 1921 he again helped conquer the disease. The funeral was attended by a large number of people.

A serious outbreak of phylloxera (a disease of the grape vine dreaded by vine growers) has been discovered in the most famous vineyards producing Rhine wine, notably in the vicinity of Rudesheim.

General Yen Chun-yang, as chief of the Chinese police force, is finding difficulty in obtaining the funds from Nanking to pay his men. He has sent one last appeal to the Finance Commissioner of Kiangsu for money to pay out August salaries, and if this appeal is unsuccessful he will send his personal secretary "to demand prompt payment."

Starting from September 1, special precautions for public safety were taken by the Shanghai and Woosung Constabulary. These included the carrying of carbines by constables on duty and the curtailment of leave of constables off duty. Instructions to this effect have been received direct from Marshal Sun Chuanfang.

Two solicitors appearing before the Walsall magistrates recently in a separation case in which the parties were George and Mary Jume Cooper, both more than 70 years of age, announced that they had agreed on a separation order for one penny per week, merely to keep the couple apart, the wife having complained of threats. The Bench made the order asked for.

Professor Einstein, the scientist, who is a delegate to the meeting of the Institute of Intellectual Co-operation now being held at Geneva, has expressed the opinion that the ether is not susceptible to movement but simply provides a field for the propagation of gravitation. The ether, he said, is an immobile transmitter of action through space. Light, like electricity and weight, passes through the medium of the ether, which conducts their energy without participating in their vibrations and without diminishing them, because it does not possess the qualities of inertia. This may appear symbolic, said Prof. Einstein, but it is a symbol the scientific world cannot ignore.

M: Stewart addressed the gathering on the results of the China Tariff Commission and the outlook for the Lancashire cotton trade in that country.

Mr. J. Nienhuys, one of the founders of tobacco-growing in Deli, Sumatra, recently celebrated his ninetieth birthday. This pioneer of one of the most flourishing cultures in the Dutch East Indies, started for the D.E.I. at the age of twenty-three. Native labour was proved a disappointment; and success only came when he recruited Chinese in Penang. In 1869, the Deli Co. was founded. Mr. Nienhuys personally directed affairs in Deli until 1871, when he returned to Holland. He is still, however, a Director of the Deli Co.

His Excellency Chao Phya Rama Raghob, the favourite and A.D.C. of the late King of Siam, is expected to arrive in Britain shortly. He is described by the Bangkok correspondent of the "Morning Post" as a figure in many ways recalling the days of "Grand Viziers" and the Thousand and One Nights. A few years ago Chao Phya Ram, as he is generally known, was the most powerful man in Siam. At the beginning of the present reign he was superseded in his position as A.D.C., the new king having no favourites, and his motto being "retrenchment," Chao Phya Ram is still a young man, only a little over thirty. He is bringing with him on his first visit to Europe a young wife of seventeen. He intends to make an extended stay in Britain, and it is understood that he may go into residence at Oxford.
