## [6] OPPOSITION REMAINS.

Although the southern Nationalists have now occupied the cities of Wuchang, Hanyang and Hankow, they have by no means destroyed all opposition.
Marshal Wu Pei-fu must be regarded as on the decline. It is true that he has other military adherents further North but they will have been disheartened by the trend of events and especially by the treachery of Liu Tso-lung, defender of Hanyang.

For days the Nationalists hampered at the gates of Wuchang without getting in. As soon as the "bright sky, white sun" flag of the Cantonese was hoisted by one brigade of the Hanyang garrison, the other Northern divisions retired. Wuchang and Hankow were evacuated and order has been restored by the southerners now in control.

River Crossed Under Cover.

Chinese reports indicated that Wu Pei-fu may have gone down the Yangtse River on one of his gunboats but it seems more probable that, as cabled by Reuter, he went north with his troops.

General Chang Kai-shek, commander-in-chief, was on the Wuchang sector. Keeping the northerners occupied, he paved the way towards General Tang Sang-chi (his Human subordinate) coming up on the centre and striking a vital blow on Hanyang. Some of Chang's own troops also wheeled round from Wuchang, crossed the river under cover, and assisted in the subjugation of Hanyang.

Once the strategic heights above Hanyang had fallen, Han-kow was untenable.

What About Chang Tso-lin?

The net result of these captures is that Wu Peifu has lost the province which was essentially "his own." Crossing the Hupeh frontier, he goes into Honan, which is south of the Yellow River. Should he not make a stand there he will then be in the very North of China Proper.

In Honan are several thousand pro-Nationalist troops under Fan Chung-siao, better known as the "White Wolf Cub," as he is the successor of the former notorious bandit, the "White Wolf," whose men have now been embodied into an army.

There is as yet no evidence that the Fengtien party, of which Marshall Chang Tso-lin of Mukden is the head, has sent any troops into the field to help their ally, Wu Peifu.

On the other hand, developments are springing up in Kiangsi province which is to the south of the Yangtse River and to the east of Hunan.

Kiangsi comes within the domains of Marshal Sun Chuanfang, generalissimo of five provinces extending up to Shanghai and Kiangsu province.

Hitherto Marshal Sun has maintained armed neutrality. Chang Kai-shek seems to have ignored the possibility of a sudden sortie on his right flank and rear and continued to push ahead. At the moment it appears as if the Nationalists, instead of waiting for Sun Chuan-fang to come to Wu Pei-fu's aid, are "trespassing" on Kiangsi.

## [8] REPORTS BY MAIL.

### Street Fighting in Wuchang Described.

*Peking, Sept.*

Fighting for mastery of the central provinces which has been in progress for more than a week between the Cantonese forces and Marshal Wu Pei-fu became increasingly fierce yesterday. From Pagoda Hill, a mile south of Wuchang, the Cantonese fired artillery into the city and were repulsed in street fighting. The gunboats of the northern forces raked the Cantonese lines from the river, aiding the reinforcements from Honan, and stiffening General Wu Pei-fu's stubborn resistance.

A heavy attack is progressing and the Northern forces are holding their positions "at Wuchang, which has been invested for several days, although several times they have been forced to enter the city and to engage in street fighting.

Japanese Marines Land.
General Wu Pei-fu crossed over the Yangtze River to Hankow day before yesterday. Martial law prevails at Hankow, the several military authorities preventing disturbances, despite the almost complete confusion. The British, French, and American consulates are guarded by their respective gunboats. The Japanese landed 200 marines, which had a salutary effect.

Nanking advices say that General Sun Chuan-fang is sending troops to Kangsha, but apparently they are not participating actively.—Associated Press.

Shanghai Worried.

The Chinese population is actively preparing for a siege which they feel is inevitable within the next three weeks unless General Sun Chuan-fang is able to stem the advance of the Cantonese army.

Troops are being rushed to the Kiangsi border and the Arsenal is working night and day turning out rifles, machine guns and three-inch cannon. The Hankow front is 700 miles away but there is a general feeling that the Cantonese are financed and equipped by Russia which gives them a distinct advantage over the northern defenders.

There are also unmistakable indications that General Chang Tsung-chang, Fengtian tuchun of Shantung, whom General Sun Chuan-fang drove out of Shanghai 18 months ago, is planning to attack from the north while General Sun's troops are busy helping General Wu Pei-fu defend Hankow. United Press.

## [10] THIRD ACTION.

**MUCH DISPUTED POKFULAM PROPERTY.**

**A LADY PLAINTIFF.**

A claim respecting a house which has already been the subject of much litigation was heard in the Summary Court this morning.
The house in question is N.o. 31A. Pokfulam Road, the owner and former occupier of which, a Chinese lady, was the plaintiff.

Plustiff, Mrs. Chun Kwok Shi, claims from the Hang On firm, $950, $150 being 18 months' rent for a portion of the ground floor which she states was hired by the defendant for giving shelter to certain workmen and keeping their tools in; and $500 alleged to have been a loun.

Mr. D. McCallum, for the plaintiff, stated that plaintiff had owned houses in Kiu Yang Lane which she had pulled down for the purposes of re-erection facing Pokfulam Road. The Hang On firm were engaged as contractors for this work. The plaintiff was living at 31A, rokfulam Road in a completed house and in response to a request by the defendants agreed to let the workmen have a portion of the ground floor at $25 a month.
With regard to the claim for money lent, Mr. McCallum stated that immediately after the contract between the plaintiff and the defendants had been entered into for the reconstruction of the houses, the defendants wanted money to construct some kind of a store for materials and the plaintiff accordingly advanced $500.

Guild Influence.

Neither the rent nor the loan had been paid, it was further stated. The arrangement with regard to rent and the loan were only verbal; there being nothing in writing. It was agreed that the plaintiff should deduct, from the money owing on the certificates, the respective amounts; Mr. McCallum stated. The house had already been subject of two court actions and an arbitration. The architect had not been satisfied with the progress of the work which was held up during the strike, and had put an end to the contract. The contractor, as a result of influence in the contractors' guild, had made it extraordinarily difficult for the plaintiff to get the work completed.
There was a clause in the original contract, said Mr. McCallum, which stated that any dispute should go to arbitration. But the arbitrator could not deal with the matters relating to the rent and loan as they were outside the contract. It had therefore been necessary, to bring the matter to court again.
Plaintiff went into the witness box and bore out the opening statement. She said that on the certificates she still owed, defendant $2,000. This had been adjudged at the arbitration but there had been a stay of execution for the present case to be decided.

Houses Reduced.

Cross-examined by Mr. W. B. Hind, plaintiff denied that all she had allowed the contractor was a portion of the yard of her house for the storage of materials. She also denied that the original contract price for the reconstruction was $43,750, and that she had stated "her partner" could not pay more than $42,750. She had not got a partner.

Plaintiff also denied that as she was not willing to pay, the original price she compromised by offering $500 not to be included in the contracted price but to be paid on the completion of the contract.

Plaintiff stated in reply to further questions that the original contract price had provided for five-storeyed houses but that the defendant had told her that the Government would not allow this. In spite of the fact that the houses were to be reduced in size, the defendant had wanted an increase.
The case is proceeding.

## [13] "SILENT" NAVY."

**ARMIES NOT MOVING.**

1.

Last week-end the "northern" Chinese Navy was still anchored in Foochow and Amoy, appearing unlikely to blockade Swatow as rumoured.

Intelligence which arrived yesterday from Amoy (in Fukien province) indicates that there is a little uneasiness at that port.

Chinese of "the; better class have left the interior for the city and concession, bringing valuables with them, for fear of trouble breaking out on the Kwangtung frontier."

Swatow is, of course, mined. It transpires that the official families fleeing earlier in the week went to Canton for safety.

At present the only steamers calling at Swatow from Hong Kong are the O.S.K. boats and the "feeder" ships of the J.C.J.L. British shipping is not putting in there.

## [17] TWO MEN ARRESTED.

What appears to have been an attempt to commit a piracy on a Douglas Co. boat by smuggling firearms aboard was detected this morning by the arrest of a cook to the Indian guards on the s.s. "Hai Hong" and another man, at 9.30 this morning.

Acting on information received, Senior Revenue Officer G. Watt and Chinese officers kept a lookout on the wharf and observed a sampan drawing alongside the vessel. The guards' cook was in the sampan with a bundle under his arm. A Chinese officer accosted the man and on opening the bundle found that it contained two fully loaded automatic pistols, another weapon, with an empty chamber and about 100 rounds of ammunition.

On an accusation made by the cook, another man who was alleged to be looking over the side of the vessel was also arrested.
The men will be brought before the Magistrate to-morrow

## [19] “INFLICTED DAMAGE” THERE

British Gunboat Action: Latest Intelligence

Reports have been current that the British Navy has been bombarding Wanhsien, the town in Szechuan province, on the Yangtse River, which opened fire from ship and shore.

It has been stated that the port is in flames. As yet there are no official details. A Government despatch states, however, that our gunboats bombarded the town after the incident, inflicting considerable damage.

There is no information as to whether active reprisals have been taken or not subsequent to the action.

Five of the six B. & S. officers were rescued; but nothing is said as to whether the two river steamboats have been released.

As foreshadowed in yesterday's "China Mail," H.M.S. "Hawkins," flagship of the China Squadron, has proceeded to the vicinity.
The “China Mail” also learns that the despatch of cruisers from Home is being speeded up. H.M.S. “Carysfort,” “Castor” and “Dartmouth” left on September 2 for the China Station with reliefs, accompanied by the submarine L27 to replace the L9. Apparently the cruisers will be here in three weeks or so, instead of the end of October as at first anticipated, if necessary leaving the L27 behind.

H.M.S. "Durban," which is at Singapore on a southward cruise, has been detained there—it is gathered—pending instructions to return here if needed.

## [20] OFFICIAL COMMUNIQUE.

The following official report on the incident at Wanhsien is communicated for information:—
“It is confirmed that five European merchant officers were rescued and the other wounded presumed drowned. The following is a summary of the reports received:—

“The S.S. ‘Kiawo,’ manned by officers and men from H.M. ships ‘Despatch,’ ‘Mantis’ and ‘Scarab,’ proceeded up river from Ichang early on September 4 to obtain the release of the European officers and native crew confined on board the s.s. ‘Wanhsen’ and s.s. ‘Wantung’ and if possible to remove these steamers.

"The s.s. 'Kia'wo' arrived at Wanhsien' on the evening of September 5 and went alongside the 'Wanhsen' but was too heavily fired upon that it was not possible to proceed to s.s. 'Wantung' and she retired after having removed the European officers.

How Rescue Was Planned.
The officers of the s.s. 'Wantung' jumped overboard as prearranged. The captain was picked up by the 'Kiawo,' the chief officer by a French gunboat, but she retired after having removed wounded and is presumed drowned.

“H.M.S. ‘Cockchafer,’ which was at anchor at Wanhien, there-upon bombarded the town, and as far as is known inflicted considerable damage.”

“H.M.S. ‘Widgeon,’ which had arrived from Chungking, also took part.”

“The three ships returned down the river and have arrived at Ichang.”

## [21] FURTHER DETAILS.

*Rugby, Sept. 2.*

Further details received regarding the Naval action at Wanhsien on Upper Yangtse (in which the casualties amounted to five British officers and about dozen other Naval ranks) make it clear that an extremely difficult operation was attempted with great gallantry and a considerable measure of success.

The object of the small Naval forces engaged was to free if possible the British-owned trading vessels "Wanhsen" and "Wantung" which were forcibly detained by General Yan Sen, and to obtain the release of six mercantile officers of these vessels, which were otherwise manned by Chinese crews.
