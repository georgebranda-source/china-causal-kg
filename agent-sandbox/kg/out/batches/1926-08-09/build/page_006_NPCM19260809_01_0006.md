The origin of the Imperial Conference may be traced to the presence in London in 1887 of the Premiers of the self-governing Dominions, representing their nations at the celebrations of the Jubilee of Queen Victoria. Similar gatherings were held in 1897, 1902, 1907, and 1911, and in 1907 the title of "Colonial" Conference was changed to Imperial Conference. The Secretary of State for the Dominions and Colonies has invariably attended the Conference, but naturally Dominion as opposed to Colonial questions have ever been paramount and predominant.

## [17] OBITUARY.

**MISSIONARY WORKER IN CANTON.**

**MRS. M. N. KERR.**

After 53 years of missionary work in China, Mrs. Martha Noyes Kerr, of Canton, passed away at the Matilda Hospital, Hongkong, on Saturday, at the age of 86 years.

Under the American Presbyterian Mission, Mrs. Kerr for some years after her arrival in China, was a teacher and director in the True Light Seminary for Women and Girls, together with her sister, Miss Harriett Noyes.
Twenty-seven years ago, as the wife of Dr. J. G. Kerr, she helped in the foundation of the J. G. Kerr Hospital for the Insane in Canton and although her husband died a few years after its foundation Mrs. Kerr carried on with the work until last year when conditions in Canton made it impossible for her to remain there.
The remains of Mrs. Kerr will be taken to Canton to be laid to rest beside those of her husband and of her brother, the Rev. Henry V. Noyes, D.D. in the “White Cloud” cemetery.

## [19] P.I. AGITATION.

**INDEPENDENCE MOVEMENT SHAKES EAST.**

**BOTH SIDES AIRED.**

Williamstown,
Issachusetts,
Aug. 2.

Widely divergent views on the Philippine question were expressed by eminent international authorities participating in "Philippines day" under the auspices of the Williamstown Institute of Politics.

George H. Blakeslee, professor of history and international relations at Clark University, presided over the meeting in his capacity as leader of discussion on Far Eastern topics. He made an address in which he declared that the Filipinos' insistence on independence threatened grave consequences in the Orient. He said that "harmonious, efficient administration" in the islands was impossible so long as agitation in favour of separation from the United States continued.

## [23] QUAKE SHOCK.

The Manila Bulletin of August reports:—

An earthquake of considerable intensity which shook Maniln yesterday afternoon at 1.03 o'clock had its centre in the Pacific Ocean north of Camarines Sur and Albany, the observatory has discovered. In those two provinces, and in the Catanduanes Islands, the shock was quite strong, but no casualties nor damages have been reported.

## [24] MEXICAN TROUBLE.

**BANKERS URGE CALLES TO END FIGHT.**

**PEOPLE CUT RATION.**

*Mexico City, August 3.*

Ten of the leading bankers in Mexico City called on President Calles to-day and urged immediate steps toward mediation of the government's quarrel with the Catholic church as the only means of preventing a nation-wide financial panic.

They informed the president that millions of pesos were being withdrawn from deposit banks for safekeeping and that, consequently, the financial situation was becoming more acute each day. They estimated that 50 per cent. of the deposits were in the hands of the church or directly controlled by the church.

Against Plebiscite Plan.
The pressure of the economic boycott instituted voluntarily by Catholics as a means of passive resistance against the government's decrees, is beginning to be felt in all commercial lines. Catholics are purchasing only their actual requirements.

Official and public sentiment is apparently against the proposal to hold a plebiscite on the religious question. Discussion of this solution has almost died out.

It is reported in official circles, but not yet confirmed, that an American protestant clergyman was lynched by indignant citizens in Prapuato because he took the stand that the anti-religious decrees did not apply to protestants.

## [25] Moderation is Counselled

<sub>section under [24] MEXICAN TROUBLE.</sub>

Mexico City, Aug. 3.
Counselled by the leaders to keep within the paths of peace and moderation, Mexico's Catholics continued to day to pray for a solution of the problems resulting from the government's religious regulations and the withdrawal of priests from the churches.

The people in many parts of the country are buying only essentials as a protest, although the effects of the economic boycott fostered by the League for the Defence of Religious Liberty is not applicable in Mexico City.
Theatres and bull fights continue to draw the usual crowds.
Influential persons still are seeking to bring about informal exchanges between the Episcopate and the government, believing that an accord is yet possible. The government believes that danger of serious disorders has passed but armed forces are ready for an emergency.

Archbishop Del Rio and the Mexican Episcopate to-day addressed a letter to all Catholics expressing gratification at their attitude which has not been one of anger.
The courts continue to examine and release Catholic priests for failing to register with the municipal authorities.

## [28] PAN ASIATIC FLAG.

**WESTERN IMPFRIALISM SCORED AT CONFERENCE.**

*Nagasaki, August 2.*

The second day's session of the Pan-Asiatic conference was marked by several bitter denunciations of "western imperialism." Speakers promised to work for a Pan-Asiatic flag, a language which might be used as a common vehicle of communication, an "Asiatic university and other innovations based on the idea of "Asiatic unity.

A speaker for the Philippines named Verzosa told the conference that the Pan-Asiatic movement would greatly assist the Filipinos in obtaining independence. The speaker did not express any sentiments which could be construed as an attack on the United States.

The following countries are represented in the meeting by delegations ranging from one to twenty: China (Feking Shanghai, Canton, Dalren, Hankow and other places) India, Japan, the Philippines, Afghanistan, Annam, Turkey, Mongolia, Siam, Malaya.

About 1,000 Hindus held a meeting here last night to pass a resolution supporting the Pan-Asiatic Congress which is being held at Nagasaki. Leaders of the Indian community here delivered eloquent addresses urging the solution of the Indian problem and asking for more liberal laws for their country with a view to bringing about greater autonomy.

## [29] LOOKING AHEAD.

**ISLANDS SHOULD BE AUTONOMOUS.**

Williamstown, Mass., Aug. 3.
A “Philippine Free State” with a dominion status similar to that of the commonwealths of Australia and Canada was recommended as a permanent solution for the Philippine problem by Charles Batchelder, former chief of the forestry division of the department of commerce, in an address before the Institute of Politics.
He believed that the Philippines would achieve “a satisfactory place in the family of nations” on this basis, particularly if the United States should become a member of the league of nations. In this case, he said, the Filipinos would be entitled to a representative in the assembly just as each British dominion is now represented.

For Protectorate, Plan.
He also suggested that American sovereignty in the Philippines should take the form of a protectorate with a Filipino governor and a cabinet entirely responsible to the legislature. Under these circumstances, he pointed out, it would be advisable to create a commission of appeal empowered to arbitrate disagreements between America and Filipino officials. He expressed confidence that the Filipinos would be contented with such an arrangement.

Norbert Lyons of the American chamber of commerce mission to the United States, followed Dr. Batchelder and argued that permanent retention of the islands by the United States was the only way to free America from depended on British rubber, Brazilian coffee and other national monopolies. Separation from the United States, he said, would result in the economic ruin of the islands and possibly, their political ruin.

Judge Santos Heard.
Judge Jose Abad Santos delivered one of the principal addresses on the subject, "International Aspects of the Philippine Question." He said that the Philippines, if granted independence, would accord the United States all rights necessary to safeguard her special interests, in the islands "including a special treaty permitting her to maintain a suitable naval base."

He was confident that the Philippine government, in the event of independence, would be willing to grant compensation for American officials and employees who would be discharged or retired.

“As soon as the Philippines are given independence,” said Judge Santos, “and their new status recognised by the powers, we shall make application for membership in the league of nations. It is fair to assume that the Philippines would receive the same treatment as is already accorded Cuba, Haiti and Irish Free State.”

He urged that the United States government authorise the Philippine legislature to call a convention for the purpose of framing a tentative constitution, guaranteeing religious tolerance and economic security.—United Press
