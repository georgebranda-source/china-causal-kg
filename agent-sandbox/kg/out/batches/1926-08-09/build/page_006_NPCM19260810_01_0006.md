## [12] PIRACY CASE.

**WOMAN IMPRISONED FOR PERJURY.**

**FOURTH MAN GETS FREE.**

Evidence for a conviction was insufficient when a fourth man (three others were recently committed for trial at the Sessions) was charged at the Central Magistracy yesterday with participation in the piracy of a fishing vessel at Lap Sap Mei, off Tai O, on May 29. He was represented by Mr. L. D. Turner.

At the close of the case the wife of one of the former defendants was sentenced to six weeks' imprisonment for committing perjury. The Magistrate remarked that her evidence did not agree with her former statements. She was remanded for a week on bail of $100 prior to serving her sentence in order that she could appear at the Criminal Sessions.

The woman said that whilst she and her husband were on their boat the original third defendant and another man, whom she thought was the present defendant, came on board and threatened them with a revolver and a knife. Witness rowed for some distance and the two men boarded another boat. She saw the two men again about a week later when she was offered a pair of gold bangles, which she eventually accepted in lieu of boat hire.

## [13] THOMPSON MISSION

**STIRS. IRONIC COMMENT FROM NEW YORK.**

New York City, July 31.
The “New York World,” in an editorial published this morning, sarcastically calls Colonel Carmi A. Thompson “an industrious lieutenant.”

“For wasting no time on his official mission,” the World goes on to explain, “we shall have to hand the palm to Thompson. Time has been permitted him for a three week’s survey of an archipelgo of 115,000 square miles and harbouring 3,000,000 people.”

"What has he discovered?" asks the paper, and replies: "First, the Philippines must grow rubber. Second, the Philippines are not ready for self-government. Third, the Filipinos don't really care much about self-government anyway."

The World then recalls the independence resolution adopted by the legislature and the action on the plebiscite bill, taken since the arrival of Colonel Thompson in the Philippines, and concludes: "Demonstrations are one thing and rubber is another. If, thanks to the right sort of laws and thanks to the right sort of capital, the Philippines turn out to be such a next little garden for growing rubber, the United States will never wish to let them go. This, however, is not Colonel Thompson's problem. Thompson is looking for facts and apparently has already found some."—United Press.

## [14] KNIGHT OF GRACE.

H.E. the Governor (Sir Cecil Clementi) having been appointed a Knight of Grace in the Order of St. John, the following may not be out of place:

In the Venerable Order of the Hospital of St. John of Jerusalem, the Sovereign Head and Patron is H.M. The King. The Grand Prior is H.R.H. the Duke of Connaught. The Titular Prior of the Priory for Wales is H.R.H. the Prince of Wales. The Sub Prior is the Earl of Scarborough and the Prelate is the Archbishop of York.

The St. John Ambulance Association and Brigade have their origin in the Order of St. John—the most ancient Order of chivalry—which was in existence at the time of the Crusades.

Early in the eleventh century a hospital existed in Jerusalem to minister to the needs of the Christian pilgrims who came from all parts of Europe to visit the Holy City. When in 1087 the Crusaders besieged Jerusalem, the hospice was in charge of a body of men calling themselves "The Poor Brethren of the Hospital of St. John." It was around this hospital that the Order of St. John developed.

Early in the nineteenth century, the English branch of the Order (which came into existence in 1130, but had been dormant since the Reformation), was revived, and in 1888 the status of the Order was confirmed by Queen Victoria, who granted a new Charter which to-day controls the work of the English Grand Priory and its departments.

Following the advent of the St. John Ambulance Association, there was a great advance in First Aid work, the ambulance instruction classes of the Association being especially popular in the mining areas of Derbyshire and Nottingham. In 1870 the first manual of ambulance instruction was published, and it was found necessary to establish a stores depot at St. John's Gate, from which ambulance appliances and materials could be obtained.

A glance at the ambulance work of the Order to-day might serve to conclude this brief survey of the history of the Order of St. John. Classes of instruction are now held by the Association in all parts of the British Empire, including such places as Bermuda, Fiji Islands, Hongkong, and British Guiana. Over 30,000 certificates are issued annually to successful candidates, in addition to about 30,000 awards to candidates who have maintained their ambulance efficiency. Classes of instruction are also held under the auspices of the Association in connection with the military and police authorities, the railway companies, the mercantile marine and the fishing fleets. Divisions of the St. John Ambulance Brigade have quickly followed the formation of Association classes, and the strength of the Brigade in the British Empire is now nearly 50,000.

## [19] CORRESPONDENCE.

**THE MOTOR CAR "TOUT". NUISANCE.**

Sir, — Your correspondent “Chan” who takes up the cudgels on behalf of “tramp” car drivers would have us believe that the fact that they have their stands “regular or irregular” is sufficient justification for the nuisance they constitute by reason of the excessive activities of the touts.

I do not think that anyone objects to the existence of such cars, having regard to the fact that competition has a levelling effect on charges, but what does constitute a very real ground-for-grievance is the fact that one can never pass the Post Office-C.P.R. corner without being pestered by people who interrupt one's conversation and impede one's progress to the ferry with their continual interrogations as to whether "wanchee motor car."

Having suffered much at the hands of such, I seriously considered hanging notices back and front "No, I don't want a motor car" but having regard to the fact that this would cause more inconvenience than the end perhaps warranted I hit upon the scheme of informing one" persistent youngster that I wanted two motor cars, continuing my progress to my destination in the meantime. As the "lad" rose to the bait and no doubt received a

sengers existed only in mid-air. I have been freed from similar pesterships from that time forward. In fact, I am a marked man. And I glory in it.

Whether many of these touts are for cars whose rightful owners are unaware of the purpose to which the drivers are putting them I do not know. Again, that the existence of "tramp" cars as a means of competition to the cars obtainable at recognised garages does not in itself constitute a nuisance, I am prepared to admit. But everyone knows where to get such a car if he wants one and owners are only defeating their own ends by tolerating "touts" who put everybody against the practice.

"ANTI-TOUT." Hongkong, August 9.

## [20] AGITATORS FLEE.

**AVOIDING CHOLERA AT LUTCHOW.**

**EYEWITNESS'S NOTES.**

A correspondent who has just returned from Luichow's after spending over a week in that district reports:—

The local people were very friendly and practically no anti-foreign or anti-Christian spirit was manifested.

The agitators were either absent or on vacation. Perhaps they left when 80 per cent. of the people of Luichow city fled in terror from the epidemic of cholera that has ravaged the place for the last two months.

Several hundred men, women and children have died from that disease. At one time, on the main street, the only establishments that opened their doors were the Baptist Chapel, a Christian bookshop and one other shop. The epidemic is abating and the inhabitants are slowly returning.

The district is now entirely governed from Canton. Hongkong and Kwong-chow-wan are “under the boycott” ban yet grass mats and bags are allowed to be exported. Otherwise, trade is at a standstill and many people are suffering the pangs of hunger.

Luichow is a district in the southern peninsula of Kwangtung province.

## [21] OUR WATER.

**STORAGE FIGURES SLIGHTLY HIGHER. JULY STATEMENT**

Water supplies in storage in the Colony on July 31 compare favourably with figures of the same date last year. The quantity used has decreased in Hongkong and gone up for "Kowloon. Details" follow, figures being of millions (and decimals) of gallons, except where stated:—

Amount in reservoirs on July 31 was 2,114.73 million gallons against 2,097.00 in 1925. Five out of six reservoirs were level with overflow as against only three last year.

The quantity consumed was 235.17 million gallons. Taking the population at 403,320 the average works out at 18.8 gallon per head per day. Corresponding figures for the same month last year were:—261.56 million gallons; population 393,000; average 21.4 per head per day. Last year there was a constant supply in rider main districts. This year restrictions were put in from July 7 to 18; then owing to the damage by the storm, the supply was seriously disorganised further restrictions continuing till the end of the month.
