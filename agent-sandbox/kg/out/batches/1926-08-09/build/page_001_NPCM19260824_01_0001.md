From Fanton has come vernacular reports of a big free in the harbour on Sunday night not far from the Customs building on the West Bound.

It has been stated definitely that the blaze originated in a lighter which was loaded with oil, the property of the Government and intended for use on the Northern front.

For some reason this oil is supposed to have caught "fire" while waiting transit and the contagion spread in "other directions.

There were big junks near the Hongkong, Canton & Macao. Steam-built Co's wharf, in the eustody of the strike pickets.

Fanned by the breeze of the evening; the flames soon caught the high masts of the junk.

Another vessel, used as a depot ship for the examining shed organised by the Clinton Merchant Labour Bureau, is stated to have also been involved. This was secured near the Tung-On Co.s wharf.
After a fight of about three hours, the fire was extinguished, but the oil lighter is said to have been beyond salvage.

## [16] LAUNCH FOUND?

**NEWS OF "WING SHING" FROM MACAO.**

**WEEK-END PIRACY SEQUEL**

From Macao comes a report that a strange steam-launch, which might answer the description of the "Wing Shing." has been seen in Hongkong waters, going towards the Sanwui district.

On inquiry to-day, the "China Mail" was informed that neither Naval authorities nor the Police had any intelligence.

The "Wing Shing," as reported was forcibly taken away by pirates who attacked week-end fishing parties off Lantau Island on Saturday night.

There is no trace of the crew who, it is surmised, have been made to work the launch until their services are not required.

## [17] BAD BEHAVIOUR. AN ARDUOUS FIGHTING LOT.

Three women were involved in a charge of disorderly conduct at the Central Magistracy this morning.
It was started by the prosecution that the first defendant called on the third and an argument took place in regard to a money loan association. The dispute led to a fight in which they both rolled down a staircase, but they got up and started fighting again.

His Worship remarked that falling down the staircase did not seem to have quenched their ardour.

**ENTERED COAST THIS MORNING.**

The Observatory reports to-day on pressure conditions and the typhoon as under:—

## [29] ARE FRESH NEGOTIATIONS IMMUNANT?

**POSSIBLE MEDIATION.**

London, August 22.
While messages from the coal-fields report that many more miners are working, it appears that Mr. Cook's week-end campaign resulted in some indecision among the miners at Mansfield who had signed on on the local mineowners' terms of a 7½ hours day at approximately pre-strike rates of pay, and a big majority of whom had not yet resumed work. All roads leading to the pits in this district were picketed and many men who started from home intending to work have returned. Meanwhile, inquiries in Government circles show that there is no ground for Mr. Cook's suggestion that negotiations with the Government as a party may occur this week. There appears no probability of any actual negotiations in the near future, though the Government has informed Mr. Cook that one of the Ministers would be prepared to meet him at any time he desires.—Reuter.

The anticipation in South Wales of an early termination of the coal stoppage was reflected on the Cardiff Stock Exchange to day. Collery shares were very firm and in strong demand. All the leaders showed an upward tendency.
At least 6,000 miners have resumed in Nottinghamshire and Derbyshire, whilst 740 more men are working in Warwickshire than on Saturday and 400 more at Cannock Chase.—Reuter.
