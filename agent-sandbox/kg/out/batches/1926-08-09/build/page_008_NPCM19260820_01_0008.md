The Lord Mayor of London has announced that the collections in London on “Alexandra Day” amounted approximately to £48,000, which is £5,000' better than last year.

The "Times's" Madrid Correspondent states that the Board of the Ateneo Club, all except Dr. Maranon, were released after spending a week in the model prison. There are still some 40 political prisoners there. Besides the officers in the military prison.

Washington, August 12.—The Federal Reserve Board announced to-day, after receipt of reports from member banks, "that the prosperity shown by industrial production during the first half of 1926 exceeds any previous corresponding period, including the post-war years of 1919 and 1920. The reports showed a production far in advance of any predictions made earlier in the year.

Fast trips between Singapore and New York are being made these days. The Javanese Prince reached New York from Singapore in 37 days. The Blue Funnel steamer "Calchas," which arrived in Singapore on August 12, was despatched from New York on July 8, via Penang and Port Swettenham, and thus completed the trip from New York to Singapore in 34 days.

When the Norwegian vessel "Daviken" arrived at Singapore from Hoihow, with 1,020 immigrants on board the master reported that two deaths had occurred on the voyage. The ship was inspected by the port health authorities; four cases of cholera were discovered, and all the deck passengers were sent to the quarantine station, where one of the patients subsequently died and a fifth case of cholera occurred.

## [13] *(no headline: column 2)*

Mrs. Ross, the wife of Mr. S. H. Ross, of Messrs. Percy Smith, Seth and Fleming, has given birth to a daughter at the Peak Hospital.

The Rev. and Mrs. Frank T. Woodward, who are residents of Cheung Chau Island, have been congratulated on the birth of a daughter.

Mr. William Nowell, the Assistant-Director of Agriculture in Trinidad, has been promoted to be Director of Agriculture in British Guiana.

Sympathy will be extended to Mrs. and Mrs. R. C. Wilson in the loss of their first child, which died yesterday morning, shortly after birth. The funeral took place yesterday afternoon at Happy Valley.

Professor J. Anderson of the Hongkong University and Dr. Arthur Woo were passengers on the U.S. "Empress of Asia" which left Hongkong yesterday for the North. They will represent Hongkong at the joint conference of the China Medical Association and British Medical Association at Peking.

The death occurred at the General Hospital, Singapore, on August 12 of Captain Frederick James Stack, who was a well-known figure in local shipping circles. The late Captain Stack, who was 66 years of age, came up from Australia twenty years ago and took charge of one of the ships operated by the Ho Hong Steamship Company. Twelve years ago he joined the Straits Steamship Company and was in the Kinta when he retired fifteen months ago on account of ill-health.

Friends of Mr. C. M., McDonald, editor of the "China Mail," will be pleased to learn that he is recovering from the effects of an accident, while on his way Home on leave. He was a passenger on the B.S. "Largo Bay," Entering the Gulf of Aden, the ship was in a heavy sea and Mr. McDonald was thrown so badly that he broke two ribs near the spine. The heat and pain were very trying but recovery was quick and Mr. McDonald on July 15 expected to be able to go ashore at Port Said. He is now in London.

## [14] *(no headline: column 3)*

Tokyo, August 5,—The Home Office announces that 490 labour disputes, involving 36,000 labourers and including 152 strikes, occurred during the first half of this year. This indicated an increase of 340 over the same period of last year.

Rome, August 11.—The authorities to-day refused to issue permits to 630 pilgrims on the way to the fete of Madonna del Sasso in Switzerland. Last week 800 pilgrims also were stopped at the frontier. Switzerland's hotel keepers are getting anxious.

Shanghai August 12.—The price of rice, which has been advancing steadily at the rate of 10 cents a day for the list four days, yesterday took a 40 cent jump and closed with quotations at from $16.80 to $18.80 per picul. A week ago yesterday the price was $15.70 to 17.70 per picul.

H.M.S. "Plover," used as a hulk at Gibraltar, is to be placed on the sale list for breaking up. For many years the "Plover" has been employed on harbour service at Gibraltar. She was one of a class of gunboats launched in 1888 at Penbrooke, and at the beginning of the century was serving in China. Although of only 755 tons, the "Plover" mounted six 4-in. guns.

The Ministry of Finance has handed down a set of regulations governing the examination of the capital of Chinese banks, together with the instructions that no official ratification for the opening of any banks should be given unless a thorough investigation is made as to their capital in accordance with the stipulations. This step is taken as a precaution against unscrupulous merchants organising banks with little capital thus creating disorder in the financial market.

Shelfield police force boasts of a constable, Charles Milner, who has just joined up after taking degrees of Bachelor of Arts and Bachelor of Laws at Cambridge University. He appeared in his first case recently. With other officers he made a raid on a local hotel, where'setting was being carried on. Milner told the Court that the place' had the atmosphere of "betting" when he entered. The licensee was fined £30; a book-maker £50, while fines totalling another £50 were imposed on frequenters.

## [15] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL

Mr. J. Hansell, of the Hongkong and Shanghai Bank, Sungei Putani, has been transferred to Singapore.

Dr. Bridges has now taken charge of the Kedah Government's Garden, which has been started for planting vegetables on Kedah Peak.

A message to Geneva from "Sofia" says the betrothal of King Boris of Bulgaria and Princess Giovanna of Italy is considered an accomplished fact, though it has not yet been officially announced.

Sir Charles K. Mackellar, president of the Bank of New South Wales, and formerly Medical Adviser to the New South Wales Government has died at the age of 82. He devoted his life to the care of the neglected children of the State.

The fall of the roof of the Indian Infantry. Officers' Mess House at Crater, Aden, on June 26, which killed Captain MacG. Spence, also caused injuries to Captain Denyer, who is now in hospital. Two other officers who were present escaped injury.

The executive committee of the board of governors of the League of Red Cross Societies has conferred the title of Honorary Director General upon Sir Claude Hill, formerly Director-General of the League and now Lieutenant-Governor of the Isle of Man.

A duel with swords has been fought at Bois-de-Colombes, just outside Paris, between M. Robert Zevaco, a writer, and M. Suajo-Alvarez, an art critic. After a lively exchange, M. Suajo-Alvarez was wounded in the right shoulder and the seconds stopped the duel, after which the two, adversaries declared themselves reconciled and shook hands.

Rear-Admiral Harry Hesketh Smyth, C.M.G., D.S.O., has died at Sway, Hants, aged 54. He joined H.M.S. "Britannia" in 1885 and became a captain in 1914. During the war he commanded a number of ships, and in 1925 was promoted Rear-Admiral. He was mentioned twice in dispatches, and in 1917 was awarded the C.M.G. and the D.S.O. He was also made an Officer of the Legion of Honour.

Another new case of onterle fever was notified on Thursday. The patient was u Chinese from the city.

A Nationalist resolution requesting the Bavarian Government to make representations to the Reich Government with a view to the re-examination of the Dawes Report has been adopted unanimously by the Bavarian Diet.

Berlin, August 11.—Perhaps the ex-Kaiser is beginning to fear that another vote on the question of confiscating his properties may not turn out well for him, for to-day, his attorney general, suggested "new negotiations". He hinted at a possible compromise in his answer to a letter of the Prussian Premier, Herr Brauns.

The “Observer” understands that the Wembley Exhibition site will be developed as a self-contained industrial unit. Two of the largest buildings are virtually sold to an American motor manufacturing company, which is not Ford's, which will provide housing for thousands of its British employees.

As a sequel to a caning administered by his father, a Tamil school-boy, aged 16, named Raju, attempted to commit suicide by drinking about an ounce of iodine. He was unable to speak and was sent to the hospital. He was charged before Mr. W. J. H. Hughes, the Kuala Lumpur magistrate, and was bound over in $50 for three months to come up for judgment if called upon.

Vice-Admiral Crawford Maclachlan, C.B., has been placed on the retired list, at his own request, in order to facilitate the promotion of junior officers. Vice-Admiral Macluchlan first saw service as a naval cadet of the Agincourt during the Egyptian War of 1882, and was in China during the Boxer outbreak in 1900. He served on the Admiralty Committee on Bertking Accommodation of his Majesty's ships at home ports in 1910. Promoted to flag rank in 1920, he held the post of Rear-Admiral, Reserve Fleet, Rosyth, until the following year, afterwards being appointed Senior Officer, Yangtse. In the late war Admiral Maclachlan was a Grand Fleet Captain in the Royal Oak at the Battle of Jutland.

## [17] *(no headline: column 4)*

Mr. Hilaire Belloc was on his yacht "Norda" when it ran on the rocks near Folkestone. The vessel refloated on the rising tide.

Dr. J. J. W. Tillyard, Professor of Russian at Birmingham University, has been appointed to the Chair of Greek at Cardiff University.

Mr. Herbert Monckton, who died at Maidstone within a few days of his 87th birthday, was town cleric of Maidstone for 34 years, his father having previously held the same office for 37 years.

As a memorial to the late Lord Cheylesmore, the London County Council has consented to an ornamental garden, with fountain, in the Victoria Embankment Gardens, near the Belgian Memorial.

Mr. J. W. Lee, who died in Canton of cholera, is survived by his father, Mr. John Lee, of 1 Ventris Road, a police pensioner who is probably the oldest European in Hongkong. Mr. John Lee arrived in Hongkong in August 1864 (just before the arrival of the late Sir Paul Chuter) joining the Police Force the same year. He is now 83 years of age and lives with his wife in Ventris Road. The late Mr. J. W. Lee was their fifth and youngest child, and the aged couple have survived them all.

Emperors have not generally lost their thrones through humanitarianism, yet it is true that Dom Pedro H. of Brazil was deposed in 1889 chiefly on account of the feeling against him for having brought about the emancipation of the slaves. He had reigned nearly fifty years, having succeeded his father in 1840 at the age of 15. To-day some Brazil republicans remember him with honour, and indeed in 1889 they offered to compensate him for the property which he had held in Brazil as Emperor, which he in turn magnanimously refused. Among his private possessions was a cross composed of twelve large brillants, which apparently passed into the ownership of the family of an English lady of title, who sent it to Christie's. It had evidently been reset, and was valued at a last bid of £2,950.
