## [16] BOLSHEVIST ACTIVITY.

Prominence is given in the Indian papers to a question in the House of Commons on July 12 when Sir William Davison asked what action the Government of India was taking with reference to the Bolshevik activity in Afghanistan where, according to the statement "of Colonel M. Saunders," the Director of Military Intelligence in India, an Afghan Air Force was being formed with Russian pilots and mechanics, and also new aerodromes and new military roads and railways. Commander Kenworthy asked whether this was a proper question seeing that Afghanistan was an independent friendly State which was represented at the Court of St. James. Sir William rejoined that surely the Commons was entitled to enquire with regard to a report by a British official. Earl Winterton replied that Lord Birkenhead was enquiring by whose authority the published statement was made, and added that the Government was carefully watching the situation in Afghanistan.

For the benefit of those of our readers who have not fully appreciated the magnitude of Moscow's machinations and the subtlety of detail underlying her campaign against Britain and ultimately against civilisation as a whole, it may be well to quote from the Rangoon Times some of Gol. Saunders's revelations, which were first given to the public at the Saturday Club in Calcutta. The most significant of the Colonel's

statements may come as a shock to the many who still believe that in cultivating friendly relations with the Soviet the British Empire would be taking the most practical step towards the institution of a permanent world peace. Yet it has been amply proved by the speeches and writings of almost every Bolshevik leader who has given his attention to international affairs that "The Third International has declared war on the British Empire." To quote Zinovieff, "Our first weapon is propaganda and our second, force of arms. The Achilles heel of the British Empire is India." We must therefore make every effort to develop all possible lines of advance leading on India." In a later speech he added: "What we planned for India has come to pass in China, but we have hopes that India may make up leeway in a sudden and unexpected fashion."

In addition the Soviet aims at the absorption of Afghanistan. Large subsidies, mostly in the form of arms and munitions, have been given to the Afghans. The Afghan Air Force, with Russian pilots and mechanics, is being developed. It has its main aerodrome at Kabul, and landing grounds have already been made at Kandahar and Jellalabad besides those on the Termez-Kabul air route. From their present aerodromes, therefore, Russians can bomb the Indian frontier stations. The great barrier of the mountains of the North-West Frontier, so long thought to be impregnable to Russian invasion, has been metaphorically razed to the ground by aerial development and the attack on India has at last become a possibility.

It is a fact; declares Col. Saunders, that the Red Army is a more efficient fighting force than the pre-war Russian Army. It is well equipped with mechanical weapons, and great stress is laid on the use of tanks, armoured cars, aeroplanes, gas and chemical warfare. The Red Army has a large and efficient air force. It is worth noting that, whereas other nations are economising on military expenditure, the Soviet this year is increasing its military budget by fifty per cent.
These are striking disclosures, and it is little cause for wonder that the movement against Bolshevism has taken concrete shape in Shanghai, for instance, and that in Great Britain itself the popular clamour is increasing for the expulsion of the “Red” aliens, whose presence there is a perpetual menace to the whole Empire.

## [21] AJIN OMOTO

**ALLEGED MISUSE OF TRADE CHOP.**

**CHINESE SUMMONED.**

Three Chinese firms were summoned before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy yester-day for alleged infringement of the “Ajinomoto” trade mark, the property of Suzuki and Company, Tokyo, and used by them on an epicurian powder.

Mr. L. R. Andrewes represented the Hachiji Imura firm, of Praya East, the sole agents for the manufacturers and complaints in the summonses.

The case against the Yuen Hop firm was taken first. The defendants, who were represented by Mr. D. McCallum, were charged with selling, or having in their possession for sale, powder bearing a forged trade mark.

A representative of the Japanese firm gave evidence of a verbal agreement whereby sub-agents were supplied with goods at a cheaper rate. He said that investigations were made on instructions received that the firm's labels were being forged and the powder sold in Formosa, Certain illegitimate goods were found in the possession of the defendants, who claimed to be unable to differentiate between the forged and genuine labels. Witness further stated that he had not supplied the defendants with any goods for the past one year, but one tin of genuine powder was found among the forged tins.

The defendants were fined $25, the other two summons being adjourned.

## [24] NOT DULL!

**AMENITIES OF CHINA STATION.**

**WHY THE APPEAL**

It is easy to understand, ob- serves the "Naval and Military Gazette," why the "China Station is so popular with young naval officers.

It seems to promise possibilities of adventure which other foreign stations lack. Only those who know Hongkong can realise what a fine city it really is, despite its depressing climate.

There is good sport to be had on the China Station, although in this respect the East Indies Station probably has a great deal more to offer. But it is to spirit of adventure that China really appeals.

Gun-running and slave-dhowing in the Persian Gulf have shrunk to very negligible dimensions these days, although doubtless always prepared to break out actively again if given the opportunity.

But when a man joins the Yangtze or West River Patrol he knows pretty well that life is not going to be very dull. There is plenty to be done, and plenty to do in looking for it.

## [25] MOTOR OFFENCE

**MISLED BY REMARKS ON LICENCE.**

**EUROPEAN'S DEFENCE.**

Mr. H. E. Gardner was summoned before Mr. J. H. B. Nihill at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday for taking his motor car past another vehicle in a controlled area.

Evidence of the alleged offence was given by Traffic Sergeant Smith, who said that Mr. Gardner driving a motor car passed witness on his right side and also overtook a bus.

Pleading not guilty to the charge, Mr. Gardner denied that he passed the bus. He admitted that he overtook a motor cycle driven by a lady, as she appeared to be unsteady on her machine, and he thought it the safest thing to do was to get ahead. Further, Mr. Gardner pointed out the misleading nature of a section of the traffic regulations printed on the back of licences.

His Worship remarked that he found Mr. Gardner guilty of a technical offence, but in view of his defence he would caution him and bring the point to the notice of the C.S.P.

## [27] AMERICA & RUBBER. EXPERIMENTAL PLANTINGS IN CUBA.

**APPROPRIATIONS BEING MADE.**

O. F. Cokehead, member of the rubber section of the department of agriculture, is an route to Cuba to study experimental rubber plantings that have been made in several Cuban provinces, especially in Oriente, Camaguay and Guantanamo, which are believed best
Atted for rubber cultivation. A government station has been established in Santiago and many successful commercial varieties have been planted and their growth is being carefully studied.

Little interest has hitherto been taken in rubber cultivation in Cuba, but the prospects for continued low price of sugar have led leading planters to consider branching out into other tropical and subtropical products. The nearness to the United States makes the field of particular value to America, where the market would be found. Both the Cuban and American governments have made appropriations for the work, which will be conducted under the immediate supervision of the department of agriculture.
Mr. Cokehead will stop en route in Florida, where about 20 varieties of rubber have been under cultivation for several years, in an effort to find a species that will thrive so far north as the Continental United States.

Secretary of Agriculture Jardine has been conferring with the Cuban minister to Washington during the past week with prospect of organising a Cuban-American rubber corporation. Secretary Jardine says that there are strong hopes that such a company will soon begin operations.—"United Press."

## [28] MEXICAN REVOLT.

**NIPPED IN BUD ON U.S. TERRITORY.**

San Diego, Cal., August 16.
The American authorities have arrested and imprisoned 150 men, mobilised three miles on this side of the Mexican frontier, under command of General Enrique Estrada, Mexican ex-Secretary of War, when on the point of making an insurgent descent on towns across the border.

The captured include Estrada and his staff. The authorities also seized quantities of arms, an armoured truck, two one-ton truck loads of ammunition and lorries for transporting the revolutionaries; also documents giving complete plans of their projected insurrection.—Reuter's American Service.
