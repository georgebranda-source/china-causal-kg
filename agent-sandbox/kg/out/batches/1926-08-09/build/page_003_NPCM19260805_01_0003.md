## [16] SUCH IS FAME.

**MATINEE IDOL'S SUCCESS LOCALLY.**

**SEQUEL TO STRIKE.**

Mr. Sit Kok-seen, the famous Cantonese matinee idol, who is the first "star" to perform in Hongkong since the strike began last year, has been playing to capacity houses this week. The attendance was such that queues lined up each night at the Kau-U Fong theatre and seats were booked up several nights ahead. Many had to be turned away.

When the strike began last year, all the leading troupes returned to Canton. Subsequently shows were given by amateur companies and by smaller troupes who got through or were assembled from other sources.

Mr. Sit (better known as Mr. S. C. Harrey) has been in Shanghai for over a year, where he holds the position of managing director of the Ideal Motion Film Co., He is on a short business trip to Hongkong. Starting as an amateur actor a few years ago, he rose very quickly to the top and his career has been meteoric.

Other "stars" have come to Hongkong, but only to sail for America, where there are several Cantonese companies. The cutting out of the Hongkong and Macao theatres from the itinerary of the Cantonese troupes has meant a fairly big exodus to America, and Malaya. Mr. Sit Kok-seen is the first actually to appear on a Hongkong stage since last June, except for a former colleague who appeared in charity shows here, while awaiting a steamer for Seattle.

## [18] SENSIBLE TYPISTS.

**HAPPIER THAN £10-A-WEEK AMERICANS.**

*Southampton, June 25.*

Mr. Roy S. Wildman, whose copper mills in America employ 35,000 men, dispelled some of the dreams about his country as the workers' utopia of high wages, when interviewed to-day before he sailed for New York.

"Shorthand-typists in America," he said, "earn from £6 to £10 a week, but the cost of living is proportionately high. They are no happier than their less-paid sisters in England, because in their feverish anxiety to maintain the standard of living they set themselves they are able to put only a little money in the bank."

"The craze for dress, which is the result of high wages, attains ridiculous heights. Shorthand-typists earning good money in New York and other great cities arrive at their offices dressed like revue stars. The English office girl's attire is much more sensible than that of the American girl."

Tokyo, July 28.—During a children's Red Cross inauguration ceremony at Sapporo, Hokkaido, yesterday, at which 9,000 were present, about 150 children got sun-stroke. Several, it is reported, are seriously ill.

## [21] JAPANESE-SOVIET RELATIONS.

Moscow, July 24.—In an interview with the press men shortly after his arrival here from Japan, Mr. Victor Kopp, the Soviet Ambassador to Tokyo, who is home on furlough, emphasised the normal development of Japanese-Soviet relations.

In illustration, he pointed to the successful settlement of a number of different questions, specifying the Soviet-Japanese agreement on Saghallen concessions among the successes and also predicting in the very near future the conclusion of a Fishery Convention between the two countries.

Mr. Kopp also insisted on the evidence of development of commercial relations between the Russian Republic and Japan since the installation of the Russian trade delegation at Tokyo and said he had been informed that before long there would be started parleys with representatives of the South Manchuria Railway, the Chinese Eastern Railway and with the Ussuri Railway for the regulation of railway traffic and construction in Manchuria.

## [23] CHOLERA IN SHANGHAI.

The Chinese community of Chapei and Nantao is seriously alarmed at the rapid spread of the cholera epidemic. In a further petition to Dr. V. K. Ting on the subject, the Chinese merchants state that so long as the working classes are allowed to use creek water for cooking and drinking, the epidemic will continue. The petition states that the workers of innumerable cotton factories and silk flatures on the Chinese side of the "Soochow Creek" use water direct from the creek for all domestic purposes, including drinking. The petition adds that the factory management should be compelled to have sanitary wells dug. Dr. Ting has issued further orders in connection with the epidemic, and these, he says, must be obeyed. It is his stated intention to walk through the streets himself during the nights to see that the police obey his instructions and prevent the people from sleeping in the open. The regulations regarding the sale of unclean food must be tightened, and most important of all, corpses must be immediately buried, and not allowed to lie in the homes in accordance with ancient Chinese customs. Dr. Ting regards this particular custom as an especial evil.
