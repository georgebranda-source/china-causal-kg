## [3] SEAMEN'S STRIKE:

**NOW FIZZLING OUT AT HULL.**

**MORE SHIPS GET AWAY.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, Sept. 2.*

In spite of the activity of pickets at Hull to-day the unofficial strike of swamen seems to be fizzling out: 'The "Meville" sailed with a full crew, and the Wilson liner "Albania" secured a crew for the Mediterranean voyage.

There is more extensive picketing at Liverpool where the ex-Minister Shinwell is the organiser for the Marine Workers Union, but no slips have been held up.

The Megantic for Quebec and the "Oedrie" and "Carmanis" for New York are signing on hands slowly.

Shipping officials state that men are pouring in from other ports for the purpose of signing on.

## [5] CHANNEL SERVICES.

Southampton, Sept. 2.
The crews of the Southern Railway Company's cross-Channel steamers from Southampton have given notice of withdrawal.
It is noteworthy that four vessels were due to cross this evening.
London, Sept. 2.
The Southern Railway has warned intending cross-Channel passengers against travelling to Southampton as there is no possibility of any boats sailing to night owing to the strike.

## [6] INQUIRY FOR LASCARB.

According to the "Evening News," the Shipping Office has received inquiries whether Indian crews of Lascars can be supplied to South Africa, but up to the present definite orders have not been received and no Lascars have yet been despatched to South Africa.

*Capetown, Sept. 2*

The feeling is unanimous throughout South Africa that the importation of Lascar crews will be a fatal error calculated to alienate the sympathy both of the Government and of the public.

The critical question at present is whether the strike is to spread to the two maize ships, also to the steamer "Roman Star" due to leave with a record shipment of citrus fruit.

## [7] MAILS FOR HOME:

As the regular mail steamer has been held up, mails for England are being despatched to-morrow by the steamer "Umyoti" manned by a Lascar crew.

'Tha Arab firemen of the steamer 'Rhymney' have decided to strike in sympathy with the men of other vessels.

The Government has received numerous telegrams from all over the country protesting against the employment of Lascars as strikebreakers.

The Premier replying made a statement, intimating that the Government will not allow such a step and pointing out that Lascars as prohibited immigrants will not be allowed to land at South African ports even for the purpose of transhipping to other ports.
NEW ZEALAND POSITION.
Wellington, Sept. 2.
Nine overseas steamers are laid up in New Zealand waters.
The shipowners have intimated that if the men return to the ships by Saturday no punishments will be enforced. The owners emphatically declare that they will not depart from the National Maritime Board's rates.

## [8] FIVE MEN.

**SEAPLANE IN TROPICAL STORM.**

**"WE SHALL CRACK UP."**

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

San Francisco, Sept. 2.
Five men in a disabled seaplane in a rough sea drenched by a tropical storm is the picture evoked by reports of the warships searching in mid-Pacific for seaplane P59, No. 1, one of the two that started to fly to Hawaii. She wirelessed that she would probably be forced to descend at 1.45 p.m. yesterday, 150 knots from the island of Maui, owing to adverse winds and lack of fuel.

“We shall crack up if we come down in this weather,” was almost the last message received from the ‘plane. The commander is John Rogers.

For the first time in the history of aeronautics, a smoke screen was formed to conceal a dirigible. The test was made on the monster aircraft Los Angeles near Washington. This picture shows a Martin bomber just rounding the nose of the dirigible leaving its tail of smoke.

## [10] COAL CRISIS.

**CONSOLIDATING RAILWAYS.**

As a result of a conference between President Coolidge and Senator Watson of India it is understood that the Administration will to amend Eschummin's Railway Law at the coming session of congress so as to force railway consolidation after a period of six years.

It was agreed that approval of the van Sweringen proposal now before the Inter-State Commerce Commission would do much to clear the way for numerous voluntary consolidations.

Senator Watson, interviewed after the conference, stated that if the coal crisis became acute the Government would take decisive action.

## [11] POWER OF THE PRESS

**EMPIRE CONFERENCE AT SYDNEY**

**“GETTING TOGETHER.”**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Sydney, Sept. 2.*

Mr. Stokes, the Lord Mayor, civilally welcoming the Empire press conferees, hoped they would acquire a better understanding and a keener appreciation of Australia's aims and problems as a result of their visit.

Lord Burnham, President of the Empire Press Union, and Percival Phillips, special correspondent of the "Daily Mail," replied on behalf of the United Kingdom delegation.

The former said they had come desirous of uniting and coordinating newspaper energies and capacities for the common good.
Sir Pertival Phillips emphasised that the necessity was even greater than before the war for a fuller appreciation of each other's difficulties and needs and an unwavering spirit of loyalty and comradeship.
Mr. Stokes, replying to the toast of his health, said he belonged to the Labour Party. He emphasised the fact that New South Wales did not want immigrants till the present land system had been adjusted.

## [12] OBITUARY.

It is with regret that the “China Mail” records the death at noon to-day of Mr. Ho Cheuk, assistant compradore to the Hongkong and Shanghai Bank, the seventh son of Mr. and Mrs. Ho Fook, and nephew of Sir Robert Ho Tung.
Mr. Ho Cheuk was taken ill with malignant malarial fever about a week ago and passed away at the early age of 34. Sympathy will go out to his wife and children in their bereavement.

## [13] THE WEATHER.

**GOOD PROSPECTS FOR WEEK-END?**

For the 24 hours ending at noon to-morrow, the weather forecast for Hongkong reads:—East winds, moderate; fine generally.

The Royal Observatory states that the China Sea depression has filled up. Another typhoon, away out in the Pacific, has been reported. Its position at noon was within 120 miles of Lat. 22 N., Long. 130 E. It was moving North-West, towards the Loo, choos, the group of islands to the south of Japan. There is very little possibility of Hongkong being directly affected as it is so far away and on a course for North China.

## [14] LEAGUE COUNCIL

**THIRTY-FIFTH SESSION OPENS.**

**REPORT ON SLAVERY**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

The 35th session of the League of Nations Council assembled privately and drew up the agenda. Subsequently a short public sitting was held and it was decided to inter alia to submit to the forthcoming assembly the report of the temporary slavery committee raised by the New Zealand delegation at the third assembly. It is stated that the Mosul debate will be held tomorrow.

## [15] “HONAM” LEAVES:

**300 PASSENGERS AND 15 TONS OF CARGO.**

**TO ANCHOR OFF SHAMEEN.**

Crowds of Chinese this morning gathered on spots along the Praya adjacent to the Steamboat Company's Canton wharf long before the hour for departure of the s.s. Honam on its trip to Canton. They were still there, gazing, incredulously, when prompt to the minute and after the last bells had ring, the paddes revolved and the steamer left the wharf. This was at 8 a.m.

Total passengers aboard numbered a little over 300, of whom 15 were Europeans. General cargo, consisting chiefly of foodstuffs, amounted to about 15 tons.

The ship is to anchor among the men-of-war off Shameen and will not discharge at the Canton wharf which, of course, is in Chinese territory and where there might be trouble with pickets. Passengers who wish to board or leave must make their own arrangements and Shameen is also to make its own arrangements for taking off the cargo if no coolies are available.
The Honem leaves Canton at 8 a.m. to-morrow morning, arriving back at Hongkong at 3 p.m. It is probable that she will then run regularly, the “China Mail” understands.

## [16] PIOUS HOPES.

**WHEN WILL TRADE START AGAIN?**

**BUSINESS MEN'A VIEWS.**

Chinese business men are evincing great interest in the trip of the a.s. "Homen" to Canton. They think it a happy inspiration to have despatched the vessel and wondered why the matter was not broached before.

Several seen to-day by a "China Mail" reporter; the majority with extensive Canton business interests predicted that to-day would mark a definite stop forward to bring conditions to normal.

General Cheung Kai-shek is not a native of Kwongtung. He is not a politician but only a militarist and it will be advantageous to him to see trade restored. His word will be law. To-day he may be too busy to attend to strikers and labour unions and the "Homam" may be ignored. But if the stand he has taken is any criterion he will soon reduce the power of the guilds. Meanwhile, everybody is so impoverished that the strike pickets will be glad to squeeze cargo and passengers. For a time this will continue till the Hongkong workers have had sufficient opportunity to return. Tien there will be no more power behind the so-called labour politicians and that is the end.
Another was not so optimistic. He thought that intercourse would be winked at but only under substantial payment of bribes so that trade with Hongkong would be under a serious handicap.
