## [14] A Crisis Over.

Everyone who is concerned for the stability of government in Great Britain in the critical times through which the nation is just now passing, will be glad to learn that the Ministerial crisis is over, and that harmony and agreement have been restored in the Cabinet. It is rather a remarkable coincidence that two Churchills, father and son (Lord Randolph, who was Chancellor of the Exchequer in Lord Salisbury's second administration, and Mr. Winston Churchill, who holds that same office in the present Government) should each, when in office, have fallen out with the Lords of the Admiralty, and that in each case a crisis should have been precipitated over proposed naval expenditure. In the earlier case Lord Randolph stood to his guns for economies, and resigned, it being commonly supposed that he expected his resignation to be followed by the unconditional surrender of the Cabinet and his restoration to office on his own terms. The sequel, however, was entirely different. In the recent case Mr. Winston Churchill, possibly mindful of his father's fate, was less intrusigent. He did not get all his own way, but he did not resign. In this case, unlike that in which Lord Randolph Churchill was concerned, his son had the Prime Minister and a majority of Cabinet with him, and the upshot was a compromise over the naval budget which has happily restored Ministerial unity.

Sports Editor Evening Post—While there's a great hullabaloo being raised over the candidate who should face Jack Dempsey in his first re-appearance in the ring, permit me to say a few words in my own behalf as to why I feel that I should receive the preference over the other heavy-weights when the champion steps into the ring in Michigan City.

When Jim Coffey, the Dublin giant, was up in the big money and looked like he would get a shot at the crown, he ran up against the Madden punch and was knocked for a goal. I also knocked out Joe Jeannette, one of the greatest coloured heavyweights that ever entered a ring, in Jersey City.

My record conclusively shows that I will be sure to give Dempsey the fight of his life. I have met, besides the above mentioned warriors, Martin Burke and other big guns, and whipped them all. I feel that I am right in prophesying that should I get the match with Dempsey, I will give the champion a fight he will remember for a long time to come.
I am ready to sign for the match. I hope the public will back me up in my stand that I am entitled to it.
