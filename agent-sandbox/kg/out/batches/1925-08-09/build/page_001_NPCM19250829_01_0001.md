## [10] CANTON QUIET

**RED ATTACK NOT, YET BEGUN.**

**"SPASMOLIC FIRING ONLY"**

More definite news to hand from Canton gives the indication that the so-called grand offensive of the Bolsheviks has yet to materialise. There have been reports of a cross-river attack on Honam but from eyewitnesses on the spot it is gathered that the “expedition” which was sent must have been of little consequence as it attracted hardly any notice.
On inquiry at the office of the Naval authorities, the “China Mail” was informed this morning that. Canton is quiet. It was understood that the party in power were taking steps to remove all opposition and had met with a little resistance. There is no organised fighting to speak of.

## [11] Honam Position.

<sub>section under [10] CANTON QUIET</sub>

Contradictory reports have reached Hongkong since yesterday afternoon. Some say that an offensive against Honam (the island on the south bank of the river) was launched yesterday and that General Li Fook-lam's troops were routed. Over a week ago it was announced in the "China Mail" that General Li had withdrawn his forces from the busier parts of Honam into the surrounding villages, instancing some ancestral halls being used as barracks for the first time since their erection in the city of the Sung Dynasty—several centuries ago. It may be that some Red detachments have landed in Honam to disarm the skeleton corps left behind by General Li but in that event there would hardly be any fighting.

One of the best informed gentlemen in the Colony said to the "China Mail":—People do not seem to have realised that the trouble this time—although the political aspect may be much wider—has, so far, been nothing like the last scrap with the Yunnanese when there were a few determined "actions." Spasmodic firing has been heard; and that is all, but the Whampoa Cadets seemed to have achieved their purpose.

## [12] Strikers Disarmed.

<sub>section under [10] CANTON QUIET</sub>

A presumption that, during the present upheaval, the “strike” movement in Canton has occupied little attention, seems to have been borne out. As is generally known, an exodus of the civil population usually follows a crisis in Canton city. At present there is no direct communication by steamer with either Macao or Hongkong. Nevertheless, in spite of strikers' pickets, quite a number of people have left for minor ports in the river delta and others have departed by the Canton-Kowloon Railway.

In their campaign of disarming everybody the Red, soldiers in Canton have also extended their activities to the strikers. Many of those doing picket duty and individuals carrying weapons have had such forcibly taken from them. In fact, some of the strikers seem to have incurred the suspicions of the Bolshevik leaders in connection with the death of Liu Chung-oi. A rumour is current, says a correspondent, that the Canton authorities suspect a member of the Hongkong-Wuchow (Steamer) Guild, a body previously in Hongkong but closed by the Police.

## [13] Leung in Custody.

<sub>section under [10] CANTON QUIET</sub>

Most of the vernacular papers agree that General Leung Hung-kai, who has about 10,000 anti-Red troops in and around Kong-moon, is in custody, having attended a meeting at Canton when he was overpowered by the guards of the General of the Cadets.

Of the fighting that has taken place, reports the "Kung Seung Yat Po," there was a real action on that part of the second bund in the east of the city where several hundred men, attached to the divisional headquarters of a Kongmoon commander, were disarmed after being surrounded.
At Taishatau, a brigade headquarters was disposed of in the same way.
Ngal Bong-ping's flight to Kongmoon has been confirmed and Hu Han-min is still stated to be in detention at the Whampoa Academy.

## [14] HANDBAG STOLEN. UP-COUNTRY NEWS.

**EUROPEAN LADY ROBBED.**

Another bag-smutching incident has been reported to the Police. The victim was Miss Sutton, a sister at the Government Civil Hospital who was walking in May Road near Magazine Gap at the time. The thief snatched her bag and made off down the hillside, she reports. The bag itself was valued at $2 and its contents were $6.

## [15] STUDENT KILLED

**NEW KOWLOON CYCLING ACCIDENT.**

As a result of losing control of the push bike on which he was riding, a Chinese student of Kwan Mun Ho village was killed yesterday.

The scene of the accident was the step by bill at Kan Pa-Kang lying between Tsun Wan and Shamsupo.
Another student was astride of the machine at the time and he was injured, but not seriously.

## [27] . CAPE DIEMMA.

*Capetown, August 28.*

The day has been one of abortive conferences to settle the strike. Both the authorities and the shipping companies are in a dilemma as there does not appear any local way of dealing with the situation. The strike leaders admit that they signed the agreement in England, but say that they did so in order to get away from the Dominion ports where they have the whiphand to enforce a case-and-the-only way out was to pay the old rate until they reached England.
The release without stain upon the character of two men sentenced at Durban is the only development of the situation.
Stewards of the steamer Ballarat, persuaded the steward of the Arundel Castle to join the strike.
The Premier, Mr. Hertszog, has cabled Mr. Havelock Wilson stating that British seamen are taking action on their own volition and are not incited by anyone in South Africa.
EARLIER, EARLES
Durban, August 28
The shipping strike has assumed serious proportions. The Continued at foot of next Column

crew of the Balmoral Castle have refused to work, and sailing has been postponed indefinitely.

The strike has occurred at a critical period because the ports are congested with steamers waiting to remove an unprecedented maize crop.

Two strikers of the Sandgate Castle who were sentenced to fourteen days' imprisonment, have had their sentences suspended for a fortnight to enable them to reconsider their position and assist in getting the vessel away.

SITUATION IN AUSTRALIA.
Melbourne August 09

There are further indications of the Seamen's strike ending. The Trades Council of Melbourne announces that it was in nowise in charge of the strike which is now in the hands of the British seamen themselves. The liner Chital has arrived and is being loaded with stevedores who intend to work all the British manned vessels.

There was an uproar in the House of Representatives when Mr. Bruce asked leave to introduce a bill to appoint special police in connection with the seamen's strike. Mr. Charlton, a labour leader, vigorously protested against "springing" such a bill in the House.
The Senate after a vigorous debate, had adopted by a vote of 20 to 9 the ministerial motion that the Senate meet to borrow to consider special legislation and posting special police owing to the New South Wales Government's refusal to assist the Commonwealth to carry out the law in regard to possible deportations.

## [28] MACAO'S OPIUM.

**GENEVA COMMITTEE'S DISCUSSIONS.**

**OERMANY AND CHINA.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Cienva, August 28.*

The morning's private sitting of the advisory committee on open dealt with confidential matters connected with the illicit traffic. The German delegate declared that his Government would restrict the export of drugs to China in agreement with the Chinese Government.

The afternoon's public sitting dealt with the situation at Macao, where according to the Portuguese delegated Senator Ferro in the consumption of opium is relatively negligible. The British delegate Sir Malcolm Deleyingne drew attention to certain inaccuracies in the Portuguese report concerning the reports of opium from Macao.

After deciding to hold the annual session in January, the committee adjourned till August 31, when the report will be considered and approved.

## [30] AN UNDHEGROIND RAILWAY.

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, August 28.*

Details have been issued of the £32,000,000 British and American scheme for dealing with the goods traffic of London by means of an underground railway connecting all the principal goods terminals and the London Docks.
The scheme is now being considered by a Committee of representatives of the big railway companies. It is hoped the project will revolutionise the handling of overseas traffic at the Port of London, again attracting the trade lost to the Continent owing to the expense and lack of adequate handling facilities.
