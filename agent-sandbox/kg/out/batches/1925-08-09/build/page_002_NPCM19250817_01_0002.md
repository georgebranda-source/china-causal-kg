## [8] COMMERCE AND FINANCE

**SHANGHAI TRADE.**

### Increase For 1924.

The result of the year's trade at Shanghai came as a surprise to the mercantile community in general, says Mr. L. de Lana. Statistical Secretary of the Maritime Customs, in his 1924 report. Throughout the year there had been a feeling of depression, trade was reported as dull and featureless, profits as unsatisfactory, and yet the Shanghai Customs Returns show, in round numbers, a revenue collection of Hk. Tls. 27,500,000, an increase on the 1923 collection of no less than Hk. Tls. 3,640,000, the bulk of which—Hk. Tls. 3,470,000—was derived from import duties alone. Furthermore the gross value of the import trade was Hk. Tls. 488,000,000, an increase on the preceding year of Hk. Tls. 65,000,000. It should be noted also that the greater portion of the increase in revenues—Hk. Tls. 2,800,000—was reached during the first six months of the year, which represented a period of comparative peace and freedom from political disturbances. Trade during the September and December quarters was much less satisfactory, in consequence of political complication and fighting in and around the port.

To enter into a minute study of the causes which contributed to the expansion more especially of the import trade and to the discrepancy existing between the good results achieved—from the point of view of Customs revenue—and the general fdeling of dissatisfaction prevailing in the mercantile community would be beyond the limits and scope of this report; never-the-less, a brief enumeration of some of the causes which may be taken as an explanation of the situation will not be amiss.

In the first place, it should be remembered that Shanghai is the principal entrepot of China and that not all the trade that comes to it is for the local market; a good share of it is destined from the outset for other trade centres, principally the northern and Yangtze ports. In fact, the total value of foreign imports re-exported to Chinese ports was Hk. Tls. 174,000,000 which, when the value of foreign re-exports to Hongkong and foreign countries—Hk. Tls. 12,700,000—is added, leaves a net value of foreign imports into Shanghai of about 301 million tnels, and increase on the 1923 figures of about 57 millions.

Secondly, German competition, which had been practically extinguished during the European War and for some time afterwards is now reviving: thirdly, the Chinese are said to be doing more direct trade, instead of through the intermediary of foreigners, and several large Chinese firms have their own buyers in Europe and America; and, lastly, the cessation of the anti-Japanese boycott on the Yangtze and at some of the coast ports has enabled Japan to take a more active part in the import trade of China, which, in her case, was favoured also by the low value of the yen.

In spite of the continued depression in shipping and shipbuilding during the years under review the net profits is just over Tls. 100,000 more than the previous year. An interim dividend of 5 per cent. was paid and it is now proposed to pay a final dividend of 9 per cent. and carry forward Tls. 172,444,50. It was considered unnecessary to increase the reserve account which now stands at Tls. 1,250,000 or the depreciation account standing at Tls. 750,000, but it is felt that a somewhat larger carry forward is necessary owing to the present strike, which has now been in force amongst the dock company's Chinese staff for over six weeks. With regard to the land belonging to the company, your directors have come to the conclusion, after very careful consideration, that we have a greater area than required and that the land between the International Dock and Cosmopolitan Dock can best be spared. The Cosmopolitan Dock requires lengthening and repairing and two schemes have been considered:

-Lengthening and repairing the Cosmopolitan Dock, leaving for sale an area of 110 mow between the Dock and International Dock with a river frontage of 1,300 feet.

2.—Making a new Cosmopolitan Dock alongside the International Dock, leaving for sale an area of 140 mow with 1,800 feet river frontage.

Both these plots of land have a deep water frontage, in fact as deep as any in the harbour, and we are now prepared to consider offers for either; plans being obtainable from the manager, Mr. Burns. We are not in any immediate hurry to dispose of this land but the decision as to whether we shall remove or merely repair Cosmopolitan Dock will be dependent, to a certain extent, upon the offers received. If necessary we are prepared to consider part of the purchase price being covered by a mortgage. In order to increase our ability to handle every class of work, improve the quarters for employees, and generally improve the value of your property, we have authorized the expenditure of about Tls. 500,000 spread over a year or 18 months; this will considerably reduce our cash on deposit and in current account, and as we do not consider a return of capital under Tls. 25 per share worth while, the matter of reducing the capital will have to be left in abeyance until the land near the Cosmopolitan Dock is sold. On behalf of the directors, and I feel sure I may say the shareholders as well, I should like to thank Mr. Burns and the staff for the able and loyal manner in which they have carried out their work during the year under review and I trust the bonus and the provident fund, which is at last in proper working order, will be appreciated. In this connection we have decided to appoint Mr. W. S. Burns, who has been with the company 26 years, managing director, a step which I am sure will be appreciated both by our clients and staff. Before closing I consider it advisable to say a few words regarding the strike and prospects of future business. That the former is caused by agitators is beyond question and the fact that we have received numerous letters from Chinese employees who have been with us for 40 or 50 years, stating that they are perfectly satisfied with their pay and the treatment they have received, is sufficient testimony to the fair manner in which the company has dealt with them in the past, and the fact that but for these agitators they would never have ceased work. It is difficult to deal with the prospects for the future but a good deal of work is waiting to be done directly the strike is over and our strong financial position enables us, even at the present time, to look forward to the future with equanimity if not with optimism.
