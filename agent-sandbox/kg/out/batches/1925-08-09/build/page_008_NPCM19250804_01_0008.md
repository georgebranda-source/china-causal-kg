## [9] SECRET SOCIETIES.

**UNDERWORLD HELP FOR STRIKERS.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Shanghai, Aug. 3.*

It is reported that two secret societies, the membership of which is largely of the criminal class, have joined forces with labour agitators in the Chinese city and sworn allegiance to one of the most notorious labour leaders. It is believed that their intention is to blackmail Chinese firms and shops dealing in foreign goods, and Chinese in foreign employ.

A Japanese paper asserts that the Far Eastern Committee of the Third Internationale recently petitioned Vladivostok for 50,000,000 roubles for propaganda purposes in China, which petition was duly granted by the Bureau of Asiatic Propaganda.

MILL WORKERS STABBED.
Five strike breakers from a Japanese mill have been stabbed in a teashop by loafers, who are probably strikers. Two of the injured men have been removed to hospital.

## [10] BRIBERY CASE.

Gregorenko, who has been arraigned before the Mixed Court on a charge of aiding and abetting Dr. Fortunatoff in an attempted act of bribery, was acquitted when the case was resumed to-day.

The Assessor stated "that the Court was satisfied that a prima facie case had been made out against another person and that an attempt at bribery had been made," but there was not sufficient evidence against the accused.

## [14] COAL DISPUTE.

**SIX MONTHS INTERIM CONTRACT**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

Halifax (Nova Scotia), August 3. —With a view to terminate the dispute between the British Empire Steel Corporation and the striking coalminers, which has completely stopped work in the coal industry for the past five months and caused much distress and disorder, the Premier, the Hon. H. N. Rhodes, P.C., has proposed that the parties immediately arrange an interim contract for six months to embrace the 1924 working terms and the 1922 wages. Thid Provincial Government will grant a rebate of one-fifth of its coal royalties for a year, and institute a full enquiry. The Executive Pedy, representing the workers, has already recommended that the Unions should accept the proposals.

## [15] NAVAL TOURISTS.

**AUSTRALIAN WELCOME TO U.S. FLEET.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

The welcome to the United States naval visitors grows even more cordial as the end of the visit approaches. Huge crowds revisited the warships to-day. The crush was so great that fifty cases of fainting and slight injury had to be treated.

## [16] WHERE THEY GO.

**CHINESE EMIGRATION REPORT.**

The Customs authorities have discovered a widespread conspiracy, with head-quarters in Hong Kong and agencies at Australian ports, to smuggle Chinese into Australia. The alleged method is the use of birth certificates of dead Chinese.

## [19] LOCAL & GENERAL

A large gang of coolies is now employed on the sandy wasted of Happy Valley.

Letters from Mr. J. Johnstone. Jr., on board the Empress of Canada, indicate plain and pleasant sailing and plenty of work. The lady Volunteers in particular are especially distinguishing themselves.

Mr. Fu Ping-sheung who continues to write letters “under direction of His Excellency Mr. Wu Hon-man, Minister of Foreign Affairs, the Nationalist Government,” is an old boy of St. Stephen's College, and was almost the first to graduate from the Hongkong University.
