## [8] MACAO SHOTS.

**WHAMPOACADETS' FRESH OUTRAGE.**

**SEVERAL PEOPLE WOUNDED.**

(From Our Own Correspondent.

*Macno, Sept. 21.*

Under great provocation, a Portuguese Government armed launch refrained from returning the fire, yesterday afternoon, of Whampoa Cadets whose shots from Chinese territory recocheted into Macao, wounding several people.

How the incident started has yet to be investigated. Shipping in Macao waters had been interfered with. The armed launch went to investigate in Macao inner harbour, across which is the Chinese island of Lappa, the foreshore being known as Wan Tani

A boatload of pigs left Macao because the trader failed to sell—not accepting the price offered. One side of the water (really the mouth of a river) is the Macao inner harbour and the other side is Chinese water. The pig junk was interfered with as was another native boat carrying cocoanuts. The Portuguese launch investigated and was met by a fusillade from the Cadets garrisoning Wan Tsai. Nobody on the naval vessel was hit. Two or three on the water-front on the Macao side were wounded.

It is believed that both junks fell into the hands of the strikers and soldiers at Wan Tsai.

## [9] A BAD FRIEND.

Robs Hs Fellows

Three young Filipino musicians were the complaints in a case which came for hearing before Mr. R. A. D. Forrest at the Central Magistracy this morning when a fourth named Dometrio Abella appeared in the dock on a charge of larceny.

The evidence showed that all four of them were members of a troupe which had lately carried out a tour in Java with their principal, a lady. It was alleged that during the absence of the complainants from the hotel where they lived the accused went through their luggage and stole 150 pesos. He left the hotel immediately and his whereabouts were not known until the Police were informed and he was found in a boarding house at Yaumati. At the time of his arrest the accused was found to be in possession of a new suit case and to have made arrangements for getting away from the Colony unknown to the others.

The accused attempted to justify his action by stating that the others owed him money, but His Worship was not satisfied with this explanation and imposed sentence of three weeks' hard labour.

## [11] BORDER INCIDENT

**CUTTING OF TELEPHONE**

**CHINESE BOY CAUGHT.**

It will be remembered that during the recent attacks by Chinese soldiers on the border the telephone line connecting the Sheung Shui Police Station with the other Stations and the military pickets was found to be cut on several occasions.

A strong force is always maintained at Sheung-Shui, where the A.S.P. (Mr. Gerrard) also has his headquarters, hence the desire of the Chinese soldiers to isolate that Station and so prevent the rushing up of reinforcements.

During last month the military field telephone line was twice cut, and a strict vigilance on the part of the pickets to catch the perpetrators was rewarded by the arrest of a Chinese boy yesterday as he crossed the border to carry out the desired object.

It is clear that the Chinese soldiers are using small boys, who would naturally carry out the work of cutting the telephone lines with less chance of detection. The arrested boy will be brought before the Magistrate in due course.

## [13] RUBBER HOOM PRESENTS

Reports of the anti-foreign boycott having considerably diminished at Amoy have reached Hongkong. There may still be a little agitation by bodies which have little influence at present but there is, generally, no animosity to any foreign nation.

The Indo-China S. N. Co.'s "Suisang" left Amoy a few days ago, arriving here yesterday, en route for Singapore. She had on board upwards of 500 Chinese deck passengers. According to Chinese reports there were very enthusiastic scenes at Amo' when the emigrants sailed. They were, it is said, each given a small present in commemoration of the end of the anti-British crisis. These emigrants are attracted to Singapore, continues the report, by the boom in rubber.

It is noteworthy that no cargo's mentioned in the "Suisang's" report.

## [19] LOCAL LOAN?

**CHINESE MERCHANTS' PETITION DRAFTED.**

**TO-MORROW'S MEETING.**

The petition of Chinese merchants with regard to proposed measures for the provision of ready cash in order that an effort may be made to meet obligations for the taking up of goods and the transaction of any business which is possible under present conditions has now been finally prepared and is before the Government.

It is understood that the petition does not specify any particular means by which the desired money might be raised, such as the issue of loan bonds or release of bank notes, but leaves it with the Government, in the event of a decision favourable to such loan, to specify measures along which the money should be raised.

A meeting will take place tomorrow between the Government and representatives of the business community to discuss the matter.

## [20] JAPANESE NEUTRALITY.

The Foreign Office has received information that civil war is imminent between Chang Tso-lin and Feng Yu-liang. The cabinet voted unanimously for Japanese neutrality if hostilities break out. It is understood there will be no customs Conference in the event of warfare.—Reuter.

## [22] A STRIKE PLEA

**ADVANCED BY ALLEGED ROBBER.**

As his defence against a charge of complicity in a robbery in Bowrington Canal Road, one of three prisoners appearing at the Criminal Sessions this morning said that he had come from up country and not being able to get shelter, food or work as a result of his friends having left for running. He was invited to a man whom he did not know and who said he would find him work.

The man in question, said the prisoner, took him to a house and went inside. He later came out running and prisoner ran too and was caught by police. His reason for having a bangle on him stolen from the house was, he said, that the man in question had given it to him.

The sister bangle to the one found on prisoner was produced in court by the Chinese lady occupant of the shop robbed.
