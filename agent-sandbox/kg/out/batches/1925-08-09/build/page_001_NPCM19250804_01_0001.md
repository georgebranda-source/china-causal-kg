**SUN YAT-SEN'S HOME TO START?**

Strikers

There have been persistent rumours that Canton intends to embrace Communism. It is now reported that a start is to be made with a "model district."

## [9] TRAINED OFFICIALS.

*(An Occasional Correspondent.)*

News has reached Macao that Canton intends to use the Heungshan districts—just across the border—as a model of Soviet government. By beginning on a small scale, the advocates hope gradually to extend the movement all over the province. Specially trained officials have arrived at Shekki, the district capital, to make a start. It remains to be seen how the idea will be received.
[Heungshan is the birthplace of the late Dr. Sun Yat-sen.]
Java sugar intended for up-country ports has been seized in large quantities by strikers' pickets at Chinshan — opposite Macao, where the Shekki tow-boat calls. The strikers confiscated the sugar on the ground that it is British produce and, trading in such a commodity is violating the boycott order.

Some Japanese torch batteries were also seized. Instead of dumping the batteries in the river, the pickets distributed them among themselves for use to carry out other duties, when they are supposed to be the champions of the boycott movement.
A strict watch is still being kept on boats leaving for Macao but foodstuffs and passengers are still arriving, the latter generally re-shipping for Hongkong.

## [10] EDUCATION BOYCOTT.

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

Making all the money under a pretence of having to feed strikers, labour leaders now suggest appropriating flood relief funds. About $200,000 was left over from the previous flood relief campaign, contributions to which had been made by Hongkong and other ports.

## [11] CANTON UNMASKS.

**OPIUM BOILING AND GAMBLING LICENSED.**

Having started off with what it probably hoped would be looked upon as a gesture signifying its having at heart the true welfare of those at Canton, the Canton Government has been obliged to discard even the pretention of observance of its pious aspirations in regard to the suppression of opium and other evils. It has now granted permission for the resumption of opium boiling and the fees obtained from this source are to be paid into strike funds.

Gambling houses, also, are to be re-opened, and the money obtained from these sources is to be used for the purpose of making the new road from the East Bund to Whampoa, which is "to commemorate the strike."

The hold which the strikers have even in the administration of so-called justice, is apparent from the following incident.—Two launches used for the purpose of towing at Shek Ki have been seized by strikers and used for the purpose of visiting junk and boats. When the owners protested to the Magistrate at Heungshan the latter made an order for the return to them of the launches. The strikers retaliated by sending a telegram to Liu Chung-hoi, whereupon the Magistrate was deprived of his post.

## [12] SWATGW RESTRICTION

Seamen controlling affairs at Swatow have announced that all persons wishing to return to Hongkong must first obtain a permit from strike headquarters, for which they will be charged $20.
Delegates have been sent to Kongmoon to demand the balance deposited with merchants there.
Liu Chung-oi, the Labour Finance Minister, is reported to have proposed drastic financial reforms.

## [13] China

**LOYAL CHINESE.**

**IMPORTANT RESOLUTIONS CARRIED.**

**BOLSHEVISM OPPOSED.**

"That the Chinese merchants resident in the Colony are desirous of expressing their thanks and gratitude to the Governor of Hongkong for his promptness in enforcing the emergency measures for the preservation of peace in the Colony, to which they owe their safety."

That regarding the present strike, we merchants are dead against it, and that in our opinion, the striking labourers themselves are not in

favour; but they have been forced to declare a strike by pressure from outside, and terrorised to leave their employment by intimidation.

That, with regard to the enforcement of Bolshevik measures by the de facto Government in Canton, which are aimed at the disastrous destruction of commerce and serious entanglement of finance as well as cutting off the supply of foodstuff from Canton to Hongkong, as preliminary steps to Sovietise the place under their rule, we merchants are desirous of expressing our strong opposition thereto."

The resolutions were put to the meeting, and passed unanimously. Mr. Ho Kwong, in thanking those present for their attendance said that he would communicate the result of the meeting to the two Chinese Unofficial Members of the Legislative Council, and request them to convey same to the Government, so that the above resolutions may be forwarded to the Home Government by cable.

## [16] RESOLUTIONS APPROVED BY AMOY.

**PROMPT ACTION URGED.**

Strong approval and endorsement of the resolutions passed at the public meeting of residents in Hongkong are signified by the residents of Amoy in a cablegram received this morning by the Hon. Mr. P. H. Holyoak.
A combined meeting of the committee of the Trade Protection Bureau and those of the Co-operative Society of the 24 Commercial Guilds was held at noon to day on the first floor of the City Hall. Mr. Ho Kwong presided. The object of the meeting was to consider; and if thought fit, to pass the following three resolutions:

The cablegram adds that the anti-British boycott, which is causing incalculable loss to British interests, is not supported by the Chinese merchants generally; and the threatened strike is being forced on unwilling employees by direct threats of violence.

The state of terrorism existing in the Chinese city is distinctly traceable to Canton Bolshevik agencies, the message goes on to state, and the residents consider that strong and prompt action is necessary to prevent the situation in the coast ports from becoming hopelessly out of hand.

The residents of Amoy request Mr. Holyoak, as one of the conveners of the meeting in Hongkong, to wire the endorsement and statement of facts to the same quarters in London at their expense.

The cablegram signed by Mr. Hinton, Secretary of the Chamber of Commerce.

Mr. Holyoak informs the "China Mail" that he has passed on the contents of the cablegram to the Government at Home.

## [17] BHANGHAI POSITION.

The time elapsed since the meeting was held in Hongkong is not sufficient for comment, yet to have appeared in Shanghai papers, but the actual resolutions have been favourable commented on.

As regards the general situation in Shanghai, there is a slight improvement in clearances, but the attitude taken up by the Chinese Seamen's Union is delaying a settlement. Even when the boycott is lifted, it is felt among business circles that it will be a long time before conditions are established favourable to free movement of goods inland.

## [19] TYPHOON DAMAGE

**ECHO OF ARBITRATION CASE**

Damage caused to two houses in Kennedy Road by the typhoon of 1923, inspired as a factor in a case opening before the Supreme Court this morning.

Dong Tong, a builder, applied for the setting inside of an award, in respect of the houses, which was in favour of Liam Tuck, to whom he had let out the contract for building.

This contractor, through the damage caused by the typhoon, had been late with the completion of the building, and also had been put to considerable expense, and his bill included this in his list of extras.

It was claimed for the plaintiff that under the agreement entered into the contractor was not entitled to extra money for the typhoon damages Mr. F.C. Jenkins on his behalf, said that the Hon. Mr. H. W. Bird who acted as arbitrator could not have thoroughly read the agreement. The application is for the case to be remitted back to the arbitrator for rehearing or for the Court to decide the issue.
The Hon. Mr. C. G. Alnaster represents the contractor.

## [21] EXPERIMENTAL RUN.

**HAI NING LEAVES TO BRING BACK TEA.**

**POSITION OF DOUGLAS BOATS**

The s.s. Hai Ning (Douglas Steamship Company) leaves this afternoon for Foochow, the intention of the owners being that a cargo of tea should be brought back. Foochow will be the only port of call.

It will be recalled that a similar trip was accomplished a short time ago, but on that occasion Swatow and Amoy were called at and owing to difficulties experienced not all the cargo could be discharged although passengers were landed.

Probably on this occasion, as on the last, loading of tea at Foochow, or rather Pagoda anchorage, will have to be carried out by the crew of the Hai Ning owing to the strike of labour, but in any event there should be guarantee of conveyance of the goods as far as the anchorage as the launches of Messrs. Butterfield and Swire and Jardine's are being manned by Europeans and loyal cews.
Seen this morning by a “China Mail” representative, Mr. H. P. White, principal, Douglas Lapraik & Co., said that the impression that had unaccountably gone abroad that the last run of the Hai Ning was disastrous financially was quite wrong. As regarded future movements of Douglas boats they were only waiting for the cargo; there was no difficulty as regarded crew. To-day's departure was more or less an experimental run, said Mr. White, but the tea season was now in full swing.

A riosha coolie was knocked down at Connaught Road Central yesterday by a motor car driven by Dr. Li Sung. The man sustained injuries to his body and had to be taken to hospital.

## [24] No Amusements.

Everyth.ig grown on the island is wonderfully cheap. But you cannot send round the corner to the butcher's for a chopper a joint. You have to wait until somebody kills a sheep or a bullock. If you object to a few "scratch" meals, St. Helena will not suit you. But you can catch your own fish and grow bananas in your back garden.

There are only two or three shops on the island, and their stock is somewhat primitive and does not encourage large expenditure. A mail comes in once a month, and you collect your own letters at the post office.

No motors are permitted, but if you want to be regarded as a semi-millionaire you can keep a horse or a donkey. You will have to exist without theatres, kinemas, treins, tramways, moving staircases, and wireless, but you will find plenty of tennis and golf and an occasional dance.

St. Helena looks bare and forbidding from the sea, but the interior is pretty though very hilly, and its Napaleonic associations give it an added charm.

And if you want to make money you can grow flax and become a fibre "king."

## [25] SOLDIER JAILED.

**MAGISTRATE'S UNPLASANT DUTY.**

### —

Lance-Corporal Frederick Bayard, bandsman of the East Surrey Regiment, was charged before Mr. S. B. B. McElderry at the Central Magistracy this morning on two counts of theft of seven towels and a pair of shoes from Chinese shops at Queen's Road East at 10.30 p.m. yesterday.

The evidence given by a number of employees of the shops concerned was to the effect that the accused visited the shops along with five others and walked away with the goods. A police whistle was blown by one of the employees, which set Sergeant Goddard and a special consable on the track of the accused. Sergeant Goddard stated in evidence that the pair of shoes were found in the possession of the accused, the lowels being picked up near the place where he was arrested.

The defence put forward by the accused was that he was a victim of mistaken identity. He stated that after leaving the Royal Naval Canteen at 10 p.m. with five others he visited a watchmaker's shop in Queen's Road East, where he purchased a leather strip, and was later arrested on a charge of theft. Two witnesses gave evidence bearing out the accused's story.

Addressing the prisoner His Worship said: I am quite satisfied that the story of the prosecution is true, and I find your guilty of both charges. I understand you possess a good military record. It is a very unpleasant duty to send you to jail, but I have no option. I sentence you to two weeks' hard labour on each charge, the sentences to run concurrently.
