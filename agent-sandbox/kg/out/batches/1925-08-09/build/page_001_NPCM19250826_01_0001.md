## [8] FRONTIER CLASH.

**SHOTS HEARD ACROSS THE BORDER**

**POSITION NOW QUIET**

News received from the border indicate that there has been firing going on at Shumchun in Chinese territory. It is presumed by the British authorities that there had been a skirmish between the Yuet Kwan (native troops) and the Cadets.

The latest report received this morning says that the position is now quiet and no more firing has been heard.

## [9] Canton Troops Disarmed.

<sub>section under [8] FRONTIER CLASH.</sub>

Before this edition went to Press the "Wah Tsz Yat "Po" issued an extra to the effect that General Yeung Kwan-yu, a pro-Chan Kwing-ming, commander on the East River sector, had disarmed some Kuomintang troops this morning.

Those defeated were under Lam She-ngai, who, it is stated, is not in favour at Canton.

Macchi-guns were brought up into action when Lam's men refused to surrender and there were several casualties.

It is not supposed that the action has more than local significance.

## [10] THE MORAL SCOURGE

**SINGAPORE AND VENESEAL JOSEASE.**

110. Positions SERIOUS.

*(Reuter's Service.)*

The Colonial Office's Advisory Committee on Social Hygiene, in its first report, deals with the Draft Ordinance on Veneral disease at Singapore prepared by the Government of the Statuts Settlements for submission to the Legislative Council.

The Committee says the position at Singapore is serious and must be bundled at the earliest moment, especially in view of the approaching enlargement of the Naval Base. It unanimously opnes that the Ordinance does not provide an effective remedy and strongly recommends it should not be passed into law. It recommends the Government of the Strata to take measures to improve social conditions in Singapore, especially as regards the immigration of female with male Chinese; housing, education and recreational facilities, and to appoint a mixed Commission of Europeans, Chinese, and Malays, to enquire into the social and economic conditions of the poor of Singapore, and to organise a medical service at Singapore with a view to the treatment of venereal disease as an integral part of preventive medicine, and prepare to a scheme of free facilities and diagnosis of treatment for the whole population, and frame a policy looking to the early suppression of brothers and an increase in the personnel of the powers of the Chinese Protectors, in order adequately to protect women and girls, not merely Chinese, but those of other races.

The report especially recommends that every effort should be made to enlist sympathy and goodwill of the Chinese community.

## [11] KNOCKED DOWN.

**EUROPEAN INVOLVED IN TWO ACCIDENTS.**

**PORTOUERB LADY INJURID.**

Four persons were injured in a series of motor accidents at Kowloon yesterday evening.

A small Chinese girl, who was alleged to have been crossing the road in front of a motor truck owned by the Lee Hang firm, was knocked down at Shanghai Street. She suffered injuries to the head and had to be taken to the Kwong Wah Hospital.
At Coronation Road an earth coolie sustained bodily injuries by earning in the path of a motor car, which was reported to have been driven by a European.

A report was made later by Mr. Andrew Harper that he was involved in the accident, and he also informed the Police that the Portuguese lady and a child were knocked down by his car. Mr. Harper took the injured persons to Dr. Wong at Homutin, who had them conveyed to the Government Civil Hospital.
This condition of the Portuguese lady is stated to be critical.

## [19] CORRESPONDENCE.

**YESTERDAY'S MEETING.**

(To the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir,—At the mass indignation meeting held at the Theatre Royal on Tuesday evening, when the views of various members of the audience were expressed and received with general approbation, it is felt that Mr. Best, in expressing his opinion, passed certain remarks concerning Americans which, to say the least, were in extremely bad taste and in no manner relevant to the subject under the discussion.
It can only be surmised that Mr. Best's manner of speaking was probably due to the fact that his speech was impromptu. His error was that he stated his personal views of an incident which, though, maybe was of argumentative interest to himself, was no concern of the meeting.

In spite of Mr. Best it is presumed that his statements in no way expressed the view of the meeting and it must be generally felt that an apology is due to the American community here.

In view of the attitude adopted by the Americans in the Colony in the recent chaos and their plainly outapoken views, which confirm their sympathy with the British interests at stake, it will be readily admitted that a totally unwanted indictment has been made against them and it devolves upon Mr. Best to withdraw his statements as speedily as possible.

Although it has been reported that Centon's Strike Executive has asked for the Chinese warships to cut Macao off-from communication—by water; with its hinterland, vessels are still reaching this port.

## [26] OPIUM & DRUGS.

**POINTS FROM PRESENT CONFERENCE**

**NONE FOR MACAO?**

*(Reuter's Serviço.)*

*Geneva, August 25.*

The morning's sitting of the Opium Committee considered the reports of various Governments respecting smuggling.

The problem of the cultivation of coca occasioned a lengthy discussion, Sir John Jordan urging the restriction of cultivation but the Committee felt that the provisions of the recent Convention covered the matter. Sir John Campbell denied that coca was cultivated in India.

The Canadian report showed considerable decrease in the port of drugs.

M. Bourgeois (France), in an important statement, said he was convinced France will ratify the recent Convention. Sir Malcolm Delevingne drew attention to the large quantity of morphine exported from France in 1923 to Greece and Cuba. M. Bourgeois promised to enquire and to take necessary measures.

Sir John Campbell declared that India had stopped the export of opium to Macao-and Persia because of the illegitimate purpose to which the opium had been used. He added that India reserved the right to act thus in all cases where evidence of misuse was produced.

The afternoon's session of the League's Advisory Committee on Opium, which is enquiring into smuggling, disposed of the reports of the various Governments.
In connection with the Indian report, Mr. Hamilton (United States) asked why the Government had not prevented the export from British North Borneo for the Philippines. Sir John Campbell (India) replied that the Indian Government had given the matter careful consideration. The export from North Borneo was trifling compared to the 17,000 tons exported from China. Sir Malcolm Delevingne denied there was extensive smuggling from Borneo to the Philippines.

Sir Malcolm proceeded to scrutinise the Japanese report for 1923 and drew attention to the large quantity of cocaine manufactured, totalling 17,000 kilograms. Dr. Tsurumi (Japan) read a lengthy statement on the policy of the Japanese Government showing that imports and exports of manufactured opium had been strictly controlled and the manufacture restricted to the needs of the country and the external trade for medical use and scientific purposes. Sir John Campbell also drew attention to the large stock of morphine in Japan which is alleged to total upwards of a million ounces.
