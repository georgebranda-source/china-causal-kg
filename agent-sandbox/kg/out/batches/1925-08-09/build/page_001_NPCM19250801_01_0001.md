Mr. Baldwin announced that the mine owners' notices had been suspended for a fortnight and the men had been ordered by the executive to remain at work.

## [7] Obstacles Overcome.

The settlement of the coal crisis followed a series of conferences between the Premier, the parties to the dispute and the Trade Union Congress Committee.

It is understood the discussions in the morning related chiefly to the form and scope of the financial assistance proposed by the Government in order to bridge over the crisis and enable full investigation of the conditions of the industry.

A number of difficulties presented themselves, the main issue being the extent of this assistance. It is understood the owners strongly urged that Government aid should be sufficient to secure the reopening of the pits closed during the recent depression.

_Mr. Cook stated that the miners wages' and conditions would continue the same until the spring or at least nine months during the sitting of the commission of enquiry into the coal industry.
The Trade Union Congress Committee circularised the trade unions announcing that the coal owners and Miners' Federation had accepted the Government proposal and the embargo on the movement of coal had been withdrawn. Work was to be continued by all the unions involved.

## [8] Government's Assistance.

<sub>section under [7] Obstacles Overcome.</sub>

The Government's assistance to the coal mines will take the form of subvention to aid wage for 9 months till May 1, 1926, during which wages are to be paid on the basis of the 1924 agreement, but if any month wages—calculated in accordance with the recognised ratio of division between wages and profits—are anywhere lower than the 1924 minimum the deficiency will be provided by the Exchequer, while any month in which it is estimated the average profit where the subvention is payable exceeds 1s. 5d, a ton the excess will be used in reducing the subvention. The cost involved to the Exchequer cannot at present be forecasted as it must depend on the course of trade.

## [19] LOADED PISTOL.

Two Magistrates sat together, under powers invested in them by a recent “Gazette” announcement, to try an arms case at the Police Court this morning.

Divisional Inspector Blackman prosecuted two Chinese for the possession of a revolver, loaded in four chambers.

Accused pleaded “not guilty.” It is alleged that Sergeant Gough led a picket along the Shaukiwan main road. When near No. 4 bridge, which is about 150 yards from the tram terminus, he saw accused acting suspiciously and challenged them. As the sergeant and a Chinese constable went up, accused are stated to have discarded the revolver which was subsequently recovered by Sergeant Gough.

After evidence had been given and accused had denied possession, Mr. S. B. B. McElderry and Mr. R. A. D. Forrest adjourned the case till 10 a.m. on Wednesday.

Bail of $7,500 was allowed to a Chinese prosecuted at the Central Magistracy this morning by Revenue Officer Beattle for attempting to export saltfish. The case was remanded for a week when important developments are anticipated.
