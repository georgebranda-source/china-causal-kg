**HONGKONG, MONDAY, JULY 5, 1926.**

**COAL CRISIS.**

**EMPLOYERS' NEW WAGE**

**INCREASES. DETAILED.**

*Rugby, July 4.*

The owners' new wage offer, on an eight-hours' day basis, will to-morrow and Tuesday have been posted in all districts.

The first announcements published in the Warwickshire coal fields indicate that in some districts wages will be increased, instead of decreased, as compared with the pre-stoppage rates.

Whereas the owners gave an undertaking to the Prime Minister that the new terms should be guaranteed for three months, the Warwickshire owners make their offer for nine months. Under this offer the wages of a piece worker, when working on a minimum day rate, will be increased from 10/3 to 10/10 per shift and those of a day worker from 8/11 to 9/5.

A number of other classes of men will get a slight increase, whilst the lowest rate that an underground man can earn will remain at 8/6 per shift and that of surface men at 8/.-British Wireless Service.

## [14] RESULT OF DEBATES IN COMMONS.

*Rugby, July 3:*

The debates on the coal dispute which have occupied practically the whole time of the House of Commons during the week have to some extent elucidated a complicated situation. It has been at last made clear that the Government will stand by the Coal Commission's report, and is carrying out measures which it recommended should be placed on the Statutebook; and that so soon as the other parties show willingness to do the same the Government will foster negotiations by every possible means.

As to the Eight-Hours Bill, it is pointed out that this measure does not affect the acceptance of the report one way or the other. It simply removes an impediment to any negotiations involving an alteration in working hours, by making it permissible for the men to agree to work for a longer period. The miners remain as free as they were to make a settlement with or without alteration of hours.—British Wireless.

## [17] SERIOUS FLOODS.

**SITUATION DESPERATE IN MEXICO.**

Mexico City, July 3.
Continued torrential rains are rendering the flood conditions in Mexico most serious. Despatches from Salvatierra describe the situation as desperate. It is reported in the Central Plateau, known as the "granary of Mexico," the crops are virtually ruined, the railway flooded in sections and the permanent way torn up. Many bridges have been destroyed and telegraph lines are down—Reuter.

## [19] AIR PAGEANT.

**200 PLANES TAKE PART.**

Some amazing flying was witnessed at the Royal Air Force pageant at Hendon yesterday by Their Majesties the King and Queen, King Alfonso and Queen Ena, of Spain, and many other members of the Royal Houses, members of the Cabinet, numerous Military and Air Attaches of Foreign Lowers, and by over 100,000 of the public.

Nearly 200 aeroplanes of different designs took part, and gave a seven-hours' exposition of the whole art of flying.

One of the most impressive spectacles was the Royal salute by 54 aeroplanes in close formation and precise alignment, followed by remarkable evolutions with the tips of the wings almost touching.

Several types of machines not hitherto seen in public were shown, including the latest night bomber, and a coastal torpedo plane which carries a 21-inch topedo. besides machine-guns, and has a great speed, together with a Hornbill single-seater fighter of a most aggressive nature, with a 700 horse-power Rolls Royce Condor engine, which climbs with unprecedented rapidity.

The tailless aeroplane and the helicopter also participated in the display, these being part of the experimental equipment of the Air Research Department.

## [20] Radio 'Phone Orders.

<sub>section under [19] AIR PAGEANT.</sub>

The air manœuvres of the fighter squadron were directed by radio telephone and gave ample evidence of the progress of highly important air drill. The words of command from the ground to the squadron leader in the air, and repeated by him to his officers, were heard distinctly by crowds from Marconiphone loud speakers.

The four pilots who recently flew "in Fairey machines" from Cairo to the Cape and back to Britain absolutely to schedule time, covering 56,000 miles without a hitch; were given a warm welcome.

## [21] Fighting Possibilities

<sub>section under [19] AIR PAGEANT.</sub>

The possibilities of the modern fighting machine were displayed by daring dives, loops, and rolls of some of the air officers, and a display of bom's dropping was also given.

There was no accident of any kind in spite of the nature of the feats performed and the presence of so many machines close together. — British Wireless Service.

## [22] CHILDREN AT REHEARSAL

*Rugby, July 3.*

Thousands of schoolboys and girls were invited to attend the final rehearsal at Hendon yesterday of the annual Royal Air Force display, which is being witnessed to-day by King George and Queen Mary, the King and Queen of Spain, Prince Chichibu of Japan, and Air Attaches of numerous nations.

Nearly two hundred machines, including many of new and latest types, are participating in the exhibition, which will be notable for precision of manoeuvre and efficiency of control by wireless telephony system.—British Wireless.

## [25] GREATER CONTROL FOR M. CAILLAUX.

**EXPERTS' REPORT.**

... Paris, July 4...

There is no mention of a capital 'evy' or compulsory mobilisation of securities in the report of the official Committee of experts appointed to formulate a financial programme, which recommends Parliament temporarily to waive certain prerogatives regarding expenditure.

M. Canaux is given a greater degree of control in financial matters, the Committee urging retrenchment of expenditure and the revision of the sugar, drink, and business turnover taxes, and the immediate conclusion of the debt funding agreements with Great Britain and America, after which long-term loans will be negotiated.

The franc will ultimately be stabilised at a figure between the cost of living index and the prevailing market rate of the franc. "Reuter.

## [26] A Striking Passage.

<sub>section under [25] GREATER CONTROL FOR M. CAILLAUX.</sub>

The report of the committee of experts' comprises 54 foolscap pages besides technical appendices.
It emphasises that the recommendations must be taken as a whole.

The most striking passage says that the financial reconstruction is bound to cause a grave economic crisis momentarily. The stabilisation of exchange will involve a rise in home prices. Increased production costs will reduce exports. The bankruptcy of firms artificially enriched by the fall of the franc will ensue, and unemployment is inevitable, but the sooner and the more effectively the situation is faced the shorter will be the crisis.

France must depend primarily on her own efforts to restore the financial situation. The task is not in-surmountable, but it will be long and painful.

The Committee are unanimously
of opinion that the measures they
advise are calculated to ensure
righting the position.

The recommendations include the raising of the Customs duties. The opinion is expressed that the report will strengthen the hand of M. Caillaux.—Reuter.
