## [12] LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS.

Eight members of a lighter crew were remanded at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday on a charge of theft of 16 cases of tin plates imported by the Standard Oil Company. The case arose out of the alleged pilfering of cargo from the s.s. "Speybank."

The Eastern Extension Telegraph Co. notifies that telegrams for Kalgan and places in Mongolia will again be accepted for transmission via Peking; but owing to censorship at Peking all telegrams except Government telegrams will be accepted at sender's risk only.

From 18 July to 18th A fairly large water-spout was witnessed by residents of Julutong village near the shore, which created, particularly among the boatmen there. It occurred close to the shore and lifted a boat moored near the beach out of the sea on to the top of a large tree. The spout lasted for a few minutes and is proceeded towards the south channel.

Washington, July 19.—The Goodetic Coast Survey is planning to send a party to the Hawaiian Islands in December to make important scientific experiments with reference to the action of volcanoes. Edward Brown, who will head the expedition, said that the chief purpose of the experiments will be to determine the force of gravity within a volcanic crater.

Hundreds of prisoners now busy clearing away the ruins of the houses and shops at Padang Pandjang and other places on the West Coast of Sumatra. The number of killed is now estimated at 360 but there are still "bary" bodies buried under the ruins. The total damage on the West Coast is stated to be eight million builders. In Padang Pandjang, a state of siege still exists and nobody is allowed in the streets or rather what is left of the streets, after 9 p.m.

Hendaye, Franco-Spanish Frontier, July 19.—Messages from Marid say that the Spanish government has decided to court certain General Weyler for the part he is alleged to have played in the recent abortive revolutionary movement. General Weyler is said to be living at home but under constant guard. He continues to deny any part in the actual leadership of the "lot" but recent evidence unearthed by government Secret agents is said to indicate that he was one of the instigators.

The economical consequences of the earthquake for Padang Pandjang cannot of course yet be realised, but in any case the town as ceased to exist and there is no possibility of restoration. Once fairly busy inland town it gradually lost its importance with the exception of its value as the crossing point of three lines of the the, Sumatra railways, Padang Pandjang, which in a few years time will only be a memory, must make way for the more healthily situated Fort de Koek, which has for some time threatened to supplant its sister town on the plains.

An attempt to smuggle 12,000 cigarettes of "B.A.T." make into the Colony from Macao was discovered by the police yesterday. The cigarettes were found hidden in a consignment of bamboo brushes on the Macao boat. No arrests have been made.

Mr. Neville of the Chinese Maritime Customs at Holhow (Hainan Island) was fired at by strike pickets on July 15, says a report. He was returning from a visit to the French s.s. Hanoi which was then in Hoihow harbour. Four rounds were heard but nobody was hit.

The first and third of the three prisoners charged at the Criminal Sessions yesterday with robbing a gardener at a hut near Chater's Bungalow, Kowloon, were found guilty by the jury yesterday afternoon and sentenced to two years' imprisonment with hard labour. The second prisoner was discharged.

A profit of $48,369.40 was earned on industrial work done by prisoners at the Singapore criminal prison last year. Assessing the cost of making a pair of uniform trousers at 25 cents (tailoring made a profit of $92,445. Tailoring work is undertaken for the General Hospital and Government Departments (chiefly uniforms for people, postmen, policemen, etc.) and the saying to Government is very considerable. " Netting work at the prison is confined mainly to the making of tennis, cricket and hockey nets, at which long-service prisoners soon become adept; and there is a very big demand for these articles, particularly for the tennis nets. The bulk of the washing for the General Hospital is undertaken at the new electrically driven laundry at the prison.—Extracts from annual report on the prisons of the Colony for 1925.

## [17] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS.

Another favourite reason for the retention of this country is altruism.

Captain and Mrs. Harold E. Potter of Manila passed through the "Empress of Russia" for a short vacation in China and Japan.

Dr. Nicanor G. Teodoro, chief of the plant pests control division of the bureau of agriculture of the Philippines and who is also one of the members of the board of directors of the Mutual Association of the Bureau is seriously ill at the Philippine General Hospital. Mrs. Teodoro is also sick in their residence in Manila.

Mr. and Mrs. Hasselhuhn, of Kimanis, have just completed a tour of the interior from Mejalap to Jesselton by bridle path, via Keningau, Tambunan, Bundu Iuhan and Kotabelud. This makes an ideal holiday for those who have the time and are keen on riding and who appreciate the charm of camp life in British North Borneo.

The Rev. Sir Genille Cave-Browne-Cave, Bart., rector of Londesborough, East Yorkshire, has married Mary Elizabeth Wreghitt, a member of his church choir. The ceremony took place at Londesborough. Sir Genille, who is 56 years of age, was cattle ranching in Western America when he succeeded to the baronetcy. He was known as the "Cowboy Baronet." He was ordained priest in 1920 and has held curacies at Walthamstow and Bromston.

Rajah Mahendra Pratap, the founder of the "Religion of Love" and disciple of a movement for the resuscitation of Asia, arrived in Peking on July 10, from a trip to Tibet, and registered at the Central Hotel. He will leave in a few days on a visit to Japan, where he will be present at the Pan-Asiatic Conference which will be sitting at Nagasaki on August 1. He could not reach Lassa, but was obliged to return from Changdo, a point 500 miles away from Lassa, although he could exchange personal letters with the Dalai Lama.

## [19] *(no headline: column 4)*

The O.S.K. steamer "Manila Maru" left Kobe for Brazil on June 17 at 3 s'clock with 850 emigrants. Members of the Japan-Brazilian Association and 500 pupils of the Suwayama Primary School were at the pier. Twenty members of the Association also left for Brazil on a tour of enquiry into conditions vis-à-vis emigrants from Japan.

Enquiries were received from a number of Chinese in Singapore, especially employees of mercantile firms, for particulars as to how to form and run Thrift and Loan Societies, states the report on the working of Co-operative Societies during 1925, but further, propaganda and education will be required before such societies can be started.

Disquieting. rumours have reached us, says a Penang paper from "Kedah, that rabies had, made its appearance in that State. Our information—we regret its paucity and at the same time make due allowance for possible distortion—is to the effect that the disease has broken out in northern or central Kedah, and is making its way southward. Several planters are seriously perturbed, and are taking precautionary measures regarding their dogs.

The total area of reserved forests in the Straits Settlements is now 114,171 acres, equivalent to 11.5 per cent. The total area of the Colony is becoming extremely doubtful whether, in view of the increasing demand for land for vegetable gardens and other purposes, and of the inferior quality of the Singapore reserves, these reservations are worth the expense of their maintenance. The outturn of timber was 25,016 tons and of fuel and charcoal 28,441 tons, compared with 13,004 and 32,026 tons in 1924.

Urtschuk, July 20.—Forty Bulagian soldiers imprisoned by the Humanian authorities after crossing the international line near Dobrudja were attacked during the night and killed, according to a report that has passed the strict Humanian censorship. It is officially confirmed that several detaichments of Rumanian troops have been sent to patrol the boundary line and the country around Eobrudja. Small frontier encounters between scattered patrols of the two countries are growing in intensity. The official Bulgarian news agency affirms that "life and property of our nationals are unsafe in Dobrudja."

## [20] *(no headline: column 4)*

The first group of Chinese Bishop. ever appointed, will be consecrated by Pope Pius XI in a special ceremony fixed for October. It will be held in St. Peter's.

The reform of the West Hongwanji, one of the two head temples of the Shinshu Sect of Buddhism, has resulted in the retirement of the Rev. Sonyu Ohtani, the acting Superintendent Priest, from his post and giving his place to the Rev. Komyo Ohtani. A sweeping change in the personnel of the government of the Temple has further been effected.

The second annual Old Cheltonian dinner in Singapore was held in Raffles Hotel recently Mr. W. Dunman was in the chair and nine other Old Cheltonians were present. Letters of regret at being unable to attend were received from four others. It is hoped that next year all Old Cheltonians in Malaya will be able to attend, and make the function an even greater success than the last.

An unfortunate incident resulting in the death of Dr. Khun Anusorn Vejasastr, medical officer at Petchaburi, is reported as having occurred on the 3rd inst. at the Chulalongkorn Hospital, Siam. Dr. Khun Anusorn, while asleep, swallowed his artificial teeth. An X ray photograph was taken at the central hospital, and the position of the teeth located. The patient was removed to the Chulalongkorn Hospital, where all efforts to remove the obstruction were unavailing.

A telegram has been received in Singapore from Dr. W. B. Wilson notifying the death of his mother, Datine Wilson, widow of Dato (Dr.) J. P. A. Wilson, at Frinton on Sea on Friday, July 9. Recent letters indicated that Mrs. Wilson was in very poor health, and although, a transfusion of blood was made from her son to herself—the recovery has been apparently very short lived, and the news of her death will be learned with regret by a wide circle of Dr. Wilson's friends.
