## [15] MOTOR CYCLE SMASH

Whilst riding a motor cycle yesterday at Happy Valley, Mr. W. Keegan, of the P.W.D., came into collision with a tram car and sustained considerable injuries which may involve the loss of his right leg.

The scene of the accident (which occurred about noon) was near the Yung Wo Nursing Home where work is progressing in connection with the widening of the canal which leaves little room between the side of the canal and the tram-lines for vehicles travelling in the direction of the Dragon Garage.

Mr. Keegan was riding an A.J.S. solo machine at this point when he met a moving tram car which caught his off side handle bar as he was turning to the left to pass it. Mr. Keegan was thrown off violently striking the tram car whilst it was still moving and sustaining severe injuries.

## [16] GEN. SUN'S PRONOUNCEMENT

**MONROE DOCTRINE IN FIVE PROVINCES.**

General Sun Chuan-fang, military overlord of the five southeastern provinces of Kiangsu, Kiangsi, Anhui, Chekiang and Fukien, is expected shortly to establish an independent principality covering the rich territory under his control. He states that he has introduced into this area of which Shanghai is the largest city, a Monroe doctrine aimed at opposition to any interference—and hints at extending this doctrine over a larger area. Perhaps this is the first step towards fulfilment of an aspiration to rule the whole of China.

General Sun's recent scheme for a Shanghai municipality to coordinate all outlying Chinese municipal units was an example of the ambitious and shrewd nature of his plans. It is now believed that these plans go even farther, and that he intends to fashion an independent domain out of territory he now holds.

Strength for this view, and for the possibility that General Sun's plans allow for acquisition of even greater territory, was furnished by General Sun's statements quoted in the "China Times," a local Chinese newspaper, giving the context of an interview with General Sun by a Japanese reporter.

"China needs uniting," he is quoted as having stated. "I will take the Monroe doctrine which I am now applying to the five southeastern provinces, and will next apply it to the whole of China. If there is a good government in Peking I will do my best to support it, but if it is bad I shall refrain from interfering with it."

He added that he would not oppose the will of the people in any of his plans. "If I carry out my plans I shall not do anything against the mind of the people," he stated, "neither is there any reason to think that China would come to an end thereby." Referring to the alliance between Wu and Chang, General Sun said it was his hope to see them co-operating, but they had to base their alliance on morality and wisdom.

China needs urgent reforms, he went on to say. If China remains standing still it will affect both China and the foreign countries. If the militarians continue their struggle for territory and perpetuate civil war, European influence will step in and the result, thinks General Sun, will be Bolshevik development, and a Bolsheviks China would be a menace to the world. Sun predicted that within ten years the yellow people will be extinguished if China comes under the control of European nations. That is why he introduced the Monroe doctrine into the five provinces under his control.

Much importance is attached to this statement in Chinese circles, where it is construed as a feeler to prepare the ground for what General Sun eventually intends to do. The Chinese newspapers reported that his has already seized the Salt, Gabelle in the territory within his control in order to finance his new scheme.

## [17] AIM OF EUGENICS.

In a lecture on "Eugenics, Love, and Marriage" read recently before the southplace Ethical Society, Dr. Bernard Hollander said that the aim of eugenics was to breed from good stock and to prevent the multiplication of the defective, diseased, and biologically unfit. Not that human beings could be mated as were animals, for animals were perfect at birth, whereas human beings required training till they gained independence, and were meanwhile subject to all sorts of influences.

"Animals are bred for points." Dr. Hollander said, "but if we bred men the same, say, for intellect, we should probably stunt the physique or the moral nature, and if we aim-ed at general culture we should probably end by uniformity of a level mediocrity. Moreover, the most intellectual men and women are not the most fertile. Another reason why farm-yard methods are not applicable to human beings is that marriage is not a question of the head only, but also of the heart. Eugenics is, therefore, not intended to be a cold-blooded selection of partners by some outside scientific authority; it is intended to foster a growing conviction that children are not to be the mere result of chance, but are to be dependent on a wise and sane choice of a mate.

"It would be well if medical certificates were required as to the suitability of partners, to prevent persons tainted with disease from marrying. Advice might also be given as to the psychological qualifications of a prospective husband or wife, though in many cases this would be disregarded, for love is still the principal guide to marriage, and rightly so. Those who advocate a higher and more scientific conscience in matters of mating are by no means plotting against love, but against the influences that do violence to love — against reckless and thoughtless matrimony, and that based on fictitious value of wealth and worldly position."

## [18] EFFECTS OF PHILANTHROPY.

"So far as children are concerned, the object of eugenic teaching is to prevent in them any mental, moral, or physical obliquity. Unfortunately it is the sound people that limit most the number of their offspring, while the defective and unfit exercise no forethought or restraint. The capitalist limits his family because he does not want his property divided up; the professional man because for social reasons he wishes to give his children a first-rate education; the poor clerk and his like because he can hardly make the two ends meet, even without a family; and the skilled worker because often his wife has to go out and earn wages in addition to his own. ## [20] MUKDEN TO WAIT AND SEE.

**GRACEFUL RETREAT SOUGHT FOR DR. YEN.**

Peking, May 20.—According to a high personage who has just returned to the capital from Mukden, in view of his repeated declarations, it is impossible for Marshall Chang Tso-lin to abandon his former stand, by supporting, the Regency-Cabinet of Dr. Yen, which means supporting the Tsao Kun Constitution indirectly. The Mukden party will, however, remain reticent, and will not make any recommendation in regard to the Cabinet problem, but if the Chill' leaders continue to insist on monopolizing political power in disregard of others, Marshal Chang will finally be forced to withdraw the Fengtien troops to Lanchow and sever his connection with political affairs inside the Great Wall. This passive attitude on the part of Mukden is evidently responsible for the political uneasiness now prevailing.

It is understood that mediators are again active in an effort to reconcile the differences and bring about a compromise between the two parties, by finding a loophole through which Dr. Yen and the Chihli leaders may retreat gracefully. The elder statesmen have been suggested as the transitional party on whom the solution of the present crisis may rest and endeavours have been made to secure the approval of these elders before concrete plans are announced. To remove the dissatisfaction of Mukden it is pointed out that there is not the slightest intention on the part of Marshal Wu to monopolise political powers, as may be proved by the exclusion of all close adherents of the Chihli party, such as Kao En-hung, Chang Ying-hun, Sun Tan-lin, etc., from the Cabinet, and by Dr. Yen's declaration of his willingness to retire in favour of a more acceptable candidate.
