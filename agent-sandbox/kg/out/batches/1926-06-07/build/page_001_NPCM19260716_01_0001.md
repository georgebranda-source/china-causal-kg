## [10] THOUGHT FEELING.

**REMARKABLE POWERS OF PROF. LANGSNER.**

**A MONGKONG VISITOR.**

His Long Distance Telepathy Achievement.

Professor Maximlhen Langsner, a Pole who has devoted his life to the study of telepathy ("thought feeling," as he prefers to call it and hypniotism is at present in Hongkong in the course of a world tour.

The practice adopted by Prof. Langsner is to make a prolonged stay in each country, for the purpose of carrying out observations which will enable him to perfect his art. He realises that this cannot be accomplished in the space of a few weeks such as might enable a novelist to write a passably good novel with certain local colour. His study of the psychology of the peoples of the various countries included in his itinerary is extended over a considerable period. He has been on his travels for some years and intends to devote another two or three to life in China and Japan. The countries he has visited in the course of the present tour are Greece, Egypt, Persia, India or Indo-China.

Prof. Tangsner has given demonstrations in telepathy in some of the places he has visited. Such demonstrations are not, however, the main purpose of his visit. He does not wish to pose as a charlatan and it is only if there is a genuine desire among the people with whom he is staying for a demonstration in telepathy and hypnotism that he would give them such an opportunity of appreciating its possibilities. He emphasises that his feats are not illusions, in fact he prefers to have a sceptical audience.

If it should be practicable for a local demonstration to be given, Hongkong people will have the opportunity of witnessing the remarkable powers which Prof. Langsner undoubtedly possesses. Ino Indo-China, according to the Press, "this claim to read the mind of another was genuinely proved. It was the most convincing demonstration of telepathy yet given to a theatre audience."

Similar appreciation is reported from the other places he has visited, glowing accounts being given of his almost incredible demonstrations, sceptics who challenged him being reported as entirely convinced of the genuineness of his claims and subsequently becoming his friends and advocates. In Calcutta, where he was challenged to make a long "distance" telepathic communication, he detailed what was happening in certain streets in Paris and London, events which were confirmed by the manager of Reuters in Calcutta.
In Cairo, before a packed audience five lions placed in an arena were mesmerised and, in the words of one of the newspapers, "became as quiet as the docile cats of Angora." Whilst in Persia, the Professor was awarded the order of the "Sun and Lion" by the Sha and presented with a pair of Persian carpets. ## [13] PUPILS' WORK.

**PARENTS AT VICTORIA BRITISH.**

**TO-DAY'S GATHERING.**

The end of the term at Victoria British School (East Point) was made the occasion of an open day, parents and friends visiting the premises this morning and taking advantage of the opportunity of seeing the work done by the young pupils during the term and of observing the excellent system on which the school is run.

The basis on which the methods of instruction are founded is that the pupils should actively co-operate with the teachers as much as possible. In this particular, the representation of events which are under study by means of tableaux constructed by the pupils themselves is worthy of mention. The pupils are encouraged to cut out from picture books the several ingredients to the scenes, the idea being that, pictorially represented, they convey a deeper meaning than mere words can do. The story of "Hiawatha" is under study at the moment and the students have helped to construct the actual scenes, with wigwam, Indians and all complete.

Specimens of the work of the scholars in needlework, designing, stencilling, etc., were on display, also some of the examination papers. These were the object of much interest by the parents and friends who were welcomed by Mrs. E. M. Clark, Headmistress, and conducted over the premises by the staff and pupils.

Mrs. Clark, in welcoming those present, thanked the parents for their co-operation in the conduct of the school and the training of the children and expressed the hope that they would continue working together to the "same ends." Mrs. Clark also mentioned that she was always willing to cooperate in courses of special training for pupils particularly gifted along certain lines.

A very pleasing programme of dances was then gone through, the rhythm and charm of movement of the performers greatly pleasing the audience:

The younger scholars gave "The Pipes of Pan" to the accompaniment of the gramophone and the elder pupils gave "Sleep Fairies" and "Bubbles" to the pianoforte accompaniment of Miss A. E. Hendry, in charge of classes 6 and 7.

## [22] BANK CRISIS.

**MORE DOORS CLOSED IN GEORGIA.**

**REVIEW OF POSITION**

The Georgia State Banking Department announces that fifteen more Georgian Banks closed their doors to-day.

A temporary receivership had already been appointed for the Bankers Trust Company of Atlanta, whilst the Federal Judge heard a petition in bankruptcy proceedings to be brought against it by the Bank at Unatilla, Florida, which preferred certain charges. The State Banking Department, however, is of opinion that the situation is not so critical as it seems. The Banks concerned, mostly with a small capital of $20,000, are solvent. Their closing is in no way due to local conditions but to the embarrassment of the parent concern—the Bankers Trust Company.

According to experts the situation is a reflection of the recent failure in Florida caused directly or indirectly by the deflation of the land boom.—Reuter.

## [24] KIDNAPPING CASE

**VILLAGE BOY BROUGHT TO HONGKONG.**

**DEFENDANT CAUTIONED.**

Further evidence of a railway porter from Taipo and a woman occupier of a house at Second Street was called at the Central Magistracy this morning if the case in which a Chinese woman was charged with alleged kidnapping of an eleven-year old boy. Mr. E. S. C. Brooks appeared for the defence.

After hearing evidence, Mr. Lindsell considered that the charge could hardly be maintained, as the defendant had made no secret that the boy accompanied her on the train and in fact had told the principal tenant about it. He held that the boy accompanied the woman of his own accord and he did not believe the story that he was dragged into the train.

The defendant was discharged with a warning and the boy's brother, who was present in court, was ordered to look after him in future.
