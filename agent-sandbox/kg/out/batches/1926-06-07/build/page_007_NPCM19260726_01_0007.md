## [1] REPLY TO CANTON

**AMERICA AND TARIFF CONFERENCE.**

**STRAIGHT SPEAKING.**

*Peking, July 24.*

The following is the reply which the American Legation is instructing the American Consul-General in Canton to make to the communication from Mr. Eugene Chen addressed to him on July 14:

"The American Minister has read with much interest your note of July 14 addressed to me respecting the resumption of the special Turiff Conference in Peking, which I brought to his attention as requested."

"In this relation Mr. Macmurny has stated that your strong opposition to the resumption of the Conference—on—behalf of the Canton regime, as well as similar protests from representatives of other regions in China both before and since the inauguration of the Conference, are evidence of the disheartening lack of unanimity among the Chinese people in respect of the efforts of the Government of the United States jointly with other friendly Powers concerned to carry out its purpose of bringing into effect certain readjustments of its Treaty relations with China—a lack of unanimity which gives him serious concern, particularly at a moment when "there exists no Central Government supported by all sections in China and recognised by the interested Powers, with which to deal on the basis of mutuality of responsibilities such as my Government so earnestly desires to see established."

"Title American Minister believes that it is securely necessary to observe that in any fiscal and other readjustments of Treaty relationships with China, the object which his Government has in view, is to benefit China as a whole and not any individual military or political faction."

“In conclusion, Mr. Macmurray expresses his appreciation of the value of receiving information from various sections in China such as that afforded by your note of July 14 in regard to questions of mutual concern to that country as a whole and the United States.”—Reuter.

## [8] MONGOLIAN ARMY.

**NEW RUSSIAN MILITARY CONVENTION.**

A report from Riga, received by way of Reval, states that a military convention has been concluded between the Soviet Government and the Government of Mongolia. By virtue of the convention the Government of Mongolia has to increase the Mongolian army. The promulgation of a special law is contemplated, governing army reorganisation. This law, it is stated, will include an Article to regulate the enlistment of citizens of the Soviet Union into the Mongolian army. The Soviet Government undertakes to supply 2,000,000 rifles to Mongolia as well as certain quantities of ammunition and other army equipment.

## [12] AGAINST FIRE. NEW EXTINGUISHER IN HONGKONG.

**WHAT IT ALL MEANS.**

A new type of Fire extinguisher is being placed in the Hongkong market by Mr. John Smith who is the local agent of the instrument.

Mr. Smith is at present staying at the Station Hotel, Kowloon, but he will be opening very soon an office at China Building in a few days.

The new fire extinguisher will be known in the market as the "Automatic Carbonic Acid Dry Fire Extinguisher.Total."

It is claimed that the total apparatus is the only existing fire extinguisher which by means of Carbonic Acid under high pressure expels a dry fire-suffocating powder, automatically upon the burning objects, thus extinguishing any kind of incipient fires.

The effect of the Total Apparatus is attributable to the driving out on the burning object of dry extinguishing Powder (Bi-Carbonate of Soda) mixed with gaseous Carbonic Acid under high pressure and in shape of a strong jet. This is done by simply turning the wheel of the Valve. The mixture of powder-and Carbonic Acid having a specific gravity higher than the Air, covers completely and securely the burning object. The Powder (Bi-Carbonate of Soda), in contact with the heat, releases further quantities of Carbonic Acid and the cloud of Powder and Carbonic Acid cuts off from the fire the atmospheric Oxygen, thus killing with unfailing certainty, and within a few seconds, any incipient fire.

The device consists of a small container for the "Carbonic Acid" made of seamless steel and tested according to the regulations of the government. The weight "Empty" and "Filled" is marked on each container. The valve is made of pressed Bronze and is of the most reliable construction.

The other part is the Powder Container, made of welded sheet-iron and tested to the double working pressure. It contains from 12 to 18 lbs Powder.

This Powder Container is built in a simple pipe-line which c�tributes the incoming Carbonic Acid, mixes it with the Powder and forces it out under a pressure cf from 30 to 50 lbs.

## [13] GRAVES DESTROYED.

**DISASTROUS EFFECTS OF THE FLOOD.**

**CEMETERY DESPOILED.**

Residents of a few years' standing in the 'Colony will look back on the recent flood and typhoon with varying feelings. Some thought about bigger blows they have been through and others about wash-outs, but few realise that in a very small area where there was no aid, a real tragedy occurred.

The small drain which normally takes the water from the Stubbs Road level down through the Protestant Cemetery to the nullah passing the Racecourse became blocked by boulders which rolled down the hill like ninepina.

Unfortunately the "drain" was unable to cope with the torrent which flowed down from the hills and burst above the Civil Service section (lot 18), burying the lower level in anything up to six feet of silt and breaching the Cemetery wall in several places.

Boulders,.some of them quite half a ton in weight, did a lot of damage, among the head stones, smashing some to pieces and carrying others away with the flood. It is almost impossible to identify some graves, and a yawning gap, several feet deep, which contained about a dozen graves, is all that is left of a portion of this section. Headstones on these graves were completely washed away. Parts of others which were broken lie in disorder everywhere—some in the pit dug by the torrent.

Altogether about 40 graves in this section were destroyed by the flood.

Strenuous efforts were made by a representative of this paper to obtain a plan of this section so that a full list of the graves damaged or washed away could be given. This was not possible, as, strange to gay, the responsible official, on being approached in the

matter, declined to render the slightest assistance.

Damage in a lesser degree was done at the Roman Catholic Cemetery by the storm. A roadway in the left central area and a few adjoining "graves fell" in, whilst a number of wooden crosses were uprooted or placed out of position.
