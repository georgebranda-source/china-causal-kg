## [1] GENEVA "SCENE."

**ASSAILANT ARRESTED.**

*(Router's Service.)*

*Geneva, June 10.*

Count Bethlen, the Hungarian Premier, was the victim of an assault by a Hungarian who burst into the room where the Lengua holds session, and sharply slapped Count Bethlen's face, shouting "That's on behalf of the Hungarian nation."

The Italian delegate rescued Count Bethlen from his assailant, who was arrested.

Bethlen's assailant, who is named Dejusth, is the Secretary of the Hungarian Republican Party, and obtained a ticket of admission as a journalist. He declared when arrested he "wanted to build a criminal."

A later message from Geneva says that Count Bethlen was attacked while attending a private committer dealing with the question of ending the financial control of Hungary. The assailant was a Hungarian Republican.

Count Bethtlen's hotel yesterday was guarded by detectives at his request.

It is noteworthy that while the League Council yesterday agreed that the financial control of Austria should terminate at the end of June, it is still considering the question of ending the financial

control of Hungary, although Sir Austen Chamberlain, on Tuesday, stated that Mr. Jeremiah Smith, the League's Commissioner-General, considered his task ended.

There has been an extraordinary anti-Hungarian outburst in the French Press arising out of the proceedings at Geneva, particularly on account of the letter M. Briand sent to the League Secretariat recommending that the international convention suppress the currency of forgeries. Although M. Briand did not mention Hungary, his letter is described by French newspapers at a rebuff to Court Bethlen, "and his band of Pretorians and country gentlemen ruling by, terror."

Some French newspapers attack Britain for her "partial" attitude towards Hungary.

Hungary's Finances.

The League Council adopted the report of the Committee on the financial reconstruction of Hungary recommending the cessation of the functions of Mr. Jeremiah Smith from July 1.

M. Dubois, Chairman of the Committee, pointed out that Mr. Smith's departure from Hungary did not mean the end of League control in respect of the balance of the Reconstruction Loan, amounting to 83,000,000 gold crowns, and the revenues assigned to the service of the loan, but the abolition of control of the Budget.

Count Bethlen expressed the gratitude of the Hungarian people for the effort resulting in successful financial reconstruction in the short space of two years and a half.

## [5] COAL CRISIS.

**WHY RUSSIAN GOVERNMENT SENT MONEY.**

**FOREIGN OFFICE SATISFIED.**

*(Router's Service.)*

*London, June 10:*

In the House of Commons, in reply to questions, Sir W. Joyson-Hicks, Secretary of State for Home Affairs, said that the Foreign Office was satisfied that the Russian Government had sent money to Great Britain for the purposes of the general strike (Ministerial cheers).

Mr. Ramsay MacDonald, Labour Leader, pointed out that the strike leaders had refused that money.

Sir W. Joyson-Hicks continued that money was sent to the Miners' Federation by some very closely co-related bodies in Russia comprising the Soviet Government, the Third International, and other organisations, though he had not yet established that the Soviet Government itself had directly sent the money.

## [6] RAILWAY SMASH.

**TERRIFYING SPECTACLE AT CLAREMONT.**

**SPLENDID RESCUE WORK**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Capetown, June 10.*

The train involved in the railway disaster was the express from Capetown to Claremont conveying citizens to their suburban homes at the close of the day's work.

When the coupling of the train broke on the bridge at the entrance to Salt River the rear part became derailed and jolted its way into the station, presenting a terrifying spectacle.

Two coaches were caught on the pillars of the bridge and the compartments were smashed to splinters.

Many workmen on the platform immediately rendered first aid, amid scenes of terror, pending the arrival of expert help. Splendid rescue work was done, and the injured passengers were rapidly hurried to three hospitals. All except one are doing well.

## [8] FRENCH FINANCE.

**MINISTER OPTIMISTIC.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Paris, June 10.*

Heard by the Finance Committee of the Senate, M. Peret stated he had decided to avoid inflation and hoped to balance the Budget for 1927 without new taxes. The Government have drafted a plan, in defence of the franc, and the gold reserve at the Banque de France remains, and will remain intact.

The Minister recommended the restoration of confidence by political appeasement, and fiscal prudence would remove in time restrictions against the export of capital.

## [13] TOOLS, NOT CARGO.

The licensee of a native passenger craft pleaded "not guilty" at the Marine Court this morning, to a charge of using the boat for carrying other than passengers' effects. The defence was that the "cargo"—a quantity of planks and ropes—were "tools" used by painters who had worked on board the s.s. "Mausang" and the workmen were returning to shore with their implements.

Another boat-owner was fined $5 for a similar offence, which was admitted.

## [16] HAWAIIAN JUDGE.

*(Router's American Service.)*

The death has occurred of Judge Sanford Ballard Dole.

Sanford Ballard Dole.
[Judge Dole was born in the Hawaiian Islands," on April 23, 1844; his parents being American missionaries. He was educated at Oahu College, Hawaii, and Williams College, Massachusetts. He studied law in Boston, and was admitted to the Bar there. He engaged in practice in Honolulu, and was a member of the Legislature in 1884 and 1886; and was a leader in the reform movement of 1887. He was appointed Judge Supreme Court, 1887-9; placed at the head of the provisional government in 1893; becoming President of the Republic of Hawaii in 1894 holding office till 1900. When President Cleveland, in December, 1893, through Minister Willis, demanded that he should relinquish to Queen Lillie Kalani her constitutional authority, he replied, denying Cleveland's right to interfere. He was a strong advocate of the annexation of Hawaii to America, and in January, 1898, visited America in that behalf. He was Governor of Hawaiian Territory from 1900-1903; District Judge, District of Hawaiian Territory from 1903-15; and appointed by President McKinley-a-member of commission to recommend to Congress legislation concerning the Hawaiian Islands.

## [18] NEW SHANGHAI COUNTRY HOSPITAL

The grounds and buildings of the new Country Hospital, 17 Great Western Road, said to be the finest hospital of the Far East, with the possible exception of the Rockefeller Foundation in Peking, will be open for inspection of the public on Saturday Sunday and Monday, June 12, 13, and 14, from 10 a.m. to noon and 3 p.m. to 5 p.m.

The hospital will care for 125 patients but the exact date for the admission of the first patient has not yet been determined. Funds to erect the hospital were made to the Shanghai Municipal Council by an anonymous donor and the hospital is estimated to be worth about Tls. 1,500,000.

In addition, the donor has given the interest on £40,000 for certain wards and will give Tls. 10,000 for running expenses the day the hospital opens.

## [20] AMERICAN PAPER CURRENCY MADE SMALLER.

Washington, June 2.—The size of American paper currency will soon be reduced to the same dimensions as the peso bills now in use in the Philippines, according to a high-official—of the Treasury-Department. It is learned that Secretary Mellon is seriously considering a proposal of this nature.

William A. Dupuy, a government currency expert told the United Press that the new bills would be similar to these now in use, with the dimensions of all lettering and figures reduced by about one-third. He said that smaller currency would save the government $3,000,000 annually in manufacturing costs.

He said that the designs now under consideration were the result of an order by Elihu Root when he was Secretary of State under President McKinley, approving a type of currency for the Philippines, similar to American bills but smaller.

Herbert D. Brown, chief of the bureau of efficiency and chairman of the committee studying paper money, understands that Secretary Mellon is strongly in favour of the- plan.
