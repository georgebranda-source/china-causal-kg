Wireless Telegraph Services, which offer speedy and efficient means of communication, are established between Hongkong and Ships at Sea, French Indo-China, the Province of Yunnan, and Macao.

**ARTIFICIAL SILK.**

**BIG SLUMP IN SHARES**

**SELLING PRICES DOWN.**

*London, July 9.*

There is a big slump in the shares of Courtauld's, artificial silk, manufacturers, one of the most favourite counters on the Stock Exchange, following the announcement of a reduction in the interim dividend from 1/6 to 1/3.

Shares' dropped five-eighths—equivalent on market depreciation to £7,500,000.

The reduction of the interim dividend is possibly due to a policy of caution in view of the coal strike.

Courtauld's, however, are reducing their selling prices. It is believed that they are engaged in an intensive competition against their rivals, which may temporarily affect profits.—Reuter:

## [16] SUBMARINE'S FATE.

**REMOVAL OF BODIES IN NEW YORK.**

*New York, July 9*

The removal of the bodies of the men trapped in Submarine S.51 began at the Brooklyn Navy Yard. The bodies were carried to the Navy Hospital, where the identification process is carried out by a finger-print expert.—Reuter.

Eight Bodies Recovered.

The body of Lieutenant Foster was first identified. Eight bodies have been recovered, and four more are known to be in the wreckage, whilst the torpedo-room has not yet been entered.—
Reuter

## [17] TUBE STRIKE.

**FIRST SERIOUS ACCIDENT IN NEW YORK.**

*New York, July 9.*

The first serious accident during the subway employees' strike occurred to-day. A train driven by a strike-breaker rounded a curve too quickly and jumped the rails. No one was injured, but the service was stopped four hours.—Reuter.

## [18] A. TORNADO.

**BUILDINGS AND ORCHARDS RAZED.**

*Chicago, July 9.*

Two persons were killed and several seriously injured as the result of a tornado which swept Benton Harbour and caused destruction in parts of Indiana, razing buildings and orchards.—Reuter.

## [19] COTTON INDUSTRY.

**ROUNTY RECOMMENDED IN AUSTRALIA.**

The Tariff Board has considered the Government scheme to encourage the cotton industry in Australia by granting a bounty on cotton yarn spinning. The Board recommends a bounty of sixpence per lb.—Reuter.

The preliminary statement of the Church Missionary Society's income and expenditure, for the year ending March 31, discloses the fact that the gross receipts amounted to £462,000, an increase of £19,000 over those of the preceding year. It is not yet known how much of this sum is available as income to meet the expenditure of this year, and the fact that the total payments have reached the sum of £505,000 makes it clear that once again the Society has to face a considerable deficit. Serious efforts were made last year to reduce the expenditure, but the high and in some countries increasing cost of maintaining present work, combined with adverse rates of exchange, made any large and immediate reduction impracticable.

## [21] HONGKONG, SATURDAY, JULY 10, 1926.

**FOR SALE!**

**AMERICAN GOVERNMENT VESSELS.**

**NO CHANGE OF FLAG.**

*New York, July 9.*

The Shipping Board is endeavouring to sell the Government passenger boats and cargo boats, including the leviathans George Washington. President Harding, and President Roosevelt.

The bids must be satisfactory, and must be made by American citizens or companies under the existing law. Ships must continue to fly the American flag and cannot be handed over to a foreign registry.—Reuter.

## [24] FRENCH CRISIS.

**TREASURY ON THE ROCKS**

The Treasury's plight is indicated by the weekly return of the Bank of France shewing nearly a thousand million francs worth of new bank notes printed, while the Treasury funds to meet all demands amount only to 300,000,000 francs.

The Treasury has to meet the heaviest obligations at home, and abroad, and has to pay unpostponable debts. It possesses barely enough money to last for a week and thus the new measure for inflation is regarded as inevitable.—

Reuter.

Renewed heavy selling yesterday created a new low record. Frances were 193%, closing at 192½, while Belgian francs, touched 209 and closed at 207½ to the 2.—Reuter.

The fate of the new Government is in the balance, and it is agreed that to-day's debate will be decisive. It is opined that the Socialist plan for a Capital Levy will have ceased to count after M. Caillaux's speech.

It is estimated that the Government can rely on 230 votes, but the dubious factor is the attitude of the Moderates under M. Marin, who has not yet participated in the debate.

Meantime francs on London this morning were rather better at 187/37 and Belgian francs at 199/25.—Reuter.

## [25] COAL CRISIS.

**N.U.R. AND EMBARGO QUESTION.**

*London, July 9.*

The N.U.R. Conference at Weymouth has endorsed the Executive's decision not to take action on the miners' request for the imposition of an embargo on coal.—Reuter.

The official Tass Agency declares that the report that the Soviet Government has prohibited collections in Russia for British miners is without foundation. It states that Russian Trade Unionists are resolved to make regular deductions from their wages, ranging from a quarter per cent. to a half per cent. during the entire period of the British coal stoppage.—Reuter.

## [27] WAR CRIPPLES.

**PITEOUS MARCH IN PARIS ON SUNDAY...**

**FRENCH DEBT QUESTION:**

*New York, July 9.*

The Press are most interested in a march of disabled and other ex-Servicemen planned for Paris, on Sunday as a protest against the ratification of the French debt settlement with the United States. The "New York World," in a leader says that no intelligent person in this country believes that the debt settlement can last 62 years. There are devices for postponing an ultimate settlement, for which the public are not yet ready.
The "New York Times" expresses the opinion that even the most complacent American cannot, without an inward twinge consider the piteous march of 15,000 war cripples who undoubtedly speak the unuttered feelings of the majority of the French people.

The paper warns the Americans not to blind themselves to the significance of feeling abroad which, if not dissipated by every legitimate means, bids fair to cloud the international horizon for a long time.—Reuter.

## [29] JAPAN'S NAVY.

**INCREASE IN NEXT YEAR'S ESTIMATES.**

It is learned from the Navy Department that at the conference of high.Navrl officials, this afternoon, the Navy Minister presiding, estimates for the fiscal year, 1927, which will shortly be presenting to the Finance Department, were tentatively fixed at 320,000,000 yen, which is an 80,000,000 yen increase on the present fiscal year.
These figures, it is understood, include an unstated sum for the construction of auxiliary vessels for replacement.—Reuter.
