## [1] COAL CRISIS.

**DEBATE ON EIGHT-HOURS' DAY BILE.**

**SIR J. SIMON OPPOSED.**

In the House of Commons Sir A. Steel Multland, Minister of Labour, moved the second reading of the Coal Mines Bill dealing with hours. Amid Labourite interruptions and irational laughter he emphasised the permissive and temporary character of the Bill, and repudiated the suggestion that it was part of a general Government attack on hours and wages.

Mr. Stephen Walsh (Labourite, Inee) moved the rejection

## [3] Y.M.C.A. YAPP.

**IMPRESSIONS OF THE FAR EAST.**

*London, juné 28.*

On his return from his world tour, Sir Arthur Yapp, Chairman of the Y.M.C.A., said that he was tremendously impressed by the "development in the Far East, especially in Japan, and said it was an object lesson to see the up-to-dateness of cities like Shanghai and Tokyo.

They were infinitely ahead of us as regards electrical development, while a more gratifying note was the intense thirst for knowledge by the young.

He added his admiration for Japanese knowledge, and endorsed the brilliant medical man "who told me Japan was better served regarding research work than Britain, America and Germany put together."—Reuter.

## [8] KIDNAPPED.

**CHURCH WORKER LURED AWAY.**

**HELD FOR RANSOM.**

Douglas, Arizona, June 28.
Mrs. Aimee Semple McPherson, evangelist of Los Angeles, who has been missing since May 18, was found to-day in a hospital here by William McCafferty, editor of the "Douglas Dispatch," who had known her in former years. Mrs. McPherson was identified over the telephone after a conversation with her mother by Mr. McCafferty. The identification was established by a scroon Mrs. McPherson's hand. "Associated Press."

## [9] Tells Her Story.

<sub>section under [8] KIDNAPPED.</sub>

From a cot in the hospital where she was in an exhausted condition. Mrs. McPherson told the story of her kidnapping at Ocean Park beach, near Los Angeles. She said she was coming out of the water where she had been swimming on May 18 when she met a woman who said her baby was dying, and asked her to accompany her and her husband to pray for the child. They entered a sedan and immediately two men and a woman threw a coat over her head. A sweet odour made her unconscious and the next she knew was when lying ill on a bed.

Her captors told her they were holding her for $500,000 ransom from the church in Los Angeles. One of the men burned her fingers with a lighted cigar in a futile attempt to force her to answer catch questions to identify her mother, Mrs. Kennedy of Los Angeles, when they should begin negotiations for the ransom. They also threatened to give or sell her to a Mexican called Felipe if she did not answer. They threatened to cut off her hair and her scarred finger and send them to her mother in order to prove that she was being held a prisoner. The kidnappers expected to negotiate for the ransom through Mrs. Kennedy.
