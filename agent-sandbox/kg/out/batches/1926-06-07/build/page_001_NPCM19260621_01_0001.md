## [6] ROBBERY SEQUEL.

**BRAVE FORLWHO GAVE CHASE COMPLIMENTED.**

**SENTENCES DEFERRED.**

There was a sequel at the Criminal Sessions this morning to an early morning robbery at Connaught Road, West, on May 5, which was followed by a chase in the course of which one of the robbers was shot in the neck. Tang Nam, Ng Po-ying and Ng Hau were charged with complicity in the robbery in question and pleaded guilty.

The conduct of one of the fokis of the firm which was robbed (engaged in the importing and exporting of drugs and pharmaceutical wares) was the subject of reference.

Mr. Ar Dyer Ball, acting for the Attorney-General, said that it was entirely due to the action of Sau Lin-sau that the Crown had been able to prosecute in this case. He had left the shop very shortly after the robbery and found five people who, he thought, were robbers. He called in the assistance of the police and himself gave phase to the five men. These were a subsequently found to be armed.

There can be no doubt, whatever, said Mr. Dyer Ball, that it was as a result of his action that the first and second prisoners were arrested.

With regard to the actual robbery itself, it was mentioned in the charge that nearly $2,000 worth of goods had been taken, also personal effects from inmates in the shape of jewellery.

## [8] PIRATE CAUGHT.

**JUNKMAN TRACES ROBHER TO MACAO**

**ENTERPRISE SUCCEEDS.**

On his own Inlative, a local junkman followed up some pirates who robbed him near Hongkon waters and one of the gang was arrested in Macao.

On June 12, the junk left Man King Sha (in the Tung Kun diet, Kwangtang province) with 25 riuls of bananas and bamboo. The boat was handled by a crew of Two men.

The destination was Tai O, at one extremity of "Lantao Island, a part of the Colony of Hongkong." When 5 miles of Tai O (then in Chinese waters, as the boundary runs right up to Tai O,) a fishing junk came up. Four men, armed with rifles and revolvers, transferred the cargo (valued at $250) and a few articles of clothing. Four others remained in the pirate craft, standing by. After staying four hours, the pirates left in the direction of Macao (about 20 miles away).

Instead of reporting to the Tai O police station, the junkman decided to sail also for Macao. There he recognised one of the alleged pirates and had the man arrested. He then came on to Hongkong to make his report.

## [10] HARBOUR OFFENCES.

**HARD TO CATCH, BUT DEEMED SERIOUS.**

Comment was passed by the Marine Magistrate (Lt.-Com. G. F. Hole, R.N.) this morning on an offence in which the culprits were not easy to catch.

Native craft people sometimes dumped earth in the harbour, without permission. They kept a lookout for the Police launches and were, accordingly, difficult to bring to book.

To make an example of one brought up this morning, His Worship took a serious view and imposed a fine of $20 (or three weeks). The fine was paid.

A junk master was fined $2 for anchoring his boat in such a way as to obstruct free access to the "Star" ferry wharf, Kowloon.

For anchoring in the southern fairway, another boatman was fined $5.

## [11] MUTINY SPREADS.

**CANTON POLICE TURN ON**

**AVOID THE STRIKERS7**

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

*Canton, June 19,*

To-day the spirit of mutiny spread from the Nationalist Army (two divisions of which refuse to go to the front until they are paid their three months' arrears of pay) to the "Canton-police, force. In a new trial of strength between the strike pickets and the police, the pickets were once against victorious.
The trouble began this evening in front of the Custom House, and on the Bund. About 40 members of the First and Second Corps of the Marine and Land Detectives of the Strikers Union met there face to face. There has been bad blood between these two corps for some weeks, because each charged that the other profited unduly from the sale of confiscated goods which brought in about $1,600.

As soon as the fight started daggers were drawn, and during the course of the fight eight men were so badly wounded that they were later given hospital treatment.

Only two city policemen were nearby, and they ran at once to Police substation No. 9 to make a report. The officer in charge, at once ordered 60 patrolmen to the scene, but the rank and file of the men refused to obey the order, and, instead, begged a move to attack their officers. The police officers then fired their revolters into the air, in order to attract aid, but when the shooting began, the mutineers fled, and have not since reported for duty.

## [13] Authritien Anxious

<sub>section under [11] MUTINY SPREADS.</sub>

This mutiny of the police, coupled with yesterday's mutiny of the soldiers of General Cheung Fat-fui, is causing grave anxiety to the authorities.

The Central Kuomintang, in a last effort to withhold power from the hands of General Chang Kai-shek, has voted to establish its own Military Affairs Department. A similar department is included in Chang Kai-shek's tentative plans for a military headquarters organisation, and great interest centres upon which one will really function.

Mystery surrounds the sudden elevation to power of General Li Chai-sum, the Kiangsi commander who heads the Fourth Army. He has not only been made General Chang's Chief-of-staff, but has also been named as vice-president of the Whampoa Military and Naval Academy. In some quarters this courting of General Li Chai-sum's power is taken as a reflection of the rising tide of'mutiny' and discontent in Chang's own forces.

## [14] Lawless. "Labour."

Yesterday five Chinese were arrested by strike pickets as they were attempting to approach the Hongkong steamer in sampans. The owners of the sampans were also taken to the strikers' jail.

The beligerency and lawlessness of many of the unions is causing grave disquiet to many of the merchants and residents of Canton.

The day before yesterday, all members of the Yee Yee Fong Association (the restaurant and tea house owners of the city) ceased operations because the employees demanded all instead of half of the proceeds received from the sale of "remnants." Yesterday representatives of the union tracked down many of the eating house owners, and, at the point of a gun, forced many of them to sign the new union agreement. To-day the owners met, and by unanimous vote decided to close their establishments regardless of the documents which now bear their shops and signatures.

## [17] Hosts To Americans

<sub>section under [16] WHO HAVE BEEN ABROAD.</sub>

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

This afternoon, the Euro-American Returned Students Association held their annual reunion on Dutch Folly, the little island opposite the Canton Bund, which has just been converted into a very attractive park. More than 300 Chinese who have been to foreign universities were the hosts to the American colony of Canton and Shameen, and 289 of the hosts were graduates of American universities. The affair was nominally in honour of the South-China Association, and almost without exception the Americans here were present. The Consul-General, Mr. Douglas Jenkins and Captain Constein of the U.S.S. "Helena" were among the guests, and most of the faculty of the Canton Christian College were present.

After the musical entertainment, tea was served on large bouts anchored by the island. Refreshments were served; and the party lasted until a late hour.

Mr. Y. M. Lin, in his opening address, emphasised the fact that this was the second international gathering of its kind ever held in Canton, and said that such a friendly reception would have been impossible had Canton not be enjoying the most stable Government in its history.

"More than 100 former university students are in Canton, men who have left various colleges and universities because of alleged radical attitudes. They are now forming an 'Oppressed Students' Union,' and will elect officers on June 24."

The All-China Students' Union will start its annual meeting here on July 15. A proposal is being drafted for the unification of all student organisations in China, and this will come before the convention for approval.

## [18] MAGISTRACY ITEMS.

**AMATEUR THIEF'S FEAR OF THE POLICE.**

A Chinese cock who was charged at the Central Magistracy this morning with theft of $1,500 from his master told an extraordinary story by maintaining that after he "extracted the " money (three $500 notes) from a safe he was overcome by fear of the police and burned them.

Sub-Inspector Wutt said that defendant took the key of the safe from under his master's pillow while he was asleep and robbed the money. The police had tried in every possible way to get defendant to say where he hid the money, but he insisted that he placed the notes in a chatty and set fire to them.
Defendant was sentenced to six months' hard labour.

## [19] MISSION TO LEPERS

**LOCAL CHINESE TO THE FORE.**

**H.K. AUXILIARY FORMED.**

As a result of the meeting at the Y.W.C.A., on Wednesday evening last, when the Rev. T. C. Wu, General Secretary of the Chinese Mission to Lepers, Shanghai, addressed a gathering of ladies and gentlemen, a further meeting of the temporary committee then formed, was held yesterday, Sunday, at the residence of Mr. Li Chor-chi, "Dunheved," Robinson Road, to discuss further arrangements for the organization of a local auxiliary.

The re-elected Committee is composed of the following ladies and gentlemen:—Messrs. Li Chorchi (Chairman), Lo Po-yin (Vice-Chairman), Mrs. Wong Kwok-shuen, Andrew Ling (Secretary), Mrs. Ma Wing-chan (Treasurer), Wong Hin-chi (Publicity Agent), Miss Shin, Mrs. Wong See-koo, Miss Wong Shuet-hing, Dr Arthur Woo, Andrew Cheung, Cheung Wing-kue, James Y. Lee and Mr. John. Geldart as Honorary Adviser.

As stated by Mr. Wu at Wednesday evening's meeting, the objective of his address then was fourfold, (1) Christian, (2) Philanthropic, (3) Preventative, (4) Medical. A cure for leprosy in its early stages was discovered some years ago in Chaumoolgra oil, and several cases of the disease are known to have been entirely cured through its agency. The Mission in Shanghai which was founded a few months ago is in great need of funds to carry on this important work of preventing the spread and effecting the cure whenever possible of this terrible disease. For instance, there are two leper settlements not so very far from Hongkong, at Sheklung and Tungkun, which are under the direct charge of Catholic and German missionaries respectively, who receive a small monthly allowance from the Canton authorities to look after the immediate needs of the sufferers at these two places, but the funds are quite insufficient to provide for the medical treatment of the lepers.

The object in view of the local auxiliary will be to collect funds which are to be remitted to the head organisation in Shanghai which will undertake to supply free of charge the treatment throughout the various leper settlements throughout China; and thus commence the first step of restricting the further spread and effect an ultimate wiping out of the scourge in China, where the sufferers are estimated to be one million in number, which prevalence is attributed to the lack of the necessary precautions of segregation and care of these pariahs of mankind. Through the medium of this new organisation in China, it is expected that the lot of the sufferers will not be as full of hopeless misery, as it has been heretofore, that with the lately developed interest in their welfare by sympathetic people, they may find their existence more tolerable.

Further preliminaries are being carried out before the local auxiliary will be in full working order, and it is to be hoped that the movement will receive universal support.

Mr. Wu addressed many boys' and girls' schools during the past week and at each institution subscriptions were generously donated for the "deserving work"; he spoke in eulogistic terms of the sympathetic attitude of all classes in regard to the cause, especially amongst the students everywhere who expressed the utmost eagerness to help the new organisation.

Mr. Wu leaves for Amoy tomorrow by the "Sinkiang" for Amoy en route for Shanghai.
