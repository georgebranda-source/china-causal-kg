## [7] LOUD IN PRAISE.

**NEW P. & O. BOAT COMES TO HONGKONG.**

**GOOD AS ON ATLANTIC.**

Praise that the accommodation and decorations were as good as on Atlantic liners such as the Cunard and White Star companies was bestowed by passengers for Hongkong, who came out by the P. & O. Co.'s new boat, the "Ranpura."

Fine weather prevailed throughout the whole voyage and there was nothing exceptional. Leaving London on June 4 and Singapore exactly a month later, the "Ranpura" got "alongside the Kowloon wharf early this morning and will leave for Shanghai to-morrow.

How to Get Fresh Air.
In the Red Sea, it was very hot indeed but the “punkah louvahs” saved our lives, said one of those on board, to a “China Mail” man who called. As the name denotes, this is a new system of ventilation for the cabins, saloons, etc., by which fresh air is pumped in, instead of the usual fans. In this manner, the air is changed 42 times an hour and is highly appreciated by travellers. It is a system which is being installed on all new P. & O. liners.

Sister ship. to the "Ranchi," "Rawalpindi," and "Rajputana," the "Ranpura" was built specially for the Bombay mail service. It is thought that she will only make one trip to this part of the world and will then revert to the Bombay-London run. From what is known, however, this class of ship will gradually replace the ones on the China service.

Captain A. M. King, D.S.C., was pleased with the reception he received at all the new ports of call.

A picture is reproduced on page 6 of this issue and the list of passengers is given elsewhere. But to appreciate the splendour of the vessel's appointments, her cabins-de-luxe, one must go on board. Of interest to some will be the royal suite in which the King of Belgium travelled out three trips ago.

Every cabin on the ship is an outside or port-hole cabin. Of the public rooms, one traveller told the “China Mail” man that the comfort and charm were such that even the booklets did not do justice.

Impressing "Sight"

The crew comprises 120 Europeans and 213 Asiatics. In addition to 103 passengers, the "Ranpura" brought out the mail, this being made up of 357 bags of letters and 183 bags of parcels. Of cargo carried, 856 tons were for Hongkong and 2,740 tons for ports further North.

Externally, the “Ranpura” exhibits unusually graceful lines and nobility of appearance. Those who crossed the harbour early enough this morning were impressed by the sight of the liner coming in. Alongside the wharf to-day, she was eagerly scanned by passengers on the ferry. As a local shipping man remarked, the call here of the “Ranpura” is another mark in Hongkong's mercantile importance.

## [11] COAL CRISIS.

**YORKSHIRE OWNERS GIVE**

IN

*London, July 6.*

The Yorkshire miners have solidly declined to resume on the coal-owners terms.—Reuter

*London, July 7.*

The Yorkshire coal owners, whose action led the Government to abandon its intention of forcing the passage of the "Coal Mines Bill, have informed the Government to day that they will not press their proposals with which the Government was dissatisfied, namely, the allocation of 85 per cent. of the profits to the miners and 15 per cent. to the coal owners, but revert to the pre-strike allocation of respectively 87 and 13 per cent.

Lieut. Col. G. R. Lane-Fox, Under-Secretary of State for Mines, stated in the Commons that over a million tons of coal had been imported from the Continent and the United States by private companies during the two-months ended on July 1.—Reuter.

Accelerating Bill.

In the House of Lords, after expressing gratification that the Yorkshire owners had changed their attitude, Lord Cecil announced that he would now proceed with the Eight-Hours Bill, the third reading of which will be taken to-morrow.
—Reuter.

German View.

The German Miners' Union have rejected a proposal from Mr. Frank Hodges that an international mining crisis might be solved by decreasing the working hours.

The German miners are of the opinion that it is impossible to equalise British and German hours, owing to the complete difference.—Reuter.

Yorkshire's Position.

There was a further development of the coal situation this evening when the Prime Minister received a letter from the Yorkshire colliery owners stating that they were prepared to come into line with the offers made in other districts on the question of ratios, although they felt strongly that a variation in the ratio suggested by them was amply justified by the circumstances of the coal industry in their district.

It may be recalled that, whilst not contemplating any immediate reduction in wages for miners working eight hours daily, the terms offered by the Yorkshire owners provided for an alteration of ratio of the division of the proceeds of the industry as between wages and profits, which was to the disadvantage of the miners.

The Government, having come to the conclusion that the Yorkshire offer was unsatisfactory, it was announced last night by the Marquis of Salisbury that the Government reserved to itself complete freedom of action with respect to the final passage of the Coal Mines Bill, which for a temporary period permits miners to work eight hours instead of seven.

## [13] A TANGLED CASE.

**DEMANDING MONEY WITH THREATS.**

**YOUNG WOMAN INVOLVED.**

Details of a police trap and how a fireman of the Central Fire Station walked into it at the M. Y. San tea-house last night were given before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning when the man with two others were charged with demanding by threats a sum of $10.

This woman was not present in court, but the name and address being given by the defendant, the case was adjourned until to-morrow.

## [17] COUNCIL CONTEST?

**TWO MEMBERS STAND FOR ELECTION**

**TEMPORARY VACANCY.**

There is every prospect of the unofficial Justices of the Peace being called on to decide which of two candidates will represent them on the Legislative Council during the absence of Sir Henry Pollock.
The vacancy will be for three months duration during the time Sir Henry is to be away in Canada. The two candidates are Mr. C. G. Alabaster, K.C., and Dr. W. V. M. Koch.

Whether or not the two candidates will take steps to bring their candidature before the public eye, the contest is likely to be a spirited one. Many residents may recall in this connection the contest between the late Mr. A. R. Lowe and the Hon. Mr. H. W. Bird for similar representation some ten years ago in the course of which the late Mr. Lowe published an election address and brought his candidature before the public by means of posters.

Mr. C. G. Alabaster will be nominated by Sir Henry Pollock and seconded by Mr. Paul Lauder, general manager of the Union Insurance Society of Canton, Ltd.

Dr. Koch will be nominated by Mr. F. C. Jenkin, barrister-at-law, and seconded by Mr. A. S. D. Cousland, a partner in the firm of Messrs. Alex. Ross & Co.

Mr. Alabaster has served on the Legislative Council on previous occasions and has acted as Attorney General. He is a member of the Sanitary Board and has taken a keen interest in matters pertaining to the public welfare. He took a prominent part in the activities of the Constitutional Reform Association. Mr. Alabaster holds several important Masonic offices.

Dr. Koch, who is a partner in the firm of medical practitioners—Drs. Harston, Black, Balcan, Koch and Taylor, was formerly in the Government Civil Hospital. As a member of the Sanitary Board he has proved most active in raising questions bearing on the public health of the Colony.

## [19] ARMED ROBBERY.

**ATTEMPT AT YAUMATI FAILS.**

**WOMAN STABBED**

An armed robbery in the Yaumati district at 8 o'clock last night was averted by the cries of a Chinese widow, the sole occupant of a tea-house at Waterloo Road, but the enraged gang, numbering three men, wounded the woman with a knife before escaping in the direction of Kowloon City.

The robbers entered the hut by pushing open the door and asked for a bottle of mineral water. They soon disclosed their real motive for the visit when they started to ransack the hut. The widow's shouts to "save life" scared the robbers away.

## [21] PRINCE GEORGE.

**ACCEPTS POSITION AS PATRON.**

**NAUTICAL RESEARCH.**

With the consent of the King, Lieutenant H.R.H. Prince George, R.N., has accepted the position of Patron of the Society for Nautical Research, says the "Times" of June 8. An announcement to this effect was made at the annual meeting at the Royal United Service Institution by Admiral Sir George Hope, chairman of the council.

It is well known that the King has taken a great interest in the work of this society, and particularly in the movement which it carried through successfully to preserve and restore the Victory at Portsmouth. In accepting, with much pleasure, the post of Patron, Prince George said in a letter:—“I am very glad to have some connection with the Society, the work of which I have always admired.”

The appointment of the Prince almost coincides with the tenth anniversary of his entry into the Navy, for it was in the summer of 1916 that he passed the qualifying examination for a naval cadetship, and he entered the College at Osborne in September of that year. He was promoted to midshipman in January, 1921, lieutenant in February, 1924, and lieutenant on February 15 of this year. Since January, 1925, he has been serving in the “Hawkins,” flagship on the China Station.

The Prince, who was 23 years of age in December, is the only son of their Majesties who is keeping to a naval career. A keen supporter of the society from its inception was the late Marques of Milford Haven, who, as Admiral Prince Louis of Battenberg, was the first president. On his death in 1921, Admiral of the Fleet Sir Doveton Sturdes was elected to the office, and it was during his régime that the "Save the Victory Fund" was inaugurated. Sir Doveton Sturdee died a year ago, and was succeeded by Admiral of the Fleet Lord Beatty, who has been a member of the society since its foundation in 1910.
