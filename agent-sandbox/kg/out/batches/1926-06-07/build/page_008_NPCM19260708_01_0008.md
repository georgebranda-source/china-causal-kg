Hankow, July 1. The "Central China Post" learns from a most authoritative source that the Peking Conference has ended with the arrangement that Marshal Chang Tao-lin take over complete charge of Northern hostilities with the assistance of Marshal Chi Hsieh-yuan, while Marshal Wu Pei-fu, with the assistance of Marshal Sun Chuan-fang, looks after the situation in the South. Marshal Wu Pei-fu is expected back here within ten days.

The evening school in the Chinese Y.M.C.A. will have the closing exercises for the spring term to tomorrow, the 9th instant, at 7:30 p.m.

The furnishings for the new branch of the Chinese Y.M.C.A. in the new Bank Building opposite Sincere's, are about ready. The rooms will be available for members in a short time.

A new class for "Chinese Y.M.C.A. members who cannot swim, and perhaps a second for members who can swim only little, will be opened next Monday night, the 12th instant, at 7.30, at the Association.

Feking, June 25.—Fifty million dollars has been requested of the central government by Tupan Chang Tsung-chang according to the "Che Hue Yi Pao." This sum represented the expenditure of the Shantung's military authorities in the recent and present campaign against the Kuominchun, it is claimed.

Tokyo, June 30.—The Finance Department has issued a communiqué announcing that the rate of interest on loans to be advanced for the latter half-year by the Hypothec Bank, the Hokkaido Colonial Bank, and all the agricultural and industrial banks throughout the country will be raised to from 0.1 per cent. to 0.3,

Tokyo, June 30.—The Sino-Japanese Joint Art Exhibition was closed to-day after a great success. Exhibits sold totalled 126, of which 116 were Chinese masterpieces, including four purchased by the Department of the Imperial Household, nine by the Foreign Office, two by His Royal Highness Prince Kuni, and one by H. R. H. Prince Higashi Fushimi.

The “Central China Post” has received several complaints of assaults on foreign ladies, by the women cotton pickers on Peking Road. On June 30 a well-dressed Chinese woman who was walking with the pickers but whose clothes showed no signs of having been in close proximity with cotton deliberately went up to a foreign lady in a riesha and slapped her on the back. It might be just as well as these cotton pickers are big enough of a nuisance already for the police to keep a close watch on the Peking Road so as to prevent our own womenkind from suffering further indignities.

New Haven, Conn., May 28.—Arthur B. Hersey of Tientsin, China, is the winner of the first of the Benjamin F. Barge Mathematical prizes for members of the Freshman class at Yale University, it was announced to-day.

A reception in honour of Mr. Hsiung Hsi-ling, an ex-premier who is now in Shanghai in connection with the Anti-opium activities, was given by Mr. Hsu Yuan, Commissioner of Foreign Affairs of Shanghai, at the Foreign Affairs Bureau on July 3. Many local officials, military and civil, were present.

Los Angeles, June 29.—“Times” says to-day that Mae Murray, movie actress, who was married yesterday to David Divani, re-embraced the Catholic faith at the wedding. The bridegroom was, baptized as a member of the church just prior to the wedding. The actress said her three previous marriages were not Catholic, so this is the first time she considered that she was really married.

Mundelein, Illinois, June 24.—King Alfonso of Spain today conferred the grand cross of Isabella on Cardinal George W. Mundelein of Chicago. The presentation was made by Cardinal Enrico Reig y Casanova, primate of Spain, who said the honour was "in appreciation of the extraordinary service" to the Catholic church for which Spain was striving so ardently on this continent.

The engagement of Virginia E. Bier, of Wheeling, W. Va. to Harold A. James, of Newhaven, Conn., United States Etymologist to China, was announced at a large tea-dansant given by Mrs. S. E. Taylor and Mr. J. L. Bier at the Astor House on the 20th. The wedding is expected to take place in September after which the happy couple will leave China for their future home in the United States.

Copenhagen, May 21. — The Danish Supreme Court has rejected the appeal of the Scandinavian-Baltic Methodist Bishop Anton Bast against a High Court sentence of three months' imprisonment for fraud.

Commander P. F.P. Berryman, R.N., who has been appointed to the river gunboat Wigeon, which carries with it the appointment of Senior Naval Officer on the Upper Yangtze, joined the Royal Navy in 1905, was promoted lieutenant in 1910 and commander in December, 1924. He was with the cruiser Gloucester when the late war began and saw a lot of service overseas and in the North Sea. Latterly, he was in command of destroyers in reserve at Portsmouth.

Hannibal, Mo.: May 27.—Erection of statues of Tom Sawyer and Huckleberry Finn constitutes a "tribute to the eternal boy," Dean Walter Williams, of the University of Missouri school of journalism, declared in an address prepared for delivery at unveiling exercises here to-day. Bronze lifesize figures of Mark Twain's famous literary characters were presented to the city by Mr. George A. Mahann, of Hannibal, president of the State Historical Society of Missouri, and his wife and son, Dulany Mahan.

The corner stone of the new foreign Y.M.C.A. building in Bubbling Well Road, Shanghai, has been laid. The ceremony was performed by Captain Robert Dollar and Dean Symons was in attendance. The new building is in an advanced stage of completion, and judging by its outward appearance up to date, should prove a handsome addition to the Bubbling Well Road. If the inside is as pleasant to live in as the outside is to look at, the new building should be a success.

Mukden, June 20.—There are altogether 237,989 persons residing in Mukden, according to the latest census taken.

St. Louis, June 29.—Five Cardinals of the Catholic Church participated in the ceremony de-
dicating the new cathedral of St. Louis to-day. The cardinals left Chicago yesterday on a tour of the
United States which will take them as far as the Pacific coast.

San Diego, Calif., June 29.— Sixteen foreign rum running vessels are hovering off shore today and are attempting to land 96,000 cases of liquor valued at $4,750,000, before the Fourth of July, according to prohibition agents. The coastguard cutters and launches are watching the gun runners.

Peking, July 1.—Mr. Silas Strawn, American delegate to the Tariff Conference addressing the Peking Rotary Club to-day declared that the approaching observance day offers all thinking Chinese the opportunity to ponder what services a George Washington or an Abraham Lincoln might perform for China in unifying the Republic.

One of the Special Constables in Shanghai has received the following in a letter from Home:- One "Special" on duty during the recent strike in England bore down upon a child who was pushing a small wheelbarrow on the pavement, and ordered him to join the rest of the traffic in the roadway: "Garn away!" the child replied. "I seen a real 'un, and he sed nuffin."

Tokyo, June 28.—According to press reports, as regards the construction of auxiliary vessels, navy authorities as well as others concerned are greatly agitated owing to the deaking out of the intention of the finance authorities that the expense to be appropriated in the coming Budget for the construction of auxiliary vessels should be limited to one year's expenditure.

Washington, June 30,—Canada's strengthening of the border patrol will be followed next week by the addition of more than 100 men on the Canadian border by the United States, it was announced to-day, General Lincoln C. Andrews, head of the prohibition enforcement machinery, said the bulk of the force would be stationed in the vicinity of Detroit from which place the biggest liquor leak from Canada exists.
