## [1] VUCHOW- INCIDENT.

In the House of Commons, replying to the Rt. Hon. C. P. Trevelyan (Labour, "Newcastle-on-Tyne"). in regard to the British Senior Naval Officer at Wuchow closing the port to Chinese shipping on June 24, the Rt. Hon. Sir Austen Chamberlain, Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs, after reciting the facts of the case, said that "His Majesty's Government still awaited detailed information, but in view of the instructions "issued by the Canton Government that facilities should be provided, it was inclined to consider that the responsibility for the incident lay with the local Chinese authorities.

Mr. Trevelyan asked if there was any precedent for "Naval Officers using their discretion" in relations with foreign Powers without reference for the Home Government? Sir Austen Chamberlain replied that there was plenty of precedent. Happily for the British Empire and the peace of the world our Naval Officers had shown themselves most capable of using their discretion. Ministerial cheers.

## [3] SALT REVENUE.

**AGREEMENT WITH CHIHI**

The Finance Ministry and the Chihli Tupan have now formally concluded the salt agreement, providing the Chihli authorities do not interfere with the salt revenue but render assistance to the salt administration in carrying out its orders.

The Chihli authorities undertake to abolish and not to introduce in any form illegal taxation, and to assist the transportation of salt.

The Salt Administration under, takes to pay the Chihli Tupan $150,000 in the middle and end of each month, provided the administration first make provision for all loan payments, for which it is responsible under the Reorganisation Loan Agreement, and provided these sums are available for collections in the Changtu district.

The administration also agrees to pay certain outstanding subsidies, amounting to $450,000. Reuter.

## [7] . MIXED COURT.

Mr. V. K. Ting, interviewed by the "Sun Pao" confirmed the reports recently published that the negotiations regarding the "rendition of the Mixed Court had considerably progressed.

He declared, "generally speaking, that the terms of the proposed agreement were the same as published in the Press," though, after consultation, the Consular Body had some alterations made.
Mr. Ting stated that the draft of the agreement had been duly approved by Marshall Sun Chuan-fang and other provincial authorities, but at least a week must elapse before it can be signed.
It will be signed in the name of the Provincial Government, though Sun Chuan-fang has reported the matter to the Waichiaopu and has asked its consent.

“We decided to sign, whether consent was obtained or not.”

After "signing the" provincial agreement, the particulars will be worked out, the most important of which are the questions in which criminal cases affect the peace and order of the Settlement, and what departments of the Court shall remain, and which shall be abolished.

These and other questions will be taken up by the Committee of Legal Experts comprising three Chinese and three foreigners. At present the agreement may be cancelled at any time, if the Central Government can make definite plans for the complete rendition.

The enforceable “period” for a provisional agreement will be three years. Mr. Ting declared that the foreign lawyers’ agitation against the Settlement was merely the result of a quicker and easier Settlement problem.

The foreign lawyers' delegation is going to Peking this morning to interview the Diplomatic Corps.

The five lawyers in the delegation are Messrs. Otto Fischer, B.D. Rodger, A.M. Preston, Du Pac Du Marsoulies and Marakami.

## [10] TWO SHIPS ASHORE.

Business was practically stagnant in the City this morning. Numbers had been prevented from getting to office by the cessation of all harbour traffic and few Peak residents faced the walk down with the high wind and possibility of the typhoon striking. Peak residents who braved the journey had to surmount fallen branches of trees and other debris brought down by the gale. A visit paid to the P.W.D. and Supreme Court this morning found the former practically deserted and the latter completely so. The same applied to nearly all offices.

A danger with which pedes-trians were faced was that of possible falls from roofs and of sign boards. Several of these latter were noticed with broken supports and constituting a grave danger to passers by.

"Water-gates were up in all parts of Chinatown, to prevent another wash-out." The big department stores were closed and the Sun Co. had their shutters nailed down.

Spectators were on the waterfront. Motor vehicles found no great difficulty in proceeding and there were ricshas aplenty.

Enguities made by the "China Mail" this warning elicited the information that it was not thought that the present unfavourable weather conditions would prevent temporary Peak train service from being brought into operation, of Saturday. The service would be between Kennedy Road and Plantation Road with the stations there as a terminal. It would perhaps be three weeks before it would be possible to resume the full service the "China Mail" representative was informed.
