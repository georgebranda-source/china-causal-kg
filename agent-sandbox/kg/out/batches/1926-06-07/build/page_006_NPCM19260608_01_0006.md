Reference was also made in the House of Commons to the Tariff Conference, when the British attitude was once again definitely laid down. At the same time, the Under-Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs pointed out that, before completing the negotiations, the British Government would undoubtedly expect China at least to initiate measures extending the hope of the ultimate abolition of likin. ## [15] A CIVIL WAS LEVY.

Hankow, May. 22. — Foreign firms in Wanhsien were startled last week to receive a manifesto from the Chinese Chamber of Commerce of that city, demanding that they contribute to the campaign expenses of the various warring Generals in that territory. This is the first time that such a demand has been made upon foreigners in the Yangtze Valley, and protest has been made to the consulates of the foreign firms concerned.

The demand is known to have originated from General Yang Sen, who has issued orders for the collection of $180,000 from the Chinese Chamber of Commerce of Wanhsien, and from the gentry. It was decided to levy $105,000 of this amount on the gentry. $70,000 on the business men, and to secure a cancellation of $5,000 of the demand.

Chinese merchants being evidently tired of contributing to the war chests of the various war chiefs who change from day to day, and whose extortion is a continuous performance there, have decided to “pass the buck” to the foreign firms of that city, and have notified them that they will each be responsible for individual payments of $400, and up, according to the size of the firm, in order to make up a good share of the military war fund. Refusal to pay will bring dire consequences to the firms on whom demands have been made, as under those circumstances the Chinese Chamber threatens to issue drafts on those firms “payable to the troops, who will be authorised to collect direct from the foreign firms.

Only a few hours, not even one whole day, was given for the foreign firms to make their contributions, under threat of collection by force of arms. None of the firms have paid as yet, and the case is now being investigated by the Consular Departments of the nationals concerned.

Following is a translation of the document which was presented to the foreign firms of Wanhsien by the Chinese Chamber of Commerce, in its illegal demand for blood money to swell the coffers of the fighting Worlds of Szechuen.

From the Chamber of Commerce at Wanhsen, to all foreign firms; "Gentlemen:"

"With reference to the loan of $180,000 to meet the expenses for munitions of war and transportation by the Headquarters from the gentry and merchants of Wanhsien, we have to state that the matter has been discussed with the Magistrate and Chief Lily of the Bureau and an agreement has been arrived at. Besides the $105,000 for which the gentry will assume the responsibility, the merchants should assume responsibility for $70,000. The Magistrate and the Chief of the Bureau have requested the Commander-in-Chief to waive the remaining $5,000. Yesterday the directors of the various guilds were invited to a meeting. It was proposed for the approval of the meeting. The various guilds were all willing to do their best. Thereupon the question was publicly considered and the money was apportioned. It was unanimously decided that you should advance $400. This sum is to be paid in full to-day in order to save the situation and to tide over the barrier of difficulty. If the money is not paid after the expiration of the time limit, this Chamber will be compelled to issue a draft on you to the troops who will collect direct from you. Do not complain that no notice in advance is made."

Stamp of the Wanhsen Chamber of Commerce." May 11, 1926.—"Central Press."

## [18] GRUESOME HONAN TRAGEDY.

**DREADFUL STORY OF FIENDISH CALLOUSNESS.**

A correspondent to the "Peking and Tientsin Times" writes:—

I do not wish you to state in your paper from what town the undermentioned report comes, nor from whom but no doubt the facts which I personally vouch for will be interesting in view of the proposed abolition of extraterroriality.

A short time ago a number of bandits after being tortured were led out to the execution ground near the river. They were made to kneel in a row and then the beheading began. About a dozen unable to endure seeing the sufferings of their companions, made a rush for the river, and in full view of thousands they were shot down like ducks, some slowly bled to death, staining the water with their blood, some were drowned. Only one succeeded in escaping to the other side of the river. After a short run he was caught, brought back to the place where the crowds waited, tied to a pole and slowly skinned alive. This extra punishment was meted out to him for running away.
Another form of punishment was devised for a man and a woman who had been caught when attacking the bandits in the country. They were both buried alive outside the gate of the city. Some of the people had gone to see this new form of punishment, and mentioned how they had seen the earth moved about by the dying agonies of the poor creatures who were being slowly suffocated. The reason given for this awful punishment was that one of the officers on this occasion had been killed by the bandits.

## [20] MARSHAL SUN INTERVIEWED.

**THE GREAT NEED OF RE-ADJUSTMENT.**

Interviewed by a representative of the "Nihon Dempo News Agency," at Nanking, on May 27, Marshal Sun Chuan-fang stated that, rumours were current that the troops under his command had clashed with the forces of General Chang Chung-chang in Hsuchow, and that he, in cooperation with General Chin Yunho, was planning to drive away General Chang from Shantung Province, but both are quite groundless.

There is no misunderstanding between General Chang and himself.

Even if any trouble occurs between them, there is no need for attack on such a small province by the allied forces, which are far mighter than General Chang's.

It is not the time for China to continue any internal strife, but to realise the great need of readjustment. Political upheavals, followed by civil war, cause a great loss not only to China herself, but also to her neighbour, Japan.

If the situation in China turns to the age of rival chiefs, the European Powers, who are watching for unguarded moments, will not fail to extend their influence in this country. Moreover, Soviet Russia will not lose any opportunity to bolshevise China, and then Japan, most probably. Japan is now in a critical state with her social conditions, for there is a wide gulf between the upper and lower classes.

If China were placed under the sphere of influence of the European Powers, the yellow race in the Far East will probably be overthrown within ten years. This is the important point to which deep consideration must be given by the Japanese nation. China should be unified sooner or later, and he (the Marshal) has a plan to carry out his scheme on the East Southern Monroe principle. He intends to support any good Government which may be organised, and not interfere with any bad Government particularly. Asked if there is any possibility

of the unification of China, by the combined efforts of Marshals Chang and Wu who joined hands recently, Marshal Sun replied that it is desirable that both Marshals should go hand in hand for the welfare of the country, but they must go together with moral and mutual understanding, otherwise they may fail to fulfil their important duties.

As regards the anniversary of the May 30 affair, Marshal Sun firmly said that if the students and labourers dared to organise any anti-foreign movement, he would not fail to maintain strict order over any demonstrations.

## [21] NORFOLK HOUSE

The Diplomatic Body in London is rather unusually busy with "moves" and prospective moves of residence at present. Bute House, South Audley Street, W., which has been taken by the Egyptian Government, is filled with workmen hastening the completion of redecoration so that when the King of Egypt makes his visit to London this month the legation may be ready to house him.

It is likely that a large house will be acquired before very long for the Italian Embassy. The lease of the embassy in Grosvenor Square, W., is within sufficient distance of its end to make the acquisition of another home for King Victor's representative desirable. There has been some talk of Norfolk House, the St. James's Square home of the Duke of Norfolk, being acquired, but nothing definite is known yet.

Moscow, May 16.—Furniture; pictures and bronzes from the old imperial palaces will be offered to the public in a series of auction sales this week. The articles represent what is left of the vast stores of priceless objects collected from all over Russia, after museums and private buyers had obtained what they wanted.
