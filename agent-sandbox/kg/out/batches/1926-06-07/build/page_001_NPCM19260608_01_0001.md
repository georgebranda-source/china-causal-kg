As Mr. Ho Kwong, chairman of the Twenty-four Commercial Guilds, informed the delegates yesterday, it is not exactly within the province of the Hongkong Chinese merchants to form organisations to decide how the "strike" is to be settled, the Hongkong Government having "initiated that this is a matter for the respective Governments. ## [14] CHINA'S TARIFF.

**GREAT BRITAIN AND THE CONFERENCE.**

**ATTITUDE DEFINED**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, June 7*

In the House of Commons, in reply to questions, Mr. G. Locker-Lampson, Under-Secretary of State for the Colonies, said that by accepting the resolution on the Tariff Conference on November 19, His Majesty's Government was committed to agree to the insertion in the Treaty which the Conference was endeavouring to frame of an article granting China tariff autonomy on January 1, 1929. He said that up to the present, owing to the political disturbances in China, little progress had been made in drafting the Treaty. His Majesty's Government was not committed in any other respect.

Mr. Looker-Lampson added that the agreement of the Powers to grant tariff autonomy in 1929 and the "Chinese Government's undertaking" to abolish likin by then was not an article at present, but stood expressly interdependent. His Majesty's Government, before completing the negotiations, and not pleading for an actual grant of tariff autonomy, would undoubtedly expect China at least to initiate measures extending a fair hope of the ultimate abolition of likin.

## [20] BALDWIN'S PART.

**“ONE OF GREAT TRIUMPHS OF HISTORY.”**

**FREEDOM OF EDINBURGH.**

*(British Wireless Service.)*

The Freedom of the City of Edinburgh was conferred upon the Prime Minister to-day in the presence of a large gathering.

Presenting to Mr. Baldwin the silver casket containing the Freedom, the Lord Provost, Sir William L. Sleigh, said that the Prime Minister came fresh from one of the great triumphs of history. Mr. Baldwin was the man of the hour, and it was no small satisfaction to the citizens of Edinburgh that it had fallen to them to give him the first formal expression of the gratitude which all his fellow-countrymen owed him for the way in which he had successfully brought them through one of the greatest crises in their history.

## [21] TRAFFIC DAY:

**SEQUEL TO TAXICAB SMASH.**

**POLICE COURT CASES.**

An accident in which a toxic car crashed into a post near Status Square involving injuries to a passenger was recalled before Major C. Willson at the Central Magistracy this morning when a Chinese motor car driver was charged with reckless driving. There was an additional charge against him of failing to notify his change of address.

Sub-Inspector Alexander termed this as a serious case of a motor car coming out of a side street into a main thoroughfare without giving a warning. He said that on May 25, shortly after midnight, the car came out of Wardley Street into Des Voeux Road and turned in an easterly direction. A taxicab which was then coming into town had to pull up suddenly to avoid a collision, but skidded and ran into a post. Sub-Inspector McLennan who was a passenger in the taxi, was injured and had been nine days in hospital.

Although not quite recovered from his injuries, Sub-Inspector McLennan was present in Court and intimated that he did not wish to bring any claim for compensation.

Defendant was fined $10 on the charge of failing to notify his change of address. The other charge, the Magistrate said, was a very serious one which might have involved loss of life and actually caused injuries to a police officer. He would be fined $100.

## [24] BANDITS' WORK?

**UE-COUNTRY A.P.C. MAN FILLED**

**LATE MR. PHILIPS.**

News has been received in Hongkong of the recovery from the river of the body of Mr. J. M. Phillips, manager of the A.P.C. branch at Nanning, whose capture by bandits near Taiping was reported a week or so ago. Mr. Phillips at the time was on his way to Nanning from Lungchow. A body of troops was reported at the time to have been despatched from Lungchow to locate the bandits and effect the release of Mr. Phillips, and it is presumed that the bandits in the course of their retreat killed their captive and disposed of the body.

Mr. Philips, who was about 24 years of age, came out with the Company about two years ago and was stationed for a time at Wuchow. The district in which he was captured has gained notoriety in connection with the activities of bandits which infest that area.
