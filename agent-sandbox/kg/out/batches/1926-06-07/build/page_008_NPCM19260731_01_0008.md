## [8] A SATURDAY CAUSEERIE.

After the series of brief communiques issued in connection with the meetings of the Canton and Hongkong delegates to the boycott Conference, the statement issued on the return of our delegates was'most welcome in that for the first time' concrete proposals brought forward on either side were set out, also the respective replies. Whilst indicating in general terms in these replies their attitude towards the points at issue, the delegates felt themselves unable at that stage to give an irrevocable decision except in so far as the British delegates and any possible "compensation" for strikers was concerned. This, they announced, could not be contemplated for a moment. The delegates will later meet to communicate the respective attitudes of their Governments with regard to—in the case of the Hongkong delegates, the proposed Commission of Inquiry into the anti-British boycott issue (which the Chinese delegates propose shall be empowered to determine responsibility for the Shameen-Shakee affair of June 23, 1925); in the case of the Chinese delegates, to the British Loan proposals.

Next to the boycott issue, the vagaries of "the weather have occupied pride of place in the public mind. The feeling of satisfaction engendered by the smart work of the P.W.D., which has enabled all roads but that to Castle Peak to be opened up, has given place to one of doubt as to whether, after all, it will be possible to take full advantage of the long week-end afforded by Monday's holiday, in view of adverse meteorological reports. If they eventuate, the inveterate grouser would not be alone in his claim that to add within a week to a record storm, deluge and tail-end of a typhoon would be rather too much of a good thing.

"The coal crisis has continued throughout the week without the miners' representatives deviating in the slightest from the 'not a penny off the wage or a minute off the day' attitude but legislation has been introduced which is to leave to the several mining districts the decision as to whether or not work will be resumed on an 8 hour day basis. Many miners"

"have resumed work on this basis in the face of directions to the contrary by the Executive of the Miners' Federation." A proposal was put forward by representatives of the Churches which involved a continuation of the subsidy for a period in the form of a loan. The Government has indicated that it is not favourable to this as a loan would "mortgage the future of the industry which was at present uneconomic over large areas." To-day's cables, however, indicate that the Miners' Federation intend submitting the "churches' proposals to the several districts.

As a change from the restricted sphere of Hongkong entertainment, the demonstration in hypnotism and telepathy by Professor Langsner served as a useful purpose. The Professor thinks the audience treated a serious subject with too much levity but it would be more correct to say that the smallest incidents were seized on for laughter as a release from the pitch to which the audience had been keyed up as a result of the eerie powers. the Professor undoubtedly exercised. No slight was intended on the demonstrator whom it was realised had put successfully to the practical test claims as to which many were sceptical.

Although the week's local election involved a temporary vacancy only on the Legislative—Council and the electorate was confined to unofficial Justices of the Peace of the Colony, "considerable interest was taken in the issue by the general public. The questions which a J.P. representative is entitled to raise are not, of course, entirely confined to matters concerning only those by whom he is elected and in Dr. Koch the public has a member who has already proved on the Sanitary Board his assiduity in the public welfare. His experience in an important Government position will be of considerable help to him in raising matters pertaining to the public health measures. He has already

espoused the cause of reform in this connection and outlined a constructive scheme for the institution of a Public Health Board.

The impending visit of the retiring Governor of Macao, Senhor Maia Magalhaes, has aroused considerable interest in local Portuguese circles and arrangements have been made to receive and entertain him on a scale fitting to the important position he has held and the work he has accomplished. The announcement of the appointment as his successor of an official with considerable colonial service is taken as indicating a realisation on the part of the Portuguese Government of the importance of this small but progressive Colony.

## [10] PEAK TRAMS RESUME.

As from to-morrow, the Peak Tramway Co., Ltd., hope to have their full service in operation again, between the two termini.

Repairs have been pushed ahead so that a return to normal conditions—as before the damage by the deluge—will be possible, and the non-stop cars will resume once more.

## [11] SHA U CHUNG.

**PASSENGER AND CARGO REGULATIONS.**

**PERMITS REQUIRED:**

### '

The following regulations were made by the Governor in Council under section 42 of the Merchant Shipping Ordinance, 1899, Ordinance No. 10 of 1899, on July 29:
1. No vessel shall embark or disembark, or transfer to or take transfer from any other vessel, any passenger or any cargo in the waters of the Colony at Sha U Chung, unless the person in charge of the vessel then holds a valid permit in writing in that behalf signed by the District Officer, North, and unless the embarkation or disembarkation or transference is in strict accordance with such permit.

2. The District Officer, North may, in his discretion, grant permits of the nature referred to in regulation I of these regulations: In any such permit the hours or times may be prescribed and limited, as the District Officer, North may seem fit, during or at which such embarkation or disembarkation or transference may be made to and from the vessel named therein.

## [14] FATHER'S AIR CHASE

**HOW AN ELOPING COUPLE WERE CAUGHT.**

A motor-car dashed up to the offices of Imperial Airways at the London air station recently and a determined-looking man stepped out. He hurried into the booking office, where he demanded a special aeroplane to take him immediately to an East Coast resort.

All the available large air liners were engaged in the Continental services, and a small three-seater machine was hurriedly obtained. It was in the air in less than fifteen minutes.

The passenger, after landing at his destination, hurried to the railway station and anxiously paced the platform awaiting the arrival of a train from London.

When the train arrived a girl and a man among the passengers were singled out by the aerial passenger, and after a heated argument the girl, who proved to be his daughter, was persuaded to leave her companion and return with her father by air to London.

## [16] CIVIL SERVICE.

**AMENDMENT OF PENSION MINUTE.**

The following amendment of the Pension Minute which appears in the General Orders of the Hongkong Government is published for information:—

The Pension Minute is amended by the deletion of clause No. 13 and the substitution therefor of the following:—

1. In the case of abolition of the office of an officer who is borne on the Fixed Establishment of the Colony the pension or gratuity grantable to such officer will be as follows:

If the officer has completed 120 months' gross service he may be granted a pension of one-seven-hundred-and-twentieth of his salary for each month of his service counting for pension in accordance with Clause No. 11 with an addition to such service of one month for each complete period of two months' service so counting for pension; in no case shall the addition exceed one hundred and twenty months, and in no case shall the number of months to be added to the actual service exceed that which, if added to the age of the officer, would bring that age up to sixty years. This paragraph (a) deals completely with the ascertainment of the amount of pension grantable in the case aforesaid, accordingly no increase or addition — for example the five-sixtieths of salary mentioned in clause No. 1 paragraph 1 subparagraph (2) — shall be made.
