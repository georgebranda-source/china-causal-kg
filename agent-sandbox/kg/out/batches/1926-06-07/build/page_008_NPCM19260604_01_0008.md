## [7] LOCAL AND GENERAL

In 1925 21,526 cwt., of bliscuits of a declared value of £166,766 were imported into British Malaya. Of this total 16,350 cwt., valued at £43,000, came from the United Kingdom. Corresponding figures for 1924 were:—total imports 14,252 cwt., valued at £1,017,388, imports from the United Kingdom 11,882 cwt., valued at £89,426.

An interesting situation has arisen at Hankow over the recent looting of opium shops in the native city. The Chinese authorities, seeing that they have been unable to stop this sort of robbery, have now stationed an armed policeman at each shop to protect the premises. Thus opium, which supposedly is an illegal business, is now being officially protected by Government agents.

Washington, May 24.—A White House statement to-day emphasised that the smallness of the Carmi Thompson party, which is sailing for the Philippines on June 15, will not affect the importance of the investigation. Colonel Carmi Thompson, of the President "Coolidge envoy to the Philippine Islands," emphatically denied reports that he would be the next governor general of the Philippines.

Lieutenant Munag of the constabulary, who was prosecuted at the instance of General Wood, Governor of the Philippines, for alleged killing of Moro outlaws in a pitched battle in Lanao about six years ago, was found guilty of homicide, according to a telegram received in Manila from Judge Borja of the court of first instance of, Lanao. The message did not mention the sentence given the lieutenant.

The “Shanghai Nippo” is of the opinion that a certain police authority has smuggled large quantities of arms and ammunition from Vladivostok into Shanghai in preparation of May 30. The paper adds that motor launches and motor cars were used at night to transport the contraband to a certain house in a certain road. Evidently there is nothing certain about the story.
Tokyo, May 24—The Foreign Office has decided to send councillor Hanaoka from the Constantinople Embassy to Teheran as first official envoy for investigating Persian economic conditions and to open a legation there.

Mukden, May 25.—Marshall Chang Tso-lin, with the subject of constructing a railway between Taonan, Fengtien, and Solunshan, Heilungkiang, has given instructions to Mr. Chang Hai-peng, Governor of Taonan Prefecture, to make necessary instructions.

## [8] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

The Vicar of St. Andrew's Church, Kowloon, wishes to thank the kind anonymous donor of a handsome oak notice board for the church.

During the absence of Miss H. D. Sawyer, on home leave, Miss Agnes Dulmage, B.A., will act as I'eadmistress of the Diocesan Girl's School.

Mr. R. A. Green, of the Hongkong and Shanghai Bank, has undertaken the Hon. Treasurership of "Cathedral Notes." The finances of the magazine, it is pointed out, are at a very low ebb.

A welcome is accorded in “Cathedral Notes” to new members of the choir—Misses J. and T. Thornhill, Miss Hardinge and Mr. Gahagan. It is stated, however, that there are still many vacancies.

The Bishop of London, who will be passing through the Colony at Christmas on his way to Korea, will preach at St. Andrew's Church Kowloon on December 26, at the morning service.

The al fresco concert on the Vicarage tennis court, Kowloon, has been postponed to Monday, June 21. An excellent programme is being arranged. The proceeds will be given to the C.M.A. for work in the Diocese, and also for St. Peter's Church repair fund.

Mr. C. Whitley has felt compelled to relinquish the post of Hon. Treasurer of the Young Men's Club, St. Andrew's Church. The vacant post has been filled by the appointment of Mr. J. H. Bannister who, from the first, has been a strong supporter of the work among the young men.
The death took place on May 10, at the Medical College Hospital, Calcutta, of Florence Barbara Houston, "Royal Red Cross."

The wedding took place on May 27 at St. George's church, Penang, the Rev. Keppel Garnier officiating, of Walter John Smith, of the Public Works Department, Kuala Lumpur, and Estelle Bamberger, widow of Mr. Henry Bamberger of Philadelphia. Mr. C. G. Boucher of Messrs. Stark and McNeill, was best man.

Before Mr. Hereford, District Judge, the wedding took place in Penang on May 27 of Mr. C. C. W. Liddellow, mining engineer of Bannassan, southern Siam, and Miss Mehcthurne, daughter of Nal Now Prakpatolang. The attending parties were Mr. H. A. Hacker and Khun Philip Suels of the Stamese Consulate.

Peking, May 26.—Marshall Sun Chuan-fang is stated to have now agreed to Mr. Yan Wenkak's assumption of office as Minister of Agriculture and Commerce stipulating that Mr. Chang Chien, Junior, be appointed Vice-Minister.

Tokyo, May 26.—The American Ambassador on the 24th instant called on Baron Shidehara, Minister for Foreign Affairs, at the Foreign Office and, consulted with the latter as regards the Disarmament Conference and China's Tariff Conference.

Rice prices are again steadily rising much to the consternation of the Shanghai people. The officials and merchants, who recently opened cheap sales bureaux, have expressed their anxiety and Gen. Yen Chung-yang, has promised to order more of the grain from Saigon, the Chinese stocks being insufficient. The price of rice on May 22 stood between $15 and $17. According to official reports, 14,400 piculs of rice were sold at the various bureaux last month.

An amusing little comedy has been enacted within the South Manchuria Railway zone (says the "Japan Chronicle"). Perhaps because of the inactivity of its business, the South Manchuria Railway Company has begun to extend its hands to other enterprises, and a levy of sixty sen per month on ricsha coolies entering the railway zone seemed to open vistas of profit. The Chinese coolies however, not only refused to pay but 5,000 of them withdrew from the zone. The resultant inconvenience caused railway passengers to protest very strongly, and the tax order was withdrawn, but without result. Finally the Company had to offer to subsidise the coolies at the rate of 60 sen a month, just the very amount they thought they were going to extract from the men.
"Hankow, May 26.—All the operatives of the British Cigarette Company's two factories went on strike yesterday morning as a protest against the installation of extra machinery at the Chiaokow Factory. The workers stated that this would entail the dismissal of some of their number."

The death has occurred, of Mr. George Thornton Pett. The late Mr. Pett is the retired Kalutara planter. He was formerly a Captain in the Ceylon Volunteers.

Berlin, May 21.—A judge named Jurgens who is incarcerated for malfeasance in office was attacked to-day by two fellow prisoners serving long terms and so badly beaten that he required hospital treatment.

Mr. H. E. Daunt left Kobe on the "Empress of Canada" on May 20 with Mrs. Daunt. Mr. Daunt was, until last year when he retired, manager of the Vacuum Oil Company. He does not expect to return to Japan, unless on a short visit.

Each time the Italian Premier makes an important speech before Parliament he really makes two speeches—the version heard by his auditors and another version, corrected by the Premier himself, from which the phrases held to be too strong for foreign consumption are weeded out.

"The Law Times" states that Mr. Benjamin Dickinson, Barrister at Law, has been appointed to be Pulane Judge of the Supreme Court of Ceylon. Mr. Dickinson was called by Lincoln's Inn in 1904, and is President of the District Court of Nicosia. Mr. Dickinson's first appointment was as District Commissioner, Gold Coast, 1909-16. He was Police Magistrate, Accra, 1916-17. He acted as Chief Registrar twice. He was President of the District Court of Cyprus in 1918 and acted as Pulane Judge on several occasions.

London, May 14.—Ibn Saud's three conditions regarding visits of private persons to the Erophet's Tomb are firstly, that the visit must be very simple and that the faithful should not touch the tomb in the hope of drawing a blessing from on high; secondly, though not used as a drug in the time of the Prophet, tobacco is now considered injurious to the health and is therefore banned; thirdly, Islamic doctors have always reproved all forms of amusement and distraction, for example dancing and tambourine playing etc. Therefore must be prohibited in the holy places.
Mr. and Mrs. Sydney Stephens and their young son Desmond left Kobe for England on May 20 aboard the "Empress of Canada." They expect to return to the port next October.

A verdict of "death from unknown causes," probably heart failure" was returned on May 26 on the body of Mr. Chas. Eckmann, an employee of the Kiangnan Dock and Engineering Works, Shanghai. Whilst the deceased was eating his dinner he suddenly collapsed, and by the time a doctor arrived he had succumbed.

Possibilities of 10,000 masons and carpenters in Shanghai going on strike for higher pay are opened by reports that a definite demand for increased wages has been made to the employers under threat of strike. The men who earn, on an average, 40 cents a day, have put in a demand for an increase of 20 cents per day to meet the high cost of living. It is reported that the employers meet at the Carpenters' Guild on May 26, when the demand was discussed, but no action was decided upon at the meeting.

Singapore, May 25. — The Court of Inquiry in connection with the fire aboard the "s.s. "Penang" found that the vessel was in a seaworthy condition, that there was no evidence of the cause of the outbreak of the fire, that all adequate precautions were taken, and that there was no evidence to "show any wrongful act or negligence." The loss of eight lives was due to the improper handling of boat. No. 4. The Court also found that no blame attaches to the master or officers of the ship in respect of the loss of the vessel or of life.
