**HONGKONG, FRIDAY, JUNE 18, 1926.**

**COAL CRISIS.**

**OWNERS PREPARING NEW SCHEME**

*London, June 17.*

It is understood that the mine owners are preparing a scheme for re-opening the pits on an eight-hour basis, the coalfields to be divided into three categories; firstly, pits where wages will not be reduced; secondly, pits where there will be a reduction of under ten per cent.; and thirdly, pits where the reduction, if possible, will not exceed ten per cent.

The last named will probably include the three, export districts of Durham, Northumberland and South Wales. Most pits, if possible, will be included in the first category.—Reuter's Service.

## [10] SOVIET MONEY.

**BRITISH GOVERNMENT'S FORMAL PROTEST.**

**NATCHING FURTHER ACTION.**

*London, June 17.*

In the House of Commons, Commander Oliver Locker-Lampton- (Conservative, Handsworth) asked whether the Government was considering the advisability of withdrawing its recognition of the Soviet Government in view of the offer of money from Moscow for the general strike?

Sir William Joynson-Hicks, Secretary of State for Home Affairs, replied that the Government, having protested in the most formal manner to the Soviet Government, did not propose at present to take the step indicated by Com. Oliver Locker-Lampson. The Government was equally indisposed to forbid so-called charity gifts in connection with industrial disputes, but it was carefully watching further action by the Soviet Government and its affiliated organisations, and would not hesitate to act if a change of policy were necessary.

Sir W. Joynson-Hicks declared that His Majesty's Government was satisfied that the Soviet Government and various Communists and Trade Union organisations were under a single controlling authority. He mentioned that £380,000 altogether was contributed by the All-Russian Council of Trade Unions in connection with the miners' dispute.
A discussion developed in which, in reply to Mr. Ramsay MacDonald (Labour leader) and others, Sir W. Joynson-Hicks said that the Government would arrange a discussion of the widest possible character on British-Russian relations.

It is understood that this discussion will be set down for Tuesday next. — Reuter's Service.

## [12] STOWAWAY'S DEATH

**FIRST OFFICER CHARGED WITH HOMICIDE.**

Santago (Cuba), June 17. Mr. Fritz Gerler, first officer of the Honduran steamer "Olancho," in which a Britain stowaway, William Jones, was found dead, is charged with homicide.
Members of the crew testified that Gerler locked up Jones and three Honduras, who boarded the vessel, in the storeroom near the ship's boiler. Jones protested, but the others went quietly.
Cuban Customs officers several hours later forced Gerler to open the compartment, and Jones was found dead from asphyxiation while the three Honduras were very weak—Reuter's American Service

## [16] PRIVATE LOANS.

**NO EVIDENCE OF FRENCH NEGOTIATIONS.**

**U.S. TREASURY VIEWS.**

*Washington, June 12.*

Mr. Andrew Millon, Secretary to the Treasury, notified the Senate that the American Debt Commission, after inquiry, had not found any evidence of French negotiations to raise a private loan in the United States. He indicated that the Administration would not seriously object to sanctioning such after the French Parliament had ratified the debt agreement.
Other Treasury officials are of opinion that before such a loan is given American bankers should demand the conclusion of the French negotiations for the settlement of the war debts to Great Britain as well as to the United States.

## [18] WHOSE TERRITORY.

**CHILE'S INTENTIONS NOW DECLARED:**

Aricn (Chlle), June 17.

Following the breakdown of plans for holding a plebiscite, Chile intends to resume military occupation of the disputed territory. All forces withdrawn by order of the Plebiscitary Commission are to be returned forthwith.

The Chileans consider their sovereignty of Tacna-Arica absolute, until the plebiscite is held.
The Commission having declared the proceedings ended, all the Americans, except General Lassiter, are expected to depart by June 20.
The Chilean paper "Pacifico" demands, if the plebiscite is not held, the return to Chile of Taratu in the province of Tacna, which was transferred to Peru last September.—Reuter's "American" Ser

*Americans Protected. Paris, June 17.*

Strong guards of artillerymen and sailors have been posted outside General Lassiter's house in consequence of a considerable crowd gathering there.
A similar precautionary measure has been taken in regard to other American Commissioners, but so far there has been no demonstration.—Reuter's American Service.

[A previous cable from Washington stated:—State Department officials are inclined to the view that the Plebiscitary Commission are authorised to declare the impossibility of holding a plebiscite on the Tacna-Arica dispute, which authority can be exercised any time before, during or after the balloting. This conviction seems to forecast the rejection by the arbiter, President Coolidge, of any appeal from Chile from the decision that the plebiscite is impossible.]

## [19] MEXICO'S LAWS.

**DUTCH RESERVE LIBERTY OF ACTION.**

*Mexico City, June 17.*

The Dutch Minister has sent a Note to the Mexican Foreign Office, it is reported, reserving to Dutch subjects full liberty of action in regard to petroleum and land laws. It is unofficially stated that there is every reason to believe that Holland refused to accept certain provisions in the laws.—Reuter's American Service.
