## [8] *(no headline: column 2)*

Detectives visiting a junk anchored at the Yaumatl typhoon shelter on Tuesday night found 19 revolvers, a quantity of ammunition and a small amount of opium on board. The junk had no occupants on board.

A Chinese received severe injuries to his head in alighting from a moving tram car near the Happy Valley terminus on Tuesday evening. He was taken to the Government Civil Hospital by ambulance and died yesterday morning.

"A well-known Chinese gentleman, Mr. Chen Tsze-yuan, was badly injured about the head and body while boarding a motor bus at Kowloon Tong on Sunday. It is alleged that the bus started off just as he placed his foot on the dashboard.

A further remand was made at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday in the case of the three French aulors of the s. Andre Liebon who stand charged with the unlawful possession of 16 automatic pistols and a quantity of ammunition. The case will be heard tomorrow before two Magistrates.

Two Chinese were brought before the Central Magistrate yesterday on a charge of being concerned in an attempt to obtain a gramophone and records from a pawnbroker by means of a forged pawn-ticket. On the application of Mr. R. A. Wadeson for the defence, hearing was fixed for Saturday.

Chicago, May 25.—Ninety per cent. of the undergraduates at the University of Chicago are doing 85 different kinds of work in this city to earn all or part of their college expenses, it is announced by the institution. A baking company employs 15 students on a Friday night shift to supply the extraordinary demand for bread on Saturday. These men start at 2 a.m. and turn out loaves for the next eight hours.

"Vienna, May 28.—A turbine is reported to have been invented by Edward Suess, which is expected to develop electricity from the river of 100,000 horsepower hours in one year, at a cost of only $400. Such a turbine could be erected along the Danube to supply current to Vienna at a minimum cost." A feature of the turbine is a hollowed four-blade propeller, which is so constructed that the water is held inside for a moment, then released without loss of back wash.

A new Russian newspaper called "Roch" (Speech) will shortly make its appearance in Shanghai. It will be edited by the former correspondent of the "New Shanghai Life."

Berlin, June 17.—The International authors and composers congress held at Paris founded a world organisation comprising eighteen nations. President is Robert de Flero of France. vice-President Ludwig Fulda of Germany.

The late Mr. H. B. Irving's collection of criminological literature has been presented by his widow, in memory of her husband, to the London Library. According to "The Times," there are in all about three hundred volumes dealing with crime and criminals in England, America and France, among which is Dr. Kenealy's elaborate edition in several volumes of the famous Tichborne case.

Admiral of the Fleet Sir Edward Hobart Seymour attained his 86th birthday on April 80. A Crimean veteran, he also saw service in the China War of 1857-62, when he was present at the capture of Canton, the battle of Fatshan Creek, the taking of the Peiho forts, and the operations against the rebels in China in 1862. From 1898 to 1901 he was Commander-in-Chief on the China Station, and commanded the Allied Expedition during the Boxer rising.

## [10] LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS.

Nauen radio, June 11, — The American hotel proprietors who have just returned from their inspecting tour of the chief cities in Europe have announced a scheme to build a big hotel having 200 rooms at Spitzberzen.

Zien Tsung-ong, a member of the Chapel police force, was sentenced to two years' imprisonment in the Mixed Court. ## [11] REMNANTS CLEARANCE

Interpretation of the Labour Dispute Arbitration Act in Japan which will become effective on July 1 is feared to be likely to be precarious in judicial circles, and consequently, the court, it is thought, should be the sole authority in interpreting the law, in spite of the fact that the interests of both the capital and labour will be greatly affected by the law.

Washington, June 20.—The big collections of income taxes during the present quarter reported to the treasury has boosted the prospective surplus for the fiscal year ending June 30 above $300,000,000 which is far beyond the official estimates heretofore made. Nevertheless high officials refused to day to concede that another early tax reduction was in prospect. It was impossible, they said, to gauge the real effects of the lower rates provided by the new revenue law, and the expenditures authorised next year for some time. The Secretary of the Treasury Andrew W. Mellon said $300,000,000 had been collected in back taxes.

## [12] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS.

Mr. J. Stewart McIntyre, formerly of United Engineers, has joined the firm of Wakeford and Lowndes, consulting engineers, Singapore.

The University of Glasgow has offered the Honorary Degree of Doctor of Divinity to the Rev. H. F. Wallace, Principal of the Anglo-Chinese College, Swatow.

H.I.H. Prince Chichibu, accompanied by Capt. Okazaki, started from London on June 14, for Pirbright in Surrey, where he proposed to spend 12 days camping with the officers and men of the First Battalion of the Scots Guards. He was to return to London on the 26th.

The Philadelphia Exhibition, opened in commemoration of the 150 Birthday of the United States, covers 1,000 acres, about nine times the area of the Wembley Exhibition. The Japanese-section is the most imposing among the foreign exhibits and is the most elaborate display ever shown abroad by Japan.

Mr. J. K. Innes, C.M.G., late Judge of the Supreme Court of the F.M.S. lectured recently, on "British Malaysia" before the Hants and Dorset Branch of the Royal Colonial Institute, and paid a warm tribute to the assistance given by Chinese immigrants in the wonderful transformation that has taken place in that country in the last forty years.

Dr. Gordon Thompson has been appointed by the C.M.S. to succeed Dr. Duncan Main of the C.M.S. Hangchow Hospital and Medical Training College, who is retiring at the end of the year. Dr. Gordon Thompson is expected in Hangchow at the beginning of November. Shortly after he arrives Dr. Main will say "goodbye" to Hangchow after 45 years' service.

It is notified in the "London Gazette" of May 14 that the King has approved the following Foreign Office appointments dated January 1: Mr. J. T. Pratt, C.M.G. to be one of His Majesty's Consuls-General in China; Mr. G. S. Moss, M.B.H. to be one of His Majesty's Consuls in China; and Mr. A. D. Blackburn, to be one of His Majesty's Vices-Consuls in China.

Dr. Sun Yat-sen's Literary Research Society has decided to initiate a campaign for the purpose of increasing the membership.

Word was received in Shanghai on June 25 that Colonel T. C. Goodman, U. S. Army, Retired, father of Mrs. J. W. Schoenfeld, died in Washington, D. C. June 24.

Rome, June 18.—The Pope to-day addressed an ap-stolic letter to vicars and apostolic prefects in China denying rимours that the church was acting toward political ends in Asia.

The American Consulate-General at Shanghai is interested in securing information as to the whereabouts of Mr. J. F. Woodworth. Any particulars concerning his present whereabouts will be appreciated.

The American Consulate-General is interested in securing information as to the whereabouts of Mr. Clarence W. Hunley, who is reported to have come from New York to Yokohama in the latter part of 1925, and thence to Shanghai. Any particulars concerning his present whereabouts will be appreciated.

Four of Shanghai's leading Chinese merchants will shortly leave for the South at the invitation of the Canton Nationalist Government to investigate the situation and general conditions existing in that part of China. The merchants in question are Messrs. Chen Liang-yu, Wang Hsyu-lai, Lau Ching-shul and Chun Bing-him.

## [13] *(no headline: column 4)*

The Agriculture and Forestry Ministry, Tokyo, after investigations, has come to learn that ¥400,000,000 worth or 10,000,000 koku rice is devoured annually by insects before harvest and while under storage.

According to Chinese Press, a representative of a certain German industrial magnate is in Tsingtau to enlist labourers to be sent to Germany, and very liberal remuneration is promised. A labour recruiting office will shortly be set up for the purpose.

Tokyo, June 15.—The Monopoly Bureau, Tokyo, decided to open 37 salt sales depots at important centres at an outlay of ¥350,000. From the preparation of the storing facilities for salt stocks, part of these depots will be opened on July 1, and the others on August 10. A material reduction in the salt price is expected.

Peking, June 22.—The Shansi representative in Peking reports that the Shansi main force has been driven back from Yenmen-kuan, mainly owing to transport difficulties, but the Shansi troops still occupy Tatungfu and other cities near the northern border. General Yen Hsi-shan has informed Marshal Wu Pei-fu that Shansi is secure, and requires no reinforcements.

News from the Japanese Troops in North China, as received at the Headquarters of the Kwantung Army, Port Arthur, reports the outbreak of cholera in the British Concession, Tientsin, on the 15th inst. From past experience, the port of Yingkou is known to be menaced once an epidemic appears in Tientsin, and on this account, a specialist has been sent to Yingkou from Fushun to look out for a possible invasion of the epidemic.

Referring to the withdrawal of the Chinese police from North Szechuan Road Extension, Shanghai, the vernacular newspapers state that the Chinese authorities took this step solely with the idea of maintaining good relations with the Settlement authorities. The papers point out that the Chinese police are entitled to patrol this road if they wish, but in view of the negotiations for the rendition of the Mixed Court it is considered advisable to keep the foreigners in good temper, and not to irritate them needlessly.
