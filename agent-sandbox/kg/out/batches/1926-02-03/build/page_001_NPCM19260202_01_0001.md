## [6] ALLEGED FRAUD.

**CHINESE BUSINESS MEN CHARGED:**

**$50,000 INVOLVED:**

Three Chinese were charged before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning with an alleged conspiracy to defraud the banking department of the Wing On Company of a sum of $50,000 by means of a forged deposit book.

Mr. F. C. Jenkin (instructed by Mr. C. Bulmer Johnson, of Messrs. Hastings, Dennis and Bowley) was for the prosecution. Mr. T. G. Bennett appeared for the first and third defendants. A Kim-lui and Lam Fook-chi while Mr. J. A. Gordon Leask represented the second defendant Fok Cheung-yuen.

The prosecution applied for one week's formal remand, to which no objection was offered by the defence, and the question of bail was raised by Mr. Bennett.

His Worship remarked that he understood the $50,000 obtained from the Wing On Company was not their property.

Mr. Jenkins said that the first $50,000 was the property of the Company and had been obtained under a forged deposit book. The fraud was quite a common one. A man posing as a customer of the bank would open a deposit account with the intention of withdrawing the money later. A second man would put up the money and receive a deposit book. With the expenditure of a small sum a forged deposit book would be made by a third man. With the presentation of the two books at different dates an attempt would then be made to withdraw the money twice.

Continuing, Mr. Jenkin said that the men were charged with forgery as well as conspiracy. The case was a serious one and his instructions were to oppose bail.

His Worship said that he could not allow bail under the circumstances and formally adjourned the case until February 9.

## [8] EDUCATION

**FORMER HONGKONG MAN SPEAKS OUT.**

**COLLEGE DOMINATION.**

Dr. John Stuart-Thomson, formerly of Hongkong, contributes a leading article on University life and education to the World Travel Gazette that is making some stir in the international education world. It recommends the ancient English custom in academic things of letting the colleges be managed by the faculties and students rather than be academically and athletically dominated by rich donors who are seeking advertisement, propaganda or some other private advantage.

Dr. Thomson for three years was manager at Hongkong of the Pacific Mail and Toyo Kisen Kaisha S.S. Co.'s office in Queen's Building, living at the Peak Hotel and Hongkong Club. During that time he contributed poems to Chamber's Journal, Edinburgh, the most famous being "The Alien's Farewell," December 1, 1902 and "The Alien's Return" 1904.

Dr. Thomson is a well-known constitutional and civic-zoning authority and Far East Explorer and is being run by the New Jersey press for U.S. Senator; The article makes him popular with the students and the faculties though it means an popular with the exploiters of colleges. ## [12] INDIAN'S CHINESE.

**VERY GOOD, BUT NOT INTELLIGIBLE.**

**A COURT SCENE:**

A veteran rice shopkeeper of Shaukiwan with torn clothing and a bruised leg was the defendant on a charge of assaulting an Indian constable in a case before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning.

Divisional Inspector Blackman said there had been a quarrel over a small obstruction outside defendant's shop and probably more force than was necessary had been used by the constable.

The constable stated in evidence that the defendant sat in the roadway with a basket of leaves and refused to move away after he had ordered him to do so three times. He was then abused, and had his pockets torn out.

His Worship on learning that the constable held a first class certificate for speaking Chinese asked him to repeat the order as given to the defendant. The constable complied in a gruff voice.

"Very good, but not intelligible," remarked His Worship.

Defendant was discharged.
