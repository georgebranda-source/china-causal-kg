## [6] MR. WU ANSWERED.

**GOVERNOR ON CANTON'S DUTY.**

### Drastic Measures For Strike Fomenters.

This morning's meeting of the Legislative Council was noteworthy in that before the agenda business, H.E. the Governor made statement with regard to the present boycott.

The boycott had been characterized by flagrant violations of treaty rights which it was, the duty of the Canton Government to require the Strike Committee to put an end to, he stated. Their failure to do this throws grave doubt upon its professed sincerity in desiring an early settlement, he added.

The activities of emissaries to incite a strike in Hongkong was subject of reference. It was indicated that drastic steps would taken against such if the attempts were not immediately dissued.

Following is the full text of His Excellency the Governor's remarks. His Excellency said: Your Excellency and gentlemen, I regret to inform the Council that the anti-British boycott in the Kuang-tung Province still continues in spite of the efforts made by the Hongkong Government to negotiate a settlement. This boycott originated from a strike, which according to a communiqué issued last month by the Canton Commissioner of Foreign Affairs "was instituted by Hongkong workers, not for economic reasons, but as a protest against the British Government," and which is claimed in a resolution recently passed at a special meeting of the Four Chambers of Commerce in Canton to be "a patriotic demonstration of the people for the honour of the country."

“Unlawful Tyranny.”
But, if this be so, how came it that the Canton Strike Committee was obliged from the outset to maintain thousands of strike pickets in Canton and elsewhere, in order to prevent the Hongkong workers from returning to their employment in this Colony? And, why is it that the strike is now to all in-tents and purpose? Is it thing of the past, and that, in spite of every means of intimidation, employed by the Canton Strike Committee, almost the whole body of Hongkong labourers is now again at work? Moreover, is it not certain that the anti-British boycott in the Kuang-tung Province would not last another day but for the unlawful tyranny imposed upon the Cantonese people by the Canton Strike Committee? The Strike Committee can easily put this matter to the tust. Let it withdraw all the strike pickets, abstain from all foreible interference with trade in the Kuang-tung Province, and note the result.

Kuang-tung Province, and we are persuaded that the great mass of the Cantonese people are still, as they used to be, ready and willing to trade with us in the most amicable manner to our mutual advantage. (Applause). Only the unlawful activities of the Canton Strike Committee, instigated by Bolshevik intrigue, prevent the resumption of normal relations between Canton and Hongkong on the old, familiar footing. We expect and require the Canton Government to put an end to these illegalities. We hold the Canton Government to be principals in this matter. I also wish it to be clearly understood that the Hongkong Government will never agree in principle to strike pay or to compensation for non-reinstatement of labourers. (Applause).

I must close with a word of warning. It has been brought to my notice that attempts have recently been made in Hongkong by emissaries of the Canton Strike Committee to incite a new strike. If these attempts are not immediately discontinued, the Hongkong Government will take drastic steps to put in stop to them; and should any labourers be so ill-advised as to desert their employment—a contingency which I believe to be remote—I am confident that the whole community can be depended upon to rise to the occasion, as it has done before. (Applause). The emergency organisation, which did such splendid work last year, is still in being and is ready to be put in force at any moment, should circumstances demand its use. We are determined to give full protection to the people of Hongkong, and to put down with a firm hand any conspiracy to intimidate or otherwise to cause, trouble among labourers and merchants in this Colony. In all necessary measures to that end, I know that I can rely upon the wholehearted support of this Council. (Applause).

## [7] Government As Principals.

The Canton Commissioner of Foreign Affairs argues in his communique that the principals in this matter are the Canton Strike Committee on the one side and the Hongkong Government on the other and that, therefore, a settlement should be negotiated by the Hongkong Government with the Strike Committee. I desire to make it perfectly clear that the Hongkong Government cannot negotiate in Canton with any but the Canton Government. Moreover, the boycott maintained by the Canton Strike Committee to the detriment chiefly of their own fellow countrymen is characterized by flagrant violations of treaty rights, which it is the duty of the Canton Government to respect. Consequently it is the duty of the Canton Government to require the Canton Strike Committee to put an end to such illegalities. The Canton Government has not yet done this, and its failure to do so throws grave doubt upon its professed sincerity in desiring an early settlement, and makes it difficult to give credence to the statement in the communique of the Canton Commissioner of Foreign Affairs that "the Government at Canton, though deeply interested in the issue, was not a party to the strike or boycott." The Canton Government is, of course, responsible for the maintenance of law and order in the areas under its administration; and in as much as it has left unchecked and unpunished the illegal activities of the Canton Strike Committee directed against this Colony, it is liable for all losses incurred owing to such illegal activities. (Applause).

**LOCAL ALTERATIONS.**

A prisoner who has been sent to jail in default of finding a fine may, when a Bill before Council is finally passed, obtain remission of the corresponding portion of his sentence, by part payment

This statement was made in Council to-day by the Attorney-General, the Hon. Mr. J. H. Kemp, in moving the first reading.

Other interesting points mentioned were that the power of the magistrate would be limited to imposing sentences with regard to consecutive sentences which at present could amount to as much as two years, to imposing a maximum aggregate of twelve months. In exceptional circumstances, prisoner could always be referred for trial to the Criminal Sessions, commented the Attorney-General.

The "Harbour Master is to be allowed under the new regulations to delegate to the Assistant Harbour Master his legal power as magistrate when he is prevented from sitting.

“Once a magistrate, always a magistrate,” was the gist of the reason for another clause, said the Attorney-General. The clause empowered an officer appointed magistrate and subsequently undertaking other work, to sit again as magistrate as occasion required.

A magistrate's powers being limited at present in the matter of the service of subpoena summonses (application at present having to be made in certain cases at the Supreme Court) the new Bill empowers the service by the magistrate direct.

The text of the Bill is contained in a report on another page.

## [11] BANKRUPTCY COURT

**LOCAL DEALER FAILS TO APPEAR.**

**WARRANT ISSUED.**

Owing considerable sums to European firms, a Chinese debtor named Pang Li-tin who held a wine and provision stores at 110, Queen's Road Central, failed to answer to his name at the Bankruptcy Court this morning.

Proceedings had commenced last Saturday when the debtor was subjected to searching cross-examination respecting three and a half lakhs, not bound up in this firm, which the debtor admitted having had at one time.

"This morning, the Official Receiver said that he had sent twice to the debtor's home and his firm address and had not got into touch with the man. He was given to believe that the debtor had unsonded."

Mr. D. L. Strellet, who represented the debtor in the other proceedings, said all that he could say was that he had sent for the debtor yesterday afternoon and he had not put in an appearance.

After evidence had been given by the court interpreter as to the debtor having been warned to attend to-day after the initial proceedings, a warrant was issued for the arrest of the debtor.

A petition by creditors of the Lee On. Bank was withdrawn. Mr. C. A. S. Russ (for the Bank) referred to an application in Chambers for the withdrawal and the Official Receiver (Mr. E. L. Agassiz) was satisfied that the Bank was solvent. It had to be given time to collect outstanding accounts.

Liquidator Appointed.
In the case of the Kam-Wing Bank (in liquidation) the Official Receiver said that meetings of creditors, and contributors, respectively, had been held when similar resolutions were passed unanimously, viz., that Mr. J. Hennessy Seth be appointed liquidator, under security of $50,000 and that an inspection committee of five be appointed, this number comprising creditors and/or contributors (or their qualified proxies).

At the meeting the creditors represented $295,000 and the contributors $300,000. Assets were $700,000 and liabilities just over $1,000,000.

His Lordship made the order as requested.

## [12] STEAMER AGROUND.

**LOCAL HARBOUR RED FOULED:**

**REFLOATED OFF.**

The report of the Danish steamer "Tongking," on arrival here from Singapore, this morning, informs the Harbour Office that at 7.45 a.m. the ship ground-ed in a position about 1½ cables (900 feet) from the Kowloon "Star" Ferry wharf. The depth of water was (aft) 5½ fathoms, (forward) 4½ fathoms.

The ship is of 4,244 tons registered. She was drawing about 28 feet of water at the time and was making for the Kowloon Godown Co.'s wharves.

Previously that part of the harbour had been dredged to give a depth of 31 feet at dead low water. Presumably silting has recommenced.

Two boys playing on the hillside near the University were injured yesterday in attempting to run away from a number of dogs belonging to Mr. G. W. Keeton.

Brought on to Hongkong for burial was the body of Mr. Ernest F. Fox, a British saloon passenger on the s.s. "Dampto" from Saigon, who died at sea at 10.80 p.m. on January 29.

"As well as can be expected" is the report from the Government Civil Hospital this morning when an inquiry was made about Mr. J. H. Kynoch, senior works inspector of the architectural office, P.W.D., who was operated for gastric ulcer on Tuesday, after admission.

## [14] COLONY'S RULES.

**COUNCIL REFERENCE**

A more complete list of rules and regulations existing in the Colony will be available shortly. This morning, in the Legislative Council, the Attorney General said that everyone who had had to work the old one, knew that the new was long overdue. When the Bill was passed, it was proposed to appoint Mr. Dyer Ball to edit the book.

Mr. Dyer Ball had edited excellently a new edition of the Ordinances the first edition of which would appear a few weeks after this Bill became law. Preliminary work had been put in extending over a considerable period and Mr. Nihill had been responsible for some of it. When finished, it would be a complete record of the rules and regulations existing in the Colony.

## [15] MORE ROBBERIES.

Five armed robbers took money and jewellery amounting to $101.85 from a Chinese dwelling near Eastern Street on Thursday night and escaped with the haul. Several persons were subsequently arrested on suspicion.

A robber who was foiled in his attempt by the screams of a woman at a house in Praya East early this morning bolted after cutting her slightly in the face and shoulder.

Property to the value of $16.50 was taken by thieves by breaking into the Railway Recreation Club at Chatham Road last night.
