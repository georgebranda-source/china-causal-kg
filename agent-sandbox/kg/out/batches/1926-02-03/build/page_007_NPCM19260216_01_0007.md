## [8] GERMANY'S

**BRITAIN NON-COMMITTAL**

*(Router's Service.)*

*London, Feb. 15.*

The "Daily Telegraph"'s diplomatic correspondent says that there is reason to believe that the German Ambassadors and Ministers accredited to the principal League States are being instructed to inform the Governments of those States that the German Government may have to reconsider its position toward the League if the structure of the League Council is modified before Germany has taken a seat on it.

The “Daily Telegraph’s” Geneva correspondent speaks of the possibility of Germany asking for a postponement of the meeting of the League Assembly fixed for March 8, until the various States have come to an understanding.
Sir Eric Drummond (the League Secretary) has gone to Berlin to discuss the matter with Dr. Stresemann (German Chancellor).

Router learns on high authority that there is no truth whatsoever in the report, circulated abroad, to the effect that Great Britain has agreed to the proposal that permanent sents on the League Council should be awarded to Poland, Spain, and Brazil in addition to Germany.

*More Hedging. London, Feb. 15.*

In the House of Commons, in reply to questions, Sir Austen Chamberlain, Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs, said that a special meeting of the Assembly of the League had been convened for March 8 to consider Germany's application for admission.

Asked whether he could "remove considerable apprehension both here and abroad by stating that the British Government was opposed to any considerable increase

in the Council Sir Austen Chamberlain referred to his reply on the 10th inst. "He said that he gave a considered reply in the hope that he would not be asked other questions until shortly before, he went to Geneva.

Asked whether Germany's application would be considered separately from the applications of other States, Sir Austen Chamberlain said that he was unable to answer for the League in the same specific way as he answered for the Foreign Office.

Technical Questions.

Sir Eric Drummond has arrived from Geneva and had a lengthy conversation with Dr. Strespemann. It is emphasised that Sir Eric Drummond has not come to Berlin to discuss the question of the number of seats on the League Council, which is a matter for the Governments concerned, but to confer with Dr. Strespemann on technical questions in connection with Germany's entry to the League, such as, for example, the ceremony and procedure to be adopted at Geneva when the Assembly meets and Germany's application comes up for consideration.

[Sir Austen Chamberlain in his reply on the 10th inst., said that the application of Germany, as was probably inevitable, had opened the whole question of the composition of the Council. It had led to claims from other quarters, but until it was impossible to reach a final decision as regards the course that would best serve the interests of the Council and the League. He would make a further statement before his departure to attend the Council in March, if possible, but he thought that as a good deal of difference of opinion must at first exist, it would defeat the whole object of the forthcoming meetings and destroy the League's usefulness and influence, if the representatives were bound in advance by rigid instructions which would render a common agreement impossible. The only addition which he was willing to make to the foregoing statement was that more than two other Powers had applied for seats on the Council.]

## [12] SOCIAL HYGIENE

**POSITION IN THE STRAITS SETTLEMENTS.**

**HOME GOVERNMENT MOVES**

*London, Feb. 15*

Replying to a question as to what action was being taken in connection with the report of the Advisory Committee on Social Hygiene in the Straits Settlements in 1925, Mr. L. S. Amery, Secretary of State for the Colonies said action was being taken on the general lines suggested, notably questions of Immigration of female with male Chinese, in regard to which the Governor of the Straits (Sir Laurence Guillemard) reported that there was already a continued improvement in the sex ratio.

For many years the questions of housing, education, and recreation had been engaging attention.
The Government did not think that it was necessary to appoint the suggested Mixed Commission, but the medical service was being organised on the lines suggested.
He added that whilst the Straits Government aimed at the disappearance of the brothel, progress must be gradual. Finally, the Government was paying close attention to the recommendation for an increase in the personnel and for an extension of the powers of the Chinese Protectorate.
