## [4] BANDIT ATTACK

**AMEPICAN MISSIONARY IN FUKIEN**

**SERVANT SERIOUSLY WOUNDED**

From Our Own Correspondent.)

*Amoy, Jan. 17.*

Word has just been received that the Rev. Henry A. Poppen, an American missionary of the Reformed Church in America, with his family was attacked by bandits while en route from Amoy to his station, Lungyenchow, in the interior of South Fukien. The bandits shot one of Mr. Poppen's servants, inflicting a serious wound, and then took charge of all baggage.

Almost everything of value was taken including clothes, money, and jewellery. After the baggage had been completely runsacked, the bandits were satisfied and the missionaries were allowed to proceed to their destination.

Mr. Poppen, with his wife and two children, had just returned to China from furlough. As their station is rather difficult to reach, supplies for some months to come were being carried along to Lungvenchoy. Most of these were taken along with other things.

## [5] EXPENSIVE "FIND."

**MARINE AGENT AND OLD SHIP**

**ARMS IN HOUSE.**

A "find" of arms on a vessel which he as marine dealer had bought to break up led to the appearance at the Central Magistracy this morning of the master of the Sang Loong firm.
The weapons were found in his house as a result of information given to the police.

Mr. Leo d'Almada's defence for the man was that the weapons had been placed out of the children's way and there never had been intention to use them. They had lain there since 1923 when the ship was bought.

The Police evidence was that the arms were not much good. A fine of $50 was imposed.

a) the strikers would elect three members of their Committee to be delegates with full powers to negotiate, one of them almost certainly being the Chairman of the Strike Committee.

(c) other members of the Strike Committee, probably six, and perhaps one or two additional merchants, would be present at the negotiations to switch the proceedings: and

(d) that a minor Cantonese official could be attached to the delegation as Secretary.

## [11] JAPAN'S CONSUL

**YESTERDAY'S PLEASANT GATHERING.**

The Japanese Consul-General, Mr. Y. Murakami, gave a tiffin party at the Hongkong Hotel yesterday. The gathering was quite informal, and was held in Mr. Murakami's words, "to strengthen old friendships and to make new ones."

Among those present were the Colonial Secretary, Mr. E. R. Hallifax; the Colonial Treasurer, Mr. C. McI. Messenger; the Director of Public Works, Mr. H. T.
