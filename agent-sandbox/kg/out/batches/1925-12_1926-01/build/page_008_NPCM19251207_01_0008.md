## [8] LOCAL AND GENERAL.

The International Traders, Ltd., hope to make arrangements for the reopening of the Boa Vista Hotel, Mucao, by Christmas or early New Year.

There will a Public Lecture at the Helena May Institute this evening at 5.30 p.m. given by Professor J. R. Shellshear, D.S.O., entitled: "Early Man in Borneo." Admittance free.

The Infantry Company, Scottish Company, and the Mounted Infantry Company of the Hongkong Volunteer Defence Corps participated in a field day at Fanling yesterday. A strong muster turned out and field firing practices and other training was carried out.

The Cathedral Architect reports that St. Peter's Church, West Point, is in an unsafe condition. The council of the Church have recently sanctioned urgent repairs costing almost two thousand dollars. An appeal is to be made immediately to raise this sum.

Messrs. Lane, Crawford, Ltd., have just published their general catalogue for 1926. It is a book of 225 pages and a regular mine of useful information for all householders. Without it one does not realise the wide range and variety of the goods on offer by this big departmental store, and, moreover, it is an exceedingly useful publication for checking prices. A copy should be in the possession of every family and Messrs. Lane Crawford will be pleased to supply them on application. The catalogue covers all sections of the business with the exception of the grocery department. The groceries price list, owing to the constant fluctuations, is issued monthly.

A final dividend of 5 per cent, free of Income Tax, for the year ended June 30, 1925, has been declared by the Kallan Mining Administration.

The Second Concert in aid of the Organ Fund will be given in the Cathedral Hall on Wednesday, next December, at 6 p.m. (sharp). The Hongkong Male Voice Choir--(Conductor, Mr. Stanley Collett), will assist and the soloists are Mrs. Minney, Mr. Li Chor-chi and Mr. H. E. Gardner. Tickets (one dollar) may be obtained from the Organist, at the Cathedral Hall.

Messrs. Vernon & Smyon, in their weekly share report, say:
During the week under review business in the Local Share Market opened with some enquiry and rates generally took a turn for the better though only a small volume of business resulted; sellers holding off for better prices which still show a large capital depreciation; during the latter part of the week enquiry has not been so much in evidence and although the market closes firm very little business has been negotiated. The money market still appears to be tight in spite of a possible lifting of the boycott in the near future, when this becomes a fact we believe all stocks will show considerable advancement or at least nominal rates to-day will revert to buying prices.

"The Shanghai Market has been active during the week especially in 'Rubber' and 'Cpttons' where a huge volume of business has been done both for cash and forward delivery.

With “Rubber”. And up again in Singapore the market there has been active throughout with still an upward tendency, business has been done in almost all local stocks and the market closes firm with buyers predominating. “Tin” closes firm with less business passing.

The Chinese press reports that Marshal Sun Chun-feng proposes to reorganise the Shanghai Chinese police and to increase their number to 5,000, strong in order to preserve peace and order locally.

A certain busy City man in London has a very good method of dealing with barbers. As soon as he is seated in the chair he says: "Cut the whole three short." "What three sir," asks the barber. "Hair, whiskers, and chatter," replies the busy man.

One really extraordinary achievement of the Mormons was the erection of a great building with absolutely perfect acoustics. This is the vast Mormon Tabernacle at Salt Lake City, with its egg-shaped auditorium and seating accommodation for 9,000 people. The splendid acoustic properties of the building greatly impressed that keen observer, the late Lord Leverhulme, and he declared that it was, for once, no exaggeration to say that the dropping of a pin on the platform of the Tabernacle could really be heard at the other end of the building.

Dr. Temple, Bishop of Manchester, speaking at the luncheon of the Manchester Publicity Club, last month, warned his hearers that religion could not be worked by "stunts." Giving an example of the kind of publicity he de-precated, Dr. Temple said: "You see in America this sort of Church announcement: 'If you will come inside here you will hear a Church yell'by mussed choirs.' "The chief evil from which our age is suffering (the continued) is restlessness and dissipation. We are not sufficiently quiet at any period of life or for any length of time, to strike deep roots. In consequence we have, comparatively speaking, practically ceased to grow great individuals."

## [9] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

Rev. E. K. Quick of St. Stephen's College, leaves on the 16th inst. He is not returning. His going will be a distinct loss to local cricket.

Father José Algue, whose name is so well known throughout the East for his work at the Manila Observatory, is reported to be retiring from the Directorship of the Weather Bureau owing to failing eyesight. It is stated that in that event he will receive a life-long pension under the Philippines Retirement Act. Father Selga, the present Assistant Director, is mentioned as his successor.

The second gramophone concert was given in the lounge of the Hotel Metropole yesterday, at 9.15 p.m. The Anderson Music Co. kindly lent one of the recent Grafonola models and some of the new process records for the occasion. The programme included numbers from the operas Othello, Carmen, Rigoletto and Pagliacci and instrumental items by W. H. Squire and others. Madame Clara Butt was also represented, and the famous 850 voices record was included. The recital was under the direction of Mr. R. Wilson of Messrs. Trollope and Colls Ltd.
The seventh ordinary yearly meeting of shareholders of the China Light and Power Co. (1918), Ltd., is advertised for December 21, at 11:30 a.m.

It is an interesting fact that the Chief Scout Sir Robert Baden Powell is an artist of considerable ability. At the recent show, of pictures and sculpture by Army Officers, one of the most striking exhibits was a modelled figure of a Boy Scout. This was one of Sir Robert's productions. To the same exhibition he contributed several paintings. Sir Robert is a great believer in the educational values of art in general and modelling in particular, and at his Hampshire home his children are encouraged to follow the paternal example in a large sandpit specially prepared for them.

A large portrait of King George, by Mr. Charles Sims, Keeper of the Royal Academy, who was commissioned by the Council of that body for this one of its series of "Royal Patrons," has been rejected. Exhibited at the Academy last year, it provoked controversy because of the rather odd pose of the slight figure within mountainous robes. Sir Frank Dicksee, President of the Royal Academy, told the "Westminster Gazette" that the rejection was quite an amisable affair. "There was considerable difference of opinion," he said, "when the proposal for the purchase was brought before the Academy, so Mr. Sims decided to withdraw it rather than reopen a controversy."

Miss V. M. Aspinall leaves for home this month after your years of service at the Diocesan Girls' School, Kowloon.

Chaliapine's appearance at the Albert Hall reminds a reader of a conversation which he overheard on the occasion of his last visit. Two ladies listened with great attention to his first song; then one turned to the other and said, tentatively: "He's a tenor, isn't he?" The other looked at her, in surprise. "He sounds like a baritone!" she affirmed. Hastily they turned to the programme, and naturally discovered that the great singer is a bass. That is the result of having a voice of unusual range.

Out of Russia has come a strangely designed flat gold box, studded with precious jewels, reputed to be the cigarette case of Rasputin, "the Mad Monk," who wielded a powerful influence over the Czar and who was assassinated during the war. The case is now in the possession of M. Milan of Boston, who purchased it from Jacques H. Jopkeen. Jewellers have appraised the value of the metal, stones and workmanship at more than $2,000. Evidence that the case was the property of Rasputin is said to be good and, it is said, is strengthened by the story of a gift by the Czarina of such a case to the monk.

There will be no Watchpight service at the St. Andrew's Church on New Year's eve, as the noise of Chinese firecrackers renders a service quite impracticable.

Among the passengers who left for San Francisco by the "President Pierce" on Saturday was Mr. A. E. Dome, of the Chinese Y.M.C.A., the well-known physical instructor and baseball player.

Lieut D. A. Searle, 1st Bn. East Surrey Regt., rejoined the Unit from a course of instruction at Home per the s.s. "City of Cairo", Lieut. C. J. Yeo, of the same Regiment, returned from a similar course of instruction, per the s.s. "Kashgar".

Visiting a London hospital the Rev. "Tubby" Clayton, of "Too H" fame, found a dying ex-soldier whose one desire was to see the Prince of Wales, with whom he had been in close contact in France during the War. It was midnight before the request could be communicated to His Royal Highness, who reached the hospital just in time to grip the poor fellow's hand before he died.

At the last meeting of the Kobe Women's Club, on November 17, the Rev. W. H. Erskine spoke on "Japanese Customs and their Meaning." Mr. Erskine recently published a book on this subject, which has been very well received. His theme is one that is susceptible of a good deal of argument, as he finds analogies between Oriental and Western customs, beliefs, and ideals, and founds on these a theory of the unity of human aspiration.

On November 17, Miss Ruth St. Denis and Mr. Ted Shawn were guests of the American Women's Club at Tea. Miss St. Denis graciously favoured the club with a vivacious talk on interpretative dancing and what she concelves to be a new American renaissance for the dance. Miss St. Denis was followed by Mr. Ted Shawn who spoke of the aims and purposes of the Denishawn school of dancing, adding that they were working towards the establishment of an all-American ballet.
