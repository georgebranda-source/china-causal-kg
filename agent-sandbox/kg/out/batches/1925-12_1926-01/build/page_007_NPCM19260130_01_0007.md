## [1] LOCARNO SPIRIT

**PLHA FOR CO-OPERATION O POWER**

**PATIENCE STILL NEEDED.**

*(British Wireless Service)*

Rugby. Jan. 29.

Sir Austen Chamberlain, accompanied by his son, will arrive in London this evening at the conclusion of the first extensive holiday he has had for a very considerable period. He has been absent from England for about five weeks, having proceeded to Rapallo immediately after the mid-December meetings of the League in Geneva. He has completely recovered from the effects of influenza from which he was suffering a fortnight ago. His conversation with M. Briand in Paris yesterday was of a general character and touched on a great number of subjects in a broad way.

In his subsequent interview with newspaper correspondents, he was able to state in regard to the meeting (fixed for February 15) of the Committee charged with preparation for the Disarmament Conference that there had been no request for adjournment and that it was certain no such request would come from the British Government. If such proposal were made, he personally would regret it though it might be worth the sacrifice if the ultimate object were thereby to benefit. Any more attempt to get rid of the question would he said, be opposed by the Powers which had supported the idea at Geneva.

## [4] GREAT CORDIALITY.

### Spirit of Faithful Co-operation.

(Havaas Agency.)

*Paris, Jan: 29.*

The papers note the great cordiality of the talk between M. Briand and Sir Austen Chamberlain, which, they state, dealt also with Oriental questions, such as Tangier, Morocco, Egypt, Syria, and Chinese affairs, in a spirit of faithful cooperation, taking account of the particular interests of both nations.

In the Chamber, M. Briand declared that when he formed the Government he found a constant drain for repayment of Treasury and National Defence bonds, which amounted to 700,000,000 francs monthly. At present the drain was only for 100,000,000 francs, monthly, which was the result of restored confidence.

Rugby; Jan...29:

A banquet in honour of Prince Chichibu, of Japan, who is making a prolonged sojourn in England to study social and other conditions here, was given by the Japan Society last night.

Prince Chichibu said that during his short stay here he had already learned to know much of the charm of England, her scenery, her ancient buildings, and her culture. That knowledge made him realise the valuable work which the Society could do in extending knowledge of Japan and Japanese art, literature, and life.

It was his sincere hope that East and West might continue to learn much from each other to the lasting advantage of both and to the lasting peace of the world.

Lord Salisbury, after expressing his profound condolences with Japan on the great loss suffered by the death of Viscount Kato, alluded to the smoothness with which Japan had invariably proceeded.

The tendency was now toward a combination of Powers, Locarno was an example of the effort to include in one great international agreement a very large number of Powers. The League of Nations was another example of the same great tendency toward universal agreement along the path of peace.

In various parts of the world and in the Far East especially elements of disorder were profoundly alarming and it was of vital importance that the Powers which by long tradition and habit of national discipline were able to act as a check and control upon forces of disorder should be themselves as nearly allied as possible. Japan was one of those Powers.

## [8] NO SHACKLES.

**LABOUR DESIRES PERFECT FREEDOM.**

**CONDITION OF LIBERAL HELP.**

*(British Wireless Service.)*

*Rugby, Jan. 29.*

Referring to the suggestion of co-operation in Parliament between the Labour and Liberal Parties. Mr. Philip Snowden, Labour M.P. for Colne Valley, contemplated the possibility of

Labour, being represented by the largest Party, with the Liberal holding the balance of power. If, in the next Parliament, the Liberals were willing to support Labour, his own Party, he said, would be ready to accept from any section anything which that section would give and of which Labour approved.

Labour was willing to co-operate with any Party in getting something they wanted to secure; but no Labour Government would ever accept help from any other group except on the condition that it had perfect freedom in the constituencies and was not shackled in the slightest degree.

*London, Jan. 28*

The late Viscount Kato is the subject of long eulogistic obituary notices in the Press. The London morning papers dwell on his friendship for Britain, which was instrumental in initiating the Anglo-Japanese Alliance.

The Morning Post," in a leader, regrets the departure from the written alliance, which provided a basis for joint policy in China, which was never needed more than to-day. It says that a common understanding by all the Powers may not be possible, but the immediate needs of the situation are served by close accord between Britain and Japan.

## [11] JAPANESE TRIBUTE:

This morning's papers unanimously deplore Viscount Kato's death, paying high tribute to the ex-Premier's qualities.

Most papers consider that the Kenseikai Ministry should remain in office, as an early stabilisation of the political situation outweighs all other considerations.

The general consensus of opinion in the papers and among publicists is that Mr. Wakatsuki is fully qualified for the Premiership, though the "Jiji" reports that a section of the Upper House consider the Kenseikai well advised to form a coalition with the Seiyuhonto, under Baron Yamamoto, who, it is stated, commands the confidence of both Parties.

## [14] SHIPPING DEAL

**SALE OF HUGO STINNES FLEET.**

**PROVISIONAL AGREEMENT.**

*(Router's Service.)*

Negotiations for the sale of the entire Hugo Stinnes Fleet to the Deutsch Australische Shipping Company and the Kosmos Company has resulted in a provisional agreement.

The Stinnes Fleet consists of 28 modern freight and passenger oversen steamers of a total tonnage of 140,855; and two motor ships of 10,800 tons now under construction.

The Deutsch Australische and Kosmos Companies have a capital of 18,000,000 and 12,000,000 marks, respectively. It is stated that the transaction will necessitate an increase in the capital of both companies.

## [16] PERFINENT FRENCH DEMAND

*(Router's Service.)*

*Budapest, Jan. 29.*

The forged bank notes affair continues to be the subject of enquiry. A Parliamentary Committee and a police enquiry are demanded.

The French authorities are also pursuing the enquiry. The French Police Commissioner, M. Douleot, is reported to be urging the re-examination of Count Windischgraetz and Nadosy so as to clear up the alleged discrepancies in their evidence.

M. Doucat also declared that it must be accertained where the paper used for the manufacture of the forged notes was procured and where the machinery used in the process was hidden: for the French authorities was of opinion that it would yet be found in Hungary.

The Premier, Count Bethlen, in a speech in regard to the affair, declared that if it was found the Cabinet had made mistakes the Ministers would resign. He was determined to clear up the affair. The Government had nothing to hide; the attacks from abroad originated with some despicable Hungarians, who knew that their only chance of success was to plunge honest leaders in the mira.
