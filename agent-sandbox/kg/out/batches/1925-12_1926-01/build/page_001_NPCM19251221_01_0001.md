## [10] HILSIDE MURDER.

**PRISONER WHO FLED TO SUMICHUN.**

**UREID HY A WOMAN.**

**DBTERMINED STRUGGLE WITH**

Having been entitled from across the border in Chinese territory to British territory at Sumchun by a women, Shing Wai-tat found the police waiting to arrest him in British territory on a charge of murder. He put up a determined struggle but was eventually taken into custody.

Fleiss said to be identified by several witnesses and the police allege that he is the man who, being left in charge of an Indian constable at Nguu Shi Wan for the latter to take him to the Kowloon City police station on a charge of wood-stealing shot the latter and escaped.

The Hon. Mr. A. H. Kempl Atterney-General, reconstructed in supposition at the Criminal Sessions this morning the events which led to the constable's death. He said that the facts pointed to the prisoner having pushed the constable off the path into a nullah, for here were found the constable's turban and the log of wood which the prisoner was being made to carry to the police station as evidence against him.

Dropping his burden it would rather appear that the prisoner rain up the hill with the Indian constable after him. "The latter, finding himself outstripped fires a shot," continued the Attorney-General. "The accused stops and waits for the constable to come up and then struggle with him. I do not say he set out with the intention of murdering the constable but merely to disable him sufficiently for escape. Tempers rise in the course of a struggle and after the one in question the Indian constable was left wounded on the hillside."

The law throws a peculiar attraction about a public servant performing his duty in execution of the law, continued the Attorney-General. If he is killed in the execution of this, the law will imply malice and the offender will be found guilty of murder, no matter whether the circumstances, if applying to a private individual, would justify only a charge of manslaughter.

Prisoner's Disappearance

The disappearance of the prisoner about the time that the struggle on the hillside took place was referred to by the Attorney-General. Prisoner was eventually found to have gone over the border and the wife of one of his acquaintances enticed him to British territory with a story about having disposed of a house he owned in Kowloon City.

In a statement, made after his arrest, the prisoner accused this woman's husband of having offered him a bribe of $100 to kill the constable and a further $50 for going away. The Attorney-General suggested that "filled with rage at the trick played him he might have said to himself, 'I'm in for it now but I will try and get this woman's husband into trouble.'

III Treatment Alleged.

In another statement made later the prisoner denied everything and said that he was falsely charged. He had been roughly treated when first taken to the station, he alleged. He had become silly; he did not know where, heaven was, or earth was. He was thrown into a cell.

There was no direct evidence of the shooting, stated the Attorney-General. A hawker who lived lower down, not far from the scene, heard a shot about the time and a police whistle being blown. He also saw two figures struggling, the one a constable in khaki and the other a Chinese. By the time he had reached the spot with some Europeans who lived not far away the constable was lying wounded in the face and side.

Two constables who had been with the murdered constable when the arrest of the man cutting down wood was made, identified the prisoner as the man who was arrested. They had left their comrade to take the prisoner back to the police station as they

saw other forest pillifers at work. Mr. Elsley Zeitlyn (instructed by G. K. Hall Brutton) represented the prisoner at to-day's Criminal Sessions proceedings where the case is still being heard.

## [11] China

**KOWLOON SHOOTING**

**CRIMINAL SESSIONS PROCEEDINGS.**

The prisoner who is alleged to have tired at Sergt. Clark "in Chatham Road on November 13 appeared at the Criminal Sessions this morning charged with shooting at the Sergeant " with intent to main, disfigure or disable him and to resist lawful apprehension."

Prisoner is a domestic servant employed by Capt. L. D. Joll, M.C., and the revolver alleged to have been used is the property of his master. It was left in an unlocked drawer and it is the case for the Crown that prisoner took the weapon and returned it afterwards.

Sergt. Clark detailed the incident this morning. He became suspicious of three men and followed them down Chatham Road in the direction of Holt's wharf. Seeing one of the men turn round and becoming of the opinion that they suspected he was following them, he turned into "Observatory Road for a time but retraced his steps.

Where "Cameron Road" joins Chatham Road there is a lamp and it was here that he finally called on them to stop. He commenced to search one of the men and this man threw a revolver in the direction of one of his companions. This, the sergeant alleges, is the prisoner. The man in question picked it up and made off. The sergeant fired a shot into the air and alleged that prisoner turned and fired back. Sergt. Clark fired another shot without effect and for the time being the man escaped.

Prisoner was arrested at 2, Chatham Road, Capt. Joll's residence, one of his companions who had been arrested by the police taking them there. Prisoner was found hiding under a bed. The watchman outside had seen the two servants of Capt. Joll running back to the house shortly after the time of the alleged shooting.

In a statement made shortly after arrest the accused said he fired a shot by mistake.

At the Criminal Sessions, this morning the prisoner said "I picked it up; I had it in my hand; I did not intend to shoot him."

The jury found prisoner not guilty of shooting with intent and guilty of possession.
The man who was searched first and arrested by the police on the spot pleaded guilty to being in possession of ammunition (five rounds were found on him).

Sentence will be passed on him and the other prisoner to-morrow morning.

## [12] PRAYA ACCIDENT.

**TRAM AND TRUCK COLLISION.**

**RICE CARCO IN THE SEA**

The sea front near the China Provident godowns at Kennedy Town was the scene of an unusual accident on Saturday afternoon when a two-wheeled truck loaded with rice was thrown into the harbour by a tram car colliding with it.

One of the coolies drawing the truck was also pushed into the water, but he was rescued unhurt. The rice was the property of the Tai Yuen shop, of No. 21, Connaught Road Central.

## [25] EARLIER CABLES.

There has been little fighting; both sides continue to dig entrenchments five miles on the Peking side of Peitsang. The Kuominchun (People's Army) nightly shell the Chihli forces, but there have been no infantry attacks, owing to the previous ones having been beaten off, with heavy losses.

It is believed the Kuomichun are awaiting reinforcements from Peking.

General Li Ching Lin has straightened his defences and is advancing 20 miles north-west of Yangliuching.

The Tangku situation is settled by the Ninth Division, which went to Tongshan from the vicinity of Peking, after Chang's downfall, definitely joining the Chihli forces after flirting with Feng and threatening to advance on Tientsin via Tangku.

Li Ching Lin is thus able to concentrate on the Peitsang front. The railway service to Shan-haikuan is being resumed.

## [26] CAPTURES DOUBTED

Foreign circles doubt the reported capture of Tehchow and Machang but are of opinion that communications between Li Ching, Lin and Chang Tsung Chang are still open.

According to apparently reliable reports 30,000 Shansi soldiers, serving in the Honan armies, demanded that Yen Hsi Shan (Tupan of Shansi) should pay them. Upon his refusal, they invaded Shansi and captured Liaochow. They are badly armed and equipped; and not thought likely to advance far.

A wireless message from Tientsin, received yesterday, states that Chang Tsung Chang has defeated the Honanites at Tehchow.

*TROOPS FROM KOREA. Tokyo, Dec. 19*

The War Office announces that all the troops, detailed on December 15 from Korea, have reached Mukden and have been placed under command of the Kwantung Army.

A "Kokusai" message from Mukden reports that Chang Tso Lin has decided to attack with five divisions of cavalry to-day or tomorrow.

## [27] PLEA FOR ARMISTICE.

A message from Mukden states that as frostbite is causing much suffering in the rival armies outside Mukden, General Kuo, Brigade Commander of Kuo Sung Ling's advance guard, telephoned to General Wu, Brigade Commander of the Mukden Army, suggesting an armistice. Whereupon, according to report, the latter immediately agreed.
Official advices indicate that operations are being held up owing to the extreme cold but do not confirm armistice negotiations.
