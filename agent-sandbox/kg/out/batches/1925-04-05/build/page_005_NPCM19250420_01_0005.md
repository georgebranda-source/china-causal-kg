The weather was a trifle hot for a sports meeting, and consequently the runners especially in the long distance events, had a strenuous time. A new event hitherto not included in a school sports programme was the candle race which provided some excitement, a possible winner having the light blown out almost near the winning post. The relay race open to all schools was won in excellent style by a team from St. Joseph's College.

The Headmaster (Mr. J. S. Shak) welcomed Mrs. Kotewall in a few words, after which the Hon Mr. Kotewall addressed the gathering. He said: "My wife has asked me to say a few words on her behalf before she gives away the prizes. I am sorry that I cannot speak up to-day, because I am suffering from what one of my doctors calls "smoker's throat," though my lips have never made the acquaintance of so much as a cigarette stump. Another doctor calls it a "clergyman's throat," though I have never been given to preaching, which I leave to my good lady. (Laughter). I have come to this sports meeting from another function at which I have had to have my speech read out by another man, and as I have had no time to prepare a speech for this occasion I shall have to content myself with a few remarks upon what I have seen and heard since my arrival here. I learn from your Secretary that the school records in some of the races were maintained; they might have been smashed but for the condition of the track."

Amsterdam, April 18.—The whole city was on fete to welcome the return of the Dutch airmen who flew to Java. A guard of honour, composed of students, accompanied the fliers through the crowds cheering in the streets.

Paris, April 18—For the three months of 1925 France's imports totalled 9,826,000,000 francs, and exports.10,920,000,000. For March the normal permanent revenue returns show 1,980,000,000 francs, compared with 1,961,000,000 last year.—Reuter.

Copenhagen, April 19—The dispute in the textile, boot-making, paper and other smaller industries, which hitherto has left 50,000 idle, henceforth will involve 115,000. This is due to the employers extending the lock-out, owing to the Workers' Unions rejecting the proposal of the mediators.—Reuter.
