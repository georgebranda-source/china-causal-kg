## [8] EUROPEAN ROBBED

**RAINCOAT TAKEN BY INDIAN**

**PAWNED BY "MALAY FRIEND."**

Before Mr S. R. B. McElderry at the Central Magistracy this morning, a young Indian named A. L. Rumjahn was charged with the larceny of a raincoat, valued at $20, at Wanchai on May 3.

Accused denied the charge of theft, but admitted having pawned the article on behalf of a Malay friend, for whom he acted as interpreter at the pawnshop. His friend had secured lodgings with him for the night, and the next morning asked for assistance to pawn the overcoat as he had no money. He could not find his friend, who was believed to be a seaman.

A pawnbroker, giving evidence, stated that the 'accused came to the shop with a raincoat, which he pawned for $8 and left after putting the money in his pocket. Another man was with the accused, but took no part in the transaction.

Detailing the circumstances leading to the arrest of the accused, Sergeant MacDonald stated that he was on duty at Wanchai on the night of May 3, when a ricsha coolie brought a European passenger to him as he did not know where to take him. After questioning the passenger the latter got down and went aside, as he was feeling unwell, being under the influence of liquor. Accused then came up and asked him (witness) what was the matter with the European and he replied that he was drunk. He (witness) left the scene and on returning about fifteen minutes later found that the European had recovered. He had an idea that the European had raincoat with him when he alighted from the ricsha, and on questioning him he admitted that he had one. On being requested to accompany him to the Police Station to lodge a complaint, the passenger asked him not to worry about it and left in another ricsha. Witness met accused again at 3 a.m. yesterday at Praya East. Questioned regarding the raincoat belonging to the European on the night in question, accused denied having stolen it, but admitted having pawned one on behalf of a Major friend. The pawnshop was pointed out to him (witness) and the article was recovered. He added that he had not been the European passenger again and did not know his identity.

## [12] PRAM THIEF.

**ALSO STIGALS VASSES FROM CHURCH.**

A Chinese was charged at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning with the theft of a perambulator belonging to Mr. Hall, and three vases, the property of the Royal Church.

A marine, hawker was recently sentenced to one month's imprisonment for receiving the stolen perambulator, and the hawker's wife recognised the man who sold it when she met him in the street and had him arrested.

The accused admitted the theft of the "vases" by jumping through an open window of the Church:
On the first charge the accused was sentenced to one month's imprisonment and to three months on the other charge.

## [16] PEAK TRAM TICKETS.

**DISTRIBUTED BY CHINESE TO RELATIVES**

The adjourned case against Leung Hung, a coolie of the Peak Tramway Company, for theft of a large quantity of coupon ticket-books was continued before Mr. H. R. Butters at the Central Magis: Tracy this morning.

The evidence was that the coupon tickets were found in a matshed occupied by the accused, and it was alleged that he distributed them among his relatives. A contractor's foreman came to Mr. Scott with a bundle of the missing coupons and informed him that they were given to him by Leung Chin, a relative of the acused. As a result Sub-Inspector Marks arrested Leung Chin, and he was also charged this morning with larceny by finding.

The Magistrate found both the accused guilty and sentenced them to six weeks' and two weeks' hard labour respectively.

## [17] RECKLESS CYCLIST.

**SHALL CHILD INJURED IN CHATHAM ROAD.**

At the Kowloon, Magistracy this morning, an unemployed Chinese was fined $25, or, in the alternative, one month's imprisonment with hard labour, for riding a bicycle in a reckless manner along Chatham Road last night.

It was stated that the accused knocked down Mrs. A. Ribeiro's child, causing slight injuries.

At the Kowloon Magistracy this morning, a Chinese woman was charged with receiving jewellery and money amounting to a shipment stolen from a Chinese woman passenger from Shanghai on the s.s. President Grant. The accused stated that the jewellery was actually stolen by a man, who left the articles with her for safe keeping. Sentence of six months' hard labour was imposed.

## [18] TURNED DOWN.

**CONSTITUTIONAL REFORM REJECTED.**

**HOME DECISION**

News has been received that the Secretary of State for the Colonies does not consider the time's opportunity for the introduction of Constitutional Reform as advocated by the 1,500 Hongkong residents who signed the petition and the matter is not likely to be taken further for some considerable time.

A “China Mail” representative made enquiries this morning as to when a meeting was to be convened on the subject in view of a recent statement of the late President that when a reply was received to enquiries sent home resolutions would be put to a meeting which would be duly advertised.

He was informed that Col. John Ward, M.P., who had been asked to state how the petition stood which had been sent in the name of the Colony's residents, had replied that the Secretary of State for the Colonies had been communicated with and had

expressed the opinion that the time was not "opportunes" for the introduction of such measures as the petition advocated. Col. John Ward was not "therefore disposed to press the matter in the House and it was not likely that the Association would proceed further." In the matter at any rate for some considerable time, the "China Mail" man gathered.

The petition referred to was circulated in the Colony and copies of it and other information about the Colony, it may be recalled, were sent to every member of the House of Commons. Several Parliamentarians known to be specially interested in this part of the Empire were communicated with. Through some technical defect the petition was referred back and before it could come up again the Coalition Government was dissolved. Since then no further news was received until the reply from Col. John Ward came to hand as to the attitude of the Secretary of State for the Colonies.
The exact nature of the desired changes in constitution were not actually formulated in the petition which was sent Home, but the object in sending the petition was to ensure that Parliament should be better acquainted with the position in Hongkong, it being the belief of many that the authorities at Home were genuinely in sympathy with the claims of the community and that it is practicable scheme of reform could be found they would find it. The unfavourable reply which has now been received, however, does away effectively with any hope of support being received from the Government and in the belief that the Government with its large majority would effectively quash any private member's measure it is apparently the intention of those in interested to drop the subject for the present.

## [19] $700 JUNK RAID

**ROBBERS' MIDNIGHT EXPLOIT.**

A report was made to the police by the master of a trading junk that "three men armed with revolvers and daggers entered his boat which was lying at the Yaumati shelter at midnight yesterday, and stole $700 in Chinese subsidiary coins. All the inmates were tied up, and the robbers left in a small boat belonging to the junk, which was later found on the foreshore near Dundas Street.

## [20] NOT LIKELY!

**TINSMITH AND SILK GOODS.**

The attitude of a tinsmith summoned in the Summary Court this morning in connection with a claim for goods sold and delivered was "is it likely that I should order silk" for making shoes in connection with my business?" His claim was that the order, although it was chopped with the correspondence chop of the firm, was not obligatory on him as it had been affixed by a guarantor who had no authority.

Mr. Justice Wood found in favour of the defendant who was represented by Mr. Gordon Leask. Plaintiff was represented by Mr. C. A. S. Russ.

Eight houses in McGregor Street, Wanchai, were sold in one lot for $75,000 at the China Auction Room yesterday. The property has an area of 5,295 sq. yards and comprises the odd numbers from 23 to 35. It was started at $50,000 by Mr. E.V.M.R. de Sousa and rose by bids of $1,000 and $500. The property was brought by Messrs Tang Kim and Fung Po.

## [21] SEEKING WORK.

**EUROPEAN STOWAWAY IN COURT.**

A Welshman named Frederick Owen Richards was charged before the Kowloon Magistrate this morning with stowing away on the s.s. Empress of Canada from Manila to Hongkong.

Accused said that he came from Australia to seek work in Manila, but not succeeding in this he came to Hongkong. He had served in the war and had lost his papers.
A remand was made for two weeks to enable further enquiries to be made.

## [22] AFTER REVENGE.

**CHEUNG CHAU JUNK ATTACKED.**

一

A Cheung Chau fisherman reported to the police that his junk was attacked yesterday near Mui Wan island in Chinese waters by five men armed with revolvers and two rifles. The complainant and the crew of three men were taken to the island, but he managed to escape.

The motive for the attack is believed to be found in the resentment felt by the islanders against others poaching on their reserve.
