## [1] LOCAL AND GENERAL.

**THE BUICK SIX.**

Singapore's contribution to the St. Paul's Cathedral Restoration Fund totals $24,397.98.

Owners express surprise that a car so powerful and spirited as the BUICK SIX operates so smoothly and quietly. Such outstanding performance explains why the car is winning and holding the good will of all who buy it.

"Stated to have motored at 35 miles an hour, Richard Leslie Friday, of Walthamstow, did not appear at West Ham, but sent a blank cheque. He was fined £5."

A human dynamo is the name applied to Johnny Hines, the screen comedian. The reason is seen in his acting, especially that in “Little Johnny,” at the World Theatre showing for two more days only.

The Hongkong Angling Club announces a Salt Water Competition in May under conditions which have been notified to members. Members are asked to notify the Hon. Secretary, Mr. E. B. C. Hornell if they desire to participate in the competition.

Two boys were charged at Highgate with breaking into a tennis pavilion. Magistrate: Did you go to play tennis? One boy: No; to play truant.

Introduced as a “distinguished visitor” to a gathering of Leicestershire children, the Bishop of Peterborough asked them to identify him. “You are the Pope,” was the answer.

The Rev. A. D. Stewart will conduct a series of Bible Studies at the Feleno May Institute on Friday Morning. The First will be on Friday 17, at 10.80 a.m. The meeting is open to all women.

The film, The Epic of Everest is only booked for 50 of 4,000 cinemas in Great Britain. The Daily Express scathingly comments on the fact that a wonderful picture dealing with the real heroism and real adventure apparently does not appeal to theatre managers because it contains no love interest and makes no sex appeal. This film will be shown in Hongkong.

Mr. J. T. Easterbrook and Mr. F. C. Douglas returned to the Colony by the Mishima Maru yesterday.
The Edinburgh Savings Bank funds show an increase in 1924, compared with the previous year, of £48,780. There was a steady progress in the business of the bank, and the total amount deposited in all departments is now just under £9,500,000. Over 11,000 new accounts were opened during the past year. At the annual meeting of the bank Mr. Sleigh, the Lord Provost, spoke of the appeal the savings bank made to the small investor, and pointed out that, of the amount deposited during the year, 68 per cent of the sums were under £5. Lord Sands, one of the Scottish judges, said it was estimated that practically every second person in Edinburgh was a small investor.

Major R. S. S. Paton has become President of the Garrison Recreation Club.

The Rev. Father Tlaczek is now the officiating clergyman to the Roman Catholic troops in Hongkong.

Mr. William Evans, of Tyncelyn, Llanfrothen; Merioneth, who is in his 75th year, has retired, after serving over 40 years as houndsman with the Ynysfor Hounds. He took part in 500 fox and otter hunts.

Captain J. Macready, D.S.O., who is D.A.A. and Q.M.G. of the China Command, has been promoted to the rank of Major. Major Macready is a grandson of "the great Macready," who in his day was perhaps England's greatest tragedy, and is himself an amateur actor of very high attainments.

Mr. Max Malini, the magician, who entertained a number of parties in Tokio and Yokohama, on his recent trip to Japan, has conjured up a total of Y.420 for the Yokohama General Hospital by a benefit entertainment on board the President Lincoln. The purser took that amount to Tokio when the President Lincoln returned from the United States.

Mr. and Mrs. R. E. O. Bird leave the Colony on retirement on Tuesday next. Mr. Bird is well known as headmaster of the Government's Ellis Kadoorie School, and as a cricketer—particularly as a bowler—of considerable note. Before her marriage, Mrs. Bird, as Miss Clarke was head of the Bellies Girls' School. Both are assured of the best wishes of the community in their retirement from active participation in the life of the Colony.

Reuter cables the death of John Sergeant the eminent artist; one of the most eminent portrait painters of his day, who was born in 1850 at Florence. He received his education in Italy and in France and studied under Carolus Duran. He first exhibited at the Paris Salon in 1897 and since that date has continuously exhibited there and at the Royal Academy, he was elected an A.R.A. and later was made full Academician. Prior to the war he was the most fashionable as well as one of the greatest and most during portrait painters. His principal works are El Jaleo and the portraits of Duran, Ellen Terry, Joseph Chamberlain, Mr. Carl Meyer and children, Octavin Hill, Lady Faudel Phillips. He will be remembered as the painter also of a war picture which aroused wide comment at the time it was hung at the Royal Academy. Gassed.

One case of small pox; one of diphtheria and one of enteric fever (all Chinese—small pox case imported) were notified for the 24 hours ended Tuesday last.

The hearing of the injunction by the Pacific Mail Steamship Co., against the sale by the U.S. Shipping Board of the five Trans-Pacific steamers at present operated by the Pacific Mail Company, has been postponed until April 20.

A treaty of extradition has been signed by Rumania and the United States which provides that Rumania is not now compelled to extradite persons who would suffer capital punishment in America. In Rumania capital punishment is illegal.

Two septuagenarian all-the-year-round bathers were present at the St. Pancras Swimming Club's annual dinner—Mr. G. Webster, the president, aged 77, who won a race last Christmas in Hampstead Ponds, and Mr. R. Ledger, aged 73, of the Serpentine Swimming Club.

Employment throughout Canada showed considerable recovery during February from the losses at the beginning of January, as indicated by reports of employers on February 1, Statements from 5,832 firms, employing 709,878 persons, show an increase of 16,821 more than on January 1, 1925. Improvement in manufacturing was most pronounced, while logging and construction also afforded largely increased employment. Mining and trade, however, showed contractions. Activity in all, except the Prairie Provinces, increased considerably, but the revival in Quebec was not marked.

Lt. Osborne Woods is reported to have delivered himself of the following:

"Money earned by hard work is not only honestly earned, but not easily spent. I feel that if I buckle down to work, I not only will get back some of the money I have squandered, but will regain the respect of my friends which indeed is more than money can buy."

Mr. F.E. Weatherly who married a second time at 74, and now takes silk at 76, has been described as the most prolific English song writer of the day, and once confessed that he did not know how many he had written, as a curious superstition kept him from ever counting them. But two years ago he told a newspaper representative that the actual number was well over 2,000. His "Nancy Lee" was the effort of but a few minutes and other songs which are very popular are "Nirvana," "The Deathless Army" "Darby and Joan," "We've Come Up From Somerset," "Roses of Picardy," "Thora," and the "Star of Bethlehem."

E. E. White, better known to his many friends as “Blanco”, has left Manila en route to his home in Canada where he will join his aged mother and remain with her, having retired from the great financial institution, The Sun Life of Canada, which he has served well for twenty seven years as the general representative in the Philippines and Borneo. Probably no other man has played so prominent a part in the development of golf in islands as has “Blanco” White had come, to Manila during the stirring days of ninety eight when the Americans were pouring troops into the Philippines to put down what had developed into an insurrection and already was an enthusiastic golfer. Almost immediately with the help of prominent Britishers and Americans he began to lay plans for a golf course.
