## [9] PARIS RIOT.

*(Reuter's Service.)*

A warm debate in the Chamber on the rioting in the Latin quarter on the 28th of last month terminated at three in the morning with a vote of confidence in the Government of 318 to 22. M. Albert, the Minister of Education, condemned the movement as political and refused to countenance sabotage in the university or to recognise the right of the students to appoint the professors.

According to the newspapers a recently formed committee for the defence of university liberties issued a manifesto calling on the students to declare a forty-eight hour strike.

A medical student was sentenced yesterday in connection with the disturbances of the 28th ult. He sat during his examination flanked by two policemen.

**JERUSALEM SIDELIGHTS.**

### 1.1

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*JERUSALEM, April 1*

Fifty leading institutions of learning and academic bodies throughout the world were represented at the inauguration.

Hours before the ceremony the narrow dusty road to Mount Scopus was black with vehicles. Jews, old and young, were every-

The pedlars did good business selling "Balfour chocolates, cakes and cigarettes."

Only three of the five sections of the amphitheatre up to the present have been hitherto completed. These were built five weeks ago by 300 Jews, many of whom were students.

Thousands were turned away, although many tramped long distances. For instance 400 colonists walked from Jaffa.

The procession from the University to the amphitheatre was most imposing.

The ceremony opened with the British national Anthem, after which, Jewish choir entertained the gathering with some beautiful singing in the amphitheatre which presented a remarkable spectacle, with her upon tier of closely packed humanity.

After the opening speech by Mr. Weizmann, Sir Herbert Samuel conveyed the British Government's good wishes and read a telegram from the Foreign Secretary hoping that the project would be successful, spreading culture and enlightenment on the historic soil of Palestine.

## [12] NOTABLE EXPERIMENT.

Earl Balfour proceeded to dilate on Jewish intellectual achievement in various parts of the globe for centuries past.

It was a profound mistake to suppose that the Jews, although scattered, had not borne their full share in the progress of knowledge and the growth of civilisation throughout the world.

He confidently anticipated that in the future they would be able to give even more important aid.

Earl Balfour said he was convinced that the unparalleled experiment of adapting Western methods and the Western form of university to an Eastern site and the use of an Eastern language were predestined to inevitable success, in spite of the difficulties which would be met by Dr. Welzmann, Sir Herbert Samuel and their successors.

Earl Balfour singled out the use of Hebrew as one of their problems.

## [17] BRILLIANT. RECEPTION.

An interminable procession of motor cars crawled up the slopes of the Mount of Olives last night to the Government House where Sir Herbert Samuel's brilliant reception on the eve of Lord Balfour's opening of the new Hebrew University was held.
It was a picturesque and historic assemblage and included Lord Allenby, Mr. Chaim Weizmann and numerous delegates. All Palestine was represented. Ecclesiastics of every sect from Abyssinians, Copts, from sombre black to whiteclad Dominican friars, Lord Balfour was the most noteworthy figure present, overtopping the rest and looking very fit and very cheerful.

The University was built on Mount Scopus and designed to be the centre of advanced research in all branches of science and learning, for which Palestine offers special facilities. It includes a microbiological institute to investigate local infectious diseases, which will form the nucleus of medical faculty. Another institute will form the nucleus of a chemical faculty. Still another will specialise in Jewish religion literature, law and language. Teaching will be provided for Arabs.
