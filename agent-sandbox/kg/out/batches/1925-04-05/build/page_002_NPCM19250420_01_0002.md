## [12] A FORTNIGHT'S REPORT

**FLAX**

The Irish markets have been better supplied since last report, but demand was sufficient to purchase the larger tonnage offered at unchanged prices.

Some districts have now finished scutching but growers are holding their fibre in the hope of prices advancing should there be any improvement in the li on trade. On the Continent there is practically no change to report.

Demand is poor for the low quality of Flax but there is more business in the medium and better qualities.

There has been no change in the Latvian Government's attitude during the last fortnight, but very few transactions are reported.

## [15] YARNS.

There is already a marked reduction in production, and the general policy is to keep it down till demand improves. There are more enquiries during the past few days with a larger number of orders on a small scale.

## [16] WEAVERS.

There has been a considerable amount of inquiry, and business has been booked in various lines, Check Glass Cloths, Towels, Roughs, Ducks etc., and Damasks have not been neglected. Further orders for standard lines of Dress Linens would be useful to Manufacturers at the present time, which would give an immediate fillip to the whole industry.

## [17] MERCHANTS

Housekeeping Goods.—Orders are coming in slowly, but for this time of year the quantity is disappointing. Damask business is improved somewhat, but still far from normal. Piece linens, towels, tailoring goods etc., moving but considering present prices more business is looked for.

Cambrics and Sheers have now been reduced in price from 5 to 10% under the prices of September 1924, and are now at figures which ought to ensure the Season's trade.

## [18] DRESS LINENS.

Some fair orders for Coloured Linens for quick delivery have been arranged by cable, and advices received from U.S.A. are to the effect that goods are going steadily into consumption.

Various other markets are interested in White and Coloured Dress Linens for coming season, and there is a steady demand for these goods, especially in the medium and better qualities.

## [19] *(no headline: column 2)*

*Shanghai, April 17.*

The North China Daily News, in its weekly market report, states that something akin to lethargy seems to prevail in the local markets this week. So far as exports are concerned, the influence of the Easter holidays has not yet been overcome, the silk market sharing in the prevailing sentiment. Piece-goods, which have been inactive at the auctions, have shown lower levels on the whole compared with the week previously. The yarn business has been only fair and purchases of cotton moderate A considerable amount of interest is being shown in future American cotton, which is bound to have a bearing on many classes of goods.—Daily Bulletin.

## [20] FREIGHT TO BOMBAY.

### —

The "Monthly Record" of the Manchester Chamber of Commerce announces that as a result of negotiations which have taken place between the representatives of the India Section of the Chamber and the Bombay Steam Trade Conference the freight to Bombay will be increased by 2s, 6d, thus bringing the rate up to 55s. per ton. This increase comes into operation on July 1 for a period of twelve months. The minimum rate, however, will be reduced by 5s, which brings it down to 25s.

## [22] LIGHT DUES.

### —

There can be no doubt that the suggestion to advance the light dues in order to pay for new material in the lighthouse service has caused great indignation in shipping quarters. The owners partly maintain that there should already be ample services in hand if the service is economically conducted, and the question is bound to result in a reopening of the old grievance against the dues in any form. As early as 1845 the Select Committee which sat to consider the maintenance of the lighthouse service round the coast found that the service should not be maintained by State funds and should not be a burden on any particular class of the community. Notwithstanding this and other decisions to the same effect a few years later, the Government established the system that has developed into the present light dues. At the moment they are certainly not likely to change this system, but whether they can be persuaded to abandon the idea of increasing the dues is another matter.

## [24] RUSSIAN PRODUCTS.

A number of interesting facts and figures are to be found in a New Year's speech by Finance Commissioner Sokolnikov, delivered at Leningrad. Before the war Russia, according to Sokolnikov, produced 576,000,000 pounds of cotton. In 1919 cotton production fell to 48,000,000 pounds. Last year this figure rose to 236,000,000 pounds; and next year a production of 396,000,000 pounds is anticipated. Despite this increase in the Russian cotton production, Sokolnikov declared that imports of American cotton next year would exceed pre-war figures in order to keep up with the growing requirements of the textile industry. Last year Russia produced over 400,000 tons of sugar against almost 1,200,000 tons before the war. Next year it is hoped to raise this production to 666,000 tons. Sokolnikov declared that the Soviet Government spends three-quarters as much on education as on military needs, whereas the Tsarist Government spent three times as much on the army and navy as on education.

## [25] SINGAPORE RUBBER

In their report of March 27 on the Singapore rubber market, Messrs. Guthrie and Co., state:

Values have risen rapidly, driven by a steady and heavy demand from consuming markets. Trading in New York is the dominating influence, and this market has led the way, touching the highest point recorded for some considerable period.

The volume of business in all markets is for near by positions, and prompt deliveries command a premium.

American news is goods and the London market is firm. Further heavy inroads have been made into London stocks, the quantity on hand on March 23 being 18,961 tons.
