## [7] LOCAL AND GENERAL

Washington, May 20.—After fifteen years exclusion gum has been readmitted to good standing in the navy and will be sold to the men aboard ship.

The Tientsin Grammar School Committee has decided, with the approval of the B.M.C., to inform parents that it is not considered desirable that pupils of the school should attend the Races. In no case will pupils be permitted to absent themselves from school for this purpose.

A Chinese chauffeur who caused the death of a coolie with the car in which he was driving, was sentenced to one year imprisonment by the Shanghai Mixed Court authorities. The accused was charged with causing death through inattention to his duty as a driver of a motor vehicle.

"Tokyo, May 20.—Opinion is growing in Japan that the Geneva disarmament conference is abortive.—The government also reiterates its intention of approving any reasonable and equitable proposition agreed upon. The government expressed its willingness to consider naval disarmament separately from the land forces or jointly."

In his annual report the British Resident of Pahang records that a discovery of considerable historical interest was made at Pahang Tua in 1925. This was the finding of the tomb of Muhammad Shah, the first Sultan of, Pahang. The inscription on the tombstones marking his grave gives the date of his death as the 16th Jamed Al Awal, 1880 A.H. which corresponds with September 17, 1475 A.D.

Sentence of death was on May 20 passed upon four armed robbers found guilty of being concerned in a number of armed robberies within the International Settlement during March last. A quantity of arms and ammunition was seized by the police at the time of the arrest of the condemned men whose most lucrative robbery was the theft of 2,000 coppers, 132 10-cent pieces and $6 Mex, on March 19 last from a shop-situated at No. 328 Penang Road.

Tokyo, May 20.—A Japanese passenger arriving at Yokohama to-day by the R.M.S. "Empress of Canada" from Shanghai committed suicide by jumping overboard. In a letter addressed to the captain of the vessel he stated that his action was meant as a protest against the Canadian "attempt to exclude yellow peoples."

Batavia, May 18.—The town of Sourabaya, Samarang and this city have been practically under martial law for several months as a result of the activities of the Sareka Rayat, a popular revolutionary party. The eastern and western coasts of Sumatra are in a similar situation. They have the appearance of a country ruled under martial law. For this reason, the agents of the Sarekat Rayat are compelled to carry their work in a very secret manner.

Vienna, May 16.—Following disturbances in the Apollo Theatre, the police ordered the suppression of a comedian's lines wherein the dish "pork's head" is as a "head" with the mouth wide open. It is understood that the head of the local Fascist club instigated the disturbance which broke up the show. The Italian minister thereupon made an official visit to the foreign office and requested that the lines be suppressed.

Another example of popular legend without much foundation is that in connection with "The Cheshire Cheese," an ancient and famous inn which stands in Wine Office "Court, Fleet Street. In this inn on the wall behind "a certain seat is a grease-patch, and, according to tradition, this mark was made by Dr. Johnson, who, allegedly, habitually sat there resting his bewigged head on the wall behind him. There is no proof, however, of this; there is no proof even that Johnson ever entered the establishment, and the only evidence in its favour is that Dr. Johnson's house in Gough Square is only a very short distance from the inn. But the story lives, and although many do not give it credence doubtless the landlords of "The Cheshire Cheese" have profited by it for years.

## [8] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

Mrs. Hole, wife of Lieut.-Commander G. F. Hole, R.N. (Harbour Master), and Mrs. Raworth, arrived from Home yesterday on the P. and O. s.s. "Mantua."

It is said that Mr C. W. H. Cochrane is leaving Kuala Lumpur at the end of the month, for Johore, where he will act as General Adviser: Mr. A. F. Richards will come to Kuala Lumpur—as acting Under Secretary, F.M.S.—M. M.

The marriage of Bessie Harris, elder daughter of Mr. and Mrs. C. M. Harris, Guildford, Perth. W.A., and Theodore. Watkins, British North Borneo, took place at St. Andrew's Cathedral, Singapore on May 17, the Ven. Arch-deacon Swindell officiating.

The wedding of Hilary Cameron Russel Rendle and Valerie Patricia Gleason, youngest daughter of Mr. and Mrs. P. W. Gleason, took place on May 15 at St. Mary's Church, Kuala Lumpur, the Rev. B. C. Roberts officiating. The Church, which was very prettily decorated, was well filled, the congregation including the Honourable Mr. Peel and Mrs. Peel and the Honourable Mr. Thomson. The service was choral, Mr. W. J. A. Mallon being at the organ.
A memorial service to the late Sir C. P. Chater and the late Hon. Mr. P. H. Holyoak will be held at St. John's Cathedral on Monday, at 30 p.m.

H.E. the Governor (Sir Cecil Clementi, K.C.M.G.) is presenting the Long Services Medal of the Order of St. John of Jerusalem to the Assistant Commissioner and the District Surgeon (Mr. E. Ralphs and Dr. W. V. M. Koch) at a parade of the Hongkong St. John Ambulance Brigade at the Murray Parade Ground on Monday evening at 5.15. It is anticipated that the proceedings will occupy about 15 minutes. Should the weather be unfavourable, the Parade will be held at the Volunteer Headquarters.
According to the "Straits Echo" the following changes in the Malayan Civil Service will take place shortly Mr. J. D. Hall Private Secretary to H.E. the Governor, becomes Secretary to the High Commissioner, Malay States, Mr. J. Huggins, M.G.B. succeeds Mr. Hall as Private Secretary to the Governor. Mr. T. W. Clayton Controller of Rubber will be promoted to be Commissioner, Trades and Customs, F.M.S., in succession to Mr. Charlton Maxwell, who is retiring from the service. Either Mr. J. Lorrie or Mr. Meadows Frost will go to Malacca as Resident-Councillor.

The Catholic Monthly "Religioo e Patria" has just entered the 13th year of its existence and, in spite of so many oppositions and troubles, has been able to maintain its highest reputation amongst the Catholic and Portuguese papers in the Fur East.

Two Chinese probationary interpreters of the Sanitary Department were charged before the Kowloon Magistrate yesterday with obtaining money by false pretences. In the case of one man the prosecution alleged that he received sums of $13 and $20 from a hawker by pretending to have authority to issue licences. Both cases were adjourned.

An interesting experiment in babies diet is being made at the Charity Hospital in Berlin. After the age of two months the infants are taken off milk and are fed on chopped cow's liver. The liver is found to contain better amounts and proportions of iron phosphorus and vitamins than does milk. Children fed on this diet, states the "Central News," are said to be healthier, better looking, and more intelligent than those fed by the old method.

While the King was on horseback in Windsor Great Park recently, a woman, accompanied by a child, approached him in the Long Walk. His Majesty drew up his horse, and the woman handed a bunch of primroses to the King, who, smilingly accepted it and thanked the woman.

The King's Birthday—his Majesty will be 81 on June 8—is to be celebrated at home on Saturday, June 5, when the ceremony of the Trooping of the Colour will take place on the Horse Guards Parade. In the Dominions and the Colonies the event will be celebrated on the actual day, June 8.
