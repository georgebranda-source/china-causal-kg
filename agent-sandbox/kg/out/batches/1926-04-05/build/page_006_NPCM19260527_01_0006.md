We are not a churchish or ungrateful community. We may not express in mère words all the gratitude we feel to Si't Paul, for, in moments of the greatest loss words are hard to find. But with reverence we can say that Hongkong's long period of prosperity and progress, leaving aside even the effects of the strike and the boycott, is the best monument to the most outstanding personality in our midst for over sixty years. The rise of Hongkong and the career of Sir Paul Chater are synonymous terms. To quote T. P. O'Connor once more: "Sir Paul and everything in Hongkong have been indissolubly associated. He was at the head of everything here; no enterprise got on without asking his assistance. Shipping, banking, international companies with their heads in London or in Paris—he was in them all. And he accumulated one of the largest fortunes in the Empire. He was the father of everything in Hongkong by long residence and long service. He was "the oldest British settler; he was the oldest member of the Executive Council. From his immense wealth he gave most generously to every good cause; he was thus the chief philanthropist as well as the oldest settler in this very prosperous and go-ahead Colony, one of our proudest and best creations in the Empire."

This evening the mortal remains of the best and truest friend that Hongkong has ever had will be laid to rest far from the home of his fathers—in a city which he has nobly assisted to create by his initiative, his genius and his munificence. "The labourer's task is o'er," but the gigantic nature of that task and what it has meant to the whole community shall be of everlasting memory. Whilst we would mourn with Lady Chater and other relatives in the hour of their sad bereavement, our grief will be tempered with relief that Sir Paul has been spared to a good old age and has seen numerous tangible results of his regal generosity.

Only yesterday we had the unpleasant duty of recording the death of the Hon. Mr. P. H. Holyoak, after years of solid service in the interests of the Colony. To-day our "sense of loss is attentuated by the passing of the "Grand Old Man" of the Colony, Sir Paul Chater, at his residence, Marble Hall, Conduit Road, this morning. Of epitaphs and in membriam notices it may truly be said that Sir Paul requires none; the polished sentence and the flowery eloquence of the writer or orator may be sought in vain to describe how much his life has meant to Hongkong. Tributes to his sterling, worth there must be, as there will be of all truly great men who leave their footprints on the sands of time for others to follow and to profit by precept and example. But not all the tributes could adequately do justice to one whose long life was wrapped up in the welfare of his adopted island home. There was not one phase of local life in which he did not evince practical interest. There was no scheme for the development of the Colony but had in Sir Paul an ardent supporter. There was not a charitable cause but could count on his beneficent nature expressing itself to an extent that surprised the recipients most of all. ## [18] *(no headline: column 4)*

That Sir Paul Chatter was early convinced of the future greatness of Hongkong may be gathered from this interesting extract from "Who's Who" in the Far East, published by the "China Mail," as far back as 1906:—

"In 1884 he started a godown business at Kowloon, purchasing the sea beach from the Government and erecting godowns; in 1888 amalgamated with Jardine, Matheson & Co., and established the existing Hongkong and Kowloon Wharf and Godown Co., reclaiming foreshores and erecting present godowns, and wharves; originated the Praya Reclamation in 1887 by writing to the Government and submitting a scheme, which was accepted by marine lot holders; visited England later and received the sanction of the Secretary of State to carry out the work, the foundation stone being laid at the corner of existing cricket ground by the Duke of Connaught in 1890; presented status of the Duke of Connaught to the Colony in commemoration of the event; work was concluded on the reclamation in 1905, the result being the addition to the Colony of considerable foreshore upon which has been erected some of the finest hongs east of Suez; was the first to advocate the acquirement of the present New Territory on the mainland of China, writing to the Government four years before the actual leasing of the territory; organised an appeal to the Government later by the Chamber of Commerce, the China Association, and the Unofficial Members of the Legislative Council, and successfully urged the negotiation for a lease of the territory, which was subsequently granted by China; has been identified in most public movements since arrival in the Colony; was Treasurer and afterwards Chairman of Queen's Jubilee Committee; Chairman of Diamond Jubilee Committee (then created C.M.G.); erected first Anglican Church at Kowloon; is chairman and director of principal public companies, including Hongkong and Whampoa Dock Company.

## [19] ON THE COUNCIL.

### A Contested Election.

Writing on April 1, 1924, on the completion of Siro Paul's sixty years' residence in Hongkong, it was stated:

"It was in 1886 that he was elected to a temporary vacancy on the Legislative Council as the representative of the Unofficial Justices of the Peace, though opposed by two other candidates. On the resignation of Mr. F. Sassoon in the following year, Sir Paul (Mr. Chater as he was then) although absent from the Colony at the time, was elected for the full term of six years, at the expiration of which period he was re-elected. He was again re-elected in 1899 for a further period of six years." Meanwhile (in 1898) instructions were received from the Secretary of State for two Unofficial Members to be added to the Executive Council, and Sir Paul was selected as the Senior Member, and he has continued in that capacity ever since."

## [20] AT THE UNIVERSITY. An Honorary Graduate.

On January 24, 1923, in presenting Sir Paul to His Excellency the Governor, Chancellor of the University, the Vice-Chancellor said, inter-alia:—

“It remains, Mr. Chancellor, for me to present to you, as worthy of an honorary degree, my colleague, the Hon. Sir Paul Chater, Treasurer of this University. (Cheers and applause.)”

Standing in this hall, one thinks first of Sir Paul Chater as a former partner of the late Sir Hormusjee Mody, to whose generosity we owe these buildings. Not only is Sir Paul our Treasurer but he has been so since the institution of the University. Nor is it irrelevant in this connection that when the Masons of this Colony were minded to found a scholarship in the University for the sons of members of Masonic bodies in Hongkong and South China it was the name of Sir Paul Chater they attached to that Scholarship.

In public life Sir Paul is the senior Justice of the Peace by nine years; he became a member of the Legislative Council as long ago as 1887; he has sat upon Your Excellency's Executive Council since 1896; and he was first honored by his Sovereign in 1897.

It is, however, in commercial life that Sir Paul Chater has won

the connection between commercial success and academic distinction? From those who regard the role of the financier as a purely acquisitive one such a question is not unnatural, but reflection leads me to believe that men work in different media and that idealism is to be found in every class. No more stirring story is to be found in the East than that of the transformation of Hong-Kong from a barren rock to the home of a prosperous, an orderly, and a well-governed community—(applause)—the work has called for imagination, for insight and for courage, and it is from men such as Sir Paul Chatter that those qualities had not least been required. If we wish that story to be fully reflected in the roll of our honorary graduates, then the name of Sir Paul Chatter must be added to the list.

**OVERHAULED BY GUNNER.**

### 一

Gunner A. Newman was congratulated by Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning for his public spirit in an incident which occurred at Hill Road yesterday afternoon.

Two Chinese were charged, a licensed driver with allowing another without a permit to have control of a car and the other with driving without a licence.

The Gunner stated in evidence that he was coming down the road when he observed the car skidding on a pavement and narrowly miss a collision with a wooden shed in which were two old men. On approaching the car to investigate the men rapidly changed places, and the man without a licence jumped off and bolted, but was caught by witness and handed over to a police officer.

Evidence was also given that the other man drove away the car during the temporary pursuit of the runaway, but he was traced by the police.

Defendants were fined $25 each, or fourteen days.

## [22] TURF CAREER

### [By “Patrol”]

As “Mr. Paul,” and latterly as “Sir Paul,” the G.O.M. of the Hongkong turf claims connection since 1865, his first meeting.

As steward, Judge, owner and spectator, Sir Paul Chatter has won the affection of all with whom he came into contact, from rival owner down to the most humble "mafoo" (stable boy).

Altogether, he has been connected with 61 consecutive meetings. Even in his old age, he attended training with fair regularity.

In 1884, the stable was taken over by the late Sir Hormusjee Mody ("Mr. Buxey") and Sir Paul resumed in 1912.

Sir Paul holds the record number of wins for a Hongkong meeting, namely, fifteen.

In his 26 years as owner, Sir Paul won practically every race of importance on the Hongkong turf. For some reason or other —whether intentional or not, I cannot tell—he has never been conspicuous in events for subscription griffins, the class of pony to encourage the small owner.

I remember 1916 as the year in which the "straw, white cap" swept the board, to the complete satisfaction of the regular followers of the stable. With different ponies, Sir Paul took first and second in both the Derby and the Champions, Triumphant Dahlia establishing records on each of the three days!

In 1914, 1915 and 1916, Sir Paul won the Derby; and the Champions in 1915 and 1916. Among the giants of those days were Windsor, Dahlia, Winsome, Dahlia and Perfection. Dahlia, the last named being dubbed "four legs not on the ground" by an old mafoo.

More recently, Sir Paul won a surprise Derby with Ideal Dahlia in 1923 and Glorious Dahlia was first this year.

Last year, Saucy Dahlia won the Shanghai Derby. Incidentally, the stable has a string of successes in Shanghai to its credit.

A race which has been very popular with Sir Paul in the last few years is the Governor's Cup, he winning it in 1922, 1923, 1925, and again this year.

The Chater Cup was, of course, presented by Sir Paul.
