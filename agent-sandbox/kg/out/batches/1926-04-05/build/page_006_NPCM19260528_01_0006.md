Streets, and houses, house numbers and house names, must become as familiar to him as the offices in the city are to the shroff. Let even one letter be misdelivered, or not delivered when it ought to be, and at once he finds himself in a veritable sea of troubles. Breen is an enthusiast, and would probably be the first to disclaim having performed miracles with his personnel in the direction of transforming rough diamonds into polished gems.
The report on the General Post Office for 1925 is published elsewhere in our columns today. It discloses, in spite of decreased revenue and greater expenditure, a balance of revenue that has been unbroken since 1916. Indeed, during the last ten years the balance of revenue last year was only exceeded in the preceding four years. Dollar for dollar, it may safely be deduced that the Post Office is one of the best financially administered Departments in the Colony. And it is in this respect perhaps more than many other, that its "silent service" is to be most appreciated.

With a surplus of $359,647 on last year's working it may be hoped that the Treasury will part with some of it and authorise the Public Works Department to improve the exterior of the Post Office buildings. These house other Government Departments besides the Post Office itself, but the weather-worn frontage and patch-work appearance of the edifice as a whole is scarcely creditable to a progressive Colony. Our Marine business has been conducted during 1925 under the somewhat difficult conditions which have characterised this class of insurance during recent years.

The market is still large and the amount of business restricted with the result that competition is severe and rates in consequence continue to shew a downward tendency. Another matter which still causes concern to underwriters, and which my predecessor in the chair referred to last year, is the introduction, largely as the result of competition, of risks of a nature altogether outside the usual scope of marine insurance and for which in many cases inadequate rates are paid.

Individual marine losses of magnitude and total losses have been less a feature than the many smaller but nevertheless serious losses which often escape public notice or soon fade from memory. Among the latter I would mention the unusual number of serious fires in which valuable cargoes have been involved.

I am glad to say however that in some directions there are signs of underwriters themselves endeavouring, by co-operation, to place the business on a profitable basis, particularly in the hull market in which improvements have been brought about which may later bear fruit.

Your General manager visited the United States of America last year in connection with our fire and general business there and during his visit considerable re-insurances were effected on the automobile account.

The nett premium income for the year has, as I have already pointed out, been reduced in consequence of these re-insurances, but I am pleased to be able to state that the final outcome for the year shewed that a considerable saving had resulted from the arrangements made. The Society's business in the United States of America is receiving our close consideration.
Fire and general business written by the Society and its affiliated companies in other fields shewed that the income was well maintained.

We all deplore the boycott against British trade in Canton but I cannot believe that the people of Kwangtung will continue to submit for any length of time to the illegal activities of a small section of their community. This Society has had honourable and friendly intercourse with the Cantonese for very many years, being first established in their ancient city in the year 1835, some years before Hongkong passed to the British Crown.

In my opinion, Gentlemen, there is no ill-will between the traders of Canton and those of this Colony and that they as well as we desire the resumption of peaceful business relationship and the re-establishment of the old trade conditions.

## [14] Oversea Offices.

You will be pleased to learn that the Singapore offices were opened on Saturday, September 26 last, Mr. J. Lonie, manager of Messrs. Boustead & Co., performing the opening ceremony. This building is constructed of ferro-concrete throughout, covering an area of 26,552 square feet and consists of seven fibers equipped in the most modern manner. Great credit is due to Messrs. Swan & MacLaren, the architects, for designing such a fine structure and to the United Engineers who carried out the work of construction to its present satisfactory completion.

Handsome offices have also been erected in Bond Street, Sydney, Australia. The building is a fine example of steel structure "work and was erected by Messrs. Kell & Bigby under the supervision of Messrs. Ernest A. Scott & Green," architects. It is faced with Hawkesbury stone and brick and contains a basement, ground and eight upper floors. The official opening ceremony took place on November 18 last and was attended by many of the leading members of the community. The prosperity of the Society was proposed by Mr. Dunlop, President of the Chamber of Commerce, supported by Sir Alfred Meeks and others.
Turning again to the Balance Sheet, I feel sure you will agree with me that we have reason to be proud of the fine position these figures indicate, and they will no doubt be viewed by you with the utmost satisfaction.

I now propose the adoption of the Report and Accounts and when this has been seconded I shall be glad to answer to the best of my ability any questions you may wish to ask.
