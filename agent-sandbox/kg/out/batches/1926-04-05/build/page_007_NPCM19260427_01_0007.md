## [2] BRITAIN'S BUDGET.

The Government did not intend to renew the Trade Facilities Act, which had exhausted its useful needs.

The estimated revenue on the existing basis of taxation was £804,700,000. Thus, the estimated deficit on the existing basis of taxation would be £7,941,000.

"Mr. Churchill dwell on the happy effects of the restoration and the gold standard, including the normalising of exchanges with the great gold-using Dominions, placing our return to prosperity on a solid basis."

The silk duties had worked with unexpected smoothness. He was convinced that the textile industries in Lancashire which he had been accused of ruining would regard the possibility of a repeal of the silk duties with dismay. There had been recently a check on the consumption of artificial silk, but the trade continued to progress. The foreigner paid Customs duties on artificial silk at present prices rather below last year's, whilst we secured a revenue of between £6,000,000 and £7,000,000 in a full year without making dearer the finery for poor working girls! (Ministerial cheers).

Steps will be taken to prevent a repetition of the heavy losses and dislocation of trade last year due to dumping in the interval between the announcement of the silk tax and the passage of the Budget.

## [6] RUSSIAN TREATY.

**WHAT GERMANY HAS SIGNED IN BERLIN**

**WAR CONTINGENCIES.**

*(Router's Service.)*

*Berlin, April 26.*

The text of the Russo-German Treaty has been issued and comprises four articles.

It begins by stating that both will do everything toward maintaining peace, and that they are convinced that the interests of the Russian and German peoples necessitate an intimate co-operation.

Firstly,—The basis of relations between the two Governments remains the Treaty of Rapallo, and they will remain in friendly touching in order to reach an understanding on political and economic questions.

Secondly.—Should one of the signatories, in spite of its peaceful attitude, be attacked by a third Power or several Powers, then the other signatory shall remain neutral throughout the entire conflict.

Thirdly.—If a coalition of three Powers is formed with the object of imposing an economic or financial boycott against one of the signatories, the other shall not join the coalition.

Fourthly.—The Treaty will last or five years. Before the expiry of the Treaty both parties will keep each other informed in regard to the further orientation of their political relations.

In a covering, letter to M. Krestinsky, the Soviet Ambassador, Dr. Stresemann, German Minister for Foreign Affairs, emphasises that Germany's entry into the League of Nations cannot be an obstacle to the development of friendly Russo-German relations.
The German Government is determined to assist in making the idea of peaceful settlement in international disputes a reality, but in the unlikely event of efforts contradicting this basic idea of peace being directed against Russia then Germany will oppose them as strongly as possible.

League Obligations.

The principles of German policy toward the Soviet cannot be influenced by Germany's loyal adherence to the obligations ensuing on her entry into the League concerning Articles XVI. and XVII. of the Covenant of the League on questions of sanctions, which are applicable to Russia only in the event of Russia warning against another country.

The question as to whether Russia is the attacker can be de-
delded only with Germany's full agreement, so that accusations by other countries shall not be binding for Germany if Germany is not convinced of their justification.

## [10] WAR DEBTS.

**FRENCH OFFER TO UNITED STATES**

**MUST BE REVISED.**

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

*Washington, April 26.*

The Debt Commission has decided that France must revise her debt offer, but emphasised that the latest discussions have not terminated.

The correspondent adds that one of the chief hitches is how much France can pay at the outset.

The Debt Commission is firmly against the “safety clause,” by means of which France would be relieved of her debt responsibilities in the event of Germany's failure to meet her reparations payments.

M. Henri Berenger, the French Ambassador, is not expected to insist on this clause.

M. Peret, Minister for Finance, is expected to go to London next week as the agreement with America will probably be signed in the meantime.

Belgian Agreement.

The Senate has ratified the Belgian debt agreement by 55 votes to 20.

## [18] CANTON POLITICS:

**DANDIES OF KWANGTUNG**

General Wu Te-chen, Commissioner for Police in Canton, has been dismissed.

News of this action by the Kwantung military council reached Hongkong on Sunday, and the matter has now been confirmed by the Canton vernacular papers.

A factor, which led to the report appearing to be strange, was that Mr. Wu was still at liberty in Canton after action had been taken against him.

The only inference is that Mr. Wu's name has been associated, rightly or wrongly, with the Rightist party of the Kuomintang. Most of the senior military officers are described as of the Leftist faction.

Wu still retains command of his division of soldiers who, by their spick and span uniform, have been described as the dandjes of the Kwangtung Army. He had previously tendered his resignation but was pressed to remain in office.
