## [6] MEETING.

**DETAINCED VOLUNTEER RESULTS.**

**GOOD SHOOTING.**

Increased Entries In Most Competitions.

Below are given more detailed results of the "Ritle Meeting of the Hongkong Volunteer Defence Corps, held on Sunday, and yesterday."

Considerably, more interest was taken in the various competitions this year, there being 17 entries for the Blake Shield (as against 7 last year); 18 for the Francia Cup (as against 6 last year); 19 teams for the Jar Competition (as against 8 last year); 19 for the Tile Competition (as against 7 last year), and increased entries for the Attack Competition. Machine Gun Competition and Lewis Gun Competition.

The competitions were filed under bad conditions on the first day, there being a heavy wind and poor light.

Conditions were good on the morning of the second day but during the afternoon there was slight rain at intervals.

Corps: Sergt. Maj. Westlake is to be congratulated on carrying off both individual events: No. 6. Platoon who did excellently, in the team shoots: Sergt. Tugwell, who got the "possible" in the open revolver competition, with a very well grouped target, the five shots almost touching.

The organisation of the rifle meeting entails a great deal of hard work and the thanks of the Corps are due to Insp. Frith who for many years has been responsible for the arrangements which were again excellent. He was assisted by C.Q.M.S. E. G. Stewart.

## [9] MERCHANTS MOVE.

**TO ADVISE DELEGATES.**

There being no steamer down from Canton on Mondays, and in the absence of other means of communication, there is little fresh to report from Canton.

A private letter received yesterday hints that Canton's, four Chambers of Commerce are being asked by merchants to nominate one representative to "advise" and delegates that may be appointed to negotiate a settlement of the boycott. With its stern high up, and after a rope (with which it had been towed) had snapped, a local fishing boat drifted ashore as the result of a collision with a steamer, which was leaving Hongkong harbour.

The boat was on its way from Cheung Chau Island to the fishing grounds. In the small hours of Sunday morning (April 4), it was involved in a collision with the s.s. "Po' Shan," when war Lin Tin Island.

The crew (including women and children) of nearly twenty clambered to the stern when the bow made water and were hauled over the steamer's side to safety. A sailor was badly knocked about in the face through a violent bump but there was no casualty.

The sinking boat was secured by a rope but the rope snapped and the junk ultimately drifted ashore. The crew were put off at Cheung Chau where a report was made.

## [14] SEEKING HIRE.

**MOTOR CASES AT THE COURIS**

Before Mr. R. E. Indsell at the Central Magistracy is morning. Chinese was charged on two counts of using a private motor car for public hire and reckless driving.

It was stated in evidence that the defendant touted the fare among the people going to worship at the tombs in the cemetery at Pokfulam and had succeeded in a bargain with a Singapore trader and another to bring the town for $3. He was observed by a traffic officer to be coming down the road at an estimated speed of 30 miles an hour.

Defendant denied that he was touting and said he took the passengers for no offer reason than that he wished. A lift, although they were not his friend. A fine of $25 on each count was imposed.

## [19] A VALLEY FLAT.

**WAS TENANCY ENTERED**

**INTO:**

Argument centred round an alleged verbal agreement on the part of defendant to take over a flat from February 1 at the Summary Court Court this morning

Plaintiff was Mr. J. Brook, of the Vacuum Oil Company, and defendant, Mr. H. K. Lee, of Lee and Russ.

The flat in question was at Morrison Hill Road and the claim of Mr. Brook was for $200 ($100 for a month's rent and $100 in lieu of notice).
Mr. Brook stated in evidence that he communicated with defendant as a result of an advertisement and together they made an inspection of the flat which was on the top floor, plaintiff having taken over the others for his own occupation. Defendant expressed himself as satisfied, stated plaintiff. The rooms were unfurnished but had recently been repaired and re-decorated. The agreement was that defendant was to come in on February 1.

As the defendant did not put in an appearance, plaintiff communicated with him and later saw him at the office of Lee and Russ. Defendant stated that he was willing to take the premises but could not do so at once. He offered to go in on February 20, and pay one third of the rent for the month. This did not suit witness and the premises remained unoccupied for the rest of the month. Another tenant was now in occupation.

Cross-examined by Mr. Russ. plaintiff admitted that defendant had never gone into occupation. Mr. Russ: I suggest that what really happened was that defendant stated if the house was properly furnished he would be prepared to take it.

Mr. Brook: That is not so...
Plaintiff also denied that he
had ever told Mr. Lee that he
could not have the flat.

Mrs. Arnold, 7. Middle Road, Kowloon, was called in evidence on behalf of plaintiff. She stated that she was in touch with the defendant for the letting to him of a flat but on January 19, he told her over the telephone that he had taken a flat at Happy Valley.

Mr. Russ held that this was not evidence that the flat in question was the one at Morrison. Hill Road. He held that an alleged telephonic communication was not evidence.

His Honour held that it had an important bearing on the case.

After considerable legal argument as to whether a lease had been constituted, His Honour reserved judgment.

## [22] JUNK ASHORE

**VESSEL AND OCCUPANTS**

**POLICE RESPONSE**

The trials of a trading junk in squally weather were related in a report made by the master after the vessel and the occupants were saved by a police launch.

Leaving Swatwy on March 28 with 26 Chinese passengers on board, the vessel encountered a strong wind nearing Hongkong some days later. In an attempt to anchor off Green Island, the junk was driven ashore. An Indian guard on Stonecutter Island observed the plight of the junk people and signalled for assistance by letting off a rocket. This signal drew a police launch to the scene and the junk was towed to Hongkong.

The incident was not attended with any casualties.
