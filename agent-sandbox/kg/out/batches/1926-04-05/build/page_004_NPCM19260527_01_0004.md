Not less conspicuous than his public services, were Sir Paul's generous gifts to the University, his munificent donations to churches in the Colony and other public institutions, in addition to his many acts of private benevolence.

Of Sir Paul's private life, Sir Henry said that he was possessed of an encyclopaedic memory which made his talk on every subject most interesting. By his death the Colony has lost a many-sided man of rare ability. It is hard to lose such a man, especially at the present time.

Of Mr. Holyoak, Sir Henry said in was difficult indeed to express his feelings. It was over ten years ago when he proposed Mr. Holyoak as a successor to the late Mr. Hewett, on the Council. He predicted that Mr. Holyoak would be a worthy successor and this had been amply fulfilled.
On many public occasions since, and in this Council, he had been proud to be Mr. Holyoak's lieutenant, said Sir Henry, and to second Mr. Holyoak's strenuous efforts.
