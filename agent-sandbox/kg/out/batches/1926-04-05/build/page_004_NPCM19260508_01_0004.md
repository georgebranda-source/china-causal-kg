## [12] UNCHANGED.

The Home Office this afternoon announced that the situation was unchanged, and that the country was generally quiet.

Concerted action by the striker's in London to prevent the transport of food on the road continues, but the situation is being dealt with.

Attempts were made in the provinces to cut off electricity for essential services by calling out the men from the power stations, but the strikers were successfully replaced.

The position in 74 power stations in London is quite satisfactory with the exception of Buttersea Bermondsey, Poplar, Stepney, and West Ham.

More engine crews have resumed on the Great Western Railway and a fuller train service is running. There is an increase in the trainbus, and tramway services in Lancashire.

Lyons, the well known firm of caterers, have posted up a notice in their London headquarters with the warning that the "the staff not reporting for duties before noon on Saturday will have their jobs filled."

## [14] CALM AND ORDERLY.

### Trade Union's Version

The British Worker to-day publishes district reports to the effect that the stoppage is complete, the workers are calm and orderly; and their solidarity is unprecedented.

The journal gives prominence to a declaration that the Council of the Trade Union Congress is repudiating Statements that the Trade Union leaders attempted to reopen negotiations to end the general stoppage. It declares that complete control of the negotiations is vested in the General Council, who have not had direct or indirect communication with the Government. The Council is ready to enter into preliminary discussions regarding the withdrawal of the lock-out notices, but these must be free from any condition.
It concludes:—It is fantastic for the Prime Minister to pretend at the Trade Unions are attacking the Constitution. The sole aim of the Council is to secure for the miners a decent standard of life.

Twenty-one of the leading suburban newspapers in London were published—practically in the usual form.

Several cotton mills in the Preston district were closed to-day. Others are expected to follow.

*London, May 7.*

Lord Oxford, in a message in the British "Gazette," says that whilst strikes and lock-outs may and often are as a last resort justifiable and even necessary, the challenge now thrown down and taken up is totally different. The present general strike is directly aimed at the daily life of the whole community and the people suffering least are the capitalists and plutocrats. The real victims are the workers, who in the long run will bear the burden and pay the price.

## [20] SITUATION INTENSER

### Climax Not Yet Reached

London, May 7. A communique states that there are no serious disorders in any part of the country. The work of feeding the people and maintaining the light and power for essential communications is being successfully accomplished.
Over 2,000 trains ran yesterday — nearly double that of Wednesday. There is a substantial improvement on the main lines. The suburban services are arranging for protection. The London motor buses are running most satisfactorily and constantly increasing in numbers.
But the situation is becoming inteniser. The climax has not yet been reached. Orders have been issued by the leaders of the Railways and Transport Unions to do their utmost to paralyse and breakdown, the supply of food and the necessaries of life.
Intimation.
The communique says that intimidation by disorderly crowds and picketing has occurred in many places, and may soon occur in many more. The Government has directed the authorities to repress and overcome these criminal obstructions.
The recruitment of special contactables all over the country is vigorously and rapidly pressing.
Other important measures to increase the forces at the disposal of the Government, to enable widespread protection are also being taken.
