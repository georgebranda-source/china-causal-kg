## [1] CHANG'S COUP.

**COMMUNISTS TO MAKE FRESH BID FOR POWER**

**AN UNSATISFACTORY TRUCE**

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

All is not peace and harmony in the "Kuomintang. The Communist faction of the Canton Nationalist Government is not accepting with any grace the recent political coup of General Chang Kai-shek and his supporters, whereby Communist influence in the Government is sharply curbed and clearly delineated.

This means that the struggle for supremacy will go on, and that present conditions mark only an unsatisfactory truce. The next trial of strength will take place in August, when the regular meeting of the Central Executive Committee will be held here, the protracted sessions which began May 15 having been extraordinary sessions.

At the next meeting the moderates will make a determined effort to oust the Communists entirely. Already they are charging bad faith on the part of the radicals, and the fomenting of new intrigues. The "truce" with the Communists was made for the sake of peace at home while the northern military expedition is under way, but the present attitude of the Communists is said to be frustrating that motive.

One charge heard in official circles is that the Communists manifest a spirit of obstruction toward the settling of troublesome outside affairs, such as the conciliatory settlement of the Hongkong boycott, the settlement of the Oil monopoly, and other foreign affairs. Such obstructionist tactics are held by the moderates to be tantamount to treason, for they tend to cripple the Government in the conduct of the campaign against Wu Pei-fu.

The Communists, for instance, are finding ready allies in the more radical of the labouring, and union men. One subject which came up for discussion in a star-chamber season was the matter of abolishing the strikers' and picketers' courts when the Hongkong boycott is called off. The moderates insisted that these extra-legal courts would have to be abolished, but the radicals and labour men insist that they shall continue to function to handle all cases connected with any kind of strike that may be going on. The Communists gave their support to this contention:

The Government does not dare to carry over its policies along this line by a show of force, for to do so would be openly to force all the labour and union adherents into open allegiance with the Communist elements.

Preparing For May 30.
Meanwhile preparations are under way for a gigantic meeting of memorial and protest on May 30, the first anniversary of the Shanghai incident. There will be a great deal of parading and speech making, with central meetings at the East Parade Ground and at the amphitheatre of Kwangtung University.

The police and military, however, will do all they can (as they did on May 1 and on May 7) to keep the demonstrators away from the Shakee and the general vicinity of Shumeen.

Currency Depreciation.
The General Chamber of Commerce has circularised all the guilds and important merchants of Canton concerning recent events which for a time tended to depreciate the value of the Central Bank Notes. The Chamber of Commerce announces support of the Government edict which threatens arrest and punishment not only for people who refuse to accept the notes at par, but also for those who spread disquieting rumours about the value of the paper money.

The Secretariat of the Kuomintang to-day dispatched to all unions guilds and student-workers organisations a notice to have two representatives each at the Central Koumintang office to-morrow afternoon at 2 o'clock to work cut plans for the memorial services to be held in Canton on May 30, to commemorate the Shanghai incident of last year.

At a meeting of the representatives of the labour organisations here yesterday it was voted to raise a fund to send to the striking British miners. Collections will be begun immediately.

Calgary, Alberta.—An oil boom in Calgary, based on exploration in the Turner Valley field, has done away with unemployment so far as the clerk class is concerned. According to a report just issued by Canadian Pacific Railway officials, those who are not actively engaged as Brokers have secured positions in offices connected with the oil business. One hundred and sixty-seven brokers were registered at the end of March.

## [5] SHIPPING BOARD IS GIVEN

Washington, May 14. — The United States Shipping Board was to-day granted delay until May 20 to show cause why the sale to the Dollar interests of the five Admiral-Oriental Line ships should not be enjoined.

Captain Elmer E. Crowley in a statement to the Manila press denied reports that he had resigned because of differences with the United States Shipping Board relative to the sale of five Admiral Liners to the Dollar Steamship Company.

He said that he opposed the sale while the shipping board favoured it. He said, however, this was only a minor difference in viewpoint which could not cause any bitterness.

## [8] CHINESE REVENUE STAMPS

Harbin, May 11.—Chinese revenue agents have of late been busily engaged in the process of persuading, albeit rather forcefully, the merchants and shopkeepers, to purchase the new revenue stamps which have been turned out by the Mukden printing press. It is said that as soon as a Chinese merchant living and conducting business in the railway area is found in native territory, he is promptly waylaid, carried off to the Chinese police and made to pay a huge fine.
