## [2] PIRATES BUSY.

**NEW FORM OF TERROR ON RIVER**

**DRESSED AS PICKETS**

*(From Out Own Correspondent.)*

Pukkal, Kongmoon, May 16.
An offshoot of the 'picket, evil' has now been developed to an extent by which it has become a real terror. Men are going about with strike-pickets' underarms and holding up vessels to robbery. Seven boats have been molested so far. The passengers have suffered heavy losses. Unlike the "professional" pirates, these outlaws do not take the passengers into captivity (for purposes of ransom).

Up-country merchants are forming small, transport companies, with boats running between Macao and the following ports: Chiu Lin, Ngol Hoi, Muk Chow, etc.

Over 2,000 (Canton, soldiers) who were sent from Shihiheng to suppress the plures (do Ho Tong) are staying and Packknid. They left for Canton at midnight last night. I learn that they are being sent to the Hunan border to join the Northern Expedition.

On heaving of the arrival of the troops, pieates in this yelnity immediately mobilised at Laki Ngok (opposite Kongmoon) and prepared to resist.

Leung She, the notorious pirate of the Ku-Tau Shen, has been making a clean sweep in the south-western part of the Canton River delta, attacking villages and river truille every day.

When the mobilisation order went out, the Kongmoon tow-boat "Lee Yuen" immediately left this port for Canton for fear of being commandeened for treop transport. Passengers had to be transferred to Kongmoon by rail, or by boats.

The employees of the Summing Rullway have now been embodied in a guild.

Native bankers at Sanhui city have refused to accept the Canton Government treasury notes.

"Airmen of the Kuomintang have established a branch nero: club at Kongmoon.

Because of alleged incompetency in collecting revenue, the district magistrate of Shuntak has been removed. He is Tang Kong's staunch supporter of General Li Fook-Iumi.

## [6] STEEL HELMETSARMY REVIEW

**COMMUNISTS' PARADE**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Berlin, May 24.*

The German Communist Party and the extreme Nationalist organisation, the Stahlhelm or "Steel Helmets", held reviews of their respective "Armies" yesterday—the former in Berlin and the latter in Duesseldorf.

The Communists are estimated to have numbered 30,000 and the Stahlhelmites 50,000.

Large forces of police, including mounted constables, were present, but the proceedings were orderly.

The Communist review was held in a large park in Berlin in the presence of thousands of curious spectators. The "army" was arrayed in semi-military uniform and performed military evolutions with commendable celebrity, whilst the bands played revolutionary airs. Many speeches were made denouncing anything and everybody, including the British Trade Union leaders' "betrayal" of the workers. The assembly then dispersed—with bands playing and banbers flying—singing songs including the Communists' latest favourite: "Mother, the policeman killed your child."

Two police aeroplanes circled overhead during the proceedings.

## [7] CONSUL INJURED.

**OUTRAGE BY PICKETS IN**

*(Reuter's Agency)*

*Peking, May 24.*

While removing Communist posters from the walls of the Consulate on May 23 the British Consulate Swatow was assaulted by seven pickets.

The Consul defended himself with a stick and retired into the compound. The pickets, observing that other foreigners were there, did not enter.

The Consul subsequently summoned an armed guard from the British gunboat in the harbour and informed the Chinese Foreign Commissioner that the guard would remain until he received an official apology and an assurance that no more posters would be posted on the walls of the Consulate.

The Consul also demanded that the offenders should be punished and pickets prohibited from landing on that side of the harbour.

The Congul is not seriously injured.

## [11] 650,000 WALFS.

**APPALLING CONDITIONS**

**TERRORISED INTO SILENCE.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Riga, May 24.*

There are no fewer than 650,000 waifs and strays in Russia, many of them living under ap-palling conditions, according to revelations at a sitting of the Council of the People's Commis-sars presided over by M. Rykoff and reported in the Izvestia.

It was stated that Government institutions are already housing 350,000 and the Council decided to offer inducements to other institutions and to artisans willing to take homeless children as apprentices.

The Pravda reports that in the Tula district horrible conditions are experienced by children in some Government homes. The children are terrorised into silence as regards the brutal treatment and immorality practised by the managers of these establishments.

## [18] VOLCANO ERUPTS.

**SERIOUS DISASTER IN JAPAN**

**a 120 PERSONS MISSING.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Tokyo, May 25.*

With a tremendous roar, which was heard 20 miles away, Mount Tokachi, a dormant volcano. in Hokkaido, suddenly erupted yesterday afternoon.

According to despatches from Sapporo it is reported that 60 houses were buried in lava and that 120 persons are missing mainly miners, inside the local sulphur mine.

The inhabitants of Miye a town at the base of the volicano, are fleeing in confusion as the place is seriously endangered, whilst the train service has been suspended.

## [23] OBITUARY.

**FORMER LOCAL MANAGER OF COOKS**

**MR. DE WIVELESLIE ABNEY.**

It is with regret that we have to record the death, which took place at Home on May 22, of Mr. Evelyn Edward de Wiveleslie Abney, formerly manager of the Hongkong Branch of Thos. Cook and Son.

Mr. Abney, who had spent many years in the Far East, was formerly with the Peninsular and Oriental, Steamship Navigation Company and had been stationed at Manila where he was married. It was in 1911 that he entered the service of Thos. Cook, and Son and when he left in 1924 he was Manager of the Hongkong branch. Mr. Abney, acting on medical, advice, did not return to Hongkong but became associated with the Far Eastern Department of Cooks at Pall Mall.

The news of his death came as a great shock to his many friends in Hongkong. Much sympathy will be felt for the 'widow' and young daughter of the deceased.
