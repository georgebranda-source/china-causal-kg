## [9] *(no headline: column 2)*

No development giving promise of bringing the strike to an end has occurred since the stoppage began

on Tuesday morning. The General Council of Trades Union Congress is in constant session and the Executive of the Miners' Federation met for a time this morning after which several members proceeded to consult with the General Council and others went to the House of Commons.

In to-day's issue of the strike newspaper, the "British Worker," which is published by the General Council, a reply is made to the statement of the Prime Minister that the Government was prepared to resume negotiations immediately the general strike is called off. The reply states that at this stage, with no knowledge of the subsequent line of policy that the Government intends to pursue, the General Council cannot comply with the Prime Minister's request for the unconditional withdrawal of strike notices. It indicates its readiness to resume the negotiations for an honourable settlement, but adds that any preliminary parleys with a view to discovering a basis for negotiations can only be undertaken in a free and unfettered atmosphere.

Mr. Ramsay MacDonald, the Labour Leader, has made the statement: "I am not letting a moment pass unused for some attempt to devise ways of peace and accommodation."

## [10] OFFICIAL REVIEW.

### No Shortage of Food

An official communique says:
No shortage of food is reported anywhere. There has been obstruction of food supplies at many places in the country, in some cases accompanied by violence. The Government, however, is dealing with it effectually.

In order to give widespread protection to those engaged in maintaining food supplies and other essential services in the London area, it is proposed to raise the number of special constables as quickly as possible.

Twenty-five thousand volunteers enrolled in London and the Home Counties yesterday, bringing the total in that area up to 75,000.

The mining areas are quiet, the miners observing a peaceful attitude.

The postal services, which at first suffered some delay, have improved to such extent that the London Post Offices were reported at five o'clock this afternoon to be practically clear.

## [17] TRUE ROMANCE.

**SEEKING RELATIVES ROUND THE WORLD.**

**WAR "ECHO" HERE.**

### /

To be seen Home by the Police to day is an American negro who is wandering round the world in search of relatives who became rich in the Spanish-American War, at the beginning of this century.
Moses Little is the man on this long search. Fairly well built except for lines in the face which betray a not too steadfast upbringing, he is under middle age. He has not been in Hongkong long but he has spent the better part of several hundred dollars (gold) during his stay. Still, he has enough money to see him back to the United States, it having been decided that he is not to stay.

On April 28, he presented himself at the Police Passport office for permission to go on to Manila. His action was auspicious and he was searched. On him was found a revolver, in a very smart holster, and 122 rounds of ammunition.

As far as Little knows, his people emigrated at the time of the Spanish-American War and have since made their pile. He once heard of them in England and later on believed them to be either in Singapore or Hongkong.

A remand was given by the Magistrate so that inquiries could be made.

This morning, Major C. Wilson imposed a nominal fine of $25 for possession of arms without permission, cautioned the man for not having a passport, ordered the confiscation of the weapon &c., and made the order for him to be sent away by the Police.

## [19] $4,000 GONE?

**HAS HE VANISHED?**

Entrusted with $4,000 to pay into his firm's account, a shop coolie has been "posted missing" since yesterday afternoon, and the money has not reached its destination.

The accountant of a Chinese firm at No. 105, Queen's Road Central says that he handed the money over at 1:30 p.m. Instructions were given to the foki to pay it to the Wui Loong Bank, also of Queen's Road Central. As he did not return by 3 p.m., inquiries were made, and the police were communicated with. The cash had not been paid in.

## [20] MR. KEW'S LOSS.

**JEWELLERY STOLEN.**

The window led to a bedroom in the house. Mr. Kew is of the opinion that the burglar must have got in some time between midnight and 6 a.m. yesterday. A report has been made to the Police.
