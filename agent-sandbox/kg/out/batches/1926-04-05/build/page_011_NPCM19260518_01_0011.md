There is no check of any sort, despite the fact that this is American territory, and that the Eighteenth Amendment certainly applies her morally, and in spirit, even though it appears that no competent authority has yet decided that it applies legally or in letter. But the main point is, it seems to me that we, as the Nation which long ago assumed a moral as well as a physical and political guardianship over these Eastern peoples, are very delinquent in the exercise of that guardianship when we permit such examples of the white man's moral obliquity as this to exist under the eyes of the natives. It lowers America's prestige among them, and breeds additional disrespect and scorn for the entire white race.

Moreover, such conditions are so strikingly opposed to the careful, beneficent English colonial rule in the same regard as to make one wonder if America, after all, is properly intrusted with the guardianship of an alien people. Nothing like the state of affairs here exists in any South Sea group, not even in French Tahiti. No such degenerate white men as hang about the saloons here would be permitted to remain in Samoa, Fiji, the Cook Group, or elsewhere in the British Pacific Island possessions. Does it not logically follow that England inculcates into the native mind a deeper respect for her ways and her people than can America, so long as such a disgraceful state of affairs as the unrestricted sale of liquor to the natives in the Philippines continues?

Here is a condition known to exist. The mere recital of these facts would seem to indicate, at least in part, what the United States can do to better conditions in the Islands.

(UOMINTANG IN INDO-CHINA.
Peking, April 27.—According to a despatch from Canton, the National Government has, at the request of the Executive Council of the Kuomintang at Canton, sent a protest to the French Government through the French Consul-General against the persecution and deportation of the members of the Kuomintang in Indo-China. Those deported members are expected to arrive at Canton shortly and the cause for their persecution by the French authorities was that they tried to preach the three popular principles of late Dr. Sun Yat-sen as well as the establishment of Kuomintang branches in the French Protectorate. Radical members of the Kuomintang are urging the adoption of adequate reprisals against the French in the south.—Amalgamated News Agency

But somewhat apart from strictly political considerations, and apart, also, from the acknowledged desire to encourage a more liberal investment of capital for the development of the natural resources of the Islands, is the wish of the President that a general survey be made "of what the United States is doing in the Philippines; what progress this Government is making there; what progress the people of the Islands are making; how education is progressing among the Filipino people." ## [10] CANTON COUP DETAT DENIED.

There has been no coup d'état in Canton, in spite of asseverations to the contrary in the cables from China and the reports in the Japanese press.

This authoritative information was given to a representative of the "Japan Chronicle" by Sir James Jamieson, British-Consul-General in Canton, who left Kobe for Home on April 28 by the "Empress of Australia."

According to reports that have been current for over a fortnight, the Red regime was recently ousted, and a new administration took its place. At the time of receipt of the first news, the authenticity of the report was doubted, and when subsequently no confirmation was received from other quarters, it became evident that propagandists had been at work. This theory was substantiated when news was received, from Hongkong that the Kwangtung Foreign Minister, the famous Dr. C. C. Wu, son of the late Dr. Wu Ting-fung, had taken steps to bring about a rapprochement between Hongkong and Canton. Dr. Wu is one of the Red section of the Kuomintang, the party in power in Canton.
Sir James Jamieson, who stated that he preferred not to make any public announcement on the political situation in China, said that a conference between Hongkong and Canton was still in progress, and though Hongkong hoped that a settlement of the boycott would be effected, yet up to the time that he left Canton the situation was in practically the same position as it had been for the past few months.
Sir James's declaration that he is merely going home on furlough and that he will be returning to Canton, belies the reports current in the Chinese Press that he had been recalled by his Home Government.

Sir James Jamieson came considerably into prominence during the anti-British agitation in South China last year, though his famous warning to the Canton Government, which was broadcast throughout the world the day before the students, supported by Russian officers, attempted to take the Shammen foreign concession by storm. On that occasion Sir James informed C. C. Wu that he had received information that the students had drawn lots as to who should have the honour of sacrificing their lives in taking the Shammen. Sir James solemnly warned the Foreign Minister that in the event of the threatened assault eventuating, the foreign authorities on the concession would use whatever force they considered necessary to protect it. The following day the collision occurred, and the French and British soldiers and marines fired, killing a number of the demonstrators.
Shortly afterwards Sir Austen Chamberlain cabled to Sir James Jamieson, stating that he had the support of the British Government behind him in any action which he took to protect foreign interests in Canton.

Sir James is expected to be away for about six months.

## [11] PEKING POLITICS.

Peking, May 7.—As a result of concessions on the part of the Fengtien and old Chihli factions regarding political affairs, an agreement has been reached that parliamentary members shall be re-elected and that Dr. W. W. Yen's Ministry be restored in order to endorse the command for a succeeding Cabinet to be organised. Dr. Yen's Ministry being but a makeshift to save the "race" of Marshal Wu Pei-fu, it will not last long, and Dr. Wellington Koo may possibly be appointed Premier of a succeeding Cabinet after its resignation.—"Tokyo News Agency."
