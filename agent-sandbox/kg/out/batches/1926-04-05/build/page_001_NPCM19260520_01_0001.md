Other reports which have reached the “China Mail” indicate that Kuomintang forces are moving about in different parts of the province.

Semi-official quarters predict that the vanguard of the Northern Expedition will actually be in the war zone within a matter of days.

The first big objective, it is stated, is the capture of Changsha and Yochow, two important towns in Yangtsze valley, in the centre of Marshal Wu Pei-fu's domains.

At present, there is a general in southern Hunan, who is gaining the sympathy of Canton in his struggle against the pro-Wu armies. It is proposed to make the towns of Namhung and Chehing (in northern Kwangtung) the main bases of the Expedition, which will gradually work into the neighbouring provinces of Kiang-si and Hunan. General Tan Yen-kai of the Canton Government commands a number of troops who are natives of Hunan and they will be used in the war.

Other soldiers marching to the all of arms are the Cadets and their pro-Chang regiments stationed at or near Swatow. Some of these are returning to Canton.

## [9] POWER WANING.

**CANTON'S COMMUNISTC INFLUENCE.**

**VOTE OF CONFIDENCE**

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

*Cunton, May 18.*

The passing of every day almost of every hour, renders more secure the supremacy of General Chang Kai-shek, and makes more emphatic the fact that the influence of the Communists in the Cantonese Government is greatly on the wane.

The Central Executive Committee of the Kuomintang, still in session, has given General Chang a unanimous vote of confidence and has unanimously passed his series of eight resolutions which reduce the power and prestige of the Communists.

The committee appointed to consider those eight resolutions not only gave approval, but proposed a ninth, as follows:—

“Communist members in the Kuomintang are to be limited to one-third of the membership on all committees in the higher organisations of the Party.”

## [10] Challenge To Chang?

<sub>section under [9] POWER WANING.</sub>

This ninth proviso was also given unanimous approval by the plenary session of the Central Executive Committee.

One Communist member of the committee rose and demanded an explanation of the "March 20 incident" when General Chang Kai-shek took disciplinary measures against Commander Li Chi-lung of the cruiser "Chung Shan" (formerly the "Wing Fung" and flagship of the Canton Navy).

General Chang, replying on his vn behalf, admitted having ex-ided his authority in dealing with the naval situation on March 20, but declared that an emergency situation demanded prompt and decisive action. He then invited a vote of censure and punishment from the Kuomintang, but received instead a unanimous vote of confidence, proposed by Mr. Sun Fo and seconded by Mr. Chang Ching-kiang.

## [11] Looking For War.

<sub>section under [9] POWER WANING.</sub>

The military movement to the North continues with great speed, and it is subject for comment here that a great bulk of the troops being sent to meet Wei Pei-fu's allies are those who are suspected of sympathy with the Communists, or who are, at least, considered of doubtful loyalty to General Chang. Just enough of the faithful Chang troops are going northward to frustrate any movements towards treachery, but the city and environs are entirely in the hands of General Chang and his real supporters.

On all sides is evidence that the crisis is believed to be past, and there are undisguised signs of relief in Cancon that the "Reds" were not victorious in the trial of strength which began at the Central Executive Committee meeting on Saturday.

For a time rumours of a coming Communist regime had a very bad effect upon business, but now confidence is felt on every hand.
The city is placarded with manifestoes against persons who spread rumours of impending changes, and rumour-mongers will be imprisoned and fined. The Canton City Merchants Association and other influential organisations have addressed open letters to business men and citizens generally expressing faith in the present stability, and saying there is no reason why we should be doubtful and frightening ourselves by the rumours regarding the practice of Communism.

## [13] China Federal

**VALEDICTORY.**

**A FORCE FOR GOOD**

By the departure to-day of the Rev. T. B. Powell, M.A., Assistant Chaplain of St. John's Cathedral, the Colony loses a popular padre who had interested himself in many movements connected with and apart from his duties as Assistant Chaplain of St. John's Cathedral.

Mr. Powell has been largely instrumental in the compilation of a prison library, his own personal contributions and those of residents whom he interested in the work forming the basis of what he hopes will grow into a library worthy of the opportunity afforded for brightening the lot of prison inmates. An extra effort to add to this was always made at Xmas time and Mr. Powell also interested himself in attempts to have the prison Xmas fare altered more in accordance with the spirit of the festive season.

Mr. Powell had hoped to see the formation locally of a branch of the "Toc H." movement for keeping alive the ideals of service in the observance of which so many lives were laid down in the Great War. In the words of one who assisted Mr. Powell in his local endeavours, the idea of the organisers of this movement is to "harness all the vague aspirations for good that float about in people's minds—to harness them to catch the stream of goodwill and make it drive the Mills of God which seemed particularly at the time of the movement's inception (after the Great War) to be grinding slowly."

When the founder of the movement, Mr. Clayton, paid a visit to the Far East and started branches in India and the F.M.S. it was Mr. Powell's hope that Mr. Clayton would be able to call here but the crisis which developed prevented such a visit from becoming a practical possibility.

Mr. Powell also interested himself in other progressive movements and was a force for good in all work associated with St. John's Cathedral. His many friends have subscribed to a parting gift as a mark of their personal regard for him and also of their appreciation of his work in the Colony.

Mr. Powell is a keen cricketer and has proved a tower of strength to the Hongkong interport teams by reason of his bowling. On his way home he is to play for the Hongkong Interport team against Singapore and the F.M.S.

## [17] Rumours Current.

Many rumours are still current. A correspondent of the "China Mail," who has reached Macao from up-country, is pessimistic about the "bet"—at even money—that "the boycott will "die out" by the end of the month when the pickets will mysteriously vanish.

His answer to an inquiry is that if such were the intention, the picket tribunals would certainly have speeded up their machinery for imposing heavy fines and confiscating cargoes and hulls in their hands. Such has not been done at Kongmoon, or other ports. One good sign that he is very much impressed by is that it is now possible to purchase foreign coal, in fairly large quantities, in Canton and ship, same-up-country. Hitherto the practice has been to buy the coal in Hongkong and go through the cordons by bribes, equaling $6 a ton or more. Purchase in Canton, however, brings no advantage. The cost is several dollars a ton higher and the roundabout methods of transport add to the freight to an extent which just about Balances both way—and the merchant can make no saving!
From the small ports nearer to Hongkong than Canton, and other places on the coast between this Colony and Swatow, small craft are coming here in fairly large numbers as the pickets have increased their vigilance in those districts.

## [18] HARBOUR SERVICE.

**"SOLAR STAR" ON TRIAL TRIP.**

**TO RUN NEXT WEEK.**

One of the two new "Star" ferries has been completed by the Hongkong & Whampoa Dock Company and after undergoing inclining tests in the dockyard was on trial trip yesterday when the engines were given a thorough tea. Everything proved satisfactory and the new ferry will be on the run next week.
This is the "Solar Star" and the other which is well on the way to competition is the "Night Star." Both are of similar build to the larger ferries which have enabled the service to be considerably speeded up, the upper deck of the two newer ones being slightly longer.

With the replacement of the smaller ferries still on the run by the two larger ones, the service across the harbour will be more expeditious. In rush hours it will be possible to give practically a continuous service with a ferry at each side of the wharf.

## [22] WAGES OBSTACLE.

**MINERS TO RESIST IMMEDIATE REDUCTION.**

**STILL A RAY OF HOPE**

*(Router's Service.)*

*London, May 19*

It is understood that the Executive of the Miners' Federation will recommend the delegate conference to-morrow (Thursday) to endorse the reorganisation proposals in the report of the Royal Commission, but to reject the immediate wage reduction implications contained in the Government's proposals and the Commission's report.

The policy of the Executive is based on reports from the goal fields indicating fairly general resistance to any departure from the existing wage standard at least pending the reorganisation of the industry.

It is believed that the delegates will accept the recommendation practically unanimously, but there is still some hope that the situation will change shortly.

## [27] MAGISTRACY ITEMS.

**FORMER CONSTABLE'S NEW PROFESSION.**

**KEEPING A DIVAN**

An allegation of assault by Senior Revenue Officer Watt to the extent of enforcing an admission was made by a Chinese before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy when he was charged with being in charge of an opium divan on behalf of a second man, the keeper of the divan, who was also charged with the offence.

Although there were two others who made up the smoking party, and therefore the customers of the divan, the accused failed to prove his allegation and was given double the penalty—a fine of $50, or one month's hard labour.

The excuses put forward by the second accused to account for the presence of opium cross in his box also failed; and he was fined $100; or two months' hard labour. This man had resigned his position as lukong to engage in what he described as his own business.

On the lesser charge of smoking each of the other two were fined $2. Here again one man was identified as a bad character. He had just completed ten days in prison for Impersonating a revenue officer, and when caught in the divan, he had in his possession a Colonial Secretary's Office messenger badge and a sailor's pass, both of which he could have used for imposing on people. No charges were, however, made in respect of these and they were retained.

Stolen Cheese.
Three Chinese employees of Messrs. Connell Bros. Company were charged before Major C. Wilson with the larceny of 14 boxes of "Kraft" American cheese, which were identified as the property of the firm by the manager, Mr. A.M. Horder.

The evidence showed that the boxes had been kept aside owing to being in bad condition, but when investigations were made after the arrest of a man carrying three boxes in the street, it was found that the defective cheese had been served on customers and the new boxes made use of by the accused.
All were found guilty and sentenced to three weeks' hard labour each. A fourth man, who was charged with receiving the stolen property was remanded until tomorrow when a further charge of attempted bribery may be added.
