## [9] TROPIC FLYING.

**SEAPLANES FOR FAR EAST PRACTICABLE.**

**DANISH AVIATOR'S VIEWS.**

ommander Botved On His Tokyo

If the Dutch aviator, Commander Botved, finds on his return to Canton from Hongkong that the state of the ground in Canton is no better than when he left he will discard the greater part of the ton of gasoline which is rendering impossible his ascent from there and he will then fly to Hongkong, take on new supplies and continue his flight.

Commander Botvedl spent this morning in making a survey of the Kowloon City ground as a prospective "hopping off" spot and in arranging with the local authorities for permission to fly here in the event of the need arising.

Commander Botved was interviewed by a "China Mail" reporter at tiffin time as he was about to join fliers from H.M.S. "Vindicitive" of whose assistance in the matter of advice and help for preparation of the Kowloon City ground he spoke in appreciative terms.

## [10] Canton Assistance.

<sub>section under [9] TROPIC FLYING.</sub>

Commander Botved wished also to correct any misunderstanding which might have arisen through Press reports as to objections by the Clinton Government to fliers visiting the city. The Government had given all assistance possible, he stated, having placed at his disposal the avlution bureau there and the services of mechanics.

An erroneous impression may have been gathered also from reports of non-preparedness at Canton when Commander Botved arrived. What actually happened was that Commander Botved was due in Canton on Saturday, but owing to heavy thunderstorms and bad flying conditions he had to turn back to Hanoi. Cable communication between Hanoi and Canton having been interrupted, no news was received in Canton, atmospheric conditions also rendering impossible the reception there of the wireless message sent from Hanoi.

Commander Botved therefore literally "dropped from the clouds" when he actually arrived and made his descent without the assistance of the planes the Canton Government had instructed to be in preparation to guide him. The aviation bureau with mechanics were ready to receive him when he made his landing.

He has received every assistance from them and work of draining the ground where his plane rests has been put in hand by the Government since the heavy rains.

Commander Botved will probably be returning to Canton by boat to-morrow and until he has had the opportunity of seeing what effect the draining has had it is impossible to state whether or not he will "hop off" from Hongkong.

## [11] Advised By D'Oisy.

<sub>section under [9] TROPIC FLYING.</sub>

His decision all along had been to fly to Canton, Commander Botved told the "China Mail" representative. There had never been any question before of his touching Hongkong. He had decided to fly to Canton, acting on the advice of Captain D'Oisy, the famous French flier with whom he had been in communication before he left Copenhagen and who advised him that with a land plane the descent at Canton was more practicable than at Hongkong.
Questioned with regard to the effect on 'plans of tropical heat, Commander Botved said that he had experienced no ill effects on his plane. Air currents and Indian sand storms rendered progress difficult in parts and the absence of facilities for landing with a land plane was also a great disadvantage. Greater facilities for keeping avintors constantly in touch with meteorological conditions would also be necessary if regular flying was to be instituted. "With regard to possible development between Calcutta and the Far East, it is my belief that in seaplanes will lie the more practicable solution," said Commander Botved. "Following the coast, they have landing places continually at their service where

as suitable places for land planes are few and far between."

Commander Botved spoke hopefully of the schemes for developing passenger flight between Europe and the East. Regular flights to and across India were being proved daily more practicable and Sir Sefton Brancker's survey from London to Rangoon indicated, he thought, that still further development was contemplated.

Questions with regard to cabled news of Captain D'Oisy's expressed intention to fly from Paris to Tokyo across Russia, Commander Botyed said that obviously that was the quicker way but until stations were established at points over those vast tracts of land, there was a decided element of risk attached to such a venture. He thought, however, that there must have been a mistake in the cable at a contributing to Captain D'Oisy the statement that he hoped to do it in five days.

Commander Botved's plans for his return flight are not definitely settled. He may return to Hong-kong if he "hops off" from here and finds the ground satisfactory. He may, however, choose another route.

Commander Botved reached Canton, it is interesting to note, in 80 flying hours from Copenhagen, covering a distance of 15,000 kilometres. Needless to say, he slept "like a log," to use his own words when he arrived here.

Last night he stayed at the Peak Hotel and he was accompanied in his survey and preliminary arrangement tour this morning by Mr. W. J. Hansen and Capt. Karsten Larssen.

## [12] COURT WORK.

**A BUSY DAY AT KOWLOON**

**ARMS THEFT CASE**

The case against the armourer of the Chinese cruiser "Kwong Ching" in which he was charged with theft of 16 rifles, one revolver and 220 rounds of ammunition, and alternatively with aiding and abetting in the removal of the arms from the cruiser, was concluded among other cases at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning.

Defendant was acquitted on the largeny charge, but convicted on the other to one month's hard labour.

## [13] Ammunition Find:

<sub>section under [12] COURT WORK.</sub>

The discovery of 31 rounds of ammunition in the steerage quarter of the s.s. "President Pierce" yesterday morning was mentioned when an application was made and an order issued for confiscation.

Largeeny of a plank of wood, resisting arrest; and damaging a Chinese district watchman's uniform were charges against a man who, according to the evidence, struggled with his captor in an endeavour to get the revolver.

The fight was described by the watchman in evidence who said that his whistle and baton were taken away from him and as a last resort he had to shoot the man in the leg.

Defendant explained his presence in a scavenging lane near Portland Street. Yaumati, by stating that he was an informer to the police and was engaged in watching a house suspected of preparing opium. He denied he had the piece of plank in his hand and spoke of a protest he lodged when he was charged with the theft and of an assault on him by the watchman.

In view of the wound and sufferings the man had undergone, the Magistrate said he would be sent to prison for fourteen days.

## [14] BETTER TO DIE?

Dissatisfied with the treatment they received from the mistresses of three houses in which they were working at Laichikok, four Chinese servant girls entered into a compact to end their lives together and accordingly jumped into the harbour yesterday.
The attempt, however, failed owing to the presence of the sanitary barge, the coxswain of which promptly rescued the girls and handed them over to the police.

**CHAMBER OF COMMERCE**

## [16] NOT TRUÉ

**K.C.R. TRAIN "RUNS OFF" RAUS**

**IMAGINARY INCIDENT**

For the benefit of those who travel by the Kowloon-Canton Railway, the "China Muil" is able to state that there is no truth, in a vernacular report that a train can off the rails on the British section, on April 12.

The report is that a train, with over 500 passengers, was proceeding from Kowloon to Sheungshui, and left the track about three miles from Lowu (to which point the Chinese trains from Canton now run).

On inquiry from the management, the “China Mail” learns that there has been a bitch whatever.

## [17] DOG BITES.

**POLICE INSPECTOR'S SON ATTACKED.**

**THREE OTHER CASES:**

A European boy, the son of Sub-Inspector Bloor, was one of the victims of dog bites reported to the police yesterday, and he was treated at the Government Civil Hospital. The animal owned by Mrs. Edmondson, of Wong Nei Chong Road, was sent to Kennedy Town.

Released a week ago after a period of observation, a dog owned by a Chinese at Laichikok Road again attacked a woman of that place and is now back at the camp. The woman received treatment at the Kwong Wah Hospital.
A house coolie employed at No. 58, Robinson Road and a Chinese girl were bitten in two other cases. The dogs are under observation. In the latter case the animal had previously bitten several persons.

## [19] A DRY WEEK END?

Rain is not mentioned in the Royal Observatory's forecast till noon to-morrow. It reads: East winds, moderate; overcast.

Although residents may think that “tons of rain” fell last night, the actual quantity in figures is only 1.39 inches (for the 24 hours ended at 10 a.m. to day). The total for the year is 19.64 inches against 8.60 inches (the average).
The Observatory also says, inter alia: The anticyclone is central near Tokyo. Conditions over Indo-China are uncertain.

## [21] PICKETS DOINGS

**TWO JAPANESE SEAMEN DETAINED**

Two of the crew "(Japanese) of a Japanese steamer in Canton harbour are reported to have been detained on Wednesday, only to be subsequently released on the representations of the captain.

These men are stated to have landed at Chow Tau Tsui (on Honam, opposite the city) to buy provisions. They were taken by pickets to their branch headquarters and closely questioned, says the report.

The captain confirmed their statement that they were merely obtaining ship's provisions, and they were released after a few hours.

On Tuesday, an "inspector" of the pickets, who was stationed at Samchun (the market town just beyond the British border, New Territories), was arrested by the military.

The report is that he was taken into custody by the colonel of the third regiment (stationed at Samchin) acting under orders from the officer commanding the second division. An allegation is that the inspector has been concerned with the smuggling of arms. He was taken to the second division headquarters in Canton. Representations by the Boycott Executive have been of no avail, says the report.

## [22] GAS MANTLES.

**LIMITING RIVALRY WITH GERMANY.**

**MANUFACTURERS' PACT.**

*(British Wireless Service.)*

The Board of Trade states that it is informed that an agreement has been made between the Gas Mantle Trade Association, representing most of the British manufacturers, and the German Gas Mantle Convention and certain associated manufacturers in other countries.

Under the terms of that agreement German manufacturers and associated concerns will not sell mantles in this country and certain other parts of the British Empire, whilst British makers will not sell to the Continent of Europe and to the United States of America.

There are arrangements also in respect of other markets.
The agreement is for five years.

## [23] HARD HIT.

**ARMOUR PLATE NOW A BACK NUMBER.**

**VICKERS'S COMPLAINT.**

*(British Wireless Service)*

*Rugby, April 15.*

The small amount of warship construction in this country has had a serious effect upon firms such as Vickers, Ltd.

Presiding at the annual meeting of the shareholders of the Company, Mr. Douglas Vickers said that it appeared that their armour plant in Sheffield would be almost closed down for the year. The small cruisers allowed by the Washington Convention carried very little armour plate, and as far as could be seen the total requirement of the country would not keep one plant even half employed. There were five such plants in the country.

## [26] WINES BUSINESS.

**RESULTS OF MESSRS. GANDE PRICE & CO., LTD.**

**A SATISFACTORY YEAR**

A tribute to the able manner in which the staff had looked after the students of the shareholders in spite of adverse circumstances and keen competition was paid by Mr. S. C. Pank, presiding at the nineteenth ordinary general meeting of Messrs. Gande, Price and Co., Ltd. held at noon to-day at No. 2, Ice House Street.

The chairman was supported by Messrs. Kenneth Chan and Leung Yan-po (directors), Mr. C. Bond (secretary), shareholders present being Messrs. Chow Tung-sang, Pang Kok-sui, Pang Kwok-fatt and D. Rumjahn.

Chairman's Speech

The notice convening the meeting having been read by the secretary, the chairman said:—

Gentlemen,—Before commencing the business before the meeting, I wish to express, on behalf of the directors, staff and shareholders, our deepest regret at the untimely death of our late chairman of directors Mr. C. H. Lyson. His valuable services to the company during his term of office, not only as a director but as a legal adviser, were of great assistance to his co-directors.

The report and statement of accounts for the year ending 1925 having been in your hands for the prescribed period, I propose with your permission to take them as read.

The net profit for the year under review is $5,050.42, to which must be added the balance at Profit and Loss account brought forward from 1924 of $218.96, making a total at credit of Profit and Loss account of $5,269.38. This sum your directors' recommend should be appropriated as follows: To pay a dividend of $25 cents per share on 18,105 shares, which will absorb $4,526.25 and to carry forward the balance of $743.13 to this year's account.

I regret to state, "gentlemen, that our profits show a decrease on the previous year, but with the abnormal condition of things in China together with the "very keen competition prevailing, what other results can we expect? I think you will agree with me that in spite of these adverse circumstances we have had a satisfactory year," and I am sure I am only voicing the sentiment of the shareholders when I say that the staff have in an able manner looked after our interests, especially in such strenuous times, and I hope with their continued cooperation and the early solution of the present situation, we shall have better results to place before you at the next annual meeting.

With these few remarks I have much pleasure in proposing the adoption of the report and accounts as presented and after this have been seconded I shall be pleased to answer to the best of my ability any questions the shareholders may wish to ask.

There being no questions, Mr. Pang Kok-sui seconded the adoption of the report and accounts which was carried.

## [27] Business.

<sub>section under [26] WINES BUSINESS.</sub>

Proposed by Mr. Bond' and seconded by Mr. D. Rumjahn, the meeting confirmed the appointment of Messrs. Kenneth Chan and Leung Yan-po as directors.

The chairman proposed the re-election of Messrs. Lowe, Bingham and Matthews as auditors for the current year. This was agreed to on a seconder by Mr. Leung Yan-po.

Moving that the directors fees for the current year be fixed at the same level as last year, Mr. Bond remarked that they were on the right side of the balance sheet and the working had shewn a profit. Seconded by Mr. Pang Kwok-fatt, this was approved by the meeting.

The chairman said this was all the business and that dividend warrants were ready.
