## [9] LOCAL AND GENERAL

Professor James Iarnell, one of Germany's most distinguished and successful surgeons, died recently in Berlin at the age of 78. Professor Israel was a specialist on kidney troubles.

Mr. Bucknell, Senior American Assessor at the Mixed Court, said goodbye to the Court on April 27, this being his last day in attendance officially. Mr. Bucknell is leaving for America shortly.

A report comes from London that the principal English yachting association has repealed the decision according to which nationals of the Central Powers shall be excluded from participating in English pegattas for a period of 10 years after the conclusion of peace.

Mr. Carl Ray Sadler, Cavalier of the Texas Company, Shanghai met his death as the result of a collision with a motor car driver by Hong Ah-sen, a coroner's jury in the American Court decided on April 21, but no charges were brought against the Chinese. Action, if any is taken, is up to the Municipal Police.

Word has been received in Tsingtao from a party of Germans, that they arrived in Berlin exactly twelve days after leaving Tsingtao. So far as is known this is the quickest time ever made since the war via the Siberian Railway between Tsingtao and Berlin. Ordinarily this trip has taken at least fifteen days in the past and now takes three weeks.

There was a fair attendance at the opening of the Spring Exhibition of the China Society of Science and Arts on April 24. The exhibition which is being held in the old British Post Office building in Peking Road is being held in conjunction with the British Women's Association and the American Women's Club art departments.

A series of boxing matches in which Japanese champions will take part will be held at the compound of the Yasukuni Shrine on the occasion of the semi-annual shrine festival early in May. The Japan Boxing Club is promoting the meeting. Due to the growing popularity of boxing in Japan, the War Office, which sponsored Japanese wrestling matches at the semi-annual festival for three days, has spared one day for the boxing matches.
The French Fire Brigado held an inter-company competition in the use of the fire fighting equipment at the French Park last Saturday.

One case of diphtheria was notified on Tuesday from the district of Victoria (city), the patient being Chinese.
We are officially informed that the King's Birthday parade will take place this year on June 8, at Happy Valley. Time and details will be communicated later.

At the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday, a cook brought a claim against Mrs. R. El Matthison for 16 days' wages at $18 per month and one month's wages in lieu of notice. After hearing both sides, the Magistrate held there had been a misunderstanding between the parties and recommended the payment of $10 to the complainant.

A conductor employed by the Kowloon Motor Bus Company told the Magistrate yesterday when he was charged with absconding with a sum of $10.95 that he was obliged to run away owing to his inability to replace some one-dollar notes which were blown away by the wind. He was sentenced to six weeks' hard labour.

Colonel Yen Chung-yang, Chief of the Chinese Police, has definitely made his intention known regarding Wong Ching-yuin, chairman of the Shanghai General Labour Union, who was arrested recently. According to the Chinese Press, Colonel Yen will release Wong from custody only if the labourers and students cause no trouble during next month and its many anniversaries.

Construction of the four torpedo destroyers called for in the recent Japan Diet session, is to be started soon by the Navy Department, to be completed within three years at a cost of ¥26,110,400. The Diet approved an ¥8,000,000 appropriation for the current year, ¥10,274,160 for next year and ¥7,836,240 for the following year for these destroyers, as a part of the auxiliary fleet.

For what was held to be the balance due for wines and refreshments supplied to the Stonemason's Guild, the Wing Shing Company claimed $535.60 in the Summary Court yesterday but were awarded $35.60 only, as plaintiffs admitted in the course of the case that $500 had been received. Mr. H. S. Fitzroy (Instructed by Messrs. Lee and Ruas) appeared for plaintiffs and Mr. A. J. O'Donoghue for defendants.

## [10] SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

To-morrow, the Hongkong Volunteer Defence Corps smoking concert (and prize-distribution) will take place at headquarters at 9.15 p.m.

The retirement of still another old Shanghai resident has to be recorded, Mr. Tom Wallace, after 43 years of continuous service with the Waterworks Co., having decided to retire into private life.

The funeral took place last evening at Happy Valley of Vera Gertrudes Xavier, the fifteen months old daughter of Mr. and Mrs. R. G. Xavier, No. 25, Jordan Road, Kowloon, whose death occurred on Tuesday.

The death took place at the Severance Hospital, Seoul, on the morning of April 21 of the Rev. C. H. I. Hodges, who in June last succeeded the Rev. Eustace M. Strong as chaplain of Christ Church, Yokohama.

Bishop Trollope, the Anglican diocesan in Korea, is shortly leaving for England, owing to a breakdown in health, due to heart trouble. During his residence in Korea Bishop Trollope took a keen interest in the construction of the Anglican Cathedral at Seoul.

Sir Henry Gollan, Chief Justice, has granted probate to Mr. W. E. L. Shenton of the will of the late Capt. James Henry Woollacott, master mariner. Capt. Woollacott, who died in the Government-Civil Hospital here in December 1924, left $10,000, and died in state.

Dora Chappell, daughter of the Rey James and Mrs. Chappell, well-known missionaries in Japan, was married at St Andrew's Church, Shiba, Tokyo on April 21, to James Coster Lloyd Edwards. The young couple are to reside in Wales, the home of the bridegroom.

Mr. C. J. Spiker arrived in Shanghai on May 1 for temporary duty at the American Consulate-General where he will take up the work of the Senior American Assessor during the absence of Mr. Howard Bucknell. In this work will not be new to him as he was in Shanghai in 1916-19

Miss Dorothy Gish, the famous film star has arrived in London. She is to make three British films during her visit.

The First Lord of the Admiralty, Mr. W. C. Bridgeman, spent part of the Easter recess on an official visit of inspection to Gibraltar.

Sir Hedley Le Bas, governing director of the Caxton Publishing Co., Ltd., died in a nursing home at Reigate, Surrey, on March 26 in his 58th year.

Mr. Hsu Lan-tsu, industrial commissioner of Kiangsu, and Mr. Ma Wen-chih, staff officer to Marshal Sun Chuan-fang, have arrived in Shanghai from Nanking.

Captain D. Margesson (rec. 6) beat the Prince of Wales (sor.) in the fourth round of the Houses of Parliament squash racquets, handicap at the Bath Club on March 25 by 2 games to love (18-17, 18-15).
Mr. W. J. Bellerby, part-owner of King of Clubs, which won the Lincolnshire Handicap at odds of 100 to 1 against had odds of 200 to 1 to win £2,000. He also has a "double" of 2,000 to 1 about the horse coupled with Sprig for the Grand National.
The Duke and Duchess of York have given their patronage to the ball which Lady Airlie and Mrs Gerard Leigh are organising at Claridge's Hotel, for the Prince of Wales Sea Training Hostel at Limehouse. Among those on the committee are Lady Ancaster Lady Beatty, Lady Carlisla, Lady Pembroke, Lady Chaplin, Lady Stanley, Lady Mary Kenyon-Slaney, Lady Moira Lyttelton, and Lady Kathleen Rollo.
Miss Violette Cordery, 16 25 years old, woman racing motorist, arrived in London after breaking 57 records on the Monza track near Milan, taking turns at the wheel with five other drivers day and night. A hundred thousand people lined the route taken by the funeral procession of the Dowager Queen of Denmark from Amalienborg Castle to the Central Station. The coffin was there transferred to a special train and taken to Roskilde, the burial place of the Danish kings, for interment. Prince Arthur of Connaught represented Britain at the funeral.

For the first time in history, the Sultan of Morocco is to break the laws of the Korean to visit Paris. Escorted by a brilliant delegation of the great Caids of Morocco, and Islamic leaders from Algeria, Egypt, Palestine, and Arabia, he will visit the French capital in July to inaugurate the largest mosque constructed in the Western world.

The Rev. James Sibree, F.R.G.S., now of Bombley, who celebrated his 90th birthday last month has a most interesting history. It is said that he learned his Greek Testament in waiting-rooms and trains; travelled to Madagascar in a sailing ship in 1868 to build four churches for the London Missionary Society; he was shipwrecked once, and was once nearly drowned in a twobass

Mrs. Mary Wilkin, of Summerhill, Tenterden, Kent, has left estate of the value of £81,518, with net personality £75,979. She left £2,000 each to her housemaid, Eessie Willis, and her parlourmaid, Ellen Short, £1,000 to her coachman, Charles Masters, £800 each to her cowman, George Harris, and her cock, Margaret Mallion; and one year's wages to each of her servants.

A magnificent monument to Saint Francis is to be erected in the Piazza of St. John the Lateran in Rome in honour of the seventh centenary of the death of the saint of Assisi which occurs this year.

In future all meetings of the Shanghai Philatelic Society will be held in the Kalee Hotel, as the manager has kindly arranged for a room to be at the disposal of members from 5 p.m. to 8 p.m. on alternate. Wednesdays.

The "Secul Press" announces that a Japanese "birdman" has invented "a safe, practical aeroplane." The machine is a combination of an aeroplane and a helicopter, has sixteen wings and is warranted to fly in any direction.

There are more than 180 rubber factories in Japan. The growth of the industry has been remarkable since the end of the Great War. In 1913 there were but six rubber plants. In 1917 there were 18. By 1922 this number had been increased to 152 and in 1922 the total stood at 180, where it probably remains. Few new businesses have been started in the slump which has prevailed in Japan since 1922.

Two Loke Yew Scholarships, which provide free board and tuition, and one Donor Scholarship, which provides free tuition, at the University of Hongkong, fall vacant in January, 1927. They are open to Chinese students' indigenous to British Malaya who have good school records and who are in poor circumstances. Applications for them should reach the local Inspector of Schools on or before July 31, 1926.

A large-size submarine (2,000 tons), which was built at the Kawasaki Dockyard, Kobe, and taken to the Yokosuka Naval Port in the latter past of March, is claimed by the Japanese Navy to be unique, embodying all the lessons of the European War, and her fighting efficiency is a match for ten submarines of the existing types. Submarine to be laid on the blocks are to be designed to this type in future Admiral Kato, Commander-in-Chief of the Naval Station, has inspected the submarine.
