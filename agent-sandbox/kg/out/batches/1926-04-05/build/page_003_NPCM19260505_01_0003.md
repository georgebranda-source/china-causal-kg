*Chung Chau, May 4*

In common with the surrounding areas we have been treated to a liberal supply of rain, which has delayed various building operations at Now 13, 018, 10 and 23, and has cut up some of the public which however have been attended to by the roadmen.

The gardens and newly planted trees have benefitted, and the island is looking at its best. The palms must also have interfered with the attendance at the big theatre (second one has since been in progress) for which special lunches were run from Hongkong.
Dr. and Mrs. Minett left yesterday, having stayed an extra week-end to witness the school sports.

The athletic meeting went off very successfully; the weather conditions were ideal, as it was not too hot for the students taking part in the long list of events. The local "stadium" presented an animated scene; there was a good number of visitors present. There were several new features this year. The committee had thoughtfully provided a grandstand in the shape of a matshed shelter for the accommodation of the visitors; in case of wet weather, refreshments were also provided. Then the local mission schools attended in force and contributed several musical items. Some of the Hongkong schools had sent teams for the invitation relay race, which event was keenly contested. A likely winner had the misfortune to drop the flag so putting his team out of the running. Several of the events, notably the hopping race, had to be run again owing to a dead-head.

The hill race and the ung-of-war were specially interesting. The members of the committee are to be congratulated for the successful issue of their efforts.

We are soon to lose another of the owner-residents in the person of Mr. G. E. Wetton, who leaves shortly for England.

Addressing the gathering His excellence said:

Officers' N.C.Os. and men of the Hongkong Polka Force.

Hongkong Police Force.
It am glad to have this opportunity of seeing you on parade and of congratulating you on your fine record during the critical period of the strike and boycott. In a despatch to me dated December 30th last the Secretary of State for the Colonies writes:—“I have to express my satisfaction at learning of the loyalty displayed by the Chinese members of the force and my appreciation of the work of the Police in this trying crisis.” This tribute is well deserved. Out of 533 Cantonese police, only seven deserted. Of 353 Wei-hai-well men only three deserted. Out of 105 Chinese clerks, telephone operators and sergeant interpreters, only seven deserted. Of 233 Chinese Water Police ratings, only nine seamen and five stokers deserted. Not a single coxswain or engineer left his post. This is all the more creditable because a large number of the Water Police are members of the Seamen's Union.

From the middle of June to the middle of August the European Police were on eight hours duty instead of six. No leave could be granted and all ranks had to be confined to their own districts. This was also the ease with the Indian and Chinese Contingents. No complaints of any kind were made and all ranks worked most cheerfully.

Remarkable work was done by the Police Casual Labour Department. From June 22 to July, 26 about 25,000 coolies were collected and sent out to work. In addition to supplying the men, the Police officers in charge had in great many cases to arrange for their food and for their payment. The Police officers employed in the Casual Labour Department had frequently to work from 5,45 a.m. to 9 p.m. and sometimes later.

A special word of praise is due to the Police along the frontier of the New Territory, where owing to the outrageous behaviour of armed strike pickets, our constables and our police launches were frequently under fire and we were involved in a kind of guerilla warfare. The steadiness, self-control, courage and discretion of all ranks under these conditions were admirable. The gratitude of the Colony has been well earned by the Hongkong Police Force during the past ten months and it is a great pleasure to me to have this opportunity, on behalf of the whole community, of thanking you publicly.

**MANAGING PARTNER IN TROUBLE.**

A Chinese managing partner was charged at the Central Magistracy yesterday with making false statements to the Official Receiver relating to the affairs of the Wing Shing firm, of Koshing Street, and on other counts of falsifying the account books.

Mr. E. S. C. Brooks prosecuted and Mr. L. R. Andrewes appeared for the defence.
Mr. M. W. Lo gave evidence of having prepared a sale agreement relating to the firm on the instructions of the accused. In reply to a question he said that he advised the accused to have the sale gazetted.
Other evidence was taken and the case was adorned until

Avising out of an incident at Causeway, Buy in which a Chinese was finally injured by a motor cycle," Mr. S. J. Squire was charged before Major C. Wilson at the Central Magistency yesterday afternoon with manalaughter, and also with driving a motor cycle in reckless manner.

Mr. J. A. Gordon Leask defended and Mr. H. S. Fitzroy watched proceedings on behalf of deceased relatives.

The first witness, a Chinese contable, said that he was on point duty at the junction at 9.15 p.m. on April 17 when he saw two motor cycles driven by Europeans with no pillion passengers travelling very fast towards the central district. The cycles touched each other in the middle of the road before passing the post and one of them turned into Caroline Road. The other ran over the man seated on the pavement and hit the tramway shelter. The driver of the machine fell on to the ground and was rendered unconscious. Witness also said there were two other Europeans on the scene shortly after the accident.

Dr. W. L. Paterson said the Chinese was admitted to the Government Civil Hospital and operated on immediately. Witness found the case hopeless and the man died two hours later. He thought the wound which proved fatal could have been caused by the front number plate of the motor cycle.

Witness said that Mr. Squire was also admitted. He had allight abrasions and was suffering from shock.

At this stage further hearing was adjourned until Friday afternoon.

## [5] GIRL'S HAIR ABAZE

**PERIL OF ETHER PRODUCT USED BY A HAIRDRESSER.**

一

A warning to the public of the danger caused by hairdressers washing their women customers hair with etherol, an ether product, was given by a Paris court in a case which cost the life of a 17-years-old girl.

The victim, Mlle. Duprat, was having her hair washed with etherol when suddenly the hair caught fire. The hairdresser and his assistants wrapped blankets round her to smother the flames, but she died two days later after suffering great agony.

Her father was awarded £800 damages against the hairdresser, the court finding that the latter had failed to use proper precautions.

The saloon in which the girl's hair was being washed was separated from the rest of the establishment by a glass partition, but two panes were missing. Professor Kling, director of the Municipal Laboratory, found that the ether vapour had been ignited by heating apparatus in the adjoining room.

The court laid stress on the importance of making known profession: Kling's conclusion that the use of etherol was extremely dangerous in any circumstances the liquid being so volatile that the slightest air current might cause it to be ignited from a considerable distance.
