## [12] NEWSPAPER ENTERPRISE LTD.

**COMPANY PROCEDURE**

It may be regarded as a natural sequel to the position in the Colony during the past eleven months that many limited liability companies should now find themselves "in rather an unenviable position. It would have been rather remarkable had they all been able to weather the storm. There is a great deal to be said in favour of the declaration made by Mr. Hall Brutton, at yesterday's meeting of the Hongkong Development Building and Saving Company, namely: "We have lost and we have got to face it, but for heaven's sake give the Company a chance." That sentiment could be applied with equal force to many other Companies. It is one with which shareholders are likely to agree whole-heartedly.

It is, however, unfortunate that an acrid tone crept into yesterday's meeting. Mr. Arculli was prepared to go the whole way to give the Company a chance."

## [15] TERRITORIAL ESTATES

**HOUSING DEMAND**

### —

Reporting a loss of nearly two lakhs of dollars on last year's working, reference was made today to the poor demand for houses—in some parts of the Colony—and to the effect the boycott is having, the speaker being Mr R. G. Shewan, the veteran merchant who has done so much for the Hongkong public.

As chairman of the consulting committee, Mr. Shewan presided at the second ordinary annual meeting of Hongkong and Territorial Estates, Ltd., held at the company's offices in St. George's Building. He was supported by Mr. T. G. Weall, Mr. E. M. Raymond and Mr. J. Scott Harston (of the consulting committee) and Mr. A. Braun (secretary). Shareholders present were Messrs: Allan Keith, D. W. Munton, Jas. Toppin, A. A. Botelho, I. W. Shewan, H. Dreyer and L. H. King.

The secretary read the convening notice and the auditors' report. After the customary opening to the address, Mr. Shewan said:—

## [16] Bad Property Market.

<sub>section under [15] TERRITORIAL ESTATES</sub>

The total loss for 1925 amounts to $199,826.48, and I can only regret the very unsatisfactory result of the year's work, owing greatly to the abnormal conditions which have prevailed in the Colony since last June.

For our Chinese dwelling houses at Kennedy Town, which were finally completed last July, it was impossible for us to secure anything like sufficient occupants and at the end of the year out of 148 flats available only 13 flats were occupied.

The Yaumati property has just about held its own with the previous year's earnings, but these are falling off month by month and are by no means commensurate with the interest on the purchase money, and unless this site can be properly developed, which means, of course, further capital.

It must continue to leave a loss until the real estate market shows a marked improvement on the present position.

I am glad to say that for our Chinese houses there is now a slightly better enquiry, and I can but hope that the demand may continue to improve. As to that I am sure that our friends at Canton are feeling the pressure of the Boycott as much as, if not more than we, for trade is like mercy which both blesseth him that gives and him that takes, and it is only a question of how long the trading community of Canton will put up with this foolish interference of the strikers, which is destroying their former flourishing business with Hongkong.

## [17] Company's Shares.

You will notice that there was some $306,000 due from unpaid calls. We are pressing shareholders for payment, but have not yet actually forfeited the shares as we have the right to do, as, of course, we do not want to go to extreme measures with shareholders if we can avoid it.

In accordance with the resolutions passed at the extraordinary meeting of the shareholders held on January 21, 1926, the capital of the company was increased by the creation of 50,000-seven-percent cumulative preference shares at $10 each paid up, of which so far 45,000 shares have been issued.

I might explain that we closed the books on December 31, 1925, instead of January 15 as last year, as the latter date was inconvenient in regard to payments due at the end of the year and rents due at the end of the month.

I now propose that the report and accounts as presented be adopted and passed, and that the balance of $199,826.48 at debit of profit and loss account be carried to new account. When that has been seconded, I shall be glad to answer questions from the shareholders.

## [20] ELECTION

**SENSATIONAL STORY IN THE SENATE**

**ALLEGED CORRUPTION.**

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

Senator James A. Reed (Democrat, Missouri) gave currency to a sensational story of anything between two million and five million dollars being spent in the recent Senatorial primary election in Pennsylvania when he moved the Senate to authorise an investigation of the Senatorial campaign expenses.

Mr. David A. Reed (Republican, Pennsylvania), in opposing, said he disbelieved such extraordinary stories about the corrupt use of money.

Other Republicans supported him, but on the roll call there was laughter when it was found that Republican Senators up for reelection generally joined the Democrats and insurgent Republicans in support of the motion, which was carried by 59 votes to 13.

## [27] CORRESPONDENCE

### (To the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir,—I agree with your correspondent of yesterday that a pier at Repulse Bay such as he suggested with facilities for bathing would be a decided attraction. I gathered from his remarks, however, that he was under the impression that the one which was destroyed in 1922 was erected by the Government.

The erection of the pier was, as a matter of fact, due to the enterprise of the Hongkong Hotels Company and it is to them therefore that your correspondent's remarks should be directed although, of course, the Government might interest itself in such a scheme if the Hotels Company did not see its way clear to expend the money.

With the question of Repulse Bay beach still more or less in a state of flux, it is doubtful, however, if the present is an opportune time to press for such expenditure. However, it is a concrete suggestion and "Bather" will find many in symp thy with him.
A SYMPATHISER.
