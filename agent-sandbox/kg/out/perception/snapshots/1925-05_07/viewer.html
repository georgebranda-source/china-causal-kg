Why the Hongkong strike happened, according to the China Mail

Every arrow is a causal claim printed in the China Mail, sorted by who made it. The categories are the explanations the literature attributes to British observers: Red agitators, Red strikers, Canton government support, and nationalism. Intimidation and labour grievances are included for contrast. Click any arrow to read the original sentences behind it.

How it was built

  1. Pages.
  2. Extraction. Each article was read by a local model (Qwen 3.5 9B, temperature 0, frozen prompt) that lists explicit cause → effect claims with a word-for-word quote. Denied links are skipped. A second local pass records who makes each claim and what kind of speaker they are.
  3. Categories. Cause and effect descriptions are embedded (qwen3-embedding:8b) and compared with fixed anchor sentences. A claim gets the nearest category if similarity ≥ the threshold and beats the runner-up by 0.02; otherwise "other". No category is assigned by hand.
  4. Events. Descriptions are clustered at cosine distance 0.35 into event nodes, which carry the chains in the Event view.

How to read it

  • Voice. British = the paper itself, British officials, other British. Chinese = Chinese individuals and bodies, and the Canton government or Strike Committee.
  • Asserted vs composed. In the Event view, a chain is asserted when the linked claims come from the same article, and composed when it joins claims from different articles. Composed chains can show a story nobody actually told, so treat them as leads only.
  • Absence. A missing arrow means no explicit causal sentence was found. It does not mean the paper denied it.

Limits

Unvalidated: speakers and categories are machine-assigned, and the hand-coded gold sample has not been done yet. Counts are small, so read them as presence/absence, not proportions. The embedder separates topics better than direction, so "Red agitators" vs "strikers are Red" is the least reliable distinction. Months after July 1925 cover only pages that were already OCRed, a thinner sample than May–July.
