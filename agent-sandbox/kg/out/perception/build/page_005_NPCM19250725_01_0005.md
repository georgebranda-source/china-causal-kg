## [5] CORRESPONDENCE.

**MONDAY'S PUBLIC MEETING.**

(T'o the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir,—I shall be obliged if you will do me the courtesy of printing the following letter with reference to the public meeting to be held on Monday next at 5.30:—

"It has been arranged for the City Hall but there are indications that this will prove too small in view of public interest in the resolutions to be proposed, if so, the meeting will at once be transferred to the Theatre Royal.

Amongst other resolutions concerning local matters arising out of the strike and the general crisis in China one will be specially brought forward dealing with the mistaken "Labour" and "Press" views of the situation in Hongkong and China and Hongkong legislation in particular, which it is of importance should be emphatically refuted.

I hope, therefore, that there will be a large meeting on Monday to endorse this resolution, at any rate, with no uncertain voice.

Our enemies are using insidious and ceaseless propaganda in all directions which needs counteracting as far as can be done.

I hope that Monday, night's meeting will, at least, do something to lay the truth before the public at home.

I trust that the Press here will assist that object as far as lies in their power.

P. H. HOLYOÁK. Hongkong, July 25.

## [9] RAIN COLLAPSE

**ENQUIRY RESUMED AT MAOISTRACY**

Further details of his narrow escape were given at the resumed enquiry yesterday into the Po Hing Fong disaster by Sergt. Chesterwoods.

He was walking along. Cuine Road on the morning of the 17th about 9:25 when he heard a crack and a rumble, said witness. He looked round and saw a large matshed topple over towards Po Hing Kong. Practically at the same time another matshed close by toppled, steadied itself and then shipped to the ground. He ran along Hospital Road and down the steps there. He saw a temporary contractor's doorway out of which several Chinese ran, and he ran down the stairs wondering whether there was anyone in the matsheds. While he was running down the stairs the rumbling continued, becoming louder.

The Coroner: What was your idea in running down the steps?—I thought it was simply a matter of the matsheds collapsing, and went to see if anyone was there.

Witness said that looking behind him he saw that the steps were covered in debris. A wall in front

another wall, and slid down the debris into the Tai On Terrace. He remained at the place for quite a while, but there were no further slides of importance.

Capt. Arthur: When you were walking along One Road did you notice the condition of the road?—There was about two inches of water in Caine Road.

Capt. Arthur: A certain amount of this water was running over the retaining wall?--Yes.
