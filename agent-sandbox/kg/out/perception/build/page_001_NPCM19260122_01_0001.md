## [6] GROWING GRAVITY.

**DISPUTE BETWEEN CHINA AND SOVIET.**

**HOSTILITIES NOT LIKELY.**

*(Reutor's Service.)*

*Tokyo, Jun. 21.*

The Diet re-opened this morning, after the New Year recess. Speeches were made by Viscount Kato, the Premier, and Baron Shidehara, K.E.E., the Foreign Minister.

Following their speeches this morning in the Upper House, Baron Sakatani grew attention to the growing gravity of the Sino-Russian situation and enquired about the "government's attitude." In reply, Baron Shideharstated that the matter was regarded purely as a Sino-Russian problem and unlikely to culminate in hostilities, though the Government was watching possible developments vigilantly test future action might be required.

Most of the morning papers offer no comment on the Sino-Russian friction, though the "Hochi" voices the opinion that the trouble is due to Chan Tsolin's desire to re-establish, his damaged prestige by stirring up Chinese national settlement in his favour.

The "Fleochii" forecasts in Chinese success, as the Soviet action is fettered by fear of damaging relation with Japan.

According to an “Asahi” despatch from Duiren, Chang Tso-lin ordered the arrest of M. Ivanov. President of the Chinese Eastern Railway, and the Mukden authorities to resume the railway service by force if necessary.
A despatch to the “Asahi” from Harbin reports that the gravity is increasing and the Chinese troops are preparing to concentrate in Harbin in greater numbers.

According to Press reports, M. Kopp, the Soviet Ambassador to Japan, when interviewed, declared that "the Russian Government is prepared to back up its demands by force; if necessary meeting troops with troops."

In spite of all this, the vernacular papers generally indicate that the Japanese Government and people are maintaining a calm attitude.

**HOLDS CHINA RESPONSIBLE.**

M. Karakhan on Tuesday forwarded a Note to the Wai-chia-pus saying, inter alia, that Chinese soldiers on Saturday forcibly occupied the cars in the mail train which was in readiness to depart for Harbin. When the railwaymen demanded that they leave the train the soldiers forcibly seized the engine, took off the keys of the switches, stopped r.d.lway manouvring, and prevented the passage of a loaded freight train from Kuang Cheng-tze to Chang Chun.

As regards passenger traffic the violent actions of the Chinese soldiers were still more abominable. On Sunday the Chinese Military Headquarters arbitrarily and forcibly despatched the mail train to go to Harbin without awaiting at stations for the receipt of permits, and in spite of closed semaphores which, according to the railway regulations, constitutes a crime. Consequently, the switches were destroyed at many stations. Only through the exceptional measures being taken by the railwaymen this train failed, being wrecked with the consequent loss of lives.

## [9] China 3000

**RABIES POSITION.**

**LOCAL OUTBREAK NEARING ITS END.**

**NO FURTHER REPORTS.**

No fresh occurrence of rabies has been reported to the Sanitary Department during the last few days.

The last case was that of a dog taken from Wyndham Street, which had been bitten by a pointer at Praya East. Both these have been destroyed, as reported.

Two dogs which played with the one in Wyndham Street are still under observation at Kennedy Town. In the brief interval it has not been possible to say whether they are infected. Subsequent inquiries indicate that all possible sources of spreading have been explored without success.

It is taken that this last outbreak is now in hand, the "tail end" stage having been reached.

**“BELGENLAND” TOURISTS PROGRAMME**

Ship's Bereavement.

We regret to report that one of the “Belgenland” tourists, Mrs. Edward R. Bradley, wife of the prominent race horse owner of Lexington, Kentucky, died on board the boat early last Saturday morning at Shanghai of heart failure. Mrs. Bradley had not been able to leave her cabin since the ship left Yokohama.

When the ship left Yokohama, Mrs. Bradley became ill and although her condition was not regarded as serious, she did not leave the ship when the "Belgenland" anchored at Woosung, but remained in her cabin and told her friends that she did not feel able to go ashore. About midnight on Friday night her condition grew suddenly worse and despite the efforts of the ship's doctor, death came a short while after. The cause of death is given as heart failure.

An official inquiry was held aboard the “Belgeuland” prior to the body of Mrs. Bradley being taken to Shanghai on a special tender. Arrangements are being made for sending the body back to America. It is improbable that an inquiry will be held by the United States Court authorities in Shanghai since a physician was present.

## [12] SHOP ASSISTANT AND A CHEQUE

A Chinese shop assistant was brought before the Central Magistracy this morning on a charge arising from his failure to account for a sum of $1,950, a cheque for which amount was handed to him by his master to be cashed at the bank.

Defendant's explanation was that he gave the cheque to a friend to have it cashed. This man, he alleged, sent the money through a third party who had abscodded.

Detective: Sergeant McEwen obtained an adjournment to enable him to make inquiries.

## [14] P. & O. BOAT PICKS UP FISHERMEN.

**MOTOR BOAT ABANDONED.**

How ten men were saved on the high seas by a P. & O. steamer is briefly reported by Captain J. B. Browning, R.D., R.N.R., master of the "Khyber," on arrival here from Shanghai.

The ship left Yokohama, bound South, on January 12. At 4 p.m. on January 15, the crew of ten were taken off from the Japanese fishing motor boat "Iyo Maru," which was abandoned with two anchors down.

## [19] CANTON ADVANCE.

**SOLDIERS LANDED NEAR HOIHOW**

**LOCAL GENERAL RESISTS.**

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

*Hohow, Jan. 18.*

Several hundred troops despatched by the Canton Government landed last night at Foutai about 15 miles east of Hoihow, on the island of Hainan (separated from Kwangtung province proper by the Hainan Straits).

General Tang Bunin (Tang Pun-yan), the pro-Chan commander who has vacated the mainland and whose forces now hold the island, sent several regiments, by motor cars, to fight the invaders.
There is the Golden River between Foutai and Hoihow so that the anti-Canton troops have a natural line of defence.
