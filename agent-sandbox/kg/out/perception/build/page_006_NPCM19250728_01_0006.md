## [1] CROWNING GLORY.

**MR. W. J. ERYAN'S DEATH DESCRIBED**

### ……

**"EXACT FOOTSTEPS OF CHRIST."**

*New York, July 27.*

The death of Mr. W. J. Bryan occurred almost on the eve of what he was anticipating as the crowning glory of life, namely, a trip with hundreds of followers to Palestine following as nearly as possible the exact footsteps of Christ, and his plans were virtually complete.

## [2] DIED PEACEFULLY.

The death of Mr. Bryan has stunned the country. Before lying down for the last sleep Mr. Bryan told his wife he never felt better and was ready to go to the country and wage war against Modernism.

Mr. Bryan lay down after lunch on Sunday at Dayton. His wife sent a chauffeur to wake him late in the afternoon and found him dead. His wife, who was very calm, said that she was glad that her husband had died peacefully. He desired to be buried at the Arlington National Cemetery, Washington, as he had been a Colonel in the Spanish-American War.

Hundreds of messages of condolence have arrived at Dayton. The crowd, with bared heads and tear-dimmed eyes, gathered round the house. Even Mr. Bryan's bitterest opponents paid tributes to his sincerity and honesty.

## [3] THE MODERN PRESS.

**LADY ASTOR AMONG JOURNALISTS.**

Viscountess Astor, M.P., made a racy speech at a ladies' luncheon given by the London district of the Institute of Journalists, "in the course of which she voiced some interesting opinions about the modern press.

Viscountess Astor said that when she entered Parliament the average newspaper treated her advent as a joke, and for years continued to do so. "I did not mind," she added, "because I knew that in the end we women should make a joke of you. (Laughter.) I have never been frightened—of—the—Press. Great reforms have always come in spite of the Press. The newspaper owners of the country think they would and form the opinion of the country. Nonsense! They are miles behind it, and you know it in your hearts." (Laughter.) She respected journalism as a great and noble calling, however, and thought that the members of the institute had a great opportunity in fighting for a clean Press. The country was passing through a period of "horridness" as far as what she called the "Yellow Press" was concerned, but it would not last. It did not last in America. Believing intensely that the British Empire and the United States of America had a great mission in the world she pleaded for a better news service from America, in British newspapers. The peace and prohibition movements in America, she contended, were not fairly represented here. What prohibition was doing in the way of better industrial output and better homes was not related here as it should be. "I am not a prohibitionist," she declared. "I never have been, but I am for a free and democratic vote on this question. Let the people here vote for what they want. They have never had a chance as far as drink is concerned, and they never will as long as you have the Upper House."

## [4] BOOTLE FOR SALE.

**LORD DERBY'S OFFER PRICE: £1,000,000.**

London.

Lord Derby has offered to sell to the Liverpool Corporation for £1,000,000 the family's Interests in Bootle, which comprise the freehold of the whole town with the exception of the docks.

As the currency of many of the ground leases is 999 years, the rents of which cannot be included in the meantime, the Corporation may decline the offer.

Bootle, a municipal and county borough, at the mouth of the Mersey, constitutes a north-west suburb of Liverpool. Best extensive timber yards, juts mills, and factories, it contains about 400 acres of the Liverpool docks, and here are the loading berths of the American steamers. The population totals about 70,000.
A cable last week announced that Lord Derby had sold to Green Ltd, timber merchant Chesterfield, 5,000 acres of an ancestral property in the Chester district. The project included 50 farms, 500 and 2,000 leases relating to the rivers, mills, shops and works. The price paid was not disclosed.

## [5] SHANGHAI STRIKE.

**LETTER TO GONSUL A FORGERY?**

**EAMEN'S UNION REOPENED.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

It appears that the Seamen's Union was scaled owing to a threatening letter to the British Consul, which, it was learned, emanated from the Seamen's Union. It is now claimed the letter was a forgery.

When the seamen learned that the Union was closed, they showed a very nasty mood and threatened to call out all men engaged in Chinese ships. This threat, together with the request of the Chairman of the Chinese Chamber of Commerce, who also guaranteed the behaviour of the Union officials, had the desired effect in the Union being reopened.

An hour afterwards, a tender, with a number of Chinese hands aboard, was preparing to go to the assistance of the Tateashi Maru, aground near Chinkiang, when intimidators from the Union stopped the Chinese crew of the tender from going to the rescue of an "enemy" steamer in distress.

## [7] EASY-GOING SIAM.

**LAND OF RELIGIOUS TOLERATION.**

"Siam is one of the strongholds of Buddhism, and although the modern tendency to be indifferent to religious matters is as observable in Siam as elsewhere, the people generally give as much attention to the practice of their faith as does the average resident in Christian countries in applying to this daily life the principles of his belief," says Mr. W. Nunn, late Adviser to the Siamese Government on Customs, in a paper read before the Royal Society of Arts and recorded in its Journal.

The Siamese attitude towards matters spiritual—their conception of their duty to God, their fellowmen, and themselves is simple. It is not complicated by the strife of seets, by the competition of conflicting faiths, by intellectual arrogance; nor by undisciplined enthusiasm. It is an attitude of unconcerned belief, quietly held and practised.

If one of its most noticeable results is the congregating together of large bodies of idle priests, who toil not, neither do they spin, that result is not resented commonly by the people, nor does any form of priestly tyranny spring from it. The most striking characteristic of the religious life of Siam is its wonderful tolerance. Christian missionaries are as free to work in the country as if they were in a Christian land, and their educational and medical work is encouraged officially.

It is interesting to note. as bearing upon this matter of religious toleration, that the weekly day of rest which is observed in the capital, in all Government offices, is the Christin sabbat, decreed to be an official holiday by King Chulalongkorn in the year 1800. It is to be hoped that nothing will ever happen in Siam took it of its being one country, at least, where the expression of religious belief does not disturb the good will of men.

## [8] MANY WIVES.

**DON JUAN WHO POSED AS APOSTLE OF TRUTH.**

**WIDOW VICTIMS.**

A map who described himself as the Son of Zoroaster, and sent into this world "to spread light and to preach truth," failed to impress a Berlin Court of Appeal which confirmed a sentence of twelve years' imprisonment for a long series of frauds against women. It was stated in court that "he stole their wealth after stealing their hearts."

Oto Siduwelt, the man claiming to be the apostle of truth, began his career of lying and swindling twenty years ago, and, with the aid of his wife, victimised a long list of women in different parts of Germany. He posed as a nerve specialist and a psycho-analyst, and found victim after victim among fashionable and wealthy women, who were induced to part with large sums of money after a proposal of marriage.

## [9] WIFE AS SISTER.

In Hamburg Siduwelt persuaded a widow to turn over to him her entire fortune, and in Pankow, near Berlin, he became "engaged" to another widow, and induced her to dispose of her boarding house for £1,500, which she presented to him as a wedding present.

At the betrothal ceremony Siduwelt's wife assisted and posed as his sister. When the day for the wedding arrived the couple disappeared.

During the hearing of his appeal Siduwelt "simulated righteous indignation" at the treatment he had received at the hands of the law, and complained to the judge that his mission on earth had been misunderstood.

"I am the son of Zoroaster," he exclaimed fervently. "and I have been sent to spread light and carry the torch of truth through this world, but you are treating me like a common criminal." The judge, however, refused to be convinced of his apostolic mission, and confirmed the sentence imposed.

## [10] SARTORIAL EXUBERANCE.

Of late a remarkable number of young men have been seen walking the streets as if they hoped, by a judicious use of drapery, to conceal advanced elephantiasis of the lower limbs. It was at first a startling and a provocative spectacle; but in a little while custom began, as usual, to stale the clothes of our young barbarians—to such purpose that earnest-minded people are now endeavouring to determine the moral tendency which such sartorial exuberance expresses. So, doubtless, any early Briton whimsical enough to substitute burnt cork for blue wond was hailed as the first of the pessimists. This anxiety to attach a spiritual significance to vagaries of dress is a curious one; and the Oxford undergraduate journal "Isis": is rightly sarcastic at the expense of such resolute symbolists. The mutability of fashion we have always understood to be due to three causes—the desire of tailors and dressmakers to retire early in life, the desire of a few young men and women to appear distinct from everyone else, and the desire of all other young men and women not to appear distinct from anyone else. As for our undergraduates, like Malvolio, they are, we believe, not black in their minds, though frequently they may be yellow in their legs.

## [12] SCHOOL BURNS.

**SCRAMBLE TO RESCUE TREASURES.**

**SIX HOURS' BLAZE.**

St. Wilfrid's Preparatory School near Bexhill was burnt out and 100 boarders had an unexpected holiday thrill.

When the alarm was given the boys eagerly helped to remove what they could from the school, and the playing fields adjoining were littered with desks, books, and the boys' own countless treasures.

When it became impossible for them to enter the blazing building any longer, the boys watched the Bexhill and Hastings fire brigades trying to subdue the flames.

But it was six hours before the brigades were able to leave, and then the school was gutted. Most of the clothing of the boys and the masters was destroyed, and they were left with only their blazers and flanens, which they were wearing when the fire occurred.

The boys are being housed temporarily in neighbouring schools.

## [13] OUR GIRLS OF 40.

**FLINIR OF ETERINAL YOUTH DISCOVERED!**

The modern young woman of 40" was the object of Hattering remarks by Dr. Percy Hall, of Mount Vernon Hospital, London, at the congress of the Royal Institute of Public Health at Brighton in mail week.

"Never have the gentler sex been so healthy and so attractive as they are to-day," he said. "A generation ago a woman of 40 was an old lady who wore voluminous flannel petticonts and a'shawl' round her shoulders while she sat by the fire crocheting.

To-day a woman of 40 looks 20, plays golf and tennis during the day, jazzes far into the night, and comes down first to breakfast without the temper of a bear.

She is emancipated both in body and mind, and appears to have captured the elixir of eternal youth.

What has she done? Simply cast away her superfluous clothes. By wearing silk next to her skin, short skirts, and clothing low in the neck, she allows more light and air to get to her body, and as a direct consequence she has achieved youth, beauty, and health.
