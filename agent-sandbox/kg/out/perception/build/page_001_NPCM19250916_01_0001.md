## [9] FURIOUS DRIVING.

**EUROPEAN MOTORIST'S OFFENCES.**

**JATL WITHOUT OPTION.**

Charges of furious driving of a motor car and falling to report an accident in which two Chinese pedestrians were injured at Queen's Road Central at 11.30 p.m. on September 7, were preferred against Mr. C. W. Mckenzie before Mr. S. B. B. McElderry at the Central Magistracy this morning.

At the previous hearing when the case was adjourned for developments in the condition of the injured persons who were taken to the Government Civil Hospital.
Chief Inspector W. Kent stated that the injured persons were husband and wife and belonged to the working class. The woman had since been discharged from the hospital, but the man was still there and might be able to attend Court next week. There were other occupants in the car who insisted to the defendant on the desirability of stopping after the accident, but he asked them to mind their business as he was the driver of the car. One of the occupants then turned the steering wheel and brought the car back to the scene of the accident, but the defendant again drove away.
His Worship informed Mr. F. G. Vaux, who appeared for the defendant, that a plea of guilty had already been tendered, and asked if he had anything to say.

Mr. Vaux said that he had been asked by the defendant to express extreme regret for the accident and his willingness to compensate the injured persons to the best of his ability. He considered that the defendant suffered from nervousness and was not possessed of that sense of the road which he ought to have in driving a car. The car was not a valuable one, but the defendant was prepared to sell it and use the money as compensation. Counsel asked for leniency by bringing to His Worship's notice that the defendant was a married man with two children.

The defendant bore out the pleadings of his counsel, and said that words failed to express his sorrow at the unfortunate accident. "I completely lost my nerves," he added, "and have since found out that I am not a fit person to drive a car."
The records of the Traffic Department showed that the defendant had been fined $100 on two occasions last year for similar offences when he was riding a motor cycle and there was another conviction early this year. Under the circumstances His Worship said that he could not give the defendant the option of a fine in the present instance. The sentence was six weeks' hard labour and payment of $25 as compensation to the injured persons.

## [10] PICKET'S LAXITY.

**NAMTAU'S FIVE DAYS' HOLIDAY.**

Although only a temporary phase it is interesting to note reports of laxity in the work of strike pickets at two places giving access to Hongkong.
People coming to Hongkong from Canton, via Shekki, Chinshan and Macao, report that when the tow-boat reached Chinshan (near the Macao border) no picket was to be seen on duty and everybody took sampans or ricshas to Macao without interference.

## [12] $10,000 START.

**MONEY**

**RETURNED CHINESE SUBSCRIBE**

That even the uneducated class of Chinese is awakening to the danger of Bolshevism is evident from the action of certain overseas men who are now in Hongkong.

On such who made a fortune, estimated at a million dollars, in the F.M.S., is a native of the Wai Chow prefecture. He is over 60 years of age and dresses in the ordinary garb of black pongee silk. When a youth he went to Singapore to seek his fortune, and there, under the protection of the British flag, he became wealthy.

He is now convening a meeting of clansmen and other emigrants returned from the South, to raise a loan for the anti-Red cause. In an informal chat with several he opened the list with a fixed deposit receipt for $10,000.

Pointing out that the people who have the upper hand in Canton to-day are aliens—Russians or mercenary, invaders from other provinces—the old man urged the necessity of overthrowing “the traitors who are the running dogs of the Russians.”

Unless the Cantonese who have the real interests of their province and city at heart united, they would never get rid of the unscrupulous people who are trying to ruin China to serve their own selfish ends. He was too old to fight, but he would do what he could. Hence his gift of $10,000.

## [16] SHARE MARKET.

**CHAMBER'S DECISION**

**GOVERNMENT APPROACHED**

For some considerable time rumours have been current in Ice House Street of definite Government legislation with a view to opening the share market. Opinion as to the desirability or otherwise of this has been divided, some holding that an open market in the present state will lead to low quotations not reflecting the true state of stability of the respective firms, thus benefiting the larger buyer in a position to buy at the artificial reduced-rates, others holding that a necessary preliminary to the return of business to normal is an open quotation list of shares, these latter stating that they have it on good authority that the Banks have had queries which would ensure the transaction of a considerable amount of business.

The “China Mail” is in a position to state that a quasi-moratorium scheme was advanced and submitted to the Chamber of Commerce for their opinion. This was to be in the nature of an assured protection for those who have shares at the bank as security. These shares, if the hands of the banks' clients were forced, would have to be got rid of at rates which might mean ruin to those clients, and the moratorium was to provide for the compulsory “carrying” of those shares for a certain period at the end of which it was presumably thought things would have returned to a more normal state and higher prices would prevail.

A full meeting of the Chamber of Commerce Committee, representative of all the European banks, together with the Chamber's legal advisors, very carefully considered the proposed legislation and after hearing arguments from representatives of the brokers decided unanimously that the Chamber could not recommend legislation of the nature proposed.

In connection with the application for a very substantial loan, which the “China Mail” understands has been made by certain interested persons to the Government, a correspondent who favours such a loan or subsidy written pointing out that during an acute housing shortage at Singapore the Government there came forward to help the situation by advancing money at 6 percent on any new building and rubber estates which were in difficulties at the time were informed that they could get assistance from the Planters Loan, an institution supervised by the Government.

## [17] RATES PLEA:

**LANDLORDS PETITION GOVERNMENT.**

The Hongkong and Kowloon Landlords Association has, says the "Wah Taz Yat Po," sent in a petition to the Government in connection with the payment of rates on property in the Colony.
It is explained that rates are due on a whole house even if only one floor is occupied and the other flats are vacant. The petition is that rates be levied only on actually occupied premises.

The same paper goes on to say that this question of rates is one of two main questions which will receive the Association's earnest attention. This does not conflict with the Tenants' Association nor does a proposed appeal for laxity in the carrying out of development on Crown Land purchased under terms which require building on within stipulated periods.

## [19] GOING TO DOCK.

**“HONAM” TIMPORARILY CHASES SAILINGS.**

**PADDLE BOX DAMAGE**

The s.s. "Homem," which for nearly a fortnight has maintained communication between Hongkong and Canton, being the only ship during that time to have carried the red ensign into the latter port, will have temporarily to cease sailings on account of damage done to the ship's paddle box as she lay at the buoy off Shameen in the course of the last trip. It was found as the ship was about to have that the buoy had fouled the paddle box and the assistance of a tug had to be obtained before it could be engaged.

The s.s. "Honam" was still at the wheel this morning but it was learned upon enquiry that she was about to proceed to dock. The Superintendent of the Stembont Company has made an examination, but the extent of the damage is not known. The arrangements as conceived at present are that she will still again us soon as repairs are effected.

The long arm of coincidence is seen at work in that the "eye" on the ship's paddle box, as already reported, was removed by presumably hopefully superstitious persons on Saturday night.

The passengers taken by the "Honum" on her last trip all left the ship, the foreigners by launches and motor bons, whilst a Chinese passenger jumped into the water and was not seen again. Chinese members of the crew state that in their opinion the strong current was too much for him and that he was drowned.

The "Homem brought to Hongkong 46 passengers, including 17 Chinese who either had passed or eluded pickets; also 43 bags of small and similar number of pockets of cargo.

## [23] MAY 30 RIOT.

**JUDICIAL INQUIRY NOTE**

**POLICE CHIEF'S POSITION**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Peking, Sept. 15:*

The Senior Minister has happened the Foreign Office a note on behalf of the interested Powers stating that the Powers decided to request the British, American, and Japanese Governments to designate a jurist for the commission of enquiry in order definitely to clear up the unfortunate incidents of May 30 in Shanghai and expressing a hope that the Chinese Government will see fit to appoint a Chinese jurist to sit on the commission.

The note adds that the Shanghai Municipal Council have bound themselves to abide by the findings of the commission, and the Commissioner of Police, Mr. McEuen, will be suspended without prejudice until the completion of the inquiry.

The American Commissioner is Mr. Justice E. Finley Johnson, of the Philippine Supreme Court, and the British, Sir Henry Gollan, Chief Justice of Hongkong.
