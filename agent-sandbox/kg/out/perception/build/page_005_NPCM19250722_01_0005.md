## [1] COUNTY CRICKET.

**HOBBS NEAR W. G. GRACE'S RECORD:**

### ・

Will he do is?—That is the question cricketers all over the world are asking themselves when considering the feats this season, at Home, of J. B. Hobbs, the Surrey professional batsman and All-England player. In the latest results to hand, he is given as having made another three-figure score. According to Wisden he had 110 centuries in first-class cricket to his credit at the end of last season. He made four in Australia, making his tally 114. This season he has made 11 to date, bringing his total up to 125, which is only one behind the record of 126, standing in the name of Dr. W. G. Grace.

Followers of Surrey in Hongkong—and there are quite a useful, number of them—will be pleased to read also that Hobbs is the first this year to reach the 2,000 figure, having already led to the 1,000 stage. Other good news for Surreyites includes the defeat of Kent at Blackheath and the moving up to second place in the championship as the result of another lapse on the part of Lancashire, who went down to Middlesex in spite of their bowling talent. Yorkshire have shed two more points, and there is a still chance of a keen struggle at the end when it had already seemed as if Yorkshire (the champions) would have another walk-over.

The results, as cabled, are given below, together with the table to date. Several surprises are included, especially the away-from-home victories.

## [10] REFUSED FARES.

**MAGISTRATE AS COMPLAINANT.**

**RAIN EXCUSS ACCEPTED.**

### ——

An incident which occurred in the early days of the strike had its sequel at the Central Magistracy this morning when Trufie Sub-Inspector C. T. Alexander charged before Mr. S. B. B. McElderry two taxieab drivers with refusing to accept fare.

The complainant, Mr. R. A. D. Forrest (Second Magistrate), stated in evidence that at 4.45 p.m. on June 23, he was standing outside the Post Office building with Dr. Anderson and Mr. Kinross. It was raining very heavily, but after it had subsided a little he approached one of two taxigets standing abreast at Pedder Street and told the driver to take him to Happy Valley. The driver stated that he could not start the engine, and started to open up the bonnet of his car. Witness then approached the driver of the other car with the same result. Witness then noted the numbers of the cars, and proceeded on his way by another taxicab, which arrived

One of the accused stated that shortly before the complainant wanted to hire his car he had to refuse another fare, as he had found, after cracking up, that water had entered the magneto owing to the heavy rain and he could not get the engine to start. He therefore knew that the engine was out of order and told the complainant so.

Sub-Inspector Alexander agreed that the presence of water in the magneto would prevent the engine starting, but submitted that the two accused should have pushed their cars to the rear, even as far as Blake Pier or the Company's garage, when they found that the engines were out of order; or else have reported the matter to the police. They did nothing and continued to occupy a front position on the stand.

His Worship accepted the play put forward by the defence that it was gaining heavily at the time, and discharged both the accused.

MarsMai Chang Tso-lin Left for Mukden at 11.30 last night

Chinese circles are of the opinion that the departure of Chang Tso-lin from Tient-in to Mulden last night will not materially affect the situation. It is considered that most matters for which he came down have been decided. It is understood that Chang Tso-lin is not offering any objection to Sun Yue's advance on Siamfu, nor to Lu Ying-hsiang's resignation. Chinese do not anticipate that Chang Tso-lin's departure will cause any fresh move on General Feng Yu-hsiang's part.

The newly appointed Civil Governor of Anhui, Wu Ping-hsiang has wired the Government declaring that he has persuaded the Anhui military leaders to give up the independent movement which had threatened to lead to serious developments. Wu Ping-hsiang is assuming his post at Anking on July 22. The Cabinet has decided to remit ten thousand dollars for famine relief in Hunan and has also adopted the Waichiuopu's proposal to open Wanhsien in Szechuen as a commercial port.
