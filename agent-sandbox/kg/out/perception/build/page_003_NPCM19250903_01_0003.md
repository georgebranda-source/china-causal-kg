## [23] RELICS OF THE '45.

**PRINCE CHARLIE.**

**PRINCE CHARLIE EXHIBITION.**

The Mackintosh of Mackintosh, Lord-Lieutenant of Invernessshire, opened at Fort-William an exhibition of relics of Prince Charlie and the '45 campaign.

After The Mackintosh had been welcomed by Provost Stewart, Lochiel, in a reminiscent speech, recapitulated some of the chief features of the memorable rising. Lochaber, as they knew, was the cradle, the heart, and the grave of the '45. It was at Lochnanuagh that Prince Charlie had landed. It was at Glenfinnan that he first raised his standard, and it was from Lochaber that he recruited his original army. It was to Lochaber that the Prince returned after the disaster at Culloden, and it was from Lochnanuagh that he again finally departed to France, after those perilous and romantic wanderings throughout the Western Highlands and Islands. Every nook in Lochaber spoke to them of those stirring days. At his (Lochiel's) own home at Achnacarry, the beeches which an ancestor of his had dug in, as he thought, temporarily, when he left to join the Prince could be seen from his dining-room, whilst Loch Arkaig and Glen Pean could boast of many memories of the Young Chevalier. They all knew the Prince's association with the House of Moy, and it was eminently fitting that The Mackintosh should open their exhibition.

Speaking from the steps of the Museum building... which is situated in Cameron Square, and where a large assemblage had gathered, The Mackintosh said he wished to support the efforts being made to bring back to them reminiscences of the '15 and the '45. In those far-off days they were called rebels, and in a sense they were still rebels at heart, but on an occasion like the present-he thought they might fairly let themselves go.

After touching on incidents in the two rebellions, The Mackintosh contrasted the conditions which must have existed when Prince Charlie marched with his army to Derby and those now in vogue. Paying a tribute to the scenic beauties of Lochaber, which, he said, had the highest hills and the deepest lochs, he referred to what the men and women of the district had done for their country.

Concluding with a reference to certain accoutrements recovered from Mulroy, where was fought the last clan battle between the Mackintoshes and the Macdonals of Keppoch, the Lord-Lieutenant formally declared the exhibition open, and expressed the hope that it would meet with the success which it deserved.
