## [11] COMMERCE

**LAND SLUMP**

A recent remarkable full of land prices in suburbs of Tokyo has taken pluco, and has occasioned much worry to the real estate companies which, through propaganda and manipulation in past years have artificially raised the prices for land. The high cost of living and the shortage of dwelling houses in the city limits of Tokyo in recent years has caused a rapid development of adjoining rural districts of Tokyo and farms and forests were sold in many places for residential quarters. Many real estate companies, "such as the Hackone Land and Building Company and Tokyo Land Tenement House Company, purchased large tracts of land in suburbs at cheap prices and sold them to those who intended to live in the "suburban districts. This tendency was the more remarkable after the great earthquake and fire of 1923, when people who lost their dwellings sought refuge in the suburbs, and the residential quarters were expanded with great rapidity. More than 150,000 houses are reported to have been built in these few years in Tokyo's suburbs, most of which were erected after the earthquake and fire. The purchase of large jobs of land and the erection of tenentent houses were undertaken by these real estate companies through speculation in anticipation that Tokyo's resident land quarters would be entirely removed to suburbs.

When the boom broke, the firms raised the land prices through artificial measures and mining people were induced to purchase land at high prices. A set-back started last year in economic circles and this caused active sales in houses and lands in city limits of Tokyo. Good lots of land were sold at cheaper prices. This has told severely upon the business of these land companies who intended to reap profit in the sales of suburban lands and there are few persons seeking to buy land in suburbs under the prevailing high prices. Consequently, these companies are now in an awkward position financially as no sales of suburb lands are being made at present. At the same time the land prices are falling precipitously not only in Tokyo city but also in the suburbs and the actual land prices for transaction are 60 per cent, cheaper than those named by these real estate firms. Even in Shibuya, Meguro, Kahata, Yodobushi, Tsebukuro, Mejiro, and other adjoining towns which are comparatively convenient in point of transportation and cheaper in daily living, land prices are very low and at almost half of the price which prevailed right after the September

## [12] MANY YÁCANT NOW.

According to the latest investigation made by the Tokyo Prefectural Government, vacant houses are increasing daily in Tokyo suburbs and there are now 75,000 such houses throughout the suburban districts of Tokyo. The Dojinkai, an organisation for tenement houses under subsidy of the Tokyo prefectural authorities recently erected 4,000 houses in the suburbs and more than 3,000 of these houses are left vacant despite the cheap rentals. The authorities concerned are now discussing measures to fill these vacant houses.

## [14] AND FINANCE.

**SHIPBUILDING**

**THE GERMAN YARDS:**

Some interesting details concerning the Deutsche Werft, the German shipyard which secured the much-discussed contract for the Furness With ships, are given in a message from the "M.G.C." correspondent at Berlin. The concern was founded in 1918 from very important German industrial groups—namely, the Gute Hoffnungshuttle, the A.E.G., and the Hamburg-Amerika Line. During the last year of the war and the following year the company built large shipyards on the Elbe and installed by far the most modern equipment in Germany. By its close connection with its associated organisations the company is not only able to work at low costs by effecting economies at every point in the complex process of production, but is in a position to make use of very important patents. For steam engines the Deutsche Werft have at their disposal the patents of the Gute Hoffnungschutte, for steam turbines the patents of the A.E.G., while for Diesel engines they are the German licensees of Burmeister and Wain, Copenhagen. Actually the yard was constructed and equipped during the depreciation period and, amongst others, this factor has contributed a good deal to low construction costs. The gold mark balance sheet has not been published yet, but the following assets in the balance sheet drawn up at December 31, 1923, may be noted. The paid-up capital amounted to 60 million paper marks and debentures issued in 1920 to the extent of 30 million paper marks are outstanding. Important as the advantages of being constructed when the mark was on its downward course are, more important in the competitive power of the Deutsche Werft is its close inter-relations with other industries and its modern equipment.

The British yards may not be able to make such an imposing showing in their inter-relation with feeding industries, but in technique and traditional skill this country still holds the lead. In the long run price will prove the ruling factor in the acceptance of shipbuilding tenders, but, as Mr. Lawrence Holt points out in a letter to the "Liverpool Post and Mercury," those who are ready to pay 10 per cent. more for a ship built in one of our good yards are probably wise in their generation. Mr. Holt has just returned from a visit to ten shipyards on the Continent, and the reasons he gives for the ability of Germany to build cheaper ships than our own yards are lower wages, universality of piecework, longer hours, no restriction on the use of labour-saving machines, no demarcation obstacles between trades, no redundant labour, and keener work by management and workers. In the light of these observations of a prominent shipowner there hardly seems an unanswerable case for the artificial stimulation of our shipbuilding industry by means of a subsidy, which would tend to remove an effective incentive towards the reorganisation of our yards and the modernisation of their plant.

## [15] LESS FISH EATEN.

That people are.eating less fish is a race disclosed by a five-years' review of the sea fisheries issued by the Ministry of Agriculture and Fisheries.

According to the report the average yearly consumption per head fell from 41½ lbs. for the period 1907-1910 to 88½ lbs. in 1920-1923.
"It is clean," adds the report, "that fish as presented to the consumer is not sufficiently attractive to be given a permanent position as a principal item in the menu; and that if the home market is to expand it is essential to educate the consumers to the dietetic value of good fresh fish, and at the same time ensure that supplies of such fish, at prices which will enable it to compete with all classes of meat are continuously available."

## [22] TITN FINANOIER'S DEATH

The mining industry in both Cornwall and Nigeria has lost an ardent supporter by the death, after a long illness, of Mr. Oliver Wetliered, whose appointments included the chairmanship of the Cornish Chamber of Mines, and the deputy-chairmanship of the Nigerian Chamber of Mines. He was also chairman of the Dolcoath, Geevor, and Rayfield companies.
"It was through Mr. Wethered that outside capital was found for the old Dolcoath tin mine, Cornwall, when it became a limited liability concern in 1805. That new capital gave the mine a fresh lease of life, during which it paid numerous dividends."

The Geevor mine, which was being worked on a very small scale when it was taken over and financed by Mr. Wethered, is now producing at the rate of over 500 tons black tin per annum.

Capital was also found by Mr. Wethered for a number of Nigerian tin-mining undertakings.
