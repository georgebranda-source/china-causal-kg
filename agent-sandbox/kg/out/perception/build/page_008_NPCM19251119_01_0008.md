## [20] KOWLOON SHOOTING.

**ACCUSEID COMMITTED TO SESSIONS.**

The three men arrested in connection with the shopping affair at Chatham Road on Friday night were again before the Kowloon Magistrate yesterday, when evidence of the shooting at Sergeant Clarke was taken and details were given as to the arrest of the two men at the residence of Captain Joll.

Mr. Leo d'Almada appeared for the man charged with unlawful possession of a revolver and shooting at Sergeant Clarke. Another man was charged with unlawful possession of the revolver. The case against the third man was one of unlawful assembly, and this was adjourned for a week to be dealt with summarily.

Capt. L. D. Joll, testified to being the owner of the revolver, which when he went to camp he left in a drawer. The second accused was previously engaged by him as cook, and had borne a very good character.

Mrs. Joll gavo evidence of handling, over to the police the revolver which she found in a drawer, after a second search. The weapon was at the rear of the drawer and witness could not say whether it was there when she first searched but was of the opinion, after the arrests of the two men in the house, that the revolver was put in the drawer after her first search.

Lient. Aucott, R.A., stated that on being asked by Mrs. Joll, on their return to the house, to close the doors and windows, before leaving, in case the servants should gain admittance through them, he noticed a leg protruding from under a bed in a spare room. The man was pulled out and was followed by the second and third.

The two accused were committed for trial at the Criminal Sessions.

## [21] DEATH INQUIRY.

**WEST POINT MYSTERY.**

**MURDER OR SUICIDE?**

At a death inquiry conducted by Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy yesterday, the jury were asked to determine whether it was a case of suicide or murder.

The inquiry was into the death of a Chinese who was found in a house at West Point with his throat cut and a chopper beside him. While investigations made by the police practically satisfied them that the man's injuries were self-inflicted, Dr. E.P. Minnett considered from an examination made of the body and the nature of the wounds that there was less possibility of them being self-inflicted. There had been three distinct blows on the neck. Death was stated to have been caused by haemorrhage and shock.

After further evidence the inquiry was adjourned till Wednesday afternoon.

## [23] TOOK FRIGHT.

**SAI WAN HO ROBBERY SEQUEL.**

**FIVE YEARS' SENTENCE.**

The evasion of a police searcher at Sai Wan Ho led to the arrest of a man on suspicion of having been concerned in a robbery which took place there earlier in the day.

He was taken to the police station and at an identification parade was picked out by one of the inmates of a house in Tai On Street as the man who came in company with three others when the master of the house was away.

In evidence at the Criminal Sessions this morning the woman said that the prisoner had been armed with a dagger, had taken rings from her and gagged her.

An amah ran to a little iron bridge, outside the room adjacent to the street and called "save life." The prisoner tried to drag her back but was unsuccessful and the robbers then took fright and ran away. When the police called they found that two daggers had been cast away as the men left, also wire and wood to be used for gags.

The prisoner was arrested after evading a police searcher who fired at him and brought him down. Nothing was found on him and when charged with complicity in the robbery he said "I did not know she had been robbed."
The jury convicted "and the prisoner was sentenced to five years' hard labour.

## [24] CRUEL CONSTABLE.

**SERIOUS ASSAULT ON OLD MAN.**

**JAIL WITHOUT OPTION.**

An Indian. Constable was charged at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning with misconducting himself and assaulting an old man at No. 112, AppIU Street. on October 15.

The evidence was that at 8.30 p.m. on the day in question an Indian constable visited the first floor of the house to search for opium. An old man objected to this intrusion and was struck across the head with a stick by the constable. Police whistles were blown, and a Chinese constable saw the defendant in the street when he should have been on duty at Cheung Sha Wan.

Defendant. denied the charge and also an allegation that he left another constable with whom he went on duty at 8 p.m.

His Worship said that he had gone carefully into the case and could see no possibility of defendant's innocence on the evidence before him. A fine of $25, or one month's hard labour, was imposed on the first charge. In respect of the second charge. His Worship said that a very serious assault had been committed on an old man, and he could not give defendant the option of a fine. The sentence was two months' hard labour on this charge.

## [26] WORK OF MERCY.

**ACTIVITIES OF ST. VINCENT DE PAUL SOCIETY.**

**WHERE THE MONEY GOES.**

In presenting the statement of accounts for 1924 and a report of the Society's work during the first nine months of 1925, the Council of the Society of St. Vincent de Paul feel that they can again appeal with confidence to the generosity of the Hongkong public on the occasion of the street sale of roses on Wednesday, December 2 and at the 42nd al fresco fête which will be held on December 6.
In spite of the reflection of the prevailing conditions in South China upon "Hongkong, the de-serving nature of the appeal will doubtless ensure the generous support with which it has met in previous years from the public of the Colony. For the worthy nature of the charitable work carried out by the Society to be appreciated one has only to examine the report and statement of accounts in which every transaction carried out in connection with the works of charity under the negis of the Society is clearly set out.

Over $23,000 was handled by the Society during 1924 and the largest sum expended was for relief in money and provisions, the application of this following visits paid to the homes of the poor. It should be borne in mind that in this as well as in other aspects of the work there is no distinction of nation, class or creed and that no work of charity is foreign to the Society.

## [27] POOR CHILDREN'S EDUCATION.

Another branch of work in which the Society is particularly interested is that of educating the children of the poor, to ensure the efficient management of which, an Education Sub-Committee has been organised by the Council of the Society, their chief duties being to examine and sign the weekly school reports of the students, to look after their moral and intellectual improvement and to provide them with clothes, shoes, books, stationery, etc., when required. It is gratifying to note that this new branch has been working very successfully to the satisfaction of both teachers and parents.

Owing to many of the poor having to live in small premises and congested areas constituting not only a danger to their own health but a grave moral danger for their children, it is gratifying to note that improvements have been effected and flats and cubicles in less overcrowded districts have been secured.

Other works of mercy under-taken by the Society are in connection with temporary relief and passages to Europeans and other destitutes from outside the Colony, the relief of the aged poor of the Colony at the Home in Kowloon Tong, general medical help and supply of clothing and shoes, and finding for the children under their care suitable employment. In this connection most valuable emergency work was done in the strike for which the Society has been especially thanked by the Government.
