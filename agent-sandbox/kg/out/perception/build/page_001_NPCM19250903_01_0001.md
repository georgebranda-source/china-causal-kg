## [5] SEAMEN'S STRIKE:

**NOW FIZZLING OUT AT HULL.**

**MORE SIHPS GEP AWAY.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, Sept. 2.*

In spite of the activity of pickets at Hull to-day the unofficial strike of seamen seems to be fizzling out. The "Neville" sailed with a full crew, and the Wilson liner "Albamin" secured a crew for the Mediterranean voyage.

There is more extensive picketing at Liverpool where the ex-Minister Shinwell is the organiser for the Marine Workers Union, but no ships have been held up.

The Megantic for Quebec and the "Cedric" and "Carmunia" for New York are signing on hands slowly.

Shipping officials state that men are pouring in from other ports for the purpose of signing on.

## [14] COAL CRISIS.

**COOLIDGE AND DECISIVE**

**CONSOLIDATING RAILWAYS.**

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

Swampcott, Massachusetts,

As a result of a conference between President Coolidge and Senator Watson of India it is understood that the Administration will be to amend Eschummin's Railway Law at the coming session of congress so as to force railway consolidation after a period of six years.

It was agreed that approval of the van Sweringen proposal now before the Inter-State Commerce Commission would do much to clear the way for numerous voluntary consolidations.

Senator Watson, interviewed after the conference, stated that if the coal crisis became acute the Government would take decisive action.

## [16] OBITUARY.

It is with regret that the “China Mail” records the death at noon to-day of Mr. Ho Cheuk, assistant compradore to the Hongkong and Shanghai Bank, the seventh son of Mr. and Mrs. Ho Fook, and nephew of Sir Robert Ho Tung.

Mr. Ho Cheuk was taken ill with malignant malarial fever about a week ago and passed away at the early age of 34. Sympathy will go out to his wife and children in their bereavement.

Of a retiring and kindly disposition, Mr. Ho Cheuk was always considered a genial personality by his colleagues who were impressed by his knowledge of banking. Mr. Ho Wing (Hongkong Bank), Mr. Ho Kwong (Messrs. E. D. Sassoon & Co.), Mr. Ho Iu (Mercantile Bank) and Mr. Ho Leung (Messrs. Jardine, Matheson & Co., Ltd.) are brothers to the deceased.

## [19] “HONAM” LEAVES.

**300 PASSENGERS AND 15 TONS OF CARGO.**

**TO ANCHOR OFF SHAMEEN.**

Crowds of Chinese this morning gathered on spots along the Praya adjacent to the Steamboat Company's Canton wharf long before the hour for departure of the A.S. Honam on its trip to Canton. They were still there, gazing, incredulously, when prompt to the minute and after the last bells had rung, the paddes revolved and the steamer left the wharf. This was at 8 a.m.

Total passengers aboard numbered a little over 300, of whom 15 were Europeans. General cargo, consisting chiefly of foodstuffs, amounted to about 15 tons. The ship is to anchor among the men-of-war off Shameen and will not discharge at the Canton wharf which, of course, is in Chinese territory and where there might be trouble with pickets. Passengers who wish to board or leave must make their own arrangements and Shameen is also to make its own arrangements for taking off the cargo if no coolies are available.
The Honam leaves Capton at 8 a.m. to tomorrow morning, arriving back at Hongkong at 3 p.m. It is probable that she will then run regularly, the “China Mail” understands.

## [20] PIOUS HOPES.

**WHEN WILL TRADE START AGAIN?**

**BUSINESS MEN'S VIEWS.**

Chinese business men are evincing great interest in the trip of the s.s. "Homen" to Canton. They think it a happy inspiration to have despatched the vessel and wondered why the matter was not broached before.

Several seen to-day by a "China Mail" reporter, the majority with extensive Canton business interested predicted that to-day would mark a definite stop forward to bring conditions to normal.

General Cheung Kai-shek is not a native of Kwongtung. He is not a politician but only a militant and it will be advantageous to him to see trade restored. His word will be law. To-day he may be too busy to attend to strikers and labour unions and the "Honum" may be ignored. But if the stand he has taken is any criterion he will soon reduce the power of the guild. Meanwhile, everybody is so impoverished that the strike pickets will be glad to squeeze cargo and passengers. For a time this will continue till the Hongkong workers have had sufficient opportunity to return. Then there will be no more power behind the so-called labour politicians and that is the end.

Another was not so optimistic. He thought that intercourse would be winked at but only under substantial payment of bribes so that trade with Hongkong would be under a serious handicap.
