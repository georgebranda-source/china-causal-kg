## [2] DRINK ADDICTS.

**SALVATIONISTS PRAISE FOI PROHIBITION.**

**WINNERCOM SEQUETFON GONE**

*(Router's American Service.)*

Washington April 10

When the third week of the Prohibition Conference opened, this morning a "Dryss" witness produced figures showing that the convictions under the Prohibition Law grow from 20,000 in 1922 to 38,000 in 1925.

Colonel Jenkins, a Salvation Army officer claimed that there had been great improvement in the effectiveness of the enforcement measures, and opposed the re-instatement of beer or wine. He said:—"Before Prohibition 50 per cent. of the cases that came before us were drink addicts. Since Prohibition only one per cent. of the cases are traceable to that cause, whilst winerborn seduction once so frequent, is now a thing of the past."

## [8] COMMUNISTS'

**SENSATIONAL CASE OPENS IN PARIS.**

**NOTABLE WITNESSES**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

Paris: April 19.

The trial, which is expected to last a fortnight, has opened at the Seine Assizes of two Communists, Clere and Bernadon, on a charge of willful murder in connection with a shooting affair on April 29, 1925.

The circumstances pointed carefully to an organised attack on Fascisti, of whom four were killed and eleven wounded.

Clerc and Bernadon were arrested holding revolvers. The former says that he fired in self-defence. The latter asserts that he picked up but did not fire a revolver.

A number of celebrities have been called as witnesses, including MM. Caillaux, Leon Blum, Malvy, Paul Boncour and Blasco Banez, whose connection with the case is not clear. The majority have written to the Judge regretting that they are otherwise engaged.

## [10] AMERICA. DECLINES

**NOT TO DISCUSS RESERVATION ON WORLD COURT**

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

Mr. Frank Kellogg has sent a formal notification that "Americans will not participate in the World Court Conference at Geneva on September 1 to the American Legation at Berne for delivery to the League.

Mr. Kellogg says he thought that it would be a matter for regret if the Council did anything to create an impression that there were substantial difficulties on the way of direct communication with the various signatories. The American Government does not consider that any new agreement is necessary to give effect to the conditions and reservations on the basis of which America is prepared to adhere to the World Court.

The American refusal to participate in the World Court Conference has produced disappointment in League Circles, though it is felt that the Conference will be held on September 1 in spite of that. None of the signatories has replied to the League's invitation to attend, and it is thought that the matter must now come up at the June session of the Council.

## [13] LOCARNO TREATY

**RATIFICATIONS NOT YET DEPOSITED.**

**NO DEFAULTERS ULTIMATELY**

*(British Wireless Service.)*

Rugby; April 19.

Sir Austen Chamberlain, Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs, replying to a question in the House of Commons, said that none of the contracting parties had so far deposited their ratification of the Treaty of Locarno with the League of Nations at Geneva.

A Labour member asked if there was any doubt that some Government might not ratify the Treaty.

Sir A. Chamberlain replied: 'Certainly not; there is no doubt about it.'

## [14] FILM MONOPOLIES.

**DETRIMENTAL TO BRITISH PRESTIGE.**

**AN IMPORTANT PHASE.**

The Federation of British Industries has called the attention of the President of the Board of Trade to the practical monopoly obtained by foreign film-producing concerns in cinema programmes throughout the Empire as being most detrimental to British prestige and prejudicial to the interests of the Empire, especially in those Dominions, containing large coloured populations.

The hope is expressed that the subject will be placed on the agenda for the Imperial Conference in October.

## [16] MUSSOLINI'S BID.

**FRANCO-TURKISH FEARS**

The French and Turkish Press are apprehensive concerning Signor Mussolini's intentions in North-West Africa and Asia Minor, respectively.

There have been reports of a Grneco-Italian Pact and Greek military preparations in which Italy is assisting, and on the top of this comes the news that the Turks are mobilising, and the Turkish news papers give prominence to rumours of a possible conflict with Italy, though they insist that there is "nothing abnormal in the situation."

Mussolini's visit to Tripoli has been undoubtedly responsible for the most alarming conjectures, including his making a bid for leadership of the Arab world, and also his determination to secure for Italy a role equal to that of France and Spain in Tangier.
