## [5] SERIOUS CHARGE.

*London, October 23.*

Hayley Morris and Madeline Roberts were again remanded at Uckfield Police Court. Morris was granted bail of £10,000 and Roberts £400.

The prosecution alleged that the accused were engaged in a conspiracy with the object of systematic seduction of young women and girls procured by means of advertisements in papers, or through the agency of registry offices, representing that the girls' services were required as maid companions to Roberts or to look after the dogs. The only qualifications necessary were apparently youth and attractiveness.
The prosecution arose out of Scotland Yard enquiries arising out of a complaint to the police by the father of a girl employed as a result of the advertisements.
The witnesses for the prosecution included the accused woman's 15-year-old sister, who burst into tears when asked about her relations with Morris and sobbingly pleaded: "Can't you take it from the statement I made the other day."

Last Monday's cables stated:—Mr. Hayley Morrissey, aged 37, living at Pipingford Park, an estate of a thousand acres at Nutley, Sussex, and his housekeeper, Madeline Roberts (20) were charged at Uckfield Police Court to-day with conspiring to procure a girl of fifteen "in order that Morrissey might earnestly know her." Both were remanded and bail refused. Morrissey is well-known in Sussex and is very wealthy, having made his fortune in China.

Wu Pei-fu has forwarded a manifesto to the legations, through the Consular authorities at Hankow.

## [8] REV. G. R. LINDSAY.

The “China Mail” is pleased to learn that the Rev. G. R. Lindsay, vicar of St. Andrew's Church, Kowloon is well on the way to recovery. Mr. Lindsay had a bad attack of dengue fever and is still staying at the Vicarage. ## [10] China the Initial

**ROBBERS BUSY.**

**PROGRESS MAKES THIEVES!**

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

HoiHow (Hainan Is.), Oct. 20.
National Day (October 10) and
Confucius Day (October 14) passed
off without any trouble or
demonstration. The Government
schools have opened with about
one-third of their usual enrol
ment. The students seem very
friendly.

The country is still very unsettled and robber bands are working in every district. In Vun Sang district the robbers are so bad that people are fleeing to the district city for protection or locking up their homes and sleeping hidden under bushes or trees in the open country at night.

It is reported that at Nam I recently, fifty miles south of Hoihow, a band of robbers burned about a hundred houses and killed over a hundred people. About fifty robbers were among those killed. It is an opinion among some of the Chinese that the rapid development of motor cars and roads in the island have driven chair coolies and burden bearers out of business and turned them into thieves!

A regiment in Dam Tsiu district rebelled against the Government and all the motor cars in the vicinity of Holhow were used to rush troops to that district. It is reported that the rebellion has been put down, fifty rebel soldiers captured, and the remainder forced to retire to the mountains.
The local Government has a cartridge factory supplying all ammunition necessary and guns of an inferior quality are also made in Hainan. These facts are the recently constructed roads and the phones enable the quick despatch of troops and give the present Government great advantage in case of uprisings.

On October 3, Dr. Henry M. McCandliss suffered a slight stroke of paralysis from which he is making a good measure of recovery. He completed, last July, forty years of active medical work in Hainan under the American Presbyterian Mission. He founded the Hoihow hospital and has been at the head of it up to the present time building it up to one of the large hospitals of China. As soon as he is able to travel, Dr. and Mrs. McCandliss, who also leaves behind her a large record of work accomplished among the Chinese, will leave for Hongkong and probably for América, although their plans are not yet formulated. Dr. Clarence G. Salsbury is now in charge of the hospital.

## [11] COOLIE TRAFFIC.

Because of unsettled conditions in Hainan, last week two steamers took 1,700 coolies to Singapore. The coolie traffic seems to be normal and a large number of ships are calling at Hoihow. With a modern harbour and peace in the interior Hainan will have a brilliant prospect for progress on commercial lines.

In spite of business depression the streets of Hqihow are assuming the appearance of Hongkong streets. Instead of the old narrow byways, there are paved avenues lined on either side with clean new two and three storey buildings.

### [13] $1 Dividend.

<sub>section under [12] DAIRY FARM CO.</sub>

The balance at credit of profit and loss account for the year after writing off $109,316.68 and providing $7,000 for bad and doubtful debts, amounts to $220,681.74 which includes the sum of $6,200.61 brought forward from last year. This amount your directors recommend shall be dealt with as follows:—Pay dividend, of $1 per share, on 200,000 shares, $100,000; and typhoon insurance fund, $6,704.73; carry forward $13,887.01.

In the previous year of the company's working we made a profit of $92,000 on the sale of our Kowloon property, which made possible a dividend of $1.50 per share. In the absence of such a profit during the period covered by present accounts, your directors cannot recommend any larger dividend than the $1 per share as mentioned.

The amount written off various accounts is slightly more than last year. In the 1924 accounts there was an exceptional amount of $20,487.53 written off for bad and doubtful debts. This year there has been appropriated for that purpose the sum of $7,000. It is almost impossible to gauge our bad accounts at the present time but your directors feel confident that the above amount is more than ample.

During the year our new East Point plant and stores came into complete operation, and it was thus possible to close down our Lai-chi-kok works and transfer all our stores, etc. to East Point. By doing so we hope to effect a considerable saving in this department both in fuel and labour. We have now thoroughly up to date plant and cold stores, and are thus in a satisfactory position to meet all demands for storage accommodation and ice.
