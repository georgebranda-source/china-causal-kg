## [16] [BY

Boxing is a topical subject in Hongkong at the present moment, and I considered it rather appropriate that the last mailed copy of the Cardiff "Evening Express" should contain the announcement of the inception of a movement to establish a Driscoll Memorial Fund. It has not been decided yet what form the memorial to Jimmy will take but a committee of influential South Wales sportsmen has been formed to go into the matter.
Some readers may wonder why that should be considered a matter of sufficient importance to be noted by a writer who is after all thousands of miles away from the scene of the late Jimmy Driscoll's pugilistic activities, but not, I venture to state, those who had any experience in whatever capacity of his clean sportsmanship throughout his career, his kindly nature outside the ring and, yes, inside the roped arena too. Any sportsmen interested are requested to send their shillings or dollars to the "Evening Express" Cardiff and to mark their letter "Driscoll Memorial Fund."

Those of us who are in direct communication with colliery districts in the Homeland must have been acquainted with harrowing tales of the distress among miners and their families and our instinctive protest has been tempered somewhat by the expression of opinion of experts who state definitely that the industry in its present state is not paying its way and that the action of owners in closing collieries with deposits expensive to work, or the working of them with as few men as is possible. has been forced upon them. There was trouble in the ranks of the Trade Unionists when some miners agreed to work longer than their Trade Union hours in order to keep one mine going, but the expressed opinion of the general public was one of approval at what was after all but a common-sense proposition.

It would appear that this concession on the part of a section of the miners has led the owners to consider how best they may give a quid pro quo, for details are to hand concerning a plan to operate the Vauxhall Colliery, Ruabon, North Wales, which is to be kept open after all as a result of negotiations with the owners.

The mine for three months will be hauded over to the manager and men, and the owners have agreed to waive all claims to profits made. The men have drawn up a scheme in which it is suggested that a public subscription should be opened, and should any loss ensue, then this money will be used to defray the loss. If a profit is made, or provided no loss is incurred, then at the end of three months the money subscribed to the fund will be returned to the contributors plus interest at the rate of 5 per cent. per annum.

It is actions such as these which lead to that good feeling between owners and miners which has been so woefully lacking in recent times and yet which is so vitally necessary if the industry is prosper and with it the general welfare of a large section of the population of the Principality.

## [20] *(no headline: column 5)*

After a complete remodelling and conditioning which makes her by far the most majestic round-the-world liner in regular service, the Dollar s.s. President Polk was to have arrived in Shanghai this Tuesday from New York on her third voyage around the globe.
The Polk has been laid up in New York since October 12 of last year; undergoing repairs, and alterations. She was gutted in a fire which broke out at the New York wharves the day after she arrived there from her second world cruise.

After being out of the service for six months, she will return to her old schedule in the Dollar service as one of the most palatial liners running to the Orient, says the "China Press" of last Sunday.
The passenger accommodation of the Polk has been increased until she can now carry 150 first class tourists. These include six suites in addition to a large number of rooms with showers and tub baths and an unusually large number of cabins with public bath.
Another feature of the Polk which is not found on other President liners is the enclosure of both promenade decks with pull-man windows. This takes the place of the former arrangement of a shelter deck.

A gymnasium and a children's nursery have been added to the vessel's equipment and in addition to this the social halls dining saloon, smoking rooms and other public rooms on the boat have been enlarged to accommodate the increase in the number of passengers that can be carried.
The Polk is commanded by Captain K. A. Ahlin, Mr. Fred C. Charman, one of the most popular pursers on the Oriental run, will again take up his duties on the vessel.

The already large passenger list will be increased by forty when the Polk sails from Shanghai for Hongkong June 5. The ship is due in New York July 30 after stopping at the usual ports of call for globe circling liners.

Invitations have been sent out by the Dollar steamship offices of Shanghai to friends of the company and their ladies to attend a reception and tea-dance aboard the liner on Thursday afternoon. The Polk will lay at the Dollar wharves while in Shanghai.
