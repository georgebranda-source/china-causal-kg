What are the prospects of the situation becoming materially worse?
Next to the immediate financial question, involving as it did possible further taxation that question represents the most important side of the budget speech. How long does the Government from its special vantage point consider the trouble will last?

## [12] "DRASTIC ECONOMY" ESTIMATES.

Last year the most arresting feature of the Governor's budget speech in the Legislative Council was not so much the exceptionally heavy programme of public works outlined for 1925—over $9,000,000 was set aside for public works extraordinary alone—nor yet the many improvements promised for the various Government departments in the way of increased staffs and better facilities: the most arresting feature of the budget speech last year was the financial policy His Excellency advocated, not merely for 1925, but for the much longer period implied by the words "near future." Briefly this policy was to press forward development works with every speed, even if this entailed a deficit for the year, each year, the deficit to be met from our cash balances, care being taken, of course, not to deplete these beyond a point—His Excellenceary suggested $5,000,000—where any sudden call would dislocate our finances. ## [13] FOR THE FRONT.

**CANTON STRIKERS ASKED TO VOLUNTEER.**

**PICKETB AGAIN ACTIVM.**

Ten strikers from each union in Canton have been asked to volunteer for cooling service with the Red troops on the East River front, states a report from Canton.
When the military wanted transport men they approached the Strike Executive. The latter called a meeting and it was decided to call for volunteers. The pay per month, per man, is said to be $18.

Tau, the Chinese district near Deep Bay—beyond the New Territory. A batch of new men, numbering about 200, have replaced the old strikers. The Canton obsolete gunboat "Po Pik" is anchored in the vicinity and an armed launch is cruising near the place where the ferry leaves for Hongkong. Passengers are not allowed to depart and food export is prohibited but a small quantity gets through occasionally.

Three Canton warships, the "Kong Kung," "Kwong On," and the "Ping Nam" are reported to have gone to Chinshan to assist the strike pickets.

## [18] CORRESPONDENCE.

**YESTERDAY'S EDUCATION MEETING.**

(To the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir,—Mr. Benjamin Wylie seems to have forgotten that some of the meetings of the Board of Education have been ludicrous and that most people would agree with the Headmaster of the Diocesan Boys' School when two years ago he described their meetings thus with a fearlessness and a truthfulness of which we see too little in this Colony.

Incidentally Mr. Wylie indirectly pays the Headmaster a compliment in appearing to be afraid of him. Why should the Board be afraid of criticism? Mr. Wylie himself has criticised it in the past. Is it perfect now? I only wish to mention three ludicrous things in connection with the Board viz.:—

(1) Mr. B. Wylie, Dr. T. W. Pearce and Mr. N. T. Teesdale Mackintosh (late Registrar of the H.K. University), three Members of the Board, solemnly and definitely recommended, as a Committee appointed by the Board and in an official Report to the Government that a Secondary School mainly for children from Kowloon and Taikoo should be built in the Botanical Gardens.

(2) Mr. B. Wylie himself complained that a Sub-committee of the Board of Education had been “stamping” (the word is not mine) round the schools for quite a long time when they had no authority to do so. When the Sub-committee visited the Diocesan Boys' School the Headmaster allowed the members to go round the school but pointed out that they had no authority to inspect schools. As a result of his action the Board was eventually duly authorised by H.E. the Governor to inspect schools. In this matter the Board is indebted to the Headmaster. The Board made themselves appear to be ludicrous and were helped out of their position.
8) At the last meeting of the Board the Chairman asked for "Light more Light." The Committee of the School have apparently supplied the "light" and now Mr. Wylie complains that this is not a request "May we have a conference" but was a Conference on definite lines.
I suppose we can only envy the dignity of American letters.

## [20] MISCHA LEVITZKI.

**FINAL RECITAL HERE TO-NIGHT.**

**TRIBUTE OF A CRITIO.**

Hongkong has already had opportunity to take measure of the artistic stature of Mischi Levitzki and will have another and last opportunity to do so to night, when Mr. Levitzki will give his final recital at the Theatre Royal. It is interesting to quote the critic of the "Chicago Post" on the occasion of Levitzki's recent appearance in that city. He wrote as follows: Levitzki is one of those intended by nature to play the piano. Everything he does is conceived in terms of the piano and in his wide ranging he is never tempted to transgress its bounds. There were moments of exquisite beauty in the Chopin-lovely tone colours with the inner voices weaving their melodies together in happy adjustment and with the pianistic sense governing all. It was the forefell Chopin with the man's brain back of the post's eye. At to night's recital Mr. Levitzki will again present a group entirely devoted to the works of Chopin including the military Polanais. To hear Levitzki play Chopin is a treat no music lover can afford to miss.

In conclusion I beg to ask that the definite lines on which the Conference was suggested and that the correspondence on the matter should be made public.
