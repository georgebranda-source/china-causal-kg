*(Reuter's Service.)*

That the British in India is accused of being merely thieves and robbers was one of the bitter tauts against the Government whose administration practically all Indian speakers condemned when the Assembly resumed the debate on Sir Alexander Muddman's resolution and Mr. Motilal Nelur's amendment advocating that the total responsibility of the Government be invested in the legislature with transitional reservations in respect of this army and foreign affairs.

## [6] MAIZE SHIPS.

*Capetown, Sept. 9*

The strikers have agreed with Colonel Creswell, the Labour Minister, that in order to prevent congestion of the maize export and consequent unemployment of dock labourers, maize vessels shall be taken from the jetties into the roadstead, provided a guarantee is given that the ships will not put to sea without the crews' consent.

The shipowners decline to give a general undertaking.

The strikers by arrangement with the Government ballotted regarding the sailing of the fruit ship "Roman Star." The ballot resulted in 432 to 352 votes against sailing.

## [11] YESTERDAY'S BASEBALL.

The “friendly” baseball match between the Japanese and a team from a U.S.S. “Helena” at the Happy Valley diamond yesterday afternoon had a thrilling finish, the sailors accomplishing the seemingly impossible by making the necessary number of runs in the last inning to draw level.

The sides were evenly matched, excellent pitching and fielding holding the sides down to a blank score for the greater part of the game and runners getting no further than third base.

The Japanese opened the score in the fifth inning through Kusano and S. Hachiuma on a fly by. Uno. In the next inning Y. Hachiuma put his side further up by getting Yokoi home.

The sailors entered their last inning with a three runs' deficit. They scored this number amid deafening cheers from their supporters. A faulty throw at third base proved lucky for the "gobs" and assisted in the making of the first two runs.

The Japanese failed in their last inning and the match ended in a draw.

Flight Lieut. G. E. Livock, who kept wicket and opened the battling for Malaya in the last series of triangular interport cricket matches played here, has now appeared for the Gentlemen in first-class cricket at Home.

Before the interport matches were played here last year, it was announced in the "China Mail" that Mr. Livock had already appeared for Lord Cowdray's XI against England. He did not do himself full justice in Hongkong during the stay of the seaplane carrier, H.M.S. "Pegasus" but he made a century against Kowloon. When presented with a bat to commemorate the fent he expressed himself as preferring a golf club. He hardly played any cricket here except in the triangular interport and it was thought by a few that he might desert the game.

This season the “China Mall” reported that Mr. Livock had appeared with distinction for a Middlesex club and had played for the metropolitan county in first-class matches.
