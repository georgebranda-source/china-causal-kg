## [18] CHOLERA "SCARE."

**OFFICIAL ASSURANCE IN SHANGHAI.**

**BONGKONG S IMMUNITY.**

### —

Cholera epidemics can in most cases be traced to contaminated water and it is doubtless due to the purity of the local water supply and the facilities afforded for drawing that water to all sections of the community that Hongkong, as testified to by the health returns, has experienced only one case of cholera in the last two years or so (and that one imported).

Shanghai is not so fortunate, for the Commissioner of Public Health there, Dr. C. Noel Davis, interviewed by a local newspaper, spoke of the yearly outbreak in Shanghai when infected persons come into the settlement for treatment from all parts of Shanghai, including the French Concession, Chapei and Nantao. This interview concerned the "alleged menacing epidemic of cholera at present prevailing in Shanghai."

Since the epidemic started, states a Chinese doctor in Shanghai, there have been to date 72 deaths out of around 700 confirmed cases. It was his opinion that the epidemic could be more effectively countered if the health authorities, foreign, and Chinese, co-operated. He acknowledged appreciation of the action of the Municipal health authorities in throwing open part of the Range Road Isolation Hospital for overflow cholera cases and said that it would prove very helpful in dealing with the outbreak.
In the interview already mentioned, the Commissioner of Public Health for Shanghai said that cholera epidemics were not likely to be eradicated while sanitary arrangements in the Chinese parts of the city were so inadequate and while the continued warnings by the Municipal Council were ignored. The only way to fight cholera, continued the doctor, was to sterilize all fresh fruits and vegetables, filter all drinking water, and keep flies away from food. The present situation, he added, was not serious or unprecedented.

## [42] CANTON'S LOSSES.

The strike and boycott are slowly killing themselves. Hongkong has been put to great inconvenience, but Canton has suffered no less. The great silk trade of the city is at a standstill, together with most of the foreign trade. Many large consignments of silk warehoused in Canton for shipment to foreign markets have had to be sent back to the producing districts, and a city whose commerce is thus temporarily ruined has, moreover, to support a veritable army of unemployed, intimidated into leaving their positions in Hongkong, and terrorised into remaining away. The boycott and the nationalist agitation have points of peculiar similarity. A constructive policy could make China independent of those facilities whose withdrawal, as the result of her own action, has paralysed her commerce. A constructive national policy would with for the Chinese people all that a nation of such vast proportions could desire.
In the present at in previous agitations, the Chinese are simply kicking with blindury against the pricks. An educational boy, out with last no longer than a trade boycott. The students will are most involved in the movement have decided that they will never return in the foreign educational institutions whose principal adopted the only course consistent with this principle and

duty. Some of them undoubtedly will not, though the choice will not always be theirs. But no serious effort is being made to expand native facilities for education. The thousands who have been receiving their education at the Mission schools will have to go back to them, or consider their education completed, and set out on a fight for existence, already hard enough in this undeveloped and over-populated country, that can have but one result. If only a tithe of the effort devoted to destructive movements, from the civil wars of the Militarists to the rabid ravings of the babus, were given to creative and constructive effort, there might be some hope for the immediate future of this country, and a corresponding respect and generosity among foreign nationals. There is no better or surer way to win the goodwill of the West—or to command it—than by earning and deserving it, and that can only be done by a solid, determined patriotic national effort to introduce order and good government. The world can only respect those who respect themselves.
