## [2] Hongkong Education.

(To the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir,—I have wondered, since local education has become a topic for discussion, how European ladies, diploma-ed or not, can ever hope to appreciate Chinese characteristics.

Now and then, I read of the number of qualified school-mistresses coming out to the Education Department. Some of the experts tell us that even an Oxford honours man has not got it in him to be able to adapt himself to understanding our traits and thereby making himself the most appropriate teacher. If so, how can the average diploma-ed schoolmistress do any better. ## [4] FATAL LEAP.

**SUNDAY AFTERNOON'S TRAGEDY.**

Pathetic circumstances attended the death of one of Messrs. A. S. Watson & Co.'s mineral water agents on Sunday afternoon when Mr. Mak Kip (35) of Macao jumped from a verandah and met his death. Mr. Mak, in addition to being agents for Messrs. Watson's, was employed by the Macao Electric Light Co. He had been ill for several months and had lately stayed with Mr. Peter Wong at No. 38a, Bonham Road, Breezy Point. On Sunday afternoon, while, it is believed; his mind was temporarily deranged, deceased either jumped or threw himself from the verandah.

Deceased was a very popular young man and leaves a wife and two children to mourn his loss. He had only been with Mr. and Mrs. Peter Wong for four days, for the benefit of his health, when the untimely event occurred to disturb the peace of mind of those who had his welfare at heart.

## [6] FALSE NAMES.

**SAILORS WHO CREATED TROUBLE.**

### Tramcar Incidents.

Before Mr. S. B. B. McElderry, at the Central Magistracy this morning, three "American seamen were charged on two counts of evading payment of tramway fare and obstruction.

There was some delay in the accused being brought into Court after they had been removed this morning from the custody of the Police by an escort from the U.S.S. Sacramento as it was found that they had given false names and stated that they belonged to the U.S.S. Pampanga. Their proper names were Jeff Cantrill, Harry A. Thorne and Irvine Coulter, all of the U.S.S. Helena.
A traffic officer of the Tramway Company giving evidence stated that he first saw the three accused in a car near the Ming Yuen gardens. They refused to pay the fare when called upon and gave him and the conductors considerable trouble, as well as causing inconvenience to the passengers by changing cars at Causeway Bay and again at Tin Lok Lane. Witness stated that at Bowrington bridge the third accused threatened to strike him. Witness and a Chinese constable kept up with the men after they had alighted near the bridge, and the latter went ahead later and laid information at No. 2 Police Station which resulted in the accused being taken into custody when they reached that point.

The third accused denied that he threatened to assault witness. Asked by His Worship why they gave false names, all three said that they did it in order to hide their movements from the ship.
His Worship said that the evidence he had taken was sufficient to prove their guilt and fined each of the accused $10 and $25 on the two counts respectively.

## [7] MOTOR TOUTS.

**PRIVATE LICENCES ABUSED.**

**REAPING A HARVEST.**

According to the "Wah Tsz Yat " Po," "tramp" motor-cars which now/ply for hire on the streets will soon be forbidden.
The vernacular paper explains that there are a number of motor-cars, each bought by a few adventurous spirits who run about town for hire by trips. These cars are registered under "private" licences and, as they have practically no overhead charges can operate at great profit.

Many a local resident has been accosted by youthful touts in the streets. Yesterday, a “China Mail” man who was given a “lift” in one such car, was told by the driver's assistant that they made over $25 a day and on Sunday night had to stop running because their petrol had given out. They had just bought a Dodge car for $2,550 and were confident of reaping a harvest. One advantage which the assistant pointed out was that they were no hours of restriction for cars with private licences and that the fees were much lower.

It is with regret I learn that on account of ill health His Excellence Major General Sir William Rycroft, K.C.B., K.C.M.G., had to tender his resignation as Governor of the State of British North Borneo. Sir William first arrived in this country to take up the appointment of Governor on November 21, 1922.

## [11] THE NEW KISMET.

**BEAUTIFUL SCENIC EFFECTS.**

一

It was as a spectacle that "Kismet" made its appeal when it was produced at the Garrick Theatre in 1911. It is as a spectacle that it must succeed or fail, as it was revived at the New Oxford Theatre.

But the manner of the revival is entirely different from the manner of the original production. Instead of the old, flamboyant mounting is seen something subtle in lighting effects, something that bears close relationship with that which is connected with the arts of the Cubists and the Futurists.

The story itself is nothing more nor less than a prop for a series of stage pictures.

Yet, after all, Edward Kno-block has a good tale to tell, and it seemed to be followed with in-terest. The scene is laid in the Baghdad of “The Arabian Nights,” and many strange things happen in a very brief period. How Haji, the beggar, is made a prince and sinks again to the depths is told in lines which are at odd moments apparently intended to be pactical, but which do not lift the play above its spectacular appeal.

Mr. Sam Livesay is the new Haji—a part originally played by Mr. Oscar Asche. Mr. Livesay acts with a certain power, but it appears obvious that once upon a time he saw Oscar Asche in the part. All round, the acting is more than adequate—but the scenic effects matter more than the cast.

## [12] MYSTIFIED SCIENTISTS.

**A MAN WITH SIX SENSES.**

一

What is the strange occult power possessed by Louis Kahn, the "Man with the Sixth Sense"?

His case is inexplicable to the foremost French scientists, and a former French Premier. Academicians, High Court judges, and detectives have been mystified by a display of his uncanny power. He is able to read a letter written by another person without seeing or touching it.

Kahn, who is 53 and a German who disarows German nationality, has just demonstrated his powers before an audience including M. Louis Barthou, a French ex-Premier; M. Loucheur, a former Cabinet Minister; and M. Morain, the Prefect of Police.

There were twelve persons in all present and each wrote in an adjoining room a phrase on a slip of paper which was folded eight times and kept in the closed hand.

He left the room while the ships were exchanged, and on returning said: "Though you have been exchanging your slips each one finally came back to its author." This was correct.

Then he told those present what they had written.

With the exception of an error in one word, which did not alter the sense of the phrase, Kahn faithfully rendered the contents of the twelve slips.

Striking proof of his telepathic powers was shown when, at M. Barthou's suggestion, a woman in the company wrote on her slip of paper a declaration of affection for a well known politician.

Madame, please burn your slip, far away from me so that I cannot possibly see it," said Kahn, and when the woman had done so, he pointed to M. Barthou, remarking: "The gentleman there dictated the phrase to you, so he will be able to certify if I renter it correctly." And, amid laughter, he read word for word what the woman had written.

## [13] STOP PRESS.

The students here held a meeting yesterday afternoon in the Central Park to discuss the Shanghai affair.

## [27] THEATRE.

**LILLIAN GISH IN "THE WHITE SISTER."**

Miss Gish is magnificent! No actress of this generation on stage or screen has carried the flame of sheer genius into her acting as does the frail little star of "The White Sister" now showing at the Queen's Theatre.

In filming F. Marion Crawford's story, the Director took a company to Italy. Studios and laboratories were established, and then began the making of what should prove one of truly great productions in cinema history.

Most persons are familiar with the story and many have undoubtedly longed for its presentation on the screen. The story, offering many thrills in the way of the actual eruption of Vesuvius, and a flood that sweeps away an Italian town has been closely followed. The settings are exquisite, the photography of the highest quality and Miss Gisk's supporting cast shows it was chosen with care, for the members all contribute to the general effectiveness of the film.
