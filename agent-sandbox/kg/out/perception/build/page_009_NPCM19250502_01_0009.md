## [7] BACK TO GOLD.

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

The Secretary of Commerce, Mr. Hoover, has issued a statement to the effect that with the return of the pound to a gold basis the entire international trade will be calculated in terms of gold prices. He predicts that, with a general condition of stability prices will be reached which should be reduced to the benefit of commerce and industry all over the world.

He added that the German mark prices will now be absolutely on a gold base, the Dawes plan having linked up the German coinage to a degree with the pound sterling. It is very certain that the effect of the English move will be to reduce the volume of speculative hazard in international trade, because of the elimination of risks that must be taken with currency of fluctuating value. There will probably be a small swing of prices in Britain as the adjustment is made, but the gain in stability will bring benefits likely to outweigh any losses.

## [9] WORLD PEACE.

### The Value of Old Alliances..

**THE PATH OF AMITY.**

**HOW THE NATIONS MUST CO-OPERATE:**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

Mr. Winston Churchill, in his speech at the Primrose League meeting, referring to foreign policy, said that world peace depended on faithful co-operation between the two groups of the greatest nations of today.

In the new world the Washington Agreement had woven together British, the United States and Japan in a bond of friendship and anity which should preserve the peace and freedom of the seas for many generations.

In the old world we were endeavouring to establish a British French Italian and German security pack.

Just us the Washington Agreement was not reached by casting inside the old and valued alliance with Japan but by merging it in a larger grouping with the United States, so our policy in Europe would not develop by casting inside trusted allies by whom we stood in the dark days of the war.

## [10] HOURS OF WORK.

**INTERNATIONAL ACTION NEEDED**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, May*

The House of Commons by 223 to 128 votes rejected a motion by Mr. G. Buchanan (Labour) for the second reading of a private member's Bill based on the Washington eight hours convention providing that the hours of labour in industrial occupations, except of seamen and farm labourers, should not exceed 48 a week.

Mr. D. Kirkwood (Labour), in seconding the Bill, vigorously protested against the efforts of the engineering employers to increase the hours of labour.

Mr. R. G. Clarry (Conservative), in moving the rejection of the Bill declared it would injure industry and increase unemployment. The provisions of the Washington Convention could only be effectively introduced by simultaneous legislative action in all the countries whose representatives signed the Convention.

Sir Arthur Steel Maitland, Minister of Labour, expressed his willingness if necessary to consult the representatives of other countries in regard to securing the aim of the Convention.

## [11] PRINCE IN AFRICA

*(Reuter's Serolne.)*

After a dance at the Government House last night, attended by several hundred guests, at which the Prince of Wales danced un-tiringly, the Prince was out at 9:30 this morning. His appearance occasioned a most successful unrehearsed demonstration—bands of massed students including hundreds of attractive girls in university gowns outside the Government House, a body of comic cavalry armed with brooms and brushes followed by an ox-waggond draped in blue and white and drawn by twelve oxen. The immense procession of cheering and laughing students sang in chorus a specially written song, urging the Prince of Wales not to be "down in the mouth."

The Prince was very amused and seated himself on the waggon and drove the oxen which led the procession through the city, while he accompanied the whole-hearted crowd singing "he's a jolly good fellow" to the crowded city hall, where the Prince of Wales was ceremoniously installed as chancellor of the University of Capetown, which he has occupied since the inauguration of the university in 1918, and given the honorary degree of Laws.

The proceedings were marked with good-humoured ragging, including the appearance on the platform of a little white hen wearing the varsity football colours, also a parade round the hall, a counterfeit presentment of Mr. Ramsay MacDonald and the singing songs addressed to the Prince of Wales as "dear old Ted."—Reuter.
