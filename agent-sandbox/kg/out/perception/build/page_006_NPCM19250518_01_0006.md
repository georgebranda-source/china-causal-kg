## [19] THE MODERN GIRL.

**A CONFIDENT REPLY TO HER CRITICS.**

That the modern girl is not a distinct improvement on her mother was the timidly worded motion debated recently at the Forum Club. Yet the debate was interesting because the attack was opened by Lady Barrett, who certainly has a wide experience, and countered by the Hon. Emily Kinnaird, who perhaps has a wider and more understanding knowledge of the modern girls than any other woman in England because, verteran as she is, she will always be a girl of the day.

The modern girl, said Lady Barrett, has numberless advantages which her mother did not enjoy. She has an education that constantly moves to bring her into touch with real experience, her games bring her into competition with other girls, and with boys. She may have friendships with men free from emphasis of sex. She has a hundred different choices of work with adequate rewards and always the possibility of striking out some new line untouched before by man or woman. It was almost impossible to believe that she should not reach some higher level than the previous generation had reached, and, indeed, some of today's must arrive at places far beyond what any woman had yet reached.

But the very fact that the modern girl reaped the fruits that other women had toiled for placed her at a disadvantage, for it was effort that developed consciousness and strength. "Does not the girl of to-day," asked Lady Barrett, "luck vision and is she not therefore less keen, less dependable, less devoted to work as work, and is not her sense of values false?"

## [30] IT PAYS TO—

what the late Lord Leverhulme thought about advertising:—

There is no human business activity where "get together" advantages are so essential to efficiency as in advertising. I have with my firm been actively engaged in advertising for well over 40 years, and if I might begin at the very start of all when I was a grocer, then for well over 50 years, on an ever expending expenditure justified by an equally and correspondingly increasing volume of business each succeeding year, I see no reason to warrant one single thought that some even small curtailment of advertising expenditure could not be wise or desirable. The growth of business is very human, and just as the growing youth requires ever bigger clothes, and ever more food, so does the growing business, but with this difference and with this great advantage to the growing business, that whilst nature has fixed limitations to the growth of man there are as yet no visible limitations to arrest the growth of the well-managed business. Business and advertising can only stop expanding when efficiency fails and ambition languishes.
