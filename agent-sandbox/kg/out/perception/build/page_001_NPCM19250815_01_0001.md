## [6] SPECIAL MISSION

**HERMES'S 'PLANES LEAVE THIS MORNING.**

**MISSING SHIP SOUGHT**

The two seaplanes, which left H.M.S. Hermes this morning shortly before 8 a.m. were despatched on a special mission, this being to see if they could find traces of the missing ship, Yue Ying Wa, which has not been heard of since it left Hoihow for Hongkong just a week ago with a cargo of live stock aboard.

This is the ship to which it is thought belonged the lifeboats sighted off St. John's Island during typhoon weather although the ship which reported sighting them could not get near enough to see the ship's name on them.

One of the planes which left this morning proceeded west from Green Island and the other has remained more or less in the neighbourhood of Hongkong.

The missing vessel's owners are the Chong Yue S.S. Company of Hongkong who were not able until Thursday to associate the finding of the lifeboats with the ship but they received a letter on that day informing them that the ship had left Haiphong the previous Saturday.

The Harbour Office has been notified and all ships are being requested to keep a look out for wreckage.

The master of the vessel, which it is believed must have foundered with all hands, was Captain Alex. Pompey, well-known on the China Coast. The Chief Officer was Mr. McKintosh; the Chief Engineer was Mr. H. Larard; and Second Engineer, Mr. Mackenzie. The Chinese crew of the Yue Ying Wa numbered 37 and also on board was the comprudore staff, some seventeen or eighteen all told.

## [7] SEARCHER CHARGED.

**THEFT DURING RAID**

**STORY OF A WATCH**

It was alleged this morning against a searcher employed by the police that he had taken a watch and chain from the occupants of one of the houses searched in the course of a raid in the Western district two days ago.

Mr. D. McCallum, who represented the searcher when the case was heard at the Central Magistracy, stated that his client's case was that the watch and chain were given him by the wife of the complainant in order that he might not search a certain box.

Det. Insp. T. Murphy said that the defendant was to all intents and purposes a policeman having been duly sworn in for duties, in connection with the strike. Upon a complaint being received, the defendant was searched and the witch and chain found in his breast pocket. The value of the property was about $3.

## [9] BORDER SHOTS.

**FIRING INTO BRITISH TERRITORY.**

**VUIT OOMANDERRIID.**

Incidents on the border have already being reported in which Chinese Labour pickets have fired on anyone attempting to cross from Chinese into British territory but in affluor occurring yesterday morning is more serious in that the shooting was into British territory. About 11 a.m. yesterday some Chinese men and women were carrying baskets of fruit from their own villages in British territory to Sheang Shan railway station when five rounds were fired into them by a Labour picket. This was near the To Fung Customs station where the Chinese and British frontier road are only a few yards apart.
No-one was injured although shots struck the ground near to the prospective fruit vendors who promptly dropped the baskets and took to flight. The Labour picket then crossed the frontier and took away the baskets.

## [10] “SWAG” FOUND.

**ARMED ROBBERY CHARGE SEQUEL.**

**SUCCESSFUL RAID.**

Following the coup by Detective Sub-Inspector F. Shaftain, in charge of district watchmen attached to the Secretariat for Chinese Affairs, at Yu Chow Street, Hunghom, on Thursday night, three men were formally charged before Mr. E. W. Hamilton at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning.

As was reported in yesterday's "China Mail," it is alleged that arms and ammunition, and stolen property were found on the raided premises.

All three accused were charged this morning with taking part in an armed robbery on July 31 at No. 34, Bulkeley Street, when a pawnbroker's shop lost $5,000. Two of the men faced an additional charge of unlawful possession of stolen property and were remanded for a week. The other defendant was remanded till Monday.

## [14] Canton: August 12.

From what can be seen and heard the strike leaders in Canton are going to mark time in definitely, and they seem to have the backing with which to support their huge crowd for some time to come.

It is difficult to realise that there are a few hundred "parasites" who can only earn big money when trouble is created. It is they who can approach the so-called Labour politicians, and the Soviet agents invariably deal with them. In this way they are in a position to demand a daily contribution from revenue to support all the different guides now idle in Canton; but when it comes to asking for funds for "special" objects they make their own terms and, incidentally, their own profit. As their orders are obeyed by armed pickets, their word is law. Accordingly, no matter how anxious an ordinary striker may be to get back to Hongkong, he is prevented from doing so. And as long as the leaders retain the temporary dormitories and provide the two meals a day, they have no excuse for breaking away; rather, they are not sufficiently united to act, or even think, for themselves.

This band of labour "professionals" has been increased during the strike, branch executive offices having been established at all places where there is a likelihood of men departing, via roundabout routes, for work.

To-day the rank and file are maintained as when they first came up. But there is no pocket money or money for the folks at home or in Canton. Some of the first batches who came up, and thought that they would enjoy a short holiday had a good time in the flower-boats and the Honam fantan saloons. When funds ran short they disposed of what spare cash they had with them. Then went clothing, until now some of them are virtually in rags with footwear that defends description.

The Tung Tak coolies' guild of Hongkong has been "making hay while the sun shines." Being more adept at cargo handling and claiming priority as patriots, they have displaced regular Canton workers in competition for work with the result that the Canton men are dissatisfied.

## [16] TIENTSIN QUIET.

**POLICE AND UNRULY STUDENTS.**

**FIRM ACTION TAKEN**

*(Réuter's Service.)*

The Chinese police have decided to release all the workers arrested recently provided they obtain shop guarantees to return to work and do not have anything to do with further strikes and disturbances.

All the students have not yet been released, the police demanding that those who have gone out of town return to their homes. Those having homes here are being held until they obtain guarantee that they will not again interfere with the workers and will remain in their homes until the schools recommend. Also they must agree to resign from the Students' Union.

The situation is now very quiet.

**AEROPLANE SEXTANT INVENTED.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

## [20] COAL CRISIS.

**MINERS' FEDERATION DISSATISFIED.**

**COMMISSION CRITICISED.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

Officials of the Miners' Federation conferred with Mr. Baldwin for two hours at No. 10 Downing Street this afternoon with regard to the Coal Commission, after which an agreed official statement was issued stating that the reputation fully stated their view that the personnel of the Commission should include representatives of the miners and mine owners.

The Premier, after explaining that the terms of reference would be drawn to allow the fullest and widest enquiry, stated that the Government was satisfied that in order to secure an impartial and authoritative report, the proper policy was to appoint a small impartial Commission, assisted by technical assessors, with the intention that the Commission itself should arrange for interested parties to be represented at it, taking evidence, and have adequate opportunity to examine witnesses and give the Court all possible assistance in the elucidation of facts.

The deputation was dissatisfied with the reply but expressed its intention of participating in the enquiry.

## [21] FAELEN INDEED.

**PLIGHT OF EDMUND STINNES.**

**FROM RICHES TO RUIN.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

"How are the mighty fallen," makes a suitable headline for the plight of the heirs of the late industrial king of Germany, Hugo Stinnes, whose eldest son Edmund to-day is hard pressed to pay £5,000 wages to 2,000 employees in his motor car works owing to the refusal of the banks to grant further credit.

Edmund appealed to the Prussian Government for a loan of 8,000,000 Marks to avoid closing down the works, and is approaching another bank to advance the wages due to-day.
