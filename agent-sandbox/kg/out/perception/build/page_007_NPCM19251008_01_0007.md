## [2] SECRET TALK.

**FOREIGN MINISTERS' CONFERENCE.**

**LOCARNO CONJECTURES.**

*(Router's Service.)*

*Locarno, October 7.*

An evening communiqué says the Conference to-day heard the report of the jurists on the drafting work previously entrusted to them. New suggestions were made necessitating a fresh examination by the jurists.

The Conference therefore decided to have a supplementary report for a later meeting.

The delegates left most amicably.

Mr. Austen Chamberlain accompanied by Herr Stresemann, shook hands.

The outstanding topic of today is the fact that M. Briand this morning had an hour's conversation with Herr Luther outside Locarno.

The conversation was most secret but doubtless it concerned the Eastern frontier.

## [3] MOSTLY UNTRUE.

*Locarno, October 7.*

An Italian newspaper has published a version of the text of the Pact which is declared here to be a mixture of truths and untruths, the latter considerably prepondering.

It is not proposed to publish an authorised text of the fact until it is really concluded.

Such a stage is far from being reached.

## [4] A THORNY TASK.

Messages from Berlin and Locarno indicate that Dr. Luther and Herr Stresemann's task at the Pact Conference is rendered more difficult by the critical attitude of German newspapers and political parties.

The former particularly are protesting at the report of a possible German abandonment of objections to France's standing guarantee for the Eastern Pact in return for French concessions as regards the occupation of the Rhineland.

The Nationalists have even sent a prominent Reichstag supporter to Locarno, apparently to apply a, restraining influence on the German delegates' actions, if deemed desirable,

A Locarno message says that the German delegates are prepared to accept clauses dealing with general arbitration on all Franco-German questions of a. politico-juridical character.

## [5] EARLIER CABLES.

*Locarno, October 7.*

A substantial part of the western security pact has been completed and it is expected that the jurists will add the finishing touches to-day; but the glass is not set so fair in regard to other subjects to be considered and it must not be forgotten that all questions are inter-dependent.

*Paris, October 7.*

Newspaper despatches from Locarno foreshadow a tug-of-war with the German representatives, particularly on the question of the Eastern pact.

The "Echo de Paris" says that the optimism with which the conference opened has been replaced by greater reserve. It adds that it is beginning to be realised that the Germans will play an extremely keen game, and Poland and Czecho-Slovakia are apprehensive on that score and that France will not desert them.

"L'Ere Nouvelle" says that the shadow of Russia dominates Locarno. The question is whether Germany is locking west or east. "According as this question is answered affirmatively or negatively the Locarno pourparlers signify the commencement of the construction of Europe or the inauguration of a dubious situation, heavy with peril for the Allies."

“Le Journal’s” correspondent says that French circles in Locarno declare it to be inadmissible that the proposed pact will suppress or weaken existing treaties. Should Germany try to go back on the engagements into which she entered, in the course of the negotiations before the conference, the responsibility will rest entirely with her.

## [8] SNUB FOR REDS.

**AMERICAN LABOUR'S SENSE.**

**HOLDING ALOOF.**

(Keuter's American Service.

The position of the American Federation of Labour in regard to the Russian labour movement was vividly indicated in a reply by Mr. Green, President of the American Federation, to an address by Mr. Purcell, M.P., representative of the British Trade Union Congress, who criticised the Americans as being the slowest in accepting the new social and political ideas, and hoped the American Federation would send delegations to Russia to establish close and fraternal relations with the Russian workers.

Mr. Green, amid the greatest enthusiasm, announced that the American Federation would never affiliate with the present Russian labour movement, and added: "We are not willing to pay the price of casting in our lot with a class that would destroy us if it could."

## [9] FRANC'S DROP.

**MINISTER OF FINANCE PROTESTS.**

**SPECULATIVE OPERATIONS.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

In connection with the recent fall of the franc the Ministry of Finance denounces as purely speculative the recent operations on the Paris and foreign Bourses which have resulted in a rise of several points in various foreign currencies. He announces that the Government will use the credit arrangements with Morgans for the purpose of stabilising the franc.

## [10] COLOUR PROBLEM.

*(Reuter's Service.)*

London, October 7.
A crowded meeting of the Church Congress at Eastbourne discussed race problems.

Mr. Willingdon declared it was imperative that the problem be solved by peaceful methods and not by a clash of races which would be the most ghastly tragedy the world had ever known.

The only practical solution was to treat coloured people in a spirit of absolute equality with whites.

If we wished to continue our associations with, and influence over, countries which whites had developed, it must be by not arrogating to ourselves any race superiority.

## [11] FASCIST GRIP.

**BLOW TO RIVAL UNIONS.**

*(Router's Service.)*

## [13] LINERS SAIL.

**CAPE SEAMEN'S STRIKE BROKEN.**

**DEPORTATION QUESTION.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

Cape Town, October 7.
The "Kildonan Castle" left for England leaving behind the men who deserted the ship.

The “Edinburgh Castle” and “Kenilworth Castle” are leaving for England on the 12th and 16th inst. after which there will again be a regular mail service to England.

The Supreme Court has granted an order restraining the deportation of 16 of the crew of the "Kildonan Castle," pending an appeal.

The strikers' attorney states that the Minister of Justice has undertaken that nobody will be deported before he is afforded an opportunity of showing that if he remains in South Africa he would not be a burden to the State.

A magistrate has sentenced 130 strikers on the steamer “Ballarat” to a month's imprisonment, but has granted bail pending an appeal.

## [14] STRONG REBUFF FROM MANCHESTER.

**HOW TO KEEP ORDER.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, October 7.*

Attempts of the British Fascists to enrol as special constables have been abandoned at Liverpool, Wolverhampton and Manchester.

A letter from the Chief Constable of Manchester to the commander of the Manchester Facists was read at a meeting of the Manchester City Council declining the Fascists' offer to join the special constabulary.

The Chief Constable said he was confident that Manchester citizens would in the future as in the past be ready to help the police in maintaining order in cases of emergency.

A. Councillor suggested that a copy of the letter be sent to the Home Secretary to teach him how to maintain law and order.

The Scottish Miners' Conference at Glasgow passed a resolution against expressing the opinion that it is similar to organisations that will be used against miners in the coming industrial conflict.

## [15] FORCES UNITE.

**FRENCH AND SPANISH ADVANCE.**

**MOROCCO SUCCESSES.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Fez, Oct. 7.*

The French and Spanish cavalry joined up in the Jebel Bouaidir district, north east of Syah.

This marks the development of a wide encircling movement designed to cut off a shice of Riff territory eastward of the Adjir-Kiffane line. The French infantry continues to advance northward up the valley of Chaouia towards Ajdir, in spite of very rainy weather, which has now become fine.

## [16] RUBBER OUTPUT.

**NEED FOR RESTRICTION PAST?**

*(Router's Service.)*

*London, Oct. 7.*

The claim that the Stevenson rubber restriction scheme has now served its purpose and ought forth with to be abolished/was made by a deputation of the India rubber Manufacturers' Association, the society of motor Manufacturers and Traders, the Royal Automobile Club, the Commercial Motor Users' Association, and the British Motor Cycle and Cycle Manufacturers and Traders' Union at a two-hour's conference at the Colonial Office.
Mr. Ormsby Gore, who received the deputation, assured them, he would fully represent their views to Mr. Amery, who personally was attending to the rubber situation.

## [17] EMPIRE BONDS.

**BRINGING THE COLONIES TOGETHER.**

**TOURS BY OFFICIALS?**

*(Reutor's Service.)*

*London, October 7.*

Sir Frederick Lugard presided at a luncheon at the Nigeria Club to say farewell to Sir W. Graeme Thomson, the new Governor of Nigeria. Mr. L. G. Amery, in a speech, declared it was essential that the various groups of colonies should come closer together.

The same natural and economic forces which brought scattered colonies into the Dominions was going to bring scattered administrative colonies, if not into federation, anyhow into a much closer association with each other.

Sir W. Graeme Thomson expressed the opinion that the closeness of touch between the colonies and the Colonial Office should increase greatly.

He hoped it was even possible for Colonial Office officials to visit the Colonies and suggested the formation of a standing transport committee at the Colonial Office to co-ordinate experiments between different colonies and advise in regard to new experiments.

## [18] ENGINEER'S DEATH.

**MR. D. M. DALLAS KILLED AT ILOilo.**

**FATAL FALL INTO HOLD.**

Mr. D. M. Dallas, 3rd engineer of the s.s. "Bowes Castle" was killed in an accident at Iloilo, during the ship's stay there on its way there from New York.

On September 30 at 10.30 a.m. Mr. Dallas fell into the hold. His injuries were such that he died the next day and was buried at Iloilo.
