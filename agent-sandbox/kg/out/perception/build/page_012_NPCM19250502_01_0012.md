It is interesting to learn that even clean play are paying, and have paid, in London. "Saint Jean," the last weeks of which have been announced, has celebrated its anniversary. It has been seen by half a million people, and has made as much profit as, if not more than any other production in London including the salicious ones.

The idea of having a competition between dogs to decide on the best performer of tricks must be somewhat of a novelty for a Dog Show. It is understood that the committee of the Shanghai Dog Show to be held on the Bund on May 16, contend that a dog in amateur ownership who can do clever tricks must be particularly well trained and cared for and they want to encourage owners of that type. Post entries will be accepted, only from dogs on exhibition at the Show. An Alsatian who dances the Highland Fling and provides her music simultaneously is to be a challenger.

Two cases of small-pox (Chinese) were notified for the 24 hours ended Thursday last.

Electricity plays an important part in what is claimed to be the world's largest clock. This is the new clock of a manufacturing firm which takes the place of one that for many years called attention to their Jersey City factory. The new clock, wound by a quarter horse-power motor, has a dial diameter of 50ft. The minute hand is 38ft. 3in. long, and the hour hand measures 27ft. 6in., the hands weighing together 3,825lbs. More than 200 lamps placed at regular distances around the hands make it as easy to tell the time by night us by day.

The new statistics of the advance of the foreign missionary enterprise in the world since 1900 are very interesting and make for great hopefulness. They are to be found in the World Missionary Atlas, just about to be published, a stupendous compilation of data, of Protestant missions, says the "Western Christian Advocat." In 1860 the total annual income of all Protestant missionary societies was $4,000,000. In 1900, $20,000,000; in 1923, $70,000,000. Of this later figure, i. 1923 the United States contributed $45,000,000. In 1900 the number of whites men and women missionaries was 15,000. In 1923 it was 29,000. The converts have increased from 1,235,000 in 1900 to 3,640,000 in 1923. The British Isles undoubtedly raised the larger portion of the 45,000,000 left from the American total.

At a meeting of the C.M.S. General Committee in London, a letter was read from Dr. Henslett, Bishop of South Tokyo. In it he said: "It is now generally acknowledged that Christianity in Japan has taken its place as one of the permanent religions of the country. It must be considered in the future as a native force. Whilst on a visit to Kyoto last afternoon, the Empress expressed a desire to visit three religious institutions. She therefore paid three arranged visits to a Buddhist Temple, a Shinto Shrine, and a Christian School. At the school (the famous Doshishu) her Majesty gave audience to the faculty. Then the whole body of students assembled in the School Chapel, the Empress taking her place on the rostrum. The students sang the National Anthem and a hymn. Then the Principal ascended the rostrum and while her Majesty stood with bowed head, he offered a prayer.

HANKY PANKY

Sixty-eight municipalities in Alberta during 1924 were able to finance their operations during the whole of that year without a cent of borrowed money, according to the annual report of the Provincial Department of Municipal Affairs. Out of the total of 109 municipalities in the province, only eight have outstanding debenture indebtedness. Of the 101 districts which had to borrow money for current year's expenses, all but 29 repaid the borrowings for the year. The districts have met all the debenture payments falling due in 1924.

Prince Chichibu leaves Tokyo for Englagd on the 24th inst., by a warship, probably the Izumo. He is visiting Formosa and the Loochoos and is joining the Hakozazi Maru probably in Hongkong.—Reuter.

Mr. and Mrs. B. A. Proulx, photographed shortly after their marriage at St. Joseph's Church on Monday. A reception in their honour was held at the Cariton Hotel and they left later in the day by the s.s. "President McKinley" for Shanghai where the honeymoon is being spent.

In connection with the Empire's Baby and Mother Competition a handsome silver shield is to be presented as a trophy to the township from which the winning competitors, mother and baby are entered, so that it may be hung in the town hall or some local public building, there to be evidence of the success which has been gained by a baby and a mother citizen of the township. It is now up to Hongkong!

Very few obituary notices mentioned the fact that Lord Curzon was the son of a parson—the Rev. Alfred Nathaniel Holden Curzon, 4th Baron Scarsdale. It is interesting to recall how many notable men were sons of the parsonage. These include Nelson, Drake, Sir John Moore, Wolfe, Warren Hastings, Cecil Rhodes, Jenner, Sir Joshua Reynolds, Sir Christopher Wren, Fletcher, Cowper, Samuel Taylor, Coleridge, Keble, Oliver Goldsmith, Charles Kingsley, Alfred Tennyson, and many others.

Far Easterners who have returned from leave during the past few months have expressed their appreciation of the excellent services rendered by Messrs. Thos. Cook and Son's Far Eastern Department in London. To know that one can obtain expert advice and assistance, when home on leave, for any journey or trips contemplated is indeed a great comfort, and to have arrangements for the return voyage, placed in experienced and capable hands, not only saves needless anxiety but is a lot of valuable time which is always a great consideration when on, for-though. This is one of the special facilities offered to clients.

The funeral of Mr. Louis Ardaint took "place at Shanghai at the Lokauwei cemetery. The cortege left Hospital Ste. Murie, the chief mourner being the son of the deceased, accompanied by Mr. J. Meyler, the acting French Consul General. The pull bearers were Messrs. C. Lem, Sub-Manager of the Banque de l'Indo-Chine, H. Madier, Chairman of the French Chamber of Commerce, C. J. Scott, Chairman of the Foreign Exchange Bankers Association, A. du Pac de Murseulies, J. Thesmar and M. Speolman. The ceremony, which was simple but impressive, was attended by all the French Community, and a great number of friends of all nationalities. At the cemetery, a tribute was paid to the late Mr. Ardaint by M. J. Meyrier, M. H. Medier and Mr. A. du Pac de Marsoulies. A detachment of Annamito policemen formed a guard of honour escorting the coffin from the hospital to the cemetery.

The death of W. E. Henley's widow at the age of 71, went practically unfoticed in the London papers. Quebec is setting an example in the censorship of meals. Hotels and other refreshment houses in the line of tourist traffic are in danger of losing their licence if they are not prepared to enter to the satisfaction of an official inspector. Autumnal spring chicken and bath rooms used as cool cellars will disqualify, and an undermined supply of fly-swatters must be provided.

The Prime Minister's visit to the Free Churches' Assembly at Leeds has been something more than a great personal success. As the president, Mrs. George Cadbury, put it in taking leave of Mr. Baldwin: "We all feel we must think God that the reins of government are in the hands of a Christian gentleman like Mr. Baldwin." This was her tribute to an address of great substance devoted in the main to showing how the Christian Church can help the politician in his task of governing the State.

## [17] BASEBALL LEAGUE.
