## [1] DAVIS CUP.

**AMERICA STILL THE CHAMPIONS.**

**THE DOUBLES MATCH.**

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

Philadelphia, Sept. 11.
In the challenge round of the Davis Cup, R. N. Williams and Vincent Richards (U.S.A.) beat R. Lacoste and J. Borotra (France) 6-4, 6-4, 6-3, thus retaining the Davis Cup for America.

The winners were in splendid form and maintained a superiority although the Frenchmen occasionally made startling rallies.

The third set was marked by desperate French efforts to turn the tide. All the players came to the net in a series of flashing exchanges.

Borotra made a brave fight to the end but the challengers were out-generalled and out-stroked.

For most of the match Williams was shining particularly for the winners and had his opponents almost breaking their backs to reach his dazzling volleys and sharp-angled drives.

## [5] THIRTY-THREE IN SHANGHAI:

In our correspondence columns says the "N.-C. Daily News," Lars Porsena made the somewhat extraordinary contention that "Shanghai sees more racing in a year than any three cities in the world." He admitted that he was open to challenge and certainly he would not find it easy to prove such a statement. The Settlement's annual racing programme is:—Shanghai Race Club, 14 days; Paper Hunt Club, 1 day; International Recreation Club, 18 days; total, 33 days. Now, take London. Hurst Park, Sandown Park and Kempton Park perhaps strictly speaking are not London, but the courses are as easily reached from Waterloo as Kiangwan is from the Bund and if Kiangwan is counted as Shanghai, then they must be counted as London. Hurst Park, Sandown Park, Kempton Park and Alexandra Park between them, according to the calendar in "Ruff's Guide," have 30 flat-racing days and 25 steeplechasing days, making a total for one city very considerably in excess of Shanghai's. We are not in possession of any calendars giving the fixtures for Paris, Buenos Aires, or Melbourne, but probably it would not be very wide of the mark to say that any one of these cities has more racing in a year than Shanghai, and their joint calendars must be very much in excess. An Australian friend assures us that in some months there is racing every day in Sydney and in Bombay for a long period there is racing every Saturday, apart from meetings of three or more days.

## [7] HOCKEY PROSPECTS.

**DISCUSSED AT ANNUAL MEETING.**

Confidence in the prospects for another successful season was expressed at the annual meeting yesterday of the Hongkong Hockey Club. - This took place at Volunteer Headquarters, and was presided over by Lieut. Col. L. G. Bird, D.S.O., the attendance not being large owing to the coincidence of the Rugby match between the Volunteers and H.M.S. "Hermes."

After a balance in hand of $238.88 had been reported, last season's excellent record was read and the revival of meetings between the Club and Shameen was referred to. As regarded the future, the Club would have three representatives at a meeting on Monday of the Recreation Grounds Committee which would consider a request for a double plot to serve as sports ground for the Volunteers. During the coming season there would again be three teams and playing days would be the same. The resignation of Mr. Bower from the Club was received with regret and for his organisation which had made possible the playing by the A team of 18 matches the thanks of the Club were recorded.

Present officers willing to serve again were Hon. Secretary, P.W. F. Mills; Captain, E.J. R. Mitchell; and Vice-Captain, W. Woodward. Mr. J. Wedlake was elected Captain of the second XI. and Mr. L. H. Kearne of the "A" team. Mr. G. H. Piercy who had signified his inability to continue in the office of Hon. Treasurer was succeeded by Mr. L. H. Ralphs.

The thanks of the Club were recorded to the United Services Recreation Club for the use of grounds and entertainment of visitors.

## [9] HERMES' DEFEATED

**VOLUNTEERS WIN A FAST. OPEN. CAME.**

The meeting at rugby football yesterday of teams representing H.M.S. "Hermes" and the Hong-kong Volunteer Defence Corps provided an interesting game in spite of the superiority of the Volunteers. There was plenty of open play seen, the back division of the Volunteers participating in some particularly clever movements. They were the faster team altogether although in forward play there was not much between them.

The only criticism which might be levelled against the Volunteer team was in respect of their tendency to forward passing and knocking-on but considering that "rugger" games are few and far between in a climate which is by no means conducive to soft grounds, this was not surprising. The handling was exceptionally neat and this, taken in conjunction with their superiority in pace, makes it rather surprising that they did not run up a larger score.

As it was they crossed the "Hermes" lines six times, none of the tries being converted. This was due in the first place to scorers making sure of getting down immediately they crossed the line, and however far out, and also to one or two of the kicks being charged down. The Volunteer captain would probably have done better to have let Stewart take all the kicks. His first two missed by very little and were in difficult positions and he would probably have added the extra two points in respect of two, at any rate, of the others.

Players who played a large part in the victory of the Volunteers were Lammert, whose pace and elusiveness was used to good advantage. Skinner, who seldom missed a pass if it was anywhere near him and made the going once he got it, Bowker, who looked after him well from the centre and was always on the look out for an opening, and Stewart, whose tackling and touch finding were a feature of the game and who came up with the threes whenever he had the opportunity.

The “Hermes” had one or two players of considerable merit among them but did not appear to work well together. The forwards showed an inclination to use their hands rather than their feet in the loose when a good rush might have got them over. Their ranks, by the way, were reinforced by one or two Volunteers owing to one or two of the “Hermes” team being prevented from playing.

The final score was: Hongkong Volunteer Defence Corps, 6 tries (Skinner 2, Lammert 3, and Bowker 1); H.M.S. "Hermes" nil.

## [10] LAST GASP.

**HEROIC ATTEMPT ON CHANNEL**

**WAR-SCARRED MAN FAIL.**

London

In the long history of gallant, but futile, efforts to swim the English Channel, the attempt by Lieut.-Colonel Froyberg, V.C., takes a high place.

He made an almost super-human struggle for 16½ hours, and then, exhausted and collapsing, was forcibly taken from the water.

At one point he was within 20 yards of his goal, but the relentless title swept him beyond it and away from the shore.

When three miles from Dover he discarded his costume, disclosing to the amazed spectators in the tag the war wounds with which his arms, legs, and back were scarred. With the English coast only 440 yards away, Lieutenant-Colonel B. C. Freyberg the New Zealand Victoria Cross winner, who had attempted to swim the Channel collapsed exhausted.

Freyberg, who had trained secretly, and set out on his swim from Cape Gris Nez unheralded at 8.20 p.m. reached a point three miles from Dover at 11.90 a.m., and endeavoured to float until 2 p.m., when he hoped the turning of the tide would carry him in to the shore.

He found himself drifting northwards; however, and, fighting heroically, he remained between a mile and two miles from the shore for nearly three hours.
In a final effort he managed to get within a quarter of a mile of the coast, but then, completely worn out by the long swim, he collapsed, with his goal plainly in sight. He was taken aboard the Admiralty tug which had accompanied him from Cape Gris Nez after having been over 16 hours in the water.

## [11] KOWLOON CRICKET.

**ANNUAL MEETING OF**

**A PAID STEWARD?**

An increase in members and increased profits on the year's working were the annual meeting yesterday of the Kowloon Cricket Club. The question was discussed as to whether a paid steward should be appointed to take over the duties of secretary, steward and general custodian of club property in view of the expansion of the club and the organisation entailed. This matter was eventually left over until the next annual general meeting, an amendment that a paid servant be appointed being defeated.

One of the members in the course of the meeting asked whether it was known when the Club was moving to its new ground and caused considerable amusement when in reply to the chairman's statement that two years would probably witness the change he said that he was willing to take a bet that it wouldn't be effected by 1930.

A proposal to raise the subscription $1 a month was discussed and not altogether favourably received.

A proposal to grant an honorarium of $350 to the honorary secretary and treasurer was carried unanimously.

On the proposal of Mr. V. C. Labrum the following six members were unanimously elected life members of the club, Messrs. D. Harvey, J. Hyde, H. E. Stevens, L. J. Blackburn, S. E. Green and G. Kynoch.
