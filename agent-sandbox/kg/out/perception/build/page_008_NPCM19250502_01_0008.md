## [13] EMPIRE BULWARK.

If the attendance at the annual meeting of the Hongkong branch of the Navy League on Tuesday night was out of all proportion both to the membership-and to the objects of the organisation, it is at least gratifying to know that in times of actual need there is no lack of enthusiasm. Still, as the Hon. Mr. Holyoak, the President, pointed out, there is no reason why the membership should not be 400 instead of 129. More than in many other outpost of the Empire is the work of the Navy visible here throughout the whole year. And the presence of the Australian warship Brisbane is a reminder that the Navy is not purely local but Imperial in its activities. A common staff system in the Army binds the military forces of the various Dominions by a common doctrine, and only recently arrangements were made for the interchange of Australian and British officers in the ships of the Navy and of ships in the squadrons. Hence the presence of H.M.A.S. Brisbane. This will do much to prevent the idea that local forces are only to be used for duties of a local character. In short, it is propaganda of an Imperial kind—that the Navies of the Empire are but one for all practical purposes, no less in times of peace than in war. ## [14] Talifu Disuster.

<sub>section under [11] S. MOUTRIE & CO., LTD.</sub>

Read the account of any earthquake and at once the sense of such a disaster overwhelms. In the twinkling of an eye a seismic disturbance destroys. That is all it does. And it destroys life as well as property. The quake at Talifu is not to be compared with the nature of the terrible happenings at Japan of a year or so ago. But it should not blind us to the fact of its consequences and that an appeal has gone forth for help for those who have been afflicted. There is an excellent opportunity here for the charitably inclined to exercise that splendid virtue. If charity begins at home, then wealthy Chinese have an excellent opportunity to help their nationals in a nearby province of the Chinese Republic. They will not let the onus rest with the foreigner entirely, but will see to it that supplies of help are sent off without delay. Responsible foreigners speak of the ravages of the disturbance and the photographs which it is our sad privilege to produce confirm the tale they tell. We trust there will be a generous response to the appeal which calls to be answered.

## [15] The Naval Yard.

<sub>section under [11] S. MOUTRIE & CO., LTD.</sub>

Some people say that trade follows the flag. In considering Hongkong's prosperity we are apt to leave out of consideration the twin Services which are established here. Passing the Naval yard at the tiffin and tea period is an impressive thing. The imagination is kindled at seeing hundreds of men stream through the gates. They have been engaged on some little piece of repair or overhaul on one of the mighty structures that mean so much to ours and the world's safety. These men's livelihood, depend upon H.M. Royal Naval Dock Yard. It is possible to believe that no matter what position Hongkong held in the commercial world, there would still be such a Dockyard as the Colony possesses, and the same number of men hammering, painting, riveting, going through all the varied process of overhaul and reconditioning. Behind this ant-like array of Chinese is the foreign staff. Appointed here for a time, they function silently and well. The community is probably undisturbed at their presence and probably not quite appreciative of

## [16] TERRIBLE FIVE MINUTES.

**NIGHT OF WAILING**

Vivid details have now reached Hongkong of the recent terrible earthquake that destroyed the town of Talifu, in Western Yunnan.

Miss Mary Lumsden, an English lady traveller who happened to be in Talifu at the time of the earthquake, wrote from that city on March 22:—

On March 16, 1925, shortly after 9 p.m. a very severe earthquake took place and destroyed

the town of Talifu, a city of 25,000 inhabitants in the province of Yunnan.

Talifu has from time to time experienced slight earthquake shocks, but there has never been any destruction to life or property.

CHINESE CITY DESTROYED~Confirmatory reports have been received of the destruction by fire and earthquakes of the Chinese city of Talifu, a place of about 25,000 population. The native casualties are not known, but no foreigners lost their lives.
©THE INTERNATIONAL SYNDICATE.

A very distinct shock was felt on Sunday, March 15 at 2.30 p.m. and another in the same evening. On Monday, March 16 there were several shocks. Suddenly about 9.20 p.m. (the 9 p.m. gun had gone), without almost any warning, there was a heavy shock and the walls of practically every building in Talifu fell down, and several fires broke out in different parts of the town. The earthquake only lasted about five.
There have been shocks repeatedly every day since the earthquake up to March 30, but these shocks have gradually decreased in number and intensity, and now are almost imperceptible.
The houses in Talifu were for the most part built on strong wooden frames, the walls being of stone and mud. They were generally two storeys high, the roofs were tiled and very firmly built in order to withstand the

minutes, but it was so severe that the destruction of the town was almost complete.

The fires seemed to break out in extraordinarily strong (winds which rage here during the Spring months. The walls generally fell inwards and people were buried under them.

The number of killed was, at first, very much under-estimated. This is the contrary of what happened after the earthquake in Japan. We heard first that about 500 had been killed in Tali city, but the number grew daily and now the official figure is about 1,300 inside the city. The number from all the districts round, several hundred villages, and five or six "hslen" cities, can hardly be estimated yet, but they say that outside Talifu there are at least 5,000 killed.

## [21] *(no headline: column 6)*

The foreigners in Talifu all had fortunate escapes. Mr. and Mrs. Berg's (Chinese Salt Gabelle) children were in bed in an upstairs room. The parents found the children after the earthquake buried under stones. Fortunately the children's heads were under the pillow and they were protected by thick quilts, so that no harm was done, and they were only annoyed at having to get up.
The Talifu people go to bed early and in many cases whole families were killed in bed. Also great numbers of pack ponies and donkeys have been crushed to death or burnt.

The local official General Li was very prompt in his actions after the earthquake. He first gave orders that all dead should be carried outside the city, and he provided coffins for those who could not buy them for those of their families who had been killed.

He has also concerned himself with the price of rice, and is trying to get rice from the country districts, but is experiencing much difficulty in this matter.

After the earthquake most people camped near their houses. The weather was very warm and coverings of cotton were made up as she's from the sun, and to give a little privacy. But on

Saturday night, March 21, there was heavy rain.

The price of living has been going up rapidly during the last year, and paper has been taking the place of silver. So that this disaster will lead to a lot of suffering. Probably some 100,000 people will be left destitute in Talifu and the towns and districts round.

The work of the town is gradually beginning again. It was impossible to buy any vegetables for the first few days. Now the country people are coming in with their vegetables, and shop keepers are putting up temporary stands

in the main street and selling their wares. I have been much struck with the self-control of everyone. There are a good number of petty thieves about, but the Local Official has put up a notice that they will be summarily dealt with.

Another correspondent in Yunranfu (the capital of the province) says:—

“The Yunnan provincial officials have done absolutely nothing except to forbid the killing or sale of meat—as a recognition that the earthquake was a supernatural visitation.”

Talifu, or Taiho-hsien, is situated in the province of Yunnan, on the western side of Arh-hai Lake, 168 miles west-north-west of Yunnan-fu, and is the entrepôt of trade between the province of Szechwan and Burmah. It possesses salt and other mines and marble quarries. No recent figures are available, but an old estimate puts the population at 25,000.

Mr. N. Bradley writes from Yunnan-fu that there is great distress in the Talifu district as a result of the earthquake and money is urgently needed. Subscriptions would be gratefully acknowledged by Dr. Bradley or the Rev. R. F. Lankester who would see that it was properly applied in the stricken region. The need is specially acute with regard to food.
