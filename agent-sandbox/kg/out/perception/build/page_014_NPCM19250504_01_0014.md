During last year, Manna raced only four times and it must be admitted that he proved a little difficult. Steve Donoghue had been engaged to ride him in the National Breeders' Produce Stakes at Sandown Park, the race in which so many ultimate winners of classics first have achieved success, and in a field of 12 the colt was backed down to odds of 6 to 4 on, so highly was he rated. Denoghue met with an accident shortly before and Barney Cars-lake then was given the mount. Fine horseman though he is, Carslake found the mettlesome animal a rare handful, and local people who saw the race say that Manna was all over the course. He finished an erratic run by swerving very badly twice when but even so he was only beaten three-quarters and half a length by Garden of Allah and Bucellas, respectively.

In the circumstances, Mr. Morriss did the right thing "in allowing Carslake to take the colt out in subsequent races and to show what he really could do with him. Two weeks later, with horse and jockey better acquainted the romped away with the Richmond Stakes at Goodwood. Resting until October, Manna then went for the Imperial Produce Stakes at Kempton Park, gave Picaroon 6 lb. and ran him to a length. In the following week they met on level terms in the Middle Park Stakes and there was a length and a half and a neck between them, Solario following Picaroon home in front of Manna. ## [13] 2,000 GUINEAS.

**SHANGHAI'S MESSAGES TO MR. MORRIS.**

**"EASY VICTORY."**

**DONOCHEW TO RIDE MANNA IN THE DERBY.**

After Manna's victory in the Two Thousand Guineas (one mile) at Newmarket on April 29, his owner, Mr. H. E. Morriss of Shanghai called to Shanghai stating that his colt had a very easy win. (The official verdict was 2 lengths, 4 lengths). Many cables of congratulation were sent to Mr. Morriss including one from the Shanghai Race Club.

Shanghai sportsmen and Mr. Morris's innumerable friends were caused more joy by receiving the message than by almost any sporting event in a very long time, says the "N. C. Daily News."

It is well known that for years Mr. Morriss has been striving to win one of the classic events at Home and on more than one occasion he has paid the highest price at the yearling sales, but hitherto he has not secured any animal that would make the rose jacket, black and rose hooped cap, as well known on the English race courses as the rose jacket is in China.

Manna, by the famous Phalaris out of Waffles, was bred in Ireland by Mr. J. J. Maker and acquired by Mr. Morriss at the Doncaster yearling sales, through Fred Darling, of Beckhampton, his trainer and adviser, who had to bid up to 6,300 guineas.

**FORMER KWANGTUNG GOVERNOR SUED.**

**PROBATE ACTION:**

A probate action concerning the estate of Chan A Fong, alias Chan King-yue, in which his brother, Chan Chik-yue, former Civil Governor of Kwangtung, is cited as the defendant, was to have been heard in the Supreme Court this morning before Sir Henry Cowper Gollan, Chief Justice

Mr. F. C. Jenkin, who appeared with Mr. T. Addis both instructed by Mr. A. E. Hall for the defendant, said that Mr. Chan Chik-yue was in Macao where he had been ordered by his medical advisers. He was 65 years of age and the strain of political life had imposed a tax on his physical well-being which had resulted in nervous exhaustion. His medical advisers had advocated a complete rest and had favoured a trip abroad, but his legal advisers had prevailed upon him to go to Macao.
His Lordship, emphasising that he would rather not take the case plecemeal, said that very serious issues in which Mr. Chan Chik-yue was involved would be before the Court, and he was averse to forcing the case without giving him a chance to meet the allegations.
