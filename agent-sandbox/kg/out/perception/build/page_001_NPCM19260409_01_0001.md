## [8] ILLICIT OPIUM.

**SUIAN^{*^{*}} DESPATCH BOX FIND.**

**·PURSER IMPLICATED.**

The discovery of 40 taels of illicit opium in a despatched box belonging to the s.s. "Sui An" had a sequel before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning when the purser, J. F. Azevedo, was charged with unlawful having the drug on board the vessel.

A Chinese seaman who was alleged to have been actually carrying the box in a tram car was also charged in the same case.

Mr. Leo d'Almada appeared for the defence of the purser and the other man was not legally represented. Mr. R. A. Wadeson was present to watch proceedings on behalf of the owners of the vessel, the Hongkong, Canton and Macao. Steambont Company.

On an application by Mr. d'Almada for a date to be fixed for hearing, both the defendants were remanded until 11.30 a.m. on Tuesday. Azevedo was allowed bail of $1,000 and in the case of the other man the amount was $5,000.

## [11] HONGKONG,

**NOFFICIAL MISSION.**

**CHINESE**

**CANTON'S DELEGATES.**

Although details are not given, considerable significance is attached in the vernacular papers to the visit to Canton of three Hongkong Chinese merchants, whose mission is stated to be in connection with the settlement of the boycott.

These three merchants have been prominent in the movements by the Association of District Commercial Guilds of Hongkong. They were previously in Canton with the unofficial delegations. One report says that they were taken by Mr. Kan Kum-shek to interview an official of the Canton Government.

It should also be noted that when Chinese merchant delegates last visited Canton as unofficial peace mediators, their movements were made public. There has been no announcement this time, nor has there been any communication—even strictly unofficial—as to the purport of their trip or their status.

In. Canton the consensus of opinion, is supposed to be an favour of an early peace. A quarter identified with labour is quoted as having indicated that the matter had best be left to the Canton Government. From this it is presumed, says the same report, that the Canton Government is of the opinion that the best interests would be served by its active participation in the pour-parlers.

A Canton vernacular paper attributes to a labour representative the statement that the boy-cott committee will not seek a part in the proposed conference.

The names of Dr. C. C. Wu, Mr. T. V. Soong and Mr. Chan Kung-pok are freely mentioned as the delegates to act for Canton. A private communication from Canton, dated Sunday, attributes to the mercantile leaders a desire to get all three, or any one or two of

A Chinese firm of sugar merchants in Hongkong yesterday received instructions (from its Canton branch) that shipments via Shanghai be suspended, also that unless the Shanghai market was especially favourable, purchases should not be made there for ultimate disposal in Canton. ## [12] "Through": Truins: Again?

<sub>section under [11] HONGKONG,</sub>

As from to-morrow, there will be an improved train service between Hongkong and Canton—an improvement which will be much appreciated by Chinese travellers.
Hitherto, the trains of the Chinese section (of the Canton-Kowloon Railway) have come down as far as Samchun, whence passengers must get off to walk across the bridge and, it is alleged, sometimes have to "arrange" with the boycott pickets to pass through to Lowu, where the British section trains stop.

The new arrangement is that the Chinese train will run over the bridge (into British territory) to Lowu. It is understood that the Chinese management would run their train straight down to the Kowloon terminus were it not for their shortage of rolling stock.
Each day the Canton train is expected to arrive at Lowu about 12.15 p.m. An express (1st, 2nd and 3rd class) will leave Kowloon at 11.42 a.m. to meet this train for the double object of bringing on the Canton traffic, and sending on the passengers for Canton. (via the Chinese train). The Kowloon express should arrive at Lowu at 12.22 p.m., which is ample time as the Canton up-train does not leave Lowu till 1.15 p.m. Canton passengers should be in Kowloon by about 2.30 p.m. The up-traffic should arrive at Tai-shata before sunset.

Two cases of enteric fever were notified yesterday. One patient was a Chinese and the other a Japanese.

## [13] LAUNCH.

**NEW GOVERNMENT VESSEL NO. 4 POLICE.**

Yesterday afternoon Messrs. W. S. Bailey & Co. launched from their shipbuilding yard a new Government vessel known as No. 4 Police intended for special patrol duty in the vicinity of the Colony.
The vessel was suitably decorated and the interesting ceremony of christening was bravely performed by little Miss King who has not yet attained her fifth year of age.

On the given signal the boat started down the ways and in a few brief moments gracefully took the water, amid a fusillade of crackers and much cheering. Amongst those present at the happy gathering may be mentioned:—

Mr. E. D. C. Wolfe, Mr. P. Taylor, Mr. and Mrs. T. H. King and daughter, Mr. D. Burlingham, Mr. C. G. Perdue, Mr. and Mrs. L. H. V. Booth, Mr. J. Jack.

After the ceremony, the party retired to the office of the company where light refreshments were served and many expressions of good wishes and good feeling made for the general welfare of the boat and her constructions.

Mr. "Mendham said: — Ladies and Gentlemen, on behalf of the firm of W. S. Bailey & Co., it is my pleasant duty to thank you all for coming here this afternoon to help us out with the ceremony of launching the first of a new class of vessel that is to be occupied by our Government in the interests of the Colony.

In particular we specially wish to thank the little lady here who has so bravely performed, perhaps the most important part of that ceremony, by christening the ship with her own fair hands.

That the boat has been successfully floated is a stratifying accomplishment, because it represents the first step in her career, and as all first steps, which a well-known French idiom tells us are the hardest, we may take it, that all those to follow, will be easier overcome.

It is therefore only fitting that some mark of appreciation should accompany our thanks, and it is with much pleasure that we ask our little helper to accept a small token of regard for her, labours which will always remind her of the pleasant ordeal, of christening her first ship.

Another pleasant duty the occasion affords is an opportunity to thank the staff of W. S. Bailey & Co. who have been responsible for the design and construction of the vessel and who, without exception, have given their best to make her a success.

In addition to the efforts of our staff we have received considerable assistance from the Government Marine Surveyors office in the person of Mr. Hall and Mr. Taylor, both of whom have taken a keen interest in all the details.

I make no apology for the absence of our Chief, Mr. W. S. Bailey who, as you are aware is much better occupied with the more interesting position of being homeward bound on a visit to the Old Country.

With these few words, ladies and gentlemen, I will now ask you to drink to the success of the new vessel "No. 4 Police" and wish her a long life of hard work in the accomplishment of the purpose, for which she has been created.

Hon. Mr. E. D. C. Wolfe in replying on behalf of the guest said: ladies and gentlemen, Mr. Mendham and Mr. Goodwin, it is with much pleasure that I rise to respond to the welcome accorded to us by W. S. Bailey and Co. on the occasion of the launching of our new vessel "No. 4 Police."

## [14] NO BARS.

**APPROVED PLANS NOT FOLLOWED.**

**A BUILDING CASE**

An important legal point was argued by Mr. L. D. Turner at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning in connection with a prosecution under the Building Ordinance, which resulted in a fine being reduced by $100.
The case concerned. Chau Tatshing, a contractor, who had been fined a total of $500 by Mr. J. H. B. Nihill for divergence from plan in five cases in the erection of four houses on Kowloon Inland Lot 1691 on the Kowloon City Road.

Mr. P. C. Morgan, of the P.W.D., who prosecuted in the case when it was first heard, stated that in the course of his duties he inspected the erection of the houses and discovered that reinforcing bars had been omitted from the reinforced concrete floors. He pointed out that if he had not made the inspection the concrete would have been poured in and it would have been impossible to know what amount of steel reinforcement had been put in.
Mr. Turner (for the contractor) contended that the divergence in each case was the same and pointed out that under the Ordinance the maximum fine for each specific divergence was $200. The Magistrate agreed with counsel's arguments and, two plans being involved in the charges, he altered the fines to $200 on each, or a total of $400.

## [19] NO PRECIPITATE ACTION.

*(British Wireless Service.)*

*Rugby, April 8.*

There has been some development of the coal situation today. As a preliminary to the National Conference of miners' delegates, which is to be held tomorrow, the Executive of the Miners' Federation met the Industrial Committee of the Trade Union Congress in which all trades unions are represented. There was much frank discussion at this meeting.

Although the Committee of the Trades' Union Congress is merely advisory, it must necessarily have a considerable influence on the attitude of the miners. It was therefore, not without significance that the Committee of the Trades Union Congress counselled the miners' Executive against any precipitate or spectacular action.

It emphasised that the rejection of the Coal Commission's report, or a mere desire to pick and choose what should be accepted and what should be rejected, would only tend to prejudice the miners' case in public opinion. The miners' Executive, upon leaving the Trades Union Congress Committee, considered at their offices the policy to adopt.

It was afterward announced by Mr. A. J. Cook General Secretary, that the Executive had reached a decision, which would be communicated to the miners' National Conference to-morrow. The nature of this decision was not stated.

Well informed labour correspondents, however, understand that the Executive is proceeding with caution, and will give a lead to the miners' conference which will leave the door as wide open as possible.

The issues before the conference will be wages and re-organisation. The coal owners' proposals on these matters are stated to be indefinite and lacking in clearness, and there are substantial grounds for believing that the Executive will recommend to the miners' conference that a more definite and precise interpretation of these proposals shall first be sought.

## [21] OWNERS' PROPOSALS.

In a cable dated April 1 it was stated:

The official statement of the coal owners' proposals on, the Royal Commission's report appendends replies to the Commission's recommendations ad seriatim.
The replies to a large extent express agreement with or acquiescence in the recommendations, including a willingness to engage in national as against district discussions, which they strongly advocated.

The owners are of opinion that the recommendation for a State ownership of minerals is a Parliamentary question.

They recognise that the miners will not entertain a proposal for longer hours and they therefore wish to discuss the recommendation that the definition of working time be weekly instead of daily and a greater flexibility in working hours.

The owners consider that the most urgent matter is a settlement of the minimum percentage on the basis of rates and the amount of the subsistence wage in each district.
