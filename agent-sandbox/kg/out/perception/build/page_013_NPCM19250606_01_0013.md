## [21] NEW RULES.

**BASKETBALL ANNUAL MEETING.**

**FOUR CHANGES.**

Four important changes in the playing regulations were made in the annual meeting of the joint board basketball rules committee, held last month at the University of Pennsylvania Club, New York. The body was made up of eighteen delegates, fourteen of which were present, representing the Young Men's Christian Association, Amateur Athletic Union and the National Collegiate Athletic Association.

The four changes effected by the convening body were as follows:—

1. The elimination of rule requiring players attending a jump ball to keep one hand behind the back.

2. Abolition of goal zones, two free throws being awarded to a player-fouled only when in the act of throwing for a goal from the floor.

3. Edges of the backboard to be considered in bounds.

4. Overtime periods for high schools and younger players reduced from five to three minutes each.

## [26] POINTED DEMANOS

LONDON, June 5
The Allied disarmament note to Germany makes a detailed analysis of the situation in Germany via a vis every military clause of the peace treaty and specifies the thirteen points on which infractions have occurred.

The note demands inter alia (1) reduction of the police from 180,000 to 150,000, while the military character thereof must disappear; (2) the destruction and transformation of certain types of factories; (3) the reorganisation of the German High Command and General Staff which "is similar to before the war," and reduction of the administrative personnel; (4) rectification of the military organisation of the railway system; and (5) suppression of short term enrollment in the Relchswehr and the military activities of various assistions.

The note also formulates miscellaneous demands relating to the when to the in war material and war requisitions and the nature of coastal land fortifications.

## [27] CHEUNG CHAU NOTES.

### Cheung Chau June 6.

To-day the finishing touches to the Wireless Telephone installation was completed and communication we understand is established with the Water Police outfit in Hongkong, so we are longer at the mercy of the storms which formerly damaged or broke the cable, or of raiders who might sever the shore lines.

A large tent has been erected next to the Electric Light, Power house; it will be used for Evangelistic meetings, for the benefit of the Chinese who may care to attend. The Rev. P. H. Anderson (who arrived with his family from Tung Shan, Canton) is in charge of the services, which are expected to be conducted for the next two weeks.

Several ladies and some children, who have been ordered out of Tung, Shan, by the American Consul, or account of anticipated fighting in this district have come to thine haven of refuge.

In spite of the heavy rains with which we were favoured on the King's Birthday, quite a fatilla of launches (5) fully dressed, spansed some time in the outer bay, although the afternoon cleared up nicely, the visitors, on these boats departed fairly early. The rain storm had considerably cooled things down and also given the plantations and gardens a good soaking.
We have it on good authority the repairs to the pier will not be undertaken until after the Typhoon season. It remains to be seen the structure in its present damage state will survive if a severe storm should come along, we hope it will not thus be exed.
