## [7] ANTI-RED SUCCESS.

**RIGHT WING TAKES PRISONERS.**

**ADVANCE TOWARDS RAILWAY.**

Further successes have been gained by Chan Kwing-ming's party, according to reports which have arrived during the week-end.

The anti-Red right, wing has taken Ho Yuen, in the East River region and advanced to Lung Mun. Commander Lin Yin-hung, who has been described as the Cantonese "Black Douglas" for his border exploits, has taken several hundred prisoners.

Lung Mun is considered the key to an important, fertile district close to the Canton-Kowloon Railway. The latest news is that the advance is being pushed forward to Tsang Shing, the district next to Tung Kun.

By reason of the geographical position, the right wing is in closest proximity to the Red lines and the nearest, as the crow flies, to Canton. As the main strength of the Reds is at Sheklung and Shektan it means it at real fighting is expected shortly.

## [9] A GRIEVANCE

**SANITARY ARRANGEMENTS CRITICISED.**

**SOME TRONG : VIEWS.**

"J.C." writes concerning the loading of nightsoil from the Praya at Wanchai. "Reclamation work," he says, "is going on, starting from the East and working up towards Arsenal Street. It has been apparently the custom from time immemorial, comparatively speaking, to use a certain pier of the Praya for the unpleasant operation of loading up nightsoil. As the reclamation has progressed westward, so the pier has become engulfed, and as a consequence the nightsoil lighter has come to the edge of the Praya to load. That has resulted in the work being done, and the lighter lying all night at a distance of a very few yards, twenty to windward of dwelling houses."

After referring to the distressing consequences for those in the neighbourhood, "J.C." considers the facts with a view to ameliorating conditions. "Here," he writes, "is a nightsoil junk loading up every night within a few yards of dwelling houses. Here also, is the reclamation already extending a matter of about 100 yards from the Praya. Does it not show a perfectly damnable lack of common sense, not to say common decency, on the part of our most Insanitary Board not to order the nightsoil junk to the edge of the reclamation to do their loading, so at least to go as far away as possible from human dwellings?

Let your readers just try to imagine what it must be like in the Hongkong summer night, with its sweltering and breathless damp heat, the mosquitoes, sleeplessness, etc., to have the added horror of this appalling stench cloaking one round like a pall. ## [12] MILITARY FUNERAI

**YESTERDAY'S HAPPY VALLEY CEREMONY.**

The funeral took place last evening at Happy Valley of Staff Sergt.-Major L. H. J. Gages whose death occurred at the Military Hospital in the morning.

Deceased leaves a widow t mourn his loss.

The body was conveyed to the cemetery on a gun carriage; the cortege being accompanied by the Band of the East Surrey Regiment, which played the Dead March in Saul as the procession approached Happy Valley.

The Rev. R. J. Northcott officiated at the graveside, where were gathered among others Major F. E. Milner Jones, Capt. H. N. B. Walker and Captain W. J. Thompson, officer of the Royal Army Service Corps in Hongkong. Every Corps in the Garrison was represented at the interment.

A firing party from the East Surrey Regiment fired the usual three volleys and a bugler from the same regiment sounded the Last Post.

Many wreaths were laid on the grave. The widow, on account of ill health, was unable to attend the funeral.

## [15] CHINA'S TROUBLES.

**HONGKONG UNIVERSITY'S GOODWILL.**

**VICE-CHANCELLOR'S MESSAGE.**

A “double-ten” (tenth day of the tenth month) concert at the conclusion of which they were briefly addressed by the Vice-Chancellor, and a dance which the Vice-Chancellor allowed to continue until 2 a.m., was the manner of celebration by the undergraduates of the Hongkong University of the fourteenth anniversary of the institution of the Republic of China.

At the conclusion of the concert, Mr. W. W. Hornell, C.I.E., Vice-Chancellor, speaking on behalf of the many present, said that they were glad to see that in spite of the troubles of the last few months the undergraduates were celebrating the national festival as usual. Speaking twelve months ago he had wished for the Republic of China a happy issue out of all her afflictions and he would repeat that there was no institution in the world which longed more for a peaceful and contented China than the Hongkong University.

The University of Hongkong continued the Vice-Chancellor, had no solution to offer for the present difficulties of China but he did believe that by their life at the University in friendly relations with the members as a whole students were able to leave better equipped to face the grave complications of the world of to-day. That evening's celebration was an example of the friendliness and good will which existed between all members of the University, and in the name of all the guests present, he desired to thank the undergraduates for inviting them and all those who had been responsible for the ar-ranging of, such an excellent entertainment.

## [22] FRENCH STRIKE.

**ANOTHER COMMUNIST FIASCO..**

**POLICE ON THE ALERT.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Paris, October 11.*

“Humanité,” the Communist organ, published an order for a general strike all over France on October 11 to protest against the wars in Morocco and Syria and the new taxes.

The opinion is generally expressed that the strike will fail like the transport strike in which the Parisian taxi drivers alone decided to participate.

The Government has taken all measures to suppress attempts to prevent freedom of work.

*Paris, October 10.*

The Communists' long-expected 24-hour strike principally to protest against the war in Morocco is due on October 12, according to an order issued by the headquarters late last night.

A second order calls on Communist members of the Paris transport services to strike today and remain out till their wage demands are granted.

The transport strike called by the Communists proved a fiasco. The number of strikers was in-significant and the public was in-nowise inconvenienced.

In the afternoon the police searched the offices of the Communist organ "Humanité" and seized several thousand letters inciting the workers to participate in the projected general strike.

## [23] LOST CHANCE.

**INDIA'S COTTON MILL. FNDUSTRY.**

*(Leuter's Service.)*

*Simla, Oct.11.*

In connection with the controversy regarding the remedy for the repression in the cotton mill industry, the Government of India informs the Mill Owners' Association of Bombay that it does not desire to press an enquiry by the Tariff Board since the industry does not ask the Government's protection. It points out that the Board's report would have been of great value not only to the Government and the public but also to the industry itself and would have helped in sifting hitherto unsubstantiated charges regarding Japanese dumping.
