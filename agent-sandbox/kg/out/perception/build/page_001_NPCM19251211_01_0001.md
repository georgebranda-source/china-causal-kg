## [10] WEMBLEY RESULTS.

**SMALLER SALES BUT IMPERIAL BENEFIT.**

**HON MR. HALLIFAX BACK.**

Until the expenses of restoring the site to its previous condition are known, also the complete result of sales, it is impossible to estimate the whole of the cost of Hongkong's participation in the British Empire Exhibition but the indications are that it will be in the neighbourhood of the estimates on which was based the Government grant.

The proceeds of sales in the Hongkong section were less this year than last, said the Hon. Mr. E. R. Hallifax to a "China Mail" representative this morning but he thought on the whole that the system of Government supervision had proved a success in other ways in comparison with that of private enterprise.

The health of the eighty-four Chinese exhibitors had been exceptionally good owing to the excellent supervision of Dr. Marriott in fact there had been one case of serious illness only. The delegates comprising the Chinese exhibitors had expressed their appreciation of the arrangements made for them on the way there and back; the boats used in each instance were those of the Blue Funnel line.

With regard to the disposal of Hongkong's buildings at Wembley, Mr. Halifax said that it would probably be an expense rather than a source of income. The site had to be restored to its previous condition and it was easily to be understood that with the "enormous area of other similar buildings to be disposed of and their lack of suitability in design for other purposes that this was going to be no easy matter. The Disposals Board were supposed to have something in view and were in negotiations direct with the Government but it is was difficult to see what joint schemes could be evolved.

As regarded the Empire Exhibition generally, Mr. Halifax said that although there had been immediate financial loss for the Association and for the Dominions and Colonies participating, the tone among the overseas representatives was one of optimism as to the ultimate outcome. It seemed to be agreed that value would in due time be shewn for the money expended, large as the sum concerned was — so large indeed that a repetition of the Exhibition on the same scale is hardly to be looked for. It was, however, opportune, and succeeded in the object of lying foundations for sound Imperial building, in the future. Imperial importance was stressed more than it was in 1924—and the Dominions and the Colonies were in a better position after the experience of 1924 to carry out the ideas which had led to their participation originally.

## [13] "MA CEDONIA'S" TRIP.

### Monsoon and Coastal Shipping.

On arrival here from Singapore this morning, the P. & O. steaner "Macedonia" reported:

Fresh. North-easterly winds to Pulo Manki. Thence strong N.E. monsoon to Lat. 13½ N., with frequent rain, squalls, rough head sea and swell. From Lat. 13½ N. to Hongkong, fresh monsoon.

From this it would appear that the worst part of the trip up was at the beginning, less heavy weather being encountered. nearer Hongkong.

That the “Macedonia” whose nominal horse-power is 3,000 and is of 5,650 registered tons was affected, will give an indication of what the monsoon was like. The “Macedonia” left Singapore on December 6 and was only five days coming up.

Vessels travelling south are being favoured with the wind behind them but those coming North are being held up. On one set of official charts alternative routes are given from Singapore to Hongkong. When the winter monsoon is blowing small steamers are advised to hug the Philippine coast. Ships have been known to take as many as ten or twelve days to reach "Hongkong from Singapore, in some cases having to steer due North to make a N.W. courses.

## [18] MOSUL REPORT.

**TURKISH DELEGATION'S REPLY:**

**FRESH INQUIRY SOUGHT**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

The Turkish Delegation in a statement in reply to General Baidoner's report, point out that Nestoriana and Christians have always been hostile to Turkey, and when pursued, after committing a crime, escape south of the Brussels line, abandoning their belongings.

The delegation also draws attention to the large numbers of Moslem refugees from the contested territory, whose villages, it is contended, were destroyed by aerial bombardments, and who are now living north of the Brussels line. The delegation suggests a fresh enquiry with the assistance of assessors appointed by both parties.

As General Laidoner obtained information from only one source, Tewfik Rushdi Bey, head of the Turkish Delegation, is going to Angora in a day or two, leaving Munir Bey, the Turkish Minister at Berne, to attend the Council when questions in regard to the Greece and Turkish exchange of populations will be discussed.

## [19] EARLY CABLE.

*(Reuter's Service.)*

The report of General Laidoner, the Commissioner appointed by the Council of the League of Nations to investigate the Mosul deportations, presented to the League Council, says that statements of refugees show that Turkish soldiers, commanded by officers, occupied villages and obtained arms, pillaged houses and subjected the inhabitants to atrocious violence, even massacre. General Laidoner's personal observations confirmed the pitiable condition of the refugees, who lost everything. The report emphasises the importance of the deportation of Christians in the region of the Brussel line and adds that the deportations are proceeding.

After reading the report, General Laidoner announced the receipt of a telegram from Mosul stating that all was quiet on the frontier.

The presentation of the report coincided with the receipt of a letter to the Council from the Turkish delegation announcing withdrawal in consequence of the Council's decision on the Mosul dispute.

## [21] CRUMBSOF HOPE.

**CHANCELLOR OF EXCHEQUER'S COMFORT.**

**SUBSIDY TO MINING**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

In the House of Commons, the Chancellor of the Exchequer, Mr. Winston Churchill, gave a few crumbs of comfort.

Speaking on the supplementary estimates of £9,000,000 for the coal subsidy which was agreed to without a division, he recalled that the Government decision to postpone the crisis in July was due to the possibility of a trade revival, which was thought then to be visible.

He declared that while the coal output was steadily increasing, it was hopeless to expect that the gap between export prices and cost and production would disappear.

He emphasised that the mining interest cannot be allowed to become indefinitely a pensioner upon other industries, and hoped all parties of the industry would make an effort to regain the old independent position.

He mentioned that last week's output of 5,000,000 tons was the highest since May and anticipated that the total subvention for the last four months was approximately £21,000,000.

The subvention had not led to an appreciable re-opening of the pits.

## [25] EXPERT

**VICKERS LIMITED'S BOLD POLICY.**

ENAMPLM TO THE COUNTRY
(Routor's Service)

*London, Dec. 10.*

In reaching the decision cabled yesterday, Vickers directors had the advice of Mr. Frank Dudley Docker, Mr. Reginald McKenna, and Sir William Plender, whose recommendations include besides a reconstituted Directorate, the establishment of three management boards, one of which will control the Industrial Section of the business and its subsidiary undertakings; another to control the armaments and ship building side, and a third to be the Finance Board.

These advisers consider the causes of the present situation of Vickers are general trade depression; the depreciation in foreign exchanges; the reduced earning capacity of the company's works which is attributable to the world-reduction in armament expenditure and the home production of armaments by foreign countries; the decline of ship building; foreign competition in the world markets; the financial stringency; and political unrest in Europe which has restricted the giving of credit and retarded schemes of development.

The advisers add that in carrying out after the war the sound policy of changing from swords to ploughshares, the management lacked the special experience required to direct large and varied industrial undertakings.

The advisers at the request of the board have agreed to act in a consultative capacity until the three management boards are working.

The papers congratulate Vickers on the bold reconstruction step which must sooner or later be imitated by other industrial companies if they wish to recover even in a modest degree their past prosperity.

They point out that the present market value of Vickers Ordinary Shares corresponds in the closest degree to "the value which, it is now suggested, should be placed thereon. Therefore little effect will be produced on the market.
