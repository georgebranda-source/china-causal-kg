## [1] TO TREAD WARILY.

**BRITAIN AND CHINA.**

**WELL-INFORMED CORRESPONDENT'S OPINION.**

That failing action on the part of the Chinese themselves to re-store conditions under which trade will be possible, business men in China have little to hope in the matter of an early settlement from the policy of the British Government, is to be read between the lines of an article by the always well informed political correspondent of the “Manchester Guardian Weekly,” in the August 26 issue. It would be optimistic to predict that much less than a twelve month will have to elapse before a final settlement could be reached on the lines of the elaborate machinery he outlines as that which in the considered opinion of the Government is necessary for the problem to be dealt with satisfactorily.

He writes as follows:—

The Canton Government's embargo on shipping and the rest of the troubles in China, in the absence of any central Government whose writ runs through China, is a very serious problem for the Foreign Secretary and the Foreign Office.

The Canton Government is the Government of the National People's party, a more or less "Red" party, but especially the heir of the late Sun Yat-sen's policy. The British Government is engaged at present in examining the treaties which regulate our relations with China and with Chinese ports. It is their opinion at present that the action of the Canton Government is a breach of these treaties, which provide for the completest freedom for British traders, a freedom which is obviously violated by the regulations now issued by the Canton Government.

The whole of the troubles in China are part of one problem, which is, in effect, the break-up of the central control of the Chinese Empire as directed from Peking. The British Government has not been inactive, but in view of the internal situation in China and the international situation outside China it must tread very warily. ## [2] THE IMMEDIATE PROBLEMS.

First, with regard to Shanghai we are about to hold a public judicial inquiry into the riots. This is not an attempt to whitewash anybody, but is designed to bring all the facts out and ensure that justice shall be done. We have tried to get the Chinese Government to appoint representatives, but, much to our regret, the Chinese Government has refused.

Secondly, negotiations have been begun between the diplomatic bodies at Peking and the Chinese Government on subjects connected with Shanghai though not in connection with the actual riots. One of the subjects so discussed is the representation of the Chinese on the Municipal Council of Shanghai. At present the rate-payers elect the Council, which in practice means the Europeans. The Council consists of Europeans, Americans, and Japanese, and its present chairman is an American.

Thirdly, the British Government seeks to arrange a collective answer by all the Powers to the various Chinese Notes that have been presented, and particularly to the Note of July 24, in which the Chinese complained of the unfairness of the various treaties. Our wish is that this Note should be a declaration of policy by all the Powers. The first thing is to carry out the conclusions of the Washington Conference, and the first step is the Tariff Conference, which is now very soon to be held. At the same time there follows the setting up of a commission to inquire into the whole subject of extraterritoriality.

The collective answer of the Powers to the Chinese Notes is still under consideration. It will be a slow business, because a complete consultation involves sixteen Powers with a right to be consulted. The British Government lays the great stress upon this collective action, and has the greatest interest in any action being collective, because there is an attempt in China, largely due, as the Foreign Office believes, to Russian Influence, to isolate Great Britain and represent her as the villain of the piece.

METHODIST ACTIVITY IN YUNNAN

## [3] NEW BUILDING OPENED:

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

*Yunnanfu, Sept. 8.*

On Friday, September 4; in glorious weather, the new Methodist Church building was opened in the presence of a large gathering.

The new building, which accommodates about 300 people, is about 100 feet by 40 feet, and is constructed on principles new to this city.

There are no pillars, the structure of the roof being of steel on the cantilever principle, the material for which was shipped out from England. These slender rods stretching across and upwards look very spidery, but the strength is there, and more than sufficient.

Representatives of all the Churches were seated on the rostrum, and each spoke a few words of encouragement and hope for the future of the church in Yunnanfu.

The Rev. A. Evans, who super-intended the entire work from beginning to end, is in charge of the work of the Methodist Church in Yunnanfu, and great credit is due to him for the splendid building resulting from his efforts.
The building was well-filled at this opening ceremony, and all the foreign missionaries were present.

At each side of the Church are several class-rooms shut off by folding doors from the main building, but which may be used for accommodating another 200 people if necessary. The acoustics of the building leave nothing to be desired.

After the opening ceremony, the visiting members—both Chinese and foreign—sat down to a Chinese feast in an adjoining building, and all thoroughly enjoyed it.

A collection towards the expenses of the new church was taken up during the proceedings, and $127.00 was realised.

Whatever else the church stands for, it certainly stands for Faith, as it is hoped to be filled with church members in the future.

On the Sunday following the opening, the building was taxed to its utmost capacity by the numbers present, and as there was a good deal of coming and going, it is probable that almost 1,000 people were present at some time or other on Sunday only.

At such a time as this, it seems a bold thing to build a new church in any part of China, but only the future can show the justification for it.

## [4] COAL THEFT.

**LAUNCH MEN IN TROUBLE.**

**IMPORTANT KOWLOON CASE**

Fraudulent conversion of five tons of coal entrusted to their care, with the alternative charge of larceny of the coal, was preferred against three men from the Blue Funnel launch "Poseidon" at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday. Two other members of Messrs. A. S. Watson's launch "Tai Yuk Fong" were charged with receiving the coal knowing the same to have been stolen or unlawfully obtained.

Mr. D. McCallum, appearing for the three Blue Funnel launch men, objected to two charges being preferred against his clients in alternative forms, but this was not upheld.

Mr. J. M. d'Almada Renedios defended the alleged receivers.

Mr. T. G. Bennett, who prosecuted on behalf of the complainants, the Ocean Steamship Company, asked for a committed to the Sessions.

The story for the prosecution was that the transfer of coal was noticed to be going on from the "Posidon" to the "Tai Yuk Fong." The superintendent of Holt's wharf was informed and on an inspection being made it was discovered that about five tons of coal had been transferred from the Blue Funnel launch. The boatswain was paid $860 per month to purchase coal and pay the wages of the crew of the "Tai Yuk Fong," while the requirements of the "Posidon" were attended to by the Company.

After evidence had been called the case was adjourned till this afternoon.

## [5] MOROCCO FIGHTING.

**FURTHER OFFENSIVE IMMINENT**

**FRENCH STRATEGY.**

*(Router's Service.)*

*Paris, Sept. 21.*

The "Echo de Paris" war correspondent at Morocco states that 80 battalions are shortly to participate in a big new French offensive on the Kiffane sector, north of Taza, the success of which will result in the submission of some wavering tribes and will considerably reduce the enemy zone, separating Kiffane from the Spanish post at Azibdemidor in the Melilla sector.

A communiqué from Madrid reports the enemy's gunfire on the Alhucemas sector has intensified, but everything is quiet on the Ceuta and Tetuan sector.

The new plan of reprovisioning has successfully been tested, dispensing with mules and substituting carts drawn by tanks and con-voyed by infantry, while the planks are protected by artillery barrage.

Paris newspapers learn from Madrid that a Spanish offensive is imminent, the object being to capture Abd el Krim's headquarters at Ajdir. General Primo de Rivera is reported to have placed cavalry detachments at the disposal of Marshal Petain.

## [10] PLEASANT SURPRISE.

**GERMAN TEXTILE EMBARGO RAISED.**

**LANCASHIRE PLEASED.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, Sept. 21*

Lancashire textile concerns are pleasantly surprised at the announcement of the news of the removal of all restrictions on the import of textiles to Germany.

The Manchester Chamber of Commerce last year complained to the then Premier, Mr. Ramsay MacDonald, of the German import licence system which practically excluded British textiles, and urged a modification in the Anglo-German Trade Treaty, then being negotiated.

Germany promised consideration of the complaint and has now ratified a clause of the treaty completely raising the embargo.

## [19] CHEUNG CHAU NOTES

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

No. 2 cottage has changed hands, having been sold to a former old resident, who is making improvements to the property, in the shape of additional servants quarters and a verandah.

This transaction is the 34th or 95th sale that has taken place since the settlement was started, making about 70 different owners. Some of these held their property only for a short period. Ten of the original 80 houses are still held by their builders.

The work at the Police Station seems to have taken a fresh start and the wall work soon finished. The matched which was to have been erected near No. 20 was not put up owing to some new regulation by the P.W.D., all the houses are full and there is still a demand.

The 17th of this month seems to have been a popular day for birthdays, five of the residents (perhaps more) celebrated, and birthday parties were the order; in the evening. A few hours later Mr. Stork gave a surprise party in No. 6, in the early hours of the morning a new Ray came to brighten the household; the little lady and her mother are getting on nicely, later on we had the news that a young gentleman had put in an appearance in the family of one of the Alliance Missionaries, making the third recent addition to that Mission congratulations to all.

Welcome showers have just fallen, to freshen up the gardens which were getting very dry.

## [20] SEVEN YEARS

**HEAVY SENTENCE ON PAWN-SHOP ROBBERS.**

An important factor in the evidence against three prisoners charged at the Criminal Sessions yesterday afternoon with taking part in a pawnshop robbery was that they had offered some of the stolen jewellery in exchange for opium. Other missing articles were found in the house raided by Inspector F. W. Shaftain and Chinese constables and it was here that the prisoners were found. The total value of the jewellery taken from the pawnshop was $4,900.

The prisoners were identified by witnesses at the Sessions yesterday who were in the pawnshop when the robbers made their entry and who had been put into a small room, gagged and bound.

At the resumed sitting this morning two of the prisoners were found guilty by the jury and sentenced to seven years' imprisonment, whilst the third was discharged as a result of an alibi.
