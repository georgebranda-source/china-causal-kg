## [1] “CHINA MAIL” SPORTS

**HOCKEY NOTES**

**YESTERDAY'S CLUB VICTORY**

By 5 goals to 8, the Hongkong Hockey Club 1st XI defeated the Punjab Regiment 1st XI at the U.S.R.C. ground, Kowloon, yesterday, after an enjoyable match.
With the Club forwards passing in excellent style yesterday Evans shot two goals in the first half, the second being a beautiful effort.
There was no more scoring until after half time when the Punjabis reduced the deficit to 2-1. Then Woodward found the mark, the ball going in off the Punjab goalkeeper's stick. When about three-quarters of the game had been played, Lt. Wright-Neville, playing centre forward for the soldiers, scored two goals, leveling the score at 3 all.
A nice shot by Woodward gave the Club the lead again (4-3) and Allen obtained a lovely last goal.

## [6] HARBOUR SWIM.

**CHINESE FIRST WITH BREAST STROKE.**

**INITIAL TRY WINS:**

Using the breast stroke from start to finish, Wong Ping-fan won the harbour swim for Chinese (for the Challenge Cup presented by Mr. Mona Kensan of Measrs. Butterfield & Swire) in his first attempt in the annual race.

Last year's winner, Leung Titsang was a second, beaten by 200 yards. Tan Chiu-hin, who relied on back and trudgeon strokes, was a good third. Wong finished very strongly. Second and third were comparatively fresh when the race of just under a mile was over.

Conditions were very favourable, the breeze having died down and with only a slight current setting to East which did not trouble the competitors to any great extent.

Tan took the lead at first, followed by the winner who went to the front at any early stage and was never headed. Leung made his effort after having been in the water 22 minutes. His time last year was 31 mins. 22/8 secs. The finish was at the Praya wall outside the V.R.C.

## [8] SUCH IS FAME.

**HOBBS' SOOFTHES MUSLIM WORLD**

**LONDON CARTOON OFIENDB**

(Times of Ceylon)

*London, October 3.*

Jack Hobbs has earned another niche in the temple of fame.

Mr. Durd, head of the Almacdiyya Muslim Mission in London, has written to the Press saying that Hobbs has won favour with the whole of the Muslim world by soothing their feelings.

It appears that when Hobbs was thrilling cricket enthusiasts in August, a London newspaper published a cartoon representing a gigantic figure of Hobbs overtopping pigmy statues of great figures in the world's history, amongst whom appeared Mohammed.
Mr. Dard elicited an expression of regret from the newspaper which this offended and he now publishes a letter he has received from Hobbs in the following terms:

"I am pained to know that the cartoon has offended the feelings of all Muslims."

After emphasising that he has no responsibility for newspaper cartoons, he says: "I am sorry that my success in cricket, which has been shared with me sport-loving people all over the world, has thus unfortunately become the occasion of wounding the religious susceptibility of any Muslims, who are my friends.

## [15] LOST AT SEA.

**ITALIAN ETEAMER'S**

**CREW'S PERLOUS RESCUE.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

Nova Scotia, October 21.
The Italian steamer "Ignazio Florio" from Montreal to Avonmouth has been abandoned and is sinking. The crew are safe aboard the steamer. "President Harding."

A message from New York reports that the "Ignazio Florio" suffered terribly from high seas in mid-Atlantic which swept over her. She lost her rudder and all the boats were smashed.

The second officer was swept overboard and the first officer had his leg broken. The ship was in danger of capsizing.

The "President Harding" stood by first. The attempt to get boats alongside was unsuccessful, but the crew of 37 were finally rescued.

## [16] SYRIAN REVOLT,

**PROTECTING AMERICAN LIVES.**

**DESTROYERS' MISSION**

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

Two American destroyers have been ordered to proceed from Gibraltar, where the American destroyer flotilla has just arrived, to Alexandria, for possible service at Syrian ports, and any action considered necessary for the protection of American lives and property from possible attacks by revolting Syrian tribesmen.

The request for destroyers came from the American Consul at Beirut.

State Department officials hope it will prove unnecessary to send vessels to Syria from Alexandria.

## [17] HARD FACTS

**BANKERS' APMITUDI**

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

*Washington, Oct. 21.*

Further indications show that the Government is scrutinising all proposals for private loans by American financiers, to France were forthcoming after a Conference between President Coolidge and Mr. Mitchell, President of the National City Bank of New York.

Mr. Mitchell subsequently stated that until France has settled her war, she should need to expect little sympathy from American bankers in the way of private loans.
He added that American banking officials were solidly behind the position taken up by the Government, and asserted that an American Banker attempting to float a private French loan in the present circumstances would only have a "faithy, hope and charity" proposition to offer the public.
