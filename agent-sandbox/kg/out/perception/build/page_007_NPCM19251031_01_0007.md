## [1] BRITISH TAXIS.

An announcement that the directors have under consideration the purchase of a new fleet of British made taxicabs of an improved design and the sale of the present fleet of vehicles was made at the second annual meeting of the Hongkong and Kowloon Taxi-cub Co., Ltd. held at the registered office of the Company yesterday afternoon.

Mr. H. Birkett presided. Others present were Mr. A. H. Rowe (managing director), Messrs. Fred Ellis, E. M. Raymond (directors), C. A. Fulcher, D. McNeill, A. A. Alves (shareholders).

The Chairman said:—

Gentlemen.—The report and accounts for 1924-25 having been in your hands for some days, I will, with your permission, take them as read.

Before proceeding to deal with the Company's affairs, I desire to refer to the great loss we have sustained in the death, under the tragic circumstances which you will all remember, of the late Hon. Mr. Chau Su-ki, who was a director of the company from its inception and always took the deepest interest in its affairs. Mr. T. N. Chau has been invited by the directors to fill the seat thus vacated, and I am sure he will carry on his father's excellent work.

You will note that the balance sheet shows a loss of $46,680.14, but it has to be borne in mind that $62,303.41 has been written off for depreciation, in addition to the $45,915.04 written off last year, making the total amount written off for depreciation, $108,215.45, which represents about one-third of the paid-up capital of the company and more than half the original cost of our taxicabs, and this has been accomplished in a period of only fourteen months' finding, as the bulk of our taxis did not arrive in the Colony until March, 1924, and further it shows that your directors have pursued a hold policy and made ample provision for depreciation, which I feel sure will meet with the approbation of the shareholders.

A Farming Scheme.

You will also note that the balance sheet before you refers to the period ending April 30, 1925, and since that date the affairs of the company have been conducted on a different plan: the loss of $46,680.14 is to be attributed in a large measure to the fact that our drivers, working as servants of the company, grievously abused the taxicabs, a fact which is borne out by the item in the profit and loss. account of $46,937.85 for workshop and garage charges. Furthermore, by various ways too complicated to describe here, the company was deprived by the drivers of a considerable part of its just receipts. Your directors, therefore, decided to begin the present year with a system of farming out the taxis to a syndicate formed by the drivers themselves. The syndicate pays a fixed rate for the vehicles and they have to meet their own running workshop and garage charges, thus eliminating from our accounts, the items running charges, workshop and garage charges, and trading expenses, which together amount to $210,910.87—an item which will not occur at all in the next statement of accounts to be presented to you. Furthermore, the item "office administration and expenses" has been greatly reduced as the company now employs a much smaller staff. In future, apart from these reduced office expenses, the company has only to meet the cost of depreciation, and is at the same time in receipt of a regular income from the hire of its taxis.

I would also call your attention to the item in the balance sheet of calls in arrear. These shareholders have been served with a notice under registered cover to pay the third call with interest due and informing them that in default of payment within 14 days their shares will be forfeited and sold in accordance with clause 36 (a) of our Articles of Association.

Your directors have under consideration the sale of the present fleet of taxis and the purchase of a new fleet of British made vehicles, for which purpose a visit home to England by your managing director is under contemplation, in order to select the most suitable taxicab, and you may rest assured that the result will be the putting in commission of a greatly improved type of vehicle, especially adapted to hill climbing and generally suited to local conditions, and which will last much

Longer with lower training expenses and smaller repair till than our present fleet of cartoats and for which the directors are of the opinion that there will be no need to make any further calls to meet the cost of the new fleet of taxi-cabs.
Although the balance sheet shows a loss, the results of our trading since May 1 under the new system of farming out the taxis has been very profitable and when the books are closed at the end of this month, which will be just six months' trading under the new system, your directors hope to declare an interim dividend.

I have now pleasure in moving the adoption of the report and accounts, and after this has been seconded I shall be glad to answer to the best of my ability any questions which any shareholder may desire to ask.

Shareholder's Questions.
Mr. McNeill desired information on a sum of over $60,000 appearing in the balance sheet for office administration and "expenses—a sum which he considered too large.

Mr. Rowe explained that this was due to having to upkeep three branches and pay rent. The expenses over a period of six months had entered the balance sheet figures. Formerly the Company had an office in the Asiatic Building, which cost $2,000 in rent. This had been vacated, and the expenses now amounted to only $2,000 a month.

In querying the expenses for cushion covers and drivers' uniforms, Mr. McNeill said that he had not seen a driver at any time in uniform.

It was explained that each man had been supplied with two suits, but it was difficult to get them to wear the uniforms, and they never kept them clean, so the uniforms had to be withdrawn.

Mr. Rowe also explained that the cost of uniforms was a small one, and that the total included the cost of taximeters, which represented a large sum.

A sum of over $7,000 for furniture was explained by having to fit up the old office at the Asiatic Building.

Mr. McNeill said that he had looked over the Kowloon office; but did not consider the whole place worth more than $50.

The Chairman said that the large expenses was in a large measure due to the showroom at Pedder Street which it was thought necessary with a view to larger sales. The results did not justify the expense, and as soon as the opportunity presented itself the premises were vacated.
To another query by Mr. McNeill as regards the hiring of the taxis, it was explained that three men were responsible under the scheme and that the money was collected every day.

Condition: of Vehicles.

Mr. McNeill drew attention to what he termed the bad state and filthy condition of the cars for months and months. There was no inducement to the public to engage them, as he had found for himself after spending hundreds of dollars by riding in them, and he considered that they should be looked after better by the Company. As a small shareholder he would certainly like to see the Company get on. He knew the Chinese were careless when the expenses did not come out of their own pocket, and he thought that the drivers should be pulled up a bit.

The Chairman said that the Company was being constantly pulled up by the Police: They made a thorough inspection periodically of the gears, mechanism and other things, and any cars not in order were not allowed to run.

Mr. Rowe added that prior to the strike they had 30 mechanics on the Hongkong side. They now had only about eight men, and were consequently working under great difficulties. Under the hiring scheme, the farmer has to look after the cars and do everything. He said that all the taxis are now running, and in respect of any vehicle that is not running the farmer has to pay $600 under the contract, which was for three years.

This concluded the questions and the Chairman's proposal that that report and accounts be adopted was seconded by Mr. Raymond and unanimously agreed to.

## [2] (Koulet's Netv)

The committee on tariff autonomy held its first meeting this morning.

Mr. C. T. Wang and Tsai King-kan represented China, while the twelve Powers were all represented by Ministers, and certain advisers, experts, and secretaries were also present.

Mr. Wang was elected chairman. He presented the proposals which he submitted to the conference on October 26. The representatives of all the Powers made speeches.

Mr. Hioki, in a speech elucidating the nature of the proposals made on October 26, said there were two alternatives in these.

The first plan was the inauguration of a Chinese statutory tariff with special tariffs on certain limited articles which would be agreed upon separately between China and the interested Powers.

The second plan was the inauguration of a graduated tariff at the rate provided by the treaties on the ground that a uniform tariff was unreasonable, unscientific and works to the detriment of Chinese and foreign trade.

Mr. Hoki said the first plan was a simpler and more practicable means of setting China on the road to tariff autonomy.

Mr. Strawn expressed America's willingness to go beyond the Washington treaty and accord China the relief she desired, but the American delegation wished to be fully advised regarding the plan for abolition of likin which must be accomplished coincidently with tariff autonomy.

It is understood the British expressed a somewhat similar view to America. The British are carefully considering the proposals brought forward, and are adopting a waiting attitude as the conference is only in the most preliminary stages at present.

## [4] WAR IN NORTH

**FENGTIEN ADVANCE**

*(Router's Service.)*

Sun Chuan-fang's rapid advances have cleared the Fengtian forces out of Kiangsu and have given him control of the railway as far as Pengpu, with a narrow front, but preceded by the movement of forces of the allied provinces the latter are beginning to reach Pukow from Fukien and from Kiangsi while a division has gone to Pengpu from Arhui.
With reinforcements Sun has command of 70,000 and Chang Tsung-chang of Fengtian about 80,000. The Fengtian forces are more compact and prepared and apparently intend to strike before the arrival of the allied reinforcements. 15,000 of which are being rushed up to the front.
Sun Chuan-fang went to Pengpu from Nanlang this morning and reported that the Fengtian advance had begun.

## [5] Ottawa. October 30.

The swing of the elections in favour of Conservatives is probably largely due to the general tendency of the public to favour a change of Government, owing to the dissatisfaction at high taxation and business depression.

Mr. Maighen's main appeal based on a Protective Tariff made the issue significant.
The main electoral change occurred in the Province of Ontario which is the most important industrially, although Quebec, which is also a centre of many industries, remained Liberal.

The Maritime Provinces, though said to be opposed to higher tariffs, returned a majority of Conservatives, while the Prairie Provinces returned a majority of Progressives or Liberals.

The Labour Party and Indeper dents cut a small figure.

Only one woman member was elected, namely, Miss Agnes Macphail, a Progressive who was a member of the last Parliament.

## [7] EARLIER CABLES:

The latest returns of the elections indicate that the Conservatives have a small clear majority in the new Parliament. Nine members of the Cabinet, including Mr. Mackenzie King, have been defeated.

Later.

The results of the election available early this morning show that Mr. Meighen, the Conservative leader, was returned as the head of the largest group, though whether he will have a clear majority is undecided until the returns from 25 doubtful constituencies are received. According to unofficial figures issued at three o'clock this morning the position is as follows: 118 Conservatives, 89 Liberals, ten Progressives, two independents, two Labour and 25 doubtful.

## [9] INTERNATIONAL COMMITTEE'S REPORT

*(Router's Service.)*

*London, Oct. 30.*

A meeting of the International Cotton Committee at Milan, under the chairmanship of Mr. Fredrick Holroyd, decided to protest against the publication of fortnightly cotton condition reports issued by the Washington Department of Agriculture on the ground that they were extrainely detrimental to the trade and unreliable.

Mr. Holroyd was appointed a delegate to attend the Washington conference on the standardisation of rules.
