## [8] BROTHLOVE.

**STRIKE PICKETS ATTACK VOLUNTEER.**

**NOT "AN ENGLISHMAN."**

That the man arrested by Chinese strike pickets at the Sam-chun border this week is not "an Englishman" is vouched for by a very reliable authority on the spot.

Last night a gentleman attached to the “Kowloon and district” station of the Chinese Maritime Customs (doing duty on the Chinese frontier adjoining the New Territories) came into Hongkong. He says that the person who crossed the boundary and was held by the pickets was a native of Shantung.

Conversation, heard indicates that the man claimed to be, or to have been a policeman in Hong Kong. As none of the Shantung police contingent has to be accounted for, it is presumed, if his statement is based on fact, that he was formerly in the force.

He was also heard to offer his services to the strikers as a “brother.” His 8vertures were at once turned down as his good intentions were suspected. He was seen to have been attacked forthwith and to have received a sound drubbing. Later on he was sent to Canton, in custody.

## [9] ONLY THE COAT.

**THIEF SPURNS TROUSERS AND WAISTCOAT**

**DETECTIVE'S SMART HAUL.**

Wearing the coat, and with the pawntickets for the waistcoat and trousers of a suit he had stolen, a Chinese was arrested by a Bay View Station detective on Thursday.

A burglary had been committed at the top floor of No. 16, Whitfield (Causeway Bay) as long ago as December 9, the thieves getting in through an unlocked skylight.

Two days afterwards, some pearls (part of the “swag” valued at $70) were recovered from pawn.

A second man was also caught and both came up at the Police Court this morning.

An article produced was a flannel shirt which was identified as being part of a roll of material which had not been made up prior to the theft.

The law lays down that when a theft is committed in daylight and when admittance is gained without having recourse to breaking open a door, window, etc., the charge should be only one of larceny. On this charge, and as nothing was known of the defendants, His Worship (Mr. R. E. Lindsell) imposed sentence of six weeks' hard labour each.

**GOOD CHANCE TO SEE SUN'S ECLIPSE.**

**SEVEN MINUTES IN MANILA.**

Sumatra this year attracted many scientists, several expeditions making it their point of observation of the total eclipse of the sun on January 14. But in May 1954, 28 years hence the Philippines and probably Manila will be their Mecca. The 1954 eclipse will be complete along a path which crosses the Philippines. ## [11] HONGKONG, SATURDAY, JANUARY 30, 1926.

**CANTON'S STRIKE.**

**DR. C. C. WU ADDRESSES MERCHANTS.**

**IMPORTANT RESOLUTION.**

Dr. C. C. Wu, the present Mayor of Canton addressed a combined meeting of the four Chambers of Commerce of Canton on Wednesday on the subject of negotiations to end the strike and boycott.

Dr. Wu's speech followed the general lines of the text in English published in the "Canton Gazette" and since reproduced in Hongkong.

Incidentally the speaker admitted that both the Chinese labourers and merchants of Canton had suffered Heavy loss, with those of Hongkong.

Mr. So Shiu-ching, chairman of the Strike Executive also addressed the meeting, says the "Kung Sheung Yat Po."

There were about forty merchant representatives present, continues the report. After the meeting a resolution was passed unanimously. In view of its importance, a close translation is given which, if not quite grammatical, is best understood:—

"To-day Mr. Wu, the Mayor, has announced the unofficial negotiations between the (Canton) Government, and the Hongkong Government. This meeting declares itself to be satisfied that the present strike of labourers is actually of the people, and spontaneous. As the various friends in labour have made such great sacrifices and are so steadfast, we, the various merchants, should associate ourselves (with them) as one, steadfastly assisting, to attain to the object of Hongkong fully accepting the terms for resumption of work (ending the strike)."

The "Wah Tsz Yat Po" publishes a message from its Canton correspondent to the effect that a fresh delegation of merchants is planning to come down in connection with negotiations for peace.

## [15] MASTER FINED.

**"WHO IS GOING TO PAY?"**

Captain J. M. Byrne, master of the s.s. "Shansi," was fined $100 (or two months' hard labour), at the Marine Court this morning for not bringing his ship to the quarantine anchoring on arrival here from Shanghai (a port declared to be infected with small-pox), and allowing members of the crew to go ashore.

Captain Byrne placed "guilty," explaining that he was unaware of the regulation.

The Marine Magistrate (Lieut. Com. G. F. Hole, R.N.) asked whether the owners or defendant would pay the fine: Captain Byrne replied that he did not know just then.

## [16] CHINA NEW YEAR.

**LAST "TOOTH FEAST" OF SEASON**

**THE COMING HAZAR**

Favourable weather should give an auspicious start to the bazaar always held on the eve of China New Year and due to begin next week.

Police regulations forbid the setting up of street stalls indiscriminately. Bazaar stallholders will "peg out their claims" by putting up empty cases but the fun does not start till a fortnight herice.

Householders will soon render tribute to the "Kitchen God," the chin chin joss being in the nature of a thanksgiving service for the whole year.

After this service has been gone through, most people will commence their elaborate system of laying in supplies for the New Year season.

There will be little or no change in the layout of the bazaar. As was the case last year, the whole length of Queen's Road has been kept clear. So-called "bargains" will be found in the main streets with the knick-nacks in side streets.

The general tightness of money points to financial stringency in both business and social circles and it will occasion no surprise to see things quieter than usual.

A good deal of the lucky flowers, goldfish, sweetmeats, etc., used to come from Canton. Those familiar with the trade predict that a shortage, if any, will be hardly noticeable to the casual observer.

## [22] EMPIRE EFFORT REQUIRED.

*(Reuter's Service)*

*Sheffield, Jan. 29.*

At a luncheon given in his honour by the Sheffield Chamber of Commerce, Mr. L. S. Amery, Dominions Secretary, surveying Empire trade, urged a comprehensive policy regarding migration, population, movement of capital, and the use of markets.

We dedicated that Great Britain was at present faced with the gravest economic issues; and, although there were signs of very appreciable improvement in commerce and industry, it would be a great mistake to think that the problem could solve itself without an effort on our part.

Mr. Amery showed by means of statistics that the Empire trade was the best. He declared that its capacity for expansion was infinitely greater than foreign trade.

The Government hoped that the result of its East African policy would be a very great increase in the development of Empire cotton supplies, simultaneously with the development of the natives' prosperity, which meant the growth of some of our best markets.

## [26] EPIC OF THE SEA.

**HEROIC RESCUE OF SHIP'S CREW**

**A THRILLING STORY**

(Reitert's American Serviog.)

*New York, Jan. 29.*

Britain and America are ringing with the heroic rescue of the crew of the "Antinoe," which, in the terse seamen's language of the President Roosevelt's master, Captain Fried forms a thrilling epic of the sea.

Beginning on January 24, when the wireless compass bearings showed the "Antinoe's" own estimate of her position of a hundred miles to be an error, the "President Roosevelt" arrived and pumped out oil with excellent effect, but lost sight of the "Antinoe's" for 24 hours. She found her again on January 25, heavily listing to starboard. The engine fire room was flooded and the hatches broken.

Captain Fried ordered a lifeboat to be launched, whereupon a squall hit the ship and upset the boat, two men being drowned and the others rescued with difficulty in an exhausted condition and covered with oil.

Thereafter, until January 27, continued, fruitless, manœuvres were made to got in contact by means of lines fired by the Lyle gun, also by boat. The line broke sixteen times, until the apparatus was improvised at the suggestion of an artillery expert on board. Twelve men were got off on the afternoon of January 27 and the remainder at midnight, including the Captain, who was carried on board. They had been without food for two days.

The President Roosevelt's crew was in a state of collapse.

## [27] AIR EXPLORERS.

**WARNING AGAINST PAPUA**

**AN ARMED EXPEDITION.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

### Batavín, Jan. 29.

The Amerleo-Dutch expedition has been specially warned to take the utmost precautions owing to the Papua tribes' uncertain attitude toward foreigners.

The expedition is being organised under the auspices of the Dutch East Indies Committee of Scientific Research for the purpose of an eight months' exploration of the unknown territory in the region of the Nassau Mountains, New Guinea.

The purity will use an aeroplane specially constructed in accordance with instructions given by American round the world aviators. The base of operations will be Manokwari, which will be in wireless touch with Java.

The party, which will be armed, will include Professor Sterling, scientific leader; Dr. Van Leeuwen. Director of the Botanical Gardens, and Dr. BurtonZoorg, accompanied by a military detachment of 40 men and 180 native bearers. They leave for Manokvari at the end of March.
