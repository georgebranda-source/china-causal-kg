## [2] HELD IN CHAINS.

**CRUEL TREATMENT BY CANTON STRIKERS**

The following are facts concerning the capture, by Chinese Strike pickets in Canton harbour of two Indian watchmen employed by the Asiatic Petroleuns Co. They show the efforts made to effect the release of these men, their imprisonment in chains for many days, and the cruel treatment meted out to them. They also prove the existence of a Strikers' Court and prison in Canton which was denied by Mr. C. C. Wu, Mayor of the city, and a member of the Executive and further clearly indicate the impotence of the Government to deal with the strikers in that they were unable to afford protection to subjects off a foreign nation who were left incarcerated in a strikers' prison for 18 days.

At about 5:30 p.m. on December 18, 1925, Mr. Cadman, Resident Engineer of the A.P. Co., together with two Indian watchmen, whilst on their way to Fati Installation were fired on and held up by an armed launch manned by strike pickets. They were taken to Headquarters in the city and Mr. Cadman, after a form of trial by a Strikers' Court, was handed over to the Police Bureau the same night and subsequently released and the request of the British Consul-General. The Indians were retained in custody by the strikers. The following morning, the British Vice-Consul, accompanied by the Manager of the A.P. Co., proceeded to the Foreign Office and recorded the strongest possible protest from the Consul-General in regard to the incident and demanded the immediate release of the Indians, the Vice-Consul informing the Foreign Secretary that his orders were to return without them.
Mr. Fu Ping-sheung, Secretary for Foreign Affairs, pleaded ignorance of the matter and, after discussion, despatched his Assistant, Mr. Luk, to accompany the Vice-Consul and the A.P.C. Manager to the Police Bureau. On arrival, the Vice-Consul informed the Acting Police Commissioner, Mr. Poon, that he had come to take away the two Indians whom the strikers had captured the previous day and that he could not return without them.

After some discussion between Mr. Luk and the Police authorities a letter was written to the Strike Hendouarters and delivered personally by Mr. Luk. He returned within an hour stating that the Indions would be detained until they could, be questioned, and that they would be released that afternoon.

The Vice-Consul thereupon informed the authorities that he was very dissatisfied with what had been done and stated his determination to go direct to the strike headquarters and request the release of the Indians.

The officials strongly advised against approaching the strikers whom they stated were ignorant men, who would probably insult foreigners and begged that matters be left in their hands.

The Vice-Consult, accompanied by the A.P. Co. representative, then proceeded to the Strike headquarters but was refused admittance. They were, however, informed by an English-speaking gentleman that the Indians had just been released.

On December 21, two days later, Mr. Luk, of the Foreign Office, was on the British Concession and stated that the Indians had been released from Strike Headquarters at 4 o'clock on the previous Saturday afternoon. Two days later, on December 23, the British Consul-General informed the A.P.C. that a strong protest had been despatched to the Government that day in regard to the incident. No further news of the Indians was received until December 27 when the A.P.C. heard from an Indian watchman employed by a Chinese firm that he had seen the two men in chains in the Strikers' Headquarters' prison on December 24.

This information was immediately passed to the Consultant General, who sent his Vice-Consult, accompanied by an A.P.C. representative, to the Foreign Office to endeavour to test this information and make a further attempt to effect the release of the captives.

On arrival the Foreign Office informed them that they were under the impression that the Indians had been released some days before and pointed out the possibility of their not wishing to return to the A.P.C.

However, in view of the new information they promised to make immediate enquiries. On December 31 further information was received to the effect that the Indians had been seen in chains in the strikers' prison on December 25, and the British Vice-Consult and an A.P.C. representative again proceeded to the Foreign Office, who suggested in view of the information that the Police might send inspectors with them to the strikers' prison.

They proceeded to the Police Bureau and were informed by the officials that nothing could be done against the strikers. They then returned to the Foreign Office and explained what had happened, "whereupon" Mr. Fu Ping-sheung, Foreign Minister, issued a written order to the Police to obtain the release of the Indian and informed the Vice-Consult that they would probably be set free next day.

Nothing, however, was heard for some days and on January 4 the British Vice-Consul again proceeded to the Foreign Office and was informed that the Strike Committee had promised to release the Indians, but had subsequently stated that they (the Indians) preferred to remain in Canton rather than return to the A.P.C. The Vice-Consul pointed out that he would like to see the Indians and get them to state their wishes before him and the Foreign Office promised to endeavour to arrange a meeting at the Police Bureau in the near future.

## [4] FREIGHT CHARGES.

**THE MOLLER-THORESEN CASE**

**TO-DAY'S JUDGMENT**

A case of great interest to agents of shipping firms was decided in the Summary Court this morning, the dispute in law between Eifressen and Company, of Hongkong, Swatow and Amoy, and A. P. Moller, ship owner of Copenhagen being decided in favour of the latter.

A. P: Moller was the plaintiff and Thoresen and Co., the defendants.

His Honour, Mr. Justice J. R. Wood, held that the evidence called by Thoresen's as to the recognised usage in regard to the collection of freight and the charging of commission on it had fallen short of establishing the definite practice of giving and receiving commission for freight collected, in the absence of such stipulation in the contract.

The common practice, no doubt, had been shown to exist of changing and receiving a commission on freight collected by the agent but this practice was not universal. Mr. Goggin, one of the shipping agents who had given evidence as to usage, had said that it was inconceivable that plaintiffs should have understood that the fee mentioned in their cabled contract included the duty of freight collection, but it seemed to His Honour to fall short of establishing a definite practice so prevalent in this business that it might be referred to as governing the construction of this particular contract.

His Honour thus held that Thoresen and Company were not entitled under the cabled contract to remuneration additional to the sum named in that cable and accordingly gave judgment in favour of A. P. Moller, with costs. In view of his decision, His Honour reversed a previous order under which plaintiff was to pay the cost of hearing evidence as to whether evidence on "usage" should be given. The position was, he thought, analogous to the section of the code under which the court might direct that questions of law might be raised for the opinion of the court before evidence on the actual action.

The costs of this preliminary hearing would therefore be costs in the cause.

## [7] MAGISTRACY ITEMS

**MERCHANTS ROBBED BY RELATIVES.**

Two Chinese merchants were complainants at the Central Magistracy this morning when four youthful relatives were charged before Major C. Willson with the larceny of $70G in property and money from safes in their shop at Des Voeux Road Central.
Sergeant W. E. Meadows said that, the first accused, Sze To-sing, was arrested on suspicion, and he took the detectives to the roof of the building where one of the stolen cash boxes was hidden. Another cash box was found in a trunk. The second accused was arrested on an accusation made by the first accused, and he in turn pointed out the pawnshops where a part of the stolen property had been pledged.
The first two accused were sentenced to one month's hard labour each, and the other two were discharged for lack of evidence. An order was made for the return of the property to the complainants on payment of the amounts for which the goods had been pawned, together with interest.

A Chinese woman was charged before Mr. R. E. Lindsell with the theft of jewellery valued at $195 belonging to another woman living at Hill Road.

## [9] Touting for Hire.

<sub>section under [7] MAGISTRACY ITEMS</sub>

A licensed motor driver was charged before Mr. Lindsell with using a private owned car (No. 1079) for purposes of public hire. In evidence given by a plain clothes constable he said that he was accosted outside the To Yuen restaurant at 2 o'clock yesterday morning by a boy who offered him a ride in accused's car. A bargain was struck for $2 as far as Bonham Road. The witness, however, compelled the driver to go to the Police Station on a charge of being found on the road during prohibited hours. Investigations here showed that the car was not a public vehicle.

Mr. Harry Kong, giving evidence, said that the car was left in his charge by the owner, a Chinese, who left for Canton last year. The car was used to take his friends about and also to tow disabled cars to the garage at Causeway Bay. Witness was not aware that the car had been used for public hire.

Accused was fined $25. On His Worship advising Mr. Kong to exercise better control over the car it was stated by Divisional Inspector Peter Grant that the transfer had not been notified to the Police and the car would, therefore, not be released until the return of the registered owner.

**CANTON'S BLAME.**

**STATEMENT IN THE COMMONS**

### Hongkong Government Fully

*(Router's Service.)*

In the House of Commons, replying to Mr. S. P. Vlant (Labour, West Willesdon), Mr. L. S. M. Amery, Secretary of State for the Colonies, said that the Canton Government's offer to mediate between the strikers and the Hongkong Government was based on a claim that it was not a party to the strike and boycott, but in the Hongkong Government's view that claim was vitiated by the fact that the Canton Government had taken no steps to put an end to the intimidation of workers or other illegal activities of the Strike Committee, by which alone the strike and boycott in Canton was maintained in flagrant violation of Treaty obligations. Therefore, the Hongkong Government was unable to regard the Canton Government as other than a principal on whom the responsibility for the continued boycott lay.

His Majesty's Government fully supported the attitude of the Hongkong Government, and it now remained for the Canton Government to give proof of its sincerity and translate from words into deeds its professed desire to see the present situation brought to an end.

Mr. H. W. Looker, (Conservative, South-East Essex) suggested that the Canton Government could end the strike in a moment if it wanted to, but it was influenced by its Russian advisers.
Mr. Amery was of opinion that that was so.

[Mr. Looker was formerly a partner in the local legal firm of Deacon, Looker and Harston, now styled Deacon's.]

## [11] ONE OF A SERIES.

**POLITICAL INTRIGUE IN**

**SITUATION NOW NORMAL.**

Latest reports from Canton indicate that there has been nothing of importance since Saturday afternoon.

Canton Government banknotes are now circulating as usual and the feeling of suspense has blown over.

The only difficulty is to say whether the Reds or anti-Reds, or Left Party as against the Rightists, strengthened their position in this last of a series of political coups since May last year.

What was reported in yesterday's "China Mail" as having occurred at the strike headquarters—the disarming of pickets—seems to have had an internal, political bearing, rather than any relation with foreign intercourse.

From the maze of conflicting reports it is assumed that General Chang Kai-shek or his army of Cadets, has removed possibility of opposition in Canton. Which individuals have been eclipsed as a result of the coup is not known.
