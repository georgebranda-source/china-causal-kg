## [1] RIVER MINES?

**EMPLOYING UNQUALIFIED PILOTS?**

A floating object, black, round and smooth, which might have been a mine, is reported to have been sighted by the s.s. "Honam". and to have passed halfway between Sampan Chau and Fish Stakes.

A Harbour Office's notification is posted to this effect and reporting that other vessels passing up and down the river have sighted similar objects, the French gunboat, "Argus" having met two within the last week.

Mishaps to steamers in the Canton river, reports of which have been received in Hongkong, are believed to have been due to the engagement of unqualified men as pilots. As most ships proceeding to Canton to discharge cargo do not call at Hongkong they have been at a loss to secure good pilots and the mishaps are the consequence.

There have been no serious accidents up to the present, the majority of groundings being cases of small steamers touching mud and coming off at the next high water, or after the cargo has been discharged in part. In some cases, however, vessels have been hard and fast for the best part of a week it is stated, and the work of lightening them is proceeding. Two that have been refloated are the "Nanyo Maru" No. 1 and the "Yei Maru," both of which were frequent callers at Hongkong prior to the boycott.

## [5] SERIOUS CHARGE

**ALLEGED MISAPPROPRIATION OF FUNDS**

**EUROPEAN MANAGER IN CUSTODY.**

Before Mr. S.B. B. McElderry at the Central Magistracy yesterday afternoon, J. E. Atherley, manager of Messrs. Getz Bros. & Co. (of the Orient), Ltd., was charged with alleged misappropriation of $13,400 belonging to his employers.

Mr. D. H. Blake prosecuted, and Mr. A. H. Hall appeared for the offence.

It was submitted by the defence that the case was one for the Civil Court, but His Worship did not agree with this view.

Hearing was fixed for tomorrow morning and the afternoons of the two following days. On the question of bail, which had been fixed at $20,000, His Worship said he could not make a reduction in the amount. The sum would be in cash or in two sureties to the satisfaction of the Court.

## [7] SIR A. KEITH'S THEORY.

Sir Arthur Keith, secretary of the Royal Institution, discussed before the Anthropology Section of the British Association the fragments of a skull recently discovered by Mr. F. Turville-Petre, who told the association how he found them in a robbers' cave on the western shore of the Lake of Galilee.

Only the forehead, the right cheek bone, and the right wing of a bone in the base of the skull remained. Sir Arthur said, to tell us what kind of a man they came from, but these fragments were perfectly preserved and as hard as porcelain, and they showed that the man belonged to the Neanderthal type.

It must congratulate my young friend on a veritable archaeological triumph.

The discovery is particularly important because of the place in which it was made. In this eavg on the shore of the Lake of Galilee the archaeologist's spade has laid care the records of a people who lived in Palestine 20,000 years or more before Abraham and his household made their trek eastward.

The discovery is important for another reason: The type of humanity and the kind of implements fashioned by humanity in stone and in bone, discovered in the Galilean cave, are similar to those in the "caves of France during the Moustierian period. From this discovery we can say that in remote times the dominant type of mankind spread abroad from a cridle-land. The balance of evidence in this particulate case is in favour of Europe being the cradle-land both of the man and of his culture.

It is possible, but by no means proved, that Europe, as in modern times, led the world in thought and deed.

The present discovery makes certain that the kind of man who is led in Palestine at a very remote period was of the same type and lived in the same way as the type of man in Europe.

Sir Arthur, pointing to a patch in the skull, said: "It might be thought that the fellow had a knock, but I think it looks more like an ulceration which had been superficially scraped with a flint, in something like the way in which the South Sen Islanders do their form of trepanning."

That Galilean skull belonged to a species or type profoundly different in structure from all living races of mankind.

The young explorer had found another breed of Neanderthal man—a man with a narrow, high head and with flat cheek-bones, like a Chinese or a monkey. It was evident, however, that Galilean man, so far as size of brain was concerned, had reached the average of most living races of mankind.

We must not look on him as an ancestor, Sir Arthur concluded; he was a cousin, as it were, of our ancestor.
Sir W. Boyd Dawkins said he thought Galilean man was one of the links between homo sapiens and the higher apes and belonged to a division which he called "missing links."

## [10] CHIA DI AND ALVIC

Carles Casella of Turin is in the unusual position of being both dead and alive. His troubles began in 1917, when he was wounded and taken prisoner during the Caporato disaster, and was unable to give only account of himself during the next fourteen months. After the Armistice, he came back to Italy, rejoined the army and was duly demobilised in 1919.

Casella first realised his double existence in 1927, when, wishing to get married, he applied to his native place, Suluzzo, for the necessary documents. A note from the syndic informed him that, so far as Suluzzo was concerned, Ienrico Cassella had been killed at the front, and his name occupied an honoured place on the local war memorial. By personal intervention and by the help of further corroborative evidence, Cassella succeeded in persuading the authorities that he was sufficiently alive to take a wife, but he could not get them to remove his name from the memorial. They said it would not be the look of it.

Having secured his papers, Casella applied for the bonus of one thousand a/w, to which all ex-soldiers are entitled. Here again he found himself confronted by a statement of his own death and War Offices being notoriously hard to convince, he has only just managed to secure his money.

Casella said nothing of these things to his bride, as he thought she might not like the idea of marrying a man officially supposed to be dead. So it is easier to imagine than to describe her feelings when in 1922 she received a communication from the Ex-Soldiers Association asking for a photograph of the "dear departed" to place in the album of those who had fallen for their country. It took her husband some time to make her see the humour of the situation. Shortly afterwards, an aunt of Casella's was invited to go and receive a war medal to which her dead nephew was entitled. The dead nephew went himself to fetch the medal, but was not so foolish as to try and prove himself alive. He said he was his own brother, and the medal was handed to him with suitable words of sympathy.
Casella is a good-tempered man, and he had resigned himself to the anomalies of the situation. What has really proved the last straw and has induced him to make his wrongs public, is the fact that the tax-collector has found him out in his modest abode and persists against all official evidence in considering him as very much alive.
