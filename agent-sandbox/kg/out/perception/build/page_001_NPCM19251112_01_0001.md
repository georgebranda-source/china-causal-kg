## [13] REDS ADVANCE:

**ENTERING SOUTHERN KWANGTUNG.**

**HEUNGSHAN RECAPTURED**

Heungshan is now completely in Red hands again, the anti-Red adventurers having fled without being actually threatened by military force, on account of dissent among themselves as to sweets of office.

The Red expedition against Tang Pun-yan (Chan Kwing-ming's subordinate in the south) has actually penetrated enemy territory and taken Yeungehun, a town near the southern peninsula.

## [14] FENG AND CHANG

**THE LATEST FROM THE NORTH.**

**UAN CHI-JULA PRISONER.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

There is very little authoritative news from Peking, probably owing to the censorship.

“The North China Daily News” learns from private sources that a telegram received locally states that Tuan Chi-jui is virtually a prisoner in the hands of Feng-Yu-hsiang.

Reports state that Feng's attitude is still very contradictory but the general belief is that there has been a definite break between Feng and Chang Tso-lin, while Feng's most powerful lieutenant, the Honam tupan, Yueh Wei-chun, is reported to have definitely thrown in his lot with the allied forces.

## [15] OBITUARY.

**MR. T. S. FORREST**

A brief cable from Home announces the death of Mr. Thomas Shaw Forrest, known to his friends in Hongkong as "Jock."
Mr. Forrest retired from reasons of ill-health in 1919 from the service of Jardine, Matheson & Co., Ltd., being at that time in the private office.

From 1897 to the time of his retirement, Mr. Forrest had worked with the Company, his duties entailing his presence in Hongkong, Shanghai and other places in China. He first came East in 1897, joining the Accounts department of Jardine Matheson.

He will be remembered chiefly in Hongkong for his golfing capabilities, the Championship falling to him on more than one occasion.

His ill-health did not prevent him entirely from following the game at Home and his name was noticed recently in the St. Andrew's tournament at the end of the summer.

Mr. Forrest visited Hongkong in the winter of 1920-1.

## [17] China the Initial

**HONGKONG, THURSDAY, NOVEMBER 12, 1925.**

**PRAYA COLLISION.**

**FIRE ENGINE AND MAIL VAN**

**PEDESTRIAN SEVERELY INJURED.**

A collision occurred yesterday morning between a Post Office mail van and a fire engine which resulted in injuries to five persons, one of whom is now at the Government Civil Hospital in a critical condition.

The van was standing on the side of Connaught Road near the lane separating the Post Office building from the P. and O. offices. The fire engine with the usual crew on board turned from Pedder Street near the statue; and through some circumstance which is now being investigated struck the mail van in the rear. The force of the impact was so strong that the van destroyed an electric lamp post and ran a distance of about 30 feet before it was brought to a halt by striking the pillar on the pavement at the entrance to the P. and O. building.

The van being empty, and with

the brakes out of action, broke one of its front wheels in colliding with the pillar. The fire engine was completely disabled and very badly damaged. Special emergency equipment had to be brought to the scene, and the engine was taken across the harbour to the railway workshops for repair.

In addition to the Chinese pedestrian who was severely injured by the mail van in the course of its run, a Chinese officer on the engine, a fireman, and two others sustained minor injuries. The driver of the van who was on his seat at the time was not injured.

## [20] A NARROW ESCAPE.

**MACHINERY CATCHES WORKER.**

**DRAGGED BY THE HAIR.**

A Chinese girl employed at the Tai-Kuen Knitting Factory at Portland Street, Yaumati, had a narrow escape from death when she was caught by the hair and drawn into the machinery.
With remarkable presence of mind, a workman brought the machinery to a standstill, and the girl was extricated. She sustained injuries to her scalp, and was taken to the Kwong Wah Hospital, where she is reported to be progressing satisfactorily.

## [23] GOOD RESULTS.

**EXCELLENT POPPY DAY RESPONSE**

**$300 CHEQUE FOR ONE!**

It is impossible at the present time to give the separate or total amounts of sums collected as a result of the Poppy Day efforts for Ear Haig's fund, but the organisers have enough indications to justify them in stating that the sum is in excess of the one they expected under present conditions. The final total will, however, and perhaps naturally so, be less than last year's, which was regarded as very much in the nature of a "bumper" sum.

Boxes from ships and organisations are still coming in, as also are reports from the holders. The general trend of these is that money was “tighter” this year than last, and notes of high-denomination considerably scarcer. However one of the first poppies to be sold was in the Hongkong Hotel and a local gentleman signed a cheque for $300 for it!

## [25] SOVIET CIVIL CODE.

**PROPERTY RIGHTS RESTORED:**

*Moscow, November 12.*

The presidium of the Executive has considerably amended the Civil Code with a view to annulling restrictions as regards the right of inheritance, particularly the clause limiting the sum of inheritance to 10,000 roubles.

Property will only pass to the possession of the State in the event of the absence of a will and lawful heirs.
