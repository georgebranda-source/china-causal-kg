## [2] RESTRICTION POLICY.

### How It Has Saved British Industry. (Reuter's Service.)

*London, July 13.*

The effect on the rubber output of the present high prices was discussed in the House of Commons when Mr. Rhys Davies (Lab.) asked whether, in view of the large profits of the rubber companies, of which he quoted an outstanding example, would the Government reconsider its policy of limitation of production in the Malay Peninsula.
The Hon. W. G. Ormsby-Gore replied that legislation in the Malay Peninsula was framed for the control of export of rubber. It did not restrict production. There was nothing to prevent an increase in production to meet the continued shortage and in such an event, the quantity which could be exported under the minimum scale of duty would be automatically increased. The Colonial Secretary was not prepared now to suggest to the Governor of the Strints and High Commissioner of the F.M.S. that the present high price of rubber required reconsideration of the Restriction Ordinance. A sudden, unexpected rise would mean a greater amount of rubber released on August 1, but the matter could doubtless be examined if the price remained at the present extraordinary high level.

Replying to a question as to whether he would consider the advisability of increasing the percentage of increase on August 1 from ten to twenty per cent. the Hoff. W. G. Ormsby-Gore said that the Colonial Secretary alone was unable to do that—it was a matter for legislation in the Straits Settlements, the Federated Malay States, and Ceylon. We could not suddenly do it without creating a great disturbance.

Mr. Ormsby-Gore confirmed the suggestion of Mr. J. H. Thomas that restrictions had immensely benefited Great Britain, especially in her dealings with America. He expressed the opinion that it had probably saved the British rubber industry in Malaya. He recalled that the present price of rubber was very much below the 12a. per lb. reached years ago, and he regarded the present price as abnormal.

## [4] GOLD OUTFLOW.

**AFFECTING BANKING CREDIT.**

*(Reuter's American Service.)*

The outflow of gold, which totalled $175,000,000 (gold) in the last seven months, has begun to affect the banking credit. Commercial banks find themselves in a position in sharp contrast to that which they held since the Armistice. The result is increasing calls on the Federal Reserve System for credits.

## [7] “QUITE UNTRUE.”

**BRITAIN NOT HUSHING REPORTS.**

**AN EARLY SETTLEMENT?**

*(Reuter's Service:)*

*London, July 13.*

Reuter has been officially informed that it is quite untrue that Britain is endeavouring to suppress any report with regard to the Shanghai incidents not favouring British officials.

With regard to the general situation, the Powers continue to consult in regard to lines of procedure to be followed and the possibility and scope of a tariff conference provided by the Washington Agreement of 1921.

There are differences of opinion in regard to the relative positions of the Diplomatic Corps and the Municipal Council of Shanghai but an early settlement of the matter is expected. Meanwhile a reserve is maintained in regard to the supposed findings of the Shanghai Commission, which are still sub judice.

It seems clear the question of rights ought to follow the tariffs conference when the Central Government might have funds with which to carry on. Meanwhile though the Powers are generally agreed, no decisions have been actually taken.

## [11] CORRESPONDENCE

(To the Editor of the China Mail.)

Sir,—A coolie prosecuted by Mr. Wylie for leaving without notice has been ordered to forfeit his pay for the time he worked in June, to pay Mr. Wylie $14 his July pay in lieu of notice and to pay a fine of $20, or go to jail.

Law on this matter is therefore perfectly plain and let us where possible follow Mr. Wylie's example, or at least follow the lead of Hongkong and Shanghai Hotels, Ltd., by paying no wages for June to servants defaulting in that month, and should they return re-employ them or not at our option and upon our terms, they having broken their contracts and being without claim of any kind upon us.

I put it that anyone paying defaulting servants for June is a traitor to justice and to the workers of all nationalities now doing such splendid service for us. Let us be loyal to them and to our laws and government and we can have no regrets.

**DISINFECTANT SUPPLIES.**

(To the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir,—The Sanitary Department has instructed the public to disinfect dustbins to prevent fly-breeding. "This being a matter for each household, it is only right that the individual should provide the disinfectant. But in many streets in the Chinese residential sections there are mounds of garbage. To everybody it is plain that these heaps cannot be disinfected; if they could, they would be removed. In normal times the Sanitary Department gives away tankfuls of diluted disinfectant when the house cleansing season is on. I commend to the authorities the idea of putting up these tanks at street corners, so that those who will can draw disinfectant—and thus work on both the bin at home and in the street.

*Hongkong, July 14.*

## [14] JAIL FOR A BRIBE.

A Chinese woman was charged at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday with offering a bribe of $25 to Sergeant Major Li Sze with a view to being released after she was detained in connection with the murder of a man at Lung Cho Ching. The woman's defence was that she gave the money to the detective for safe keeping as she understood that all prisoners had to leave their valuables with the police. The Magistrate imposed a sentence of three months' hard labour.
