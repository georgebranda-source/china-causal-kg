## [1] CORRESPONDENCE

### Backward Hongkong

### [To the 12/1/2007 at the China Mail]

Sir, — Last Saturday I wanted to go to the Government School Sports at "Cheung Chau". I had not the slightest objection to spending a night or even the weekend there for the memory of happy times spent at Cheung Chau is still with me. The nights provided the house is favourably situated, are cool and the solubility of Cheung Chauites is too well known to need stressing byane. Become it was impossible for me to spend the night there, however, owing to reasons into which I need not enter, I had to give up the idea of going to the sports, the last launch leaving before the sports had hardly got into their swing.

The public to which I want to direct attention; however, is not that I was edged to miss the sports but the lack of enterprise on the part of the Government in not providing, or causing to be provided, facilities for getting out to places. Term suburban, whether they be on land or water, in the development of which lies the solution of many of the housing difficulty. To give another example, if I miss a train at an absurdly early hour I am unable to get out to Fading, Emo or any other off the places which are developing along that route, consequently the inducement for me to take up my permanent abode there is correspondingly less.

The Government is too short sighted in these matters of development. They do not seem to recognize how rapidly the Colony is going forward. Take the Kowloon buses for instance. It was surely not a far-seeing policy for give even in trial to such a system or lack of system as has been tobead for so long and the Government is now having to make namels in this respects.

It is in the hope that it may be led to realise its shortcomings in other respects that I have penned the above lines.

### Boxing Decision.

(To the Editor of the China Mail)

Sir,—The letter of your correspondent "Candid" is so full of inaccuracies that he is hardly worth consideration. To quote one instance, he says that Dupree won by a street, and in the same breath he says that the margin was small. "Surely these are contradictory. Then again in the details given in the letter he has much to say about Dupree's lefts. These did not worry Cartlidge in the least for all I could see," as after all they amounted to something like an old lady caressing a baby.

The consensus of opinion at the hall was that Cartlidge was the better man on the run of the fight. I was not guided by this when I wrote my impressions on Sunday: If “Candid” had read yesterday’s morning papers the would have seen the opinion expressed that Cartlidge was clearly the winner.

Not being a Peimanist, I have made no mental calculations, and I still adhere to the belief that the decision should have gone to Cartlidge.

## [5] $ ^{*} $ Bathing Scandal.

(2) the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir.—Is Repulse Bay a reserved beach or is it not? If it is, and I have been assured that it is on very good authority, what were cookies doing undressing themselves on the beach last Sunday about 5.45 p.m. prior to disporting themselves in the water?

It's a rotten thing to debar a fellow human being from the benefit of a cooling plunge, but the line must be drawn: somewhere and really the coolies, who came, I believe, from the different building operations progressing in the vicinity, were far from clean. Also, they are not the possessors of matsheds neither are there flocks behind which they can divest themselves of their clothing. On Sunday they were visible to many from the beach or matsheds and I think something ought to be done about it.

I understood that the beach was reserved and that the police were responsible to see that the restrictions were enforced. It would be rather a fiddling afternoon job for a police sergeant to be constantly on duty there but such scenes as were witnessed there last Sunday should be prevented from recurring somehow or other.

Yours, etc.,

*Hongkong, May 5*

## [6] MARINE COURT.

For loading kerosine on the Praya, a junk-master was fined $50 at the Marine Court this morning, with the option of six weeks' hard labour.
'Defendant was represented by Mr. T. G. Bennett who entered a plea of guilty.

Another junk-master who came before His Worship (Tient-Com. G. F. Hole, R.N.) also admitted a similar offence and was given the same penalty. He was fined an additional $50 (or six weeks) for attempting to leave the harbour without permission.
