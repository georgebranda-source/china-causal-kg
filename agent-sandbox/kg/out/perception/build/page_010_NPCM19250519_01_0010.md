## [3] there are more than a million Buicks

There would not be more than a million Buicks in active use today if Buick had not, through the years, produced a motor car of unvarying and superior quality. In every detail, every Buick is an example of how well a motor car can be built.

Mah Jong, says a New York message, has gone the way of all prizes. It has felt the "cast wind" of cross words, and now sets are selling at give-away prices. The last word is the bankruptcy of the £40,000 'Pung-Chow Company' in New York, the makers of Mah Jong sets.

Referring to the bamboo folding chair so defly made by Cantonese rattian workers, which is now being imported into England, a contemporary says it is nothing very extraordinary to look at, but folds up into quite a small space with all the neatness of a Chinese puzzle, although without any of its complications. There are a good many folding chairs of one kind or another, but the Chinese have managed to combine comfort and strength without adding weight.

The steady growth in the production of farm eggs in Canada is further emphasized by the figures issued by the Federal Department of Agriculture for the year 1924, which show that the output in that year totalled 212,648,685 dozens, of the value of $50,322,439, as compared with 202,116,208 dozens, of the value of $48,770,780, in 1923.

During the 1924 calendar year, 70,613 travellers and returning residents, excluding immigrants, entered Palestine, according to a report in the March issue of the government bulletin of the Palestine Government. This figure, it is added, surpasses those of previous year. The number of immigrants, the overwhelming majority of whom were Jewish, was more than 15,000 in 1924.

In consequence of a report that mining operations in the West Riding district of Yorkshire have caused subsidence extending over 3000 acres, a commission of inspection will shortly make a tour to ascertain the extent of the damage. The first prize ($1,000) for a practical peace plan for the Chinese Republic, which was offered by the “China Weekly Review” of Shanghai, in a competition, has been awarded to Mr. Chen Yueh-fu of Hangchow, Che-kiang Province, who was once a member of the staff of the Educational yamen at Canton. The Peace Plan considers the present situation of China from the standpoint of historical background, the origin of the present trouble, the evils of civil war, a comparison of China's status with that of Egypt, Indo-China, India and Turkey; gives three suggestions whereby foreign nations may assist, and closes with three definite suggestions to: the Chinese people for solving their present troubles. The three suggestions to the Powers are to stop lending money to China, stop exporting arms to China and to bring the unfinished provision of the Washington Conference into effect; the process for the Chinese people cover the subjects of militarism and salute in public, environmental autonomy.

## [13] GENERAL

On the last voyage of the Kalgan from Bangkok to Hongkong via Swatow, a Chinese coolie passenger committed suicide by jumping overboard and a passenger died of consumption.

Exports of potatoes from St. John $ _{10} $ New Brunswick, since the opening of the season to date show a considerable increase over shipments for the same period last year. Up to April 1, 1925, shipments totalled 729,327 bags and barrels, as compared with 327,762 bags and barrels in the corresponding period of the previous year. The majority of shipments were made to Havana and Bermuda.

That the horses that proved such great friends of man during the war should not be forgotten. Prof. Josef Limburg, a German sculptor, has just completed a beautiful monument in honour of the horses which fell in the war. It represents an unsaddled horse with sad and drooping head, and is a wonderful piece of art, done with much love.

A possible new market in England may develop for Canadian timber as result of the proceedings of the Housing Committee appointed by the British Government to investigate the housing problem. It was recommended by the Committee that the most suitable type of house was one of timber, ready-made, knocked-down and suitable for shipment to Canada, according to a despatch, for submission to manufacturers of wooden houses there who desire to offer supplies.

The first large batch of immigrants to come to Canada this year, amounting to over 1,000, arrived at Saint John and Halifax from England and the Continent during the last week in March. A number of these immigrants came out under the so-called 3,000 family scheme, which is supported by the British and Canadian Government. It is expected that some 1,000 families will enter Canada this year under that scheme.

A photograph was transmitted by telephone wire from New York to Chicago in seven minutes, and arrived back in New York by air mail in nine hours and seven minutes after it was received in Chicago. Officials of the General Electric Company, which conducted the test, said that the Chicago reproduction was a very good one, sufficiently clear for newspaper production.

Boring, dull and abominable were some of the terms in which Sir Alfred Fripp, the famous surgeon, described the cooking of the average Englishwoman. He was speaking at a reception at 10, Downing-street in aid of the Y.W.C.A. movement to establish a central hostel for London working girls. Sir Alfred said that after giving the girls fresh air and exercise he trusted they would be given the common inter-cultural cooking. It there was one king which made for the commonness of dullness and even boringness of an ordinary middle class little more than the showmable cooking of the Englishwoman then. It would be glad to know of it, he said.
