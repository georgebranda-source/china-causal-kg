## [13] WILL GO HALF WAY.

**RUSSIANS AND THEIR CREDITORS.**

Mr. Stanford Talbot has resigned from the executive of the Association of British Creditors of Russia of which he was the first President. He intends to form an Anglo-Russian Conciliation Association to protest against the negative policy adopted to Russia. He is convinced that the Russians are willing to go over half way to meet their British creditors.

Mr. Talbot says that the British Government, the Association of British Creditors of Russia and the majority of Anglo-Russian industrial, merchant and banking interests have been systematically trying to drive the Anglo-Russian team with the cart before the horse, Russia must be helped if they want her to help them.

Mr. Talbot holds the opinion that it might be an agreeable surprise for British creditors if it could be made possible for the Soviet to be invited to state what it is prepared to do for creditors if a British loan of £100,000,000 could be made to Russia on a fair security.

## [16] MEMORABLE GATHERING.

The warmth of the Prince of Wales's welcome eclipsed all South African traditions. Every window and balcony was crowded; the occupants adding their quota of roaring cheers to those of the massed crowds in the streets.

A luncheon in the City Hall at which the Mayor and the Council officially welcomed the Prince was attended by 500 guests.

This was the most representative gathering in Capetown's history.

The Mayor dwelt on the assurance of a loyal reception for the Prince all over the country.

The proceedings concluded by the Assembly rising and singing, "For He's a Jolly Good Fellow!"

Among the addresses presented to the Prince of Wales on the grand parade this afternoon were those of Indian, Chinese, Malay and other Moslem bodies, as well as the “Cape coloured” committees.”

These thronged the outskirts of the grand parade and formed a vast concourse on Green Point common where the Prince reviewed the coloured Church. Lads' Brigade.

Everywhere the coloured population demonstrated its loyal enthusiasm.

*Shanghai, April 30.*

T.ie Chinese steamer Shun Chong, owned by the Yung Foong Company, bound for. Wenchow from Shanghai, has been wrecked off Tungchi Island, off Haimen. The accident occurred in a dense fog. Reports received to-day state that all hands were saved. The rescue was performed by a party of Chekiang water police, summoned to the scene by distress signals. The loss is estimated at one million dollars. The vessel's compradore is reported to have died from injuries received while rushing on deck, while the vessel was sinking.
