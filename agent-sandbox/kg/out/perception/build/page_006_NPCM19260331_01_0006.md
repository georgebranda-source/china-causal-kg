SMALL INVESTORS.
Carlyle's dictum that England is a place inhabited by forty millions of people—mostly fools needs alteration. Mostly thrifty would be heater the mark. For according to Mr Walter Runcman, there is a wider distribution of wealth in Britain than ever before £2,000,000,000 represents the savings of £16,000,000 “capitalists.” Of this sum £285,000,000 is deposited in the Post Office Savings Banks £228,000,000 in National Savings Certificates £198,000,000 in small Government holdings £470,000,000 in ordinary and industrial Life Assurance Funds £140,000,000 in Building Societies and £300,000,000 in other approved Societies. Stories of war-time squandering are still current—of people who suddenly receiving large sums of money indulged in orgies of piano-kicking and champagne drinking. That is all past—it seems to be a question of their Horatio

Local stateidians spare us information of the saving propensities of the people of Hongkong—Chinese and foreigners. It would be interesting to have some knowledge of our "small investor." They are not encouraged—unless the lure of it is strong—to indulge in share transactions as Hongkong knows it—which of course cannot be described as saving, and if they save at all, either remit Home, or place their money in the Savings Bank or on Fixed Deposit — both excellent things. The small investor should be encouraged. He should be encouraged to take a financial interest in the place in which he lives. Perhaps when the time arrives to float public loans to cover capital outlay on local improvements, residents might be given an opportunity to subscribe to them. Investors would have the backing of the Government, and would feel that their money was in safe custody and doing good. Such a plan would assuredly appeal to those Chinese with small sums at hand on which they would be satisfied with a moderate return. The suggestion seems worth consideration.

## [7] ACCIDENTS.

**LAUNCH COLLIDES WITH A JUNK.**

**NO CASUALTIES.**

Among yesterday's accidents was a collision in the harbour between a passenger junk and the steam launch "Vancouver" which fortunately was not attended with more serious consequences than damage to the junk, assessed at $120.

At the time of the mishap the junk was crossing over from the Kowloon Godowns and the launch was proceeding in the opposite direction.

A Chinese climbed on to the roof of No. 313, Nathan Road, Kowloon, with the intention of committing a burglary, but fate sent him headlong to the ground.
He was found in the morning in a helpless condition and sent to the Government Civil Hospital.
"Motor Mishap."
The driver of a private ricsha has reported to the police that while he was proceeding along Queen's Road East a motor car travelling in the same direction struck the back of his vehicle and overturned it. The ricsha was empty and the coolie was not hurt.

A Chinese woman admitted to the Kwong Wah Hospital accounted for injuries to her head by stating that she was attacked by her husband with a chopper. The assailant is reported to have absconded.

A Chinese boy suffering from a dog bite was sent to the Government Civil Hospital for treatment. The animal, owned by a Chinese, was taken to Kennedy Town.

New York, March 80.
Another onslaught of selling on the Stock Market continued all day long, being specially directed against the leading railroad shares, which reached new low levels for the year, the declines ranging from one to fifteen points.

Another drive against Hudson Motors brought down them five points to a low record of 72%. The calling in of bank loans continued on a large scale. The day's trading exceeded three million shares for the third time this year.

Rugby, March 30.
A decrease of $31,443 in the number of unemployed is shown in the latest weekly return. Considerable satisfaction is derived from the fact that the total figure of unemployed is now a little over a million. It is actually 1,890,400.
On January 4, it was 1,259,900. It has fluctuated around this figure of one and quarter millions for the last three years and its reduction below 1 million will be something of an event.
