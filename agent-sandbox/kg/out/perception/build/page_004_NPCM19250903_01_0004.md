Although in practice it appears to have given rise to certain irregularities, the Mixed Court has a solid enough legal basis for its existence in the Regulations governing the foundation of the Settlement—these, in fact, providing that the municipal authorities "shall arrest offenders of the public peace, hand them over and prosecute them before respective authorities, Chinese or other as the case may be." An argument often advanced against this practice is that a settlement for foreigners is not the proper place for trying one whose Government is outside that Settlement; but it must be borne in mind that punishment, although indicated, is not carried out in the Settlement and that whatever anomalies the working of the Mixed Court gives rise to (divided consular jurisdiction appears to be held chiefly responsible) some laws must be made, respecting the conduct of the ever increasing numbers of Chinese who since the Taiping rebellion have found protection within the Settlement borders. Similarly, if there is to be smooth working within, the Chinese committee which already advises on matters relating to the government of the Settlement must be replaced by actual Chinese representation on the Municipal Council. Far from being a disturbing element in the present negotiations the association with requests along these lines by responsible British bodies in Shanghai should be all for the good and should lead to the creation of a more favourable atmosphere for the hearing of other matters of more immediate importance.

## [16] THE PACIFIC.

How are we to describe this immensity which men call the Pacific, how deal in terms of the common speech with its uncomprehended might, with its vastness as of infinity itself? asks an American writer. And yet the inspiration lies in the sublimity of its boundless distances, in its unplumbed depths, in its many moods! Balboa, gazing long and silently from his "peak, in Darien" at the wonder of the new-found ocean, felt that inspiration, yet knew it to be too lofty for human expression. For it was made up of many elements, indistinguishable, yet each born in the glory of the unknown eastern sea. And these seemed to the discoverer to combine their power in such an appeal as never yet had found its way into the heart of mankind, and in response to which he could offer only homage and awe, reverential and silent.
Countless leagues of distance, thousands of fathoms of depth, these have little significance for the wanderer of the Pacific; but its many moods, varied as those of the Adriatic, capricious as those of Geneva, reveal to him always something unexperienced and unforecasted. Sometimes it is as gray as the Channel off the South Foreland, ominously restive as the Bay of Biscay beyond Ushant Light. Its billows roll as high as December seas off Hatteras and as menacing, and the skies above it hold clouds lowering and grim as those of Cape Horn. But again, among those mysterious and never-failing trade winds which fill one with an insatiate longing for the unknown, with a craving for high adventure beyond the far-flung horizon line, the ocean of Balboa and of Magellan and of Drake grows tranquil as a forest lake, gleaming smooth as a mirror and over it like black specks dart the flying fish plunging into the even surface with little jets of spray only to come forth again everything and pirouetting, and sucrating. Above float the fleecy trade clouds, and at a window that is also along the horizon, their golds and their roses, and their morales and their onslas seeming to be on the one side of the

ports of all the oceans. Magellan blazed its trails as the pioneers marked out the forests of the western world; Drake, seeking new lands for his sovereign, followed across the vast and desolate sweeps; Wallis and Vancouver; Cook and Bougainville; Kotzebue and d'Urville and Porter; came after; and then the whalers, swarming forth from little New England sea-ports like bees from their hives, spread over ten thousand uncharted leagues in pursuit of the giant animals of the sea. And all men who traversed the mighty ocean knew its influence, for amid the vastness that seemed of infinity itself the glory of the great Pacific had lain upon their senses.
