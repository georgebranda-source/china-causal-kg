## [11] SHANGHAI TROUBLES

The foreigners were depending their lives against a nurderous mob. I cannot admit that the responsibility rests upon the British authorities, who were unable to obtain prompt and adequate protection from the Chinese authorities, and were obliged to act for themselves. I cannot remain from reminding your excellence that I have repeatedly warned the Chinese government of the grave responsibility they are incurring by allowing the present anti-British agitaiton to proceed in checked. The deplorable events at Hankow are evidence of the justice of my warnings; I have instructed the Consular officers throughout China to do everything to prevent a recurrence of such incidents, but I must earnestly beg your Excellency to take energetic measures to discourage any agitation which leads to such dastrous results. I had the honour to show you at our interview last evening pauphicles and newspapers giving the most mendacious accounts of the happenings at Hankow, making it appear that my com-ptriots had fired upon a harmless student demonstration. I myself observed this morning in the streets of Peking a poster conveying the same absolutely false impression. I regard the dissemination of these earnings as dangerous in the extreme, for the people's disposition is uncertain, and one explosion may well be followed by others with deplorable results. I, therefore, trust that your Excellency will take early opportunity for issuing a statement which will place the Chinese public in possession of the real facts, and thus dispel an atmosphere of prejudice and misunderstanding created by these false rumours.
The note is signed by Mr. C. M. Palabet, C.M.G., British Charge d'Affaires.
