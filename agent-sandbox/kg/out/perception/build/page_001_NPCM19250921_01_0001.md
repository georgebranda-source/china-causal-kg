## [6] ANTI-RED.

**CHIEF CHANGES COLOURS.**

**CANTON CHAMELEON**

The Ying-pak, notorious as one of the first of the late Dr. Sun Yat-sen's disciples to make use of Labour and as one who took a leading part in the 1922 Seamen's Strike, is now reported to have renounced the Canton Bolsheviks and to have declared himself as a thorough anti-Red enthusiast.

Comment has been made before in the “China Mail” about the absence of his name from the present list of Canton notables, now that the so-called labour leaders have come into power. Light is thrown on the mystery by an exclusive article in the “Kung Sheung Yat Po” which states that Tse, with the influence of over 200 organisations, societies, guilds, etc. behind him, has thrown in his lot with General Chan Kwingming.

Disappointed at not getting the military governorship of Canton in the early days of the Revolution, Tse saw the power in the labour movement and espoused it for years until the advent of Soviet "advisers" in Canton. When he fled to Peking he is said to have been in dire poverty. His "kit fant" wife and a concubine were left without means. In Peking he subsisted by contributing to the "Min Pao" till Eugene Chen's arrest. Now, says the vernacular paper, he is gathering around him the more moderate of the older Kuomintang members, to make a stand against Bolshevism and for Chan Kwing-ming. ## [7] NORTH POINT FIRE

**PRINTERY AND GARAGE DESTROYED.**

**CONSIDERABLE DAMAGE.**

The Central Fire Station received a call at 8.25 p.m. last night from North Point and proceeded there with the Wanchai equipment.

The building involved was a one-storey structure which was occupied by the Man Wah printing works and also served the purposes of a motor garage.

An employee of the printing works who slept on the premises stated that shortly after a motor car had returned to the garage an explosion was heard, which was followed by an outbreak of fire.

Owing to the inflammable nature of the contents, the fire spread rapidly and the building was completely gutted. The Fire Brigade worked for one and a half hours when the stop signal was given and the danger of the adjoining houses being involved was removed.

The contents of the printing works and the garage, which contained about ten motor cars, were entirely destroyed.

The actual amount of the damage has not yet been ascertained, but it is stated to be considerable.

Shortly after 10 p.m. yesterday, a call from Reclamation Street, Mongkok was attended. The fire originated amongst a heap of empty cracker cases and other rubbish stored on a first floor verandah. Not much damage was done as the fire was quickly put out by the firemen with the use of hand buckets.

## [9] Unust

**A DIRECT LOAN?**

**CHINESE MERCHANTS AND "TIGHT" MONEY.**

**AN AMENDED PETITION:**

A well-known Chinese merchant with whom a "China Mail" representative conversed this morning, said that some such measure as the proposed loan in connection with which a petition was to go before the Government, was looked upon as the only means of bringing to an end the present state of affairs under which the Banks would advance no money on local security; no matter how high value the latter was.
Upon enquiry the “China Mail” was informed that the petition was not yet before the Government. As a result of other enquiries it was learned that the text which had appeared in the press was only provisional, it not having at the time been submitted to the Government, and that as a matter of fact it is at present undergoing important amendment.

Under the original text the petition was to be for the Government to stand security for bonds on local property, etc., carrying with them a certain amount of interest to be redeemable within a certain period. As it was afterwards doubted whether the creation of this gilt edged stock would lead to a release of money (the attitude of the Banks has been that there is not the money in hand to advance, no matter what the security) it was felt that some amendment was necessary and the matter is now under consideration. It seems probable that application will be made for a direct loan from the Government.

## [10] SOUND ADVICE.

**COOLIE GUILD NO. 1'S ADDRESS.**

**THE TRUE LABOUR SPIRIT.**

Sound advice was given by the chairman to members at a meeting, on Saturday night, of the Tung Tak Coolies' Guild (second branch) at Yaumati.

Addressing the forty members present, the chairman exhorted them to be diligent, to work in peace; not to take notice of intimidation and not to allow themselves to be made the tools of unscrupulous parties.

The speaker also expounded the benefits of thrift and drew attention to the necessity of supporting the guild by prompt payment of subscriptions.

## [11] BANISHEE CHARGED.

**EX-CONSTALABLE AGAIN JAILED**

A Chinese ex-police constable was the defendant at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning on a charge of returning from banishment.

The accused had been in the Hongkong Police Force and deserted nearly two years ago. When arrested and charged before the Magistrate his record was found to be very unsatisfactory and he was sentenced to six months' hard labour. The man was banished from the Colony on the completion of the sentence.

The accused had no explanation to offer on the present charge and was sentenced to undergo one year's hard labour.

## [15] SWATOW POSITION.

**TROOPS REPORTED LEAVING WAICHOW.**

**OTHER WARLORDS MOVE:**

Following the surrender, of Swutow to General Chau Kwingming's forces, military developments are expected at Waichow city, a strategic point in the East River region, dominating the march to Canton.

General Young Kwan-yu, who has been a staunch subordinate of Chan has, for the last few months, been nominally under General Hsu Shung-chi of Canton. Canton is now reported to be suspicious of Young's intention to march out of the city. Ostensably he is to attack somebody, but the supposition is that he will rejoin Chan now that the latter is moving.

Further news has come to hand from Swatow. It appears that operations were on a small scale, the Canton soldiers fleeing in face of the inevitable. The coup was preceded by wholesale commandeering of motor shipping and tow boats. Once the initial success was gained, a large number of pro-Chan commanders joined in the offensive against Swatow, which is now entirely in Chan's hands. The last report is that Chan's various contingents are now extending their spheres in the districts further up country.

Interested people predict that the Swatow development may have far-reaching consequences extending over large areas in China. During the week-end vernacular papers received disquieting reports of troop movements by different Yangtze warlords. War in provinces, other than Kwangtung, is not totally unexpected.

## [16] CHINA'S CUSTOMS.

**MR. CHAMBERLAIN'S**

**BRITAIN'S SYMPATHY.**

*(Rauter's Service.)*

In a letter to the British Customs delegation to China, before the latter's departure, Mr. Austen Chamberlain enumerates that the objects of the Conference are to prepare the way for the abolition of Likin and, to fulfil other relevant conditions, laid down by the Washington Treaties; to determine conditions whereon the Customs surtax up to 2½ per cent. on ordinary goods, and five per cent. on luxuries, may be levied pending the abolition of Likin.

## [17] STREET ACCIDENTS

**EUROPEAN MOTORIST INJURED**

**SKID NEAR TSIN WAN.**

A number of accidents occurred during the week-end, necessitating the removal of some persons to hospital, but none of the accidents was attended with serious consequences.

Near Tsin Wan, on the Castle Peak Road, a Chinese woman suddenly stepped in front of a motor cycle driven by European Sergeant Harle yesterday evening. In attempting to avoid striking down the woman, the machine skidded and the rider was thrown to the ground. Mr Harle was hurt in the knee and both legs and is now in hospital. His condition is not considered to be serious.

A Chinese youth, employed as a telegraph messenger, fell from a push bicycle at the junction of Queen's Road and Wyndham Street on Saturday night. He sustained injuries to his face and arm.

A motor lorry got out of control whilst being towed on the Kowloon City Road on Saturday morning and ran into a drain. A coolie who was working in the drain was slightly injured.

A tram car which turned in from the Praya on its way to Happy Valley ran into a stationary motor car which fouled the road. The motor car received a little damage.
