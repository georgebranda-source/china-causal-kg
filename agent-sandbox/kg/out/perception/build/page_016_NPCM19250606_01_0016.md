*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

*KIUNGCHOW, May 1*

The latest development in the Hainan situation is the clan fighting between the Limkos and Hakkas in the western part of the island. The Limkos are a LoI tribe, aborigines and semi-time, and far outnumber the colony of Hakkas, which has its centre south of Namoa, at Namfong. The trouble started about the first of the year when a band of Hakkas and official soldiers burned eight Limko villages which were known to be robber villages. The Limkos, of course, had to retaliate, and to date it is said that over forty villages have been razed. The Hakkas have been the more aggressive. The official soldiers who were in the region have withdrawn, saying that they were few to cope with the situation. But now Gen. Teng. governor of the island, has dispatched three ying of troops under Tang Hoang Tsiang, (regional protective police) to Nodoah which is the most important town of the region, with orders to settle the Limko-Hakka war. ## [23] The Byers City.

<sub>section under [22] CLÁN FEUD.</sub>

"Southern Star," published an official announcement of the settlement of the Byers' case a few days ago. It stated that the American Government had demanded $50,000 indemnity for the murder, but in view of Hainan's poverty, this had been reduced to $20,000. This was paid over a month ago and a receipt for the money was published in the paper. Ho Lih-tsiang, who was in command of the troops at Kachek, at the time of the murder last June, has been deprived of his official titles until he succeeds in arresting the murderers. A good many troops have been dispatched into the mountains to hunt out the bandits, but there has been no report on their success or non-success. Chen Ki-zi, a People's Army leader and Sun's adherent whom some Chinese say was responsible for Mr. Byers' death, is still at large in the mountains engaging in raids. The head-bandit, Lim Si-biau, who is also accused, has not been caught, although there is a large price on his head. Several American missionaries have returned to Kachek and are slowly opening up the work. The compound is being carefully guarded.

## [24] PRISONER PLAINTIFF

**WINS-ACTION AGAINST LANDLORD.**

A prisoner from Victoria Gaol made a successful claim for $500 yesterday in the Summary Court. The action was brought against his former landlord on the grounds that the money had been paid to him as a deposit against rent.

The defendant admitted that the chop on a receipt for the money was his chop but denied that the signature was. He had never had the money and was it likely his solicitor (Mr. Leo d'Almada) asked, that a man would pay such a large sum some months after going into a tenement and without a lease or agreement.
His Honour Mr. Justice Wood gave judgment for the plaintiff, however, holding that the onus to prove the signature was not valid was on the defendant.
Mr. G. R. Haywood appeared for the plaintiff.
