## [5] PIRACY MEASURES.

**COMPULSORY WIRELESS REGULATIONS**

**INSURING OF OFFICERS.**

### Inter-Departmental

Regulations requiring that ships must carry wireless and owners must insure officers are foreshadowed in a report which has been made public of an Inter-Departmental conference on Piracy in waters adjacent to the Colony of Hongkong.

The Conference was held some time ago but hitherto its recommendations have not been made public. Apparently the Home Colonial Office forwarded the report to the Headquarters Secretary of the Mercantile Marine Service Association and from that source is acknowledged a synopsis of the recommendations contained in a copy of the "Manchester Guardian" of April 10 which has arrived in the Colony by the latest Siberian mail.

The report is the outcome of reportations made by the organizations of shipmasters, navigating and engineer officers in regard to the measures adopted by the Governor of Hongkong for dealing with piracy in waters adjacent to the colony following a series of piratical attacks which had been made on ships sailing between Hongkong and neighbouring Chinese ports.

The organisations, according to the synopsis made several suggestions to cope with the piracy, including:—

1. That all arms and armed guards required by the Piracy Regulations be removed from every vessel, and the absolute command thereof restored to the master, as laid down in the Merchant Shipping Act, unless some better method is devised.
2. There shall be at once inaugurated a "proper system of patrol in the danger zone.
3. That all vessels subject to Piracy Regulations shall have wireless telegraphy installed and under the control of a competent operator signed on the articles.
4. That insurance be automatically effected for every master, officer, and engineer whose duty takes him into the danger zone.

The Committee, after exhaustive inquiries, now recommend that the fitting of some form of wireless apparatus and the carrying of trained operators should be made obligatory on all ships subject to the Piracy Regulations; and that as a corollary the Admiralty should be asked to consider what steps should be taken to render the naval vessels patrolling the danger zone continuously able to receive distress calls from river steamers.

They also state that the responsibility for making provision for the dependents of their officers should rest with the shipowners, and not with the Government; but, failing the shipowners instituting a system of voluntary insurance, they are of opinion that the Hongkong Government should consider the question of making it obligatory on them either to insure their officers adequately or to defray the cost of an adequate Government scheme of insurance.

## [6] POLICE MEDALS

**INSP, MURPHY TO BE DECORATED.**

Detective Inspector T. Murphy is to be decorated with the King's Police Medal, according to reports in Police circles.

It has been decided, so the report goes, that on Monday, May 18, His Excellency the Governor (Sir R. E. Stubbs) is to be present at the Central Station where Inspector Murphy is to receive his decoration.

## [11] PIRATES SHOT.

**KONGMOON RAID**

**TROUBLOUS TIMES.**

*(From Our Own Correspondent.)*

PAKKAI, KONGMOON, May 3. Three pirates who took part in the attack on this port on China New Year eve have been captured. They were sent down by train from Kongmoon and shot outside the Pakkai railway station.

This community is once more under ample protection. The Merchants' guards are once more on duty; their support is contributed to by passengers on Hongkong-bound steamers and subscriptions from local residents. Li Yat-ngok, formerly Hokshan magistrate and now a brigadier under General Leung Hung-kai, commands the new garrison here.
Although it is premature to consider details of His Excellency's actual departure it is an interesting fact and worthy of note that the final 'touches are being put to Statue Pier,' and upon enquiry, the "China Mail" was informed that it was anticipated that the pier would be ready for use on the occasion of His Excellency's departure. The actual pier is completed and the only work remaining is the polishing up of the stone archway under which cars will be enabled to drive.

In revenge for the shooting of three bandits convicted of attacks on the railway, other outlaws have made several attempts to capture that part of the Sunning Railway at Sanwuf station but were driven off. Every night the railway guards are on the qui vive.

Noting the progress as Shekki following the demolition of the city walls, the magistrate at Sanwul wishes to copy the example set him and pull down the walls at Sanwul city. The merchants and residents have protested on the grounds of the danger of bandit attack, and the prevalence of pirates.

A strike and stoppage has started at Chekhom in protest against the “nationalisation” of private property by Government notification. An appeal has been made to the Civil Governor.

## [12] SIR E. STUBBS.

### Proposed Farewell

### Functions.

**COLONY'S TRIBUTES.**

**STATUE IER TO PLAY A PART?**

It has been made known officially that H.E. the Governor, Sir Edward Stubbs, is leaving the Colony at the end of the next month and does not anticipate returning. Regret has been expressed publicly at the inability of the Secretary for the Colonies to succeed to the request of the Unofficial members of the Legislative Council that His Excellency's term of office should be extended and publicity has been given to the arrangements being made by the Chinese community to mark the occasion of His Excellency's departure by an appropriate presentation as a token of esteem and respect.

The “China MaiN” understands

that the Chinese admirers of Sir Edward Stubbs' administration are not alone in their resolve that before, departing His Excellency shall have some tangible recognition of his services to the Colony. Arrangements are being made for the holding of official functions at which Sir Edward Stubbs will be the recipient of presentations representative of the whole community. It would appear that the ladies of the Colony are equally resolved on organising a special presentation to Lady Stubbs.

## [15] WAS IT FAIR?

**WHAT DUPREE SAYS.**

Was the decision given out at the conclusion of the Cartlidge-Dupree fight the best result possible? If not, what would have been the best decision?

These questions were uppermost in the minds of the "fall house" that saw last Saturday night's main contest.
Various runnours were current this morning, one being to the effect that one of the judges gave his verdict. "Dupree won," the other judge "a draw" and, the referee's final decision was, therefore a draw.
Seldom has a boxing contest created such large interest. Those who long for an immediate return fight are doomed to disappointment. On inquiry from Mr. J. Eivook, manager of the Boxing Association, a "China Mail" reporter learned that Andre Dupree sailed by the Siberia Maru at noon to day for

Shanghai. In parting, Dupree said that he was not in the least worried by the decision. However, he was keen to come back and fight a return match with C. P. O. Jim Cardlidge, the local lightweight champion, providing the Boxing Association put up the contest.

When asked if Dupree mentioned any terms, Mr. Brock said that Dupree said nothing. Presumably, he would be willing to box again on the same terms.
No fight has been fixed up as yet for Dupree in Shanghai and his visit is for business reasons. After Captain Matty Smith fought. Rufe Canioy in Shanghai, the Municipal Council is stated to have withdrawn the boxing licence. Accordingly, it is not known if Dupree will be able to meet an opponent in public.
It is also possible that Dupree will visit Manila where the Olympic Club are after his services. Manila has already offered him a purse of $2,000 and it is likely, especially after his showing here, that Manila will concede the difference, as they can find the money. Should Dupree go to Manila, he may also return here if the return bout is to be staged.
During his brief stay here, Dupree has proved himself a class boxer and a gentleman. Cartridge showed that he had lost none of his skill and, in spite of his inactivity for such a long period, proved that he is by no means past his best and will continue to be a big abraction in the ring for a considerable period.

## [16] CHEUNG CHAU NOTES.

**GOVERNMENT SCHOOL SPORTS.**

### -

The chief event of the week's end was the third annual sports meeting of the Cheung Chau Government School, which passed off very successfully. The weather was all that could be desired not unpleasantly hot and the rain did not fall till late in the evening. There was a large attendance of visitors, most of the foreign residents were present and some of the visitors as well, members of the local Kai Fong and many of the parents and friends of the schoolboys came to shew their interest in the proceedings.

The entrance to the Ball Ground of the C. C. Residents Association was graced with a floral archway surmounted by the School flag. The Volley ball posts were converted into flag poles for the Union Jack and the Chinese flag with wreaths of flowers and streamers of smaller flags Seats and refreshments were thoughtfully provided for the visitors and on a table were displayed the prizes for which the students were to compete. The starting of the 22 events were in the able hands of Inspector Lane and Mr. Lam Pak-to.

The Judges were Messrs. L M. White, Lo Ip, Lau Wai tai, Lai Nan-sang, Tsang Pak-hong, Lo Mo-to, Lau King chi; these gentlemen had a busy time owing to the very close finishes of many of the events.

All the events (with the possible exception of the lantern race which on account of the breeze, difficulty was experienced in lighting the candles, and some finished with their lanterns in flames) were keenly contested, especially the Hill race, Team race, and the Tug of war. After the last event, the visitors and boys gathered round the

prize laden table, where were displayed silver cups, penholders, choosticks, also in kittens, found in pens, fans and other articles of a useful nature. Mr. E. Ralphs then called upon Mrs. Arnold Jones to distribute the prizes to the various winners, whose names were read out by the Headmaster. The School champion, Fung Wa-hin will have his name inscribed for the second time on the cup, having gained that distinction two years ago. Mr. C. G. Alabaster on behalf of Mrs. Arnold Jones (who was presented with a large bouquet of flowers by the school champion) thanked the students for the privilege of having been asked to carry out the pleasant task. Mr. Alabaster also noted the improvement in the arrangements for carrying out the various events and in the keenness among the students, and hoped the future gatherings would be even more successful. Mr. Ralphs after thanking various gentlemen who had worked hard to make the sports a success, called for three cheers for Mrs. Arnold Jones, Inspector Lane, Mr. Whyte, and the judges, cheers were also given for the members of the Kaifong, to those who had contributed to the prizes, fund the visitors. Mr. Alabaster, and the Headmaster. The proceedings having concluded the gathering dispersed.

The Kung-Yik is supposed to report for duty in about five days' time.
Mr. and Mrs. Anderson who have been living in Dr. Heanley's house have returned to Hongkong.
