*London, June 6.*

*Eshowes, June 6.*

Mr. Austen Chamberlain, speaking at a dinner to the visiting American and Canadian doctors yesterday evening, referred to the progress in the study of "tropical disease." He remarked that Hongkong, which was once regarded as little short of banishment on the way to eternal departure, had become one of the plums of the Colonial service, thanks to scientific discoveries and preventive measures. The same thing was happening elsewhere.

A wonderful sight arranged here for the benefit of the Prince of Wales consisted of a war dance by 5,000 picked Zulus in war-paint and fethered headdresses, creating a weird and impressive scene. With characteristic stops, cries and gyrations they moved with raised shields, creating an impenetrable barrage of metal and musale, emitting demonlacal yells, to within a yard of the royal party and terminated the outburst with dirge-like singing, the waiving of knobkerries and fierce stamping accompanied by the plaintive singing of 200 young girls.

## [7] CITY FIRE.

After leaving Eshowe the Royal train had been rounding a sharp curve of a long descent from the top of the hills when two of the front wheels of one of the rear coaches left the rails. The train was travelling slowly and stopped, almost simultaneously. No damage was done though the delay has necessitated minor modifications of the programme.

A fire occurred on the second floor of No. 20, Queen's Road East, near the junction of Arsenal Street, at 4:30 p.m. yesterday. The Fire Brigade made a prompt appearance and their efforts prevented the lower floors and adjoining houses being involved, but not before the contents of the second floor had been destroyed. Some damage was done by water to the household effects on the lower floors. It is understood that the building was insured with the Wing On Company for $2,000. The fire is believed to have originated in some electric lamp fittings.

A. Chinese who attempted to commit suicide during the weekend by jumping into the harbour from the praya wall was rescued by another Chinese and handed over to the Police.

New York, June 6.—A six-day heat wave brings reports of scores of deaths in the east and middle west. Government departments in Washington closed down early this afternoon owing to the impobscibility of working in the torrid atmosphere. Twenty died through the heat in Boston where the mercury was dallying between 98deg. and 100deg. — Reuter's American Service.

Berhm, June 6. Thirty-one out of thirty-four machines have arrived, completing the third lap of the round-eignannay flight. None of the Baby machines completed the lap. Thirty-two melchines, including six Fabies, have started the fometh lap. Reuter.
