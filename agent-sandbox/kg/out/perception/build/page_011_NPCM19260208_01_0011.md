## [10] ASAHI

LIBERAL STAND.
(British Wireless Service.)

In his speech yesterday in the House of Commons, Mr. Lloyd George stated quite clearly the extent to which Liberallism and Labour might co-operate.

The debate on the Address-in-Reply to the King's Speech had roamed widely over the question of "Public ownership and democratic control of essential services" demanded by Labour amendments.

The substance of Mr. Lloyd George's statement was that if by co-operation between the Liberals and Labour is meant the swallowing by Liberals of every form of nationalisation from the production of food to those industries which supply clothing and many of the necessaries of life and if by nationalisation is meant State control of banks and credit, and generally of all the activities of industry, then Liberalism can find no common ground of action.

Liberalism stands for enterprise, and initiative. If co-operation means the strengthening of initiative where private enterprise has failed by the resumption of State ownership in the cases of land and electricity, here the parties can fight the same battle.

The result of the debate was the defeat by 299 votes against 112 of the Labour declaration in favour of the public ownership and democratic control of essential services.

Mr. Lloyd George referred to a suggestion by a Conservative member that a conference of all the parties should be held on the subject of unemployment. With this he expressed hearty agreement.

## [11] WIRELESS IMPASSE

*Rugby, Feb., 6*

It is learned in connection with the wireless operators' strike that a deputation of members of Parliament representing each party in the House who had interested themselves in the dispute waited upon the President of the Board of Trade and the Minister of Labour yesterday afternoon.

The Government representatives stated that the employers had sent a communication stating their willingness to negotiate, providing the men returned to duty, and if the negotiations were unavailing, to submit the dispute to arbitration.

The Deputation is urging the men's officials and the men to accept this suggestion, and it is hoped a settlement will be arrived at.

*(Router's Service.)*

*London, Feb. 6...*
