## [10] EGYPTIAN DECREE.

**SAFEGUARDING PUBLIC SECURITY**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

Pilmy Pasha Issa, Minister of the Interior has stated that the subjected of Political Organisations Law was to safeguard public security, eliminate harmful societies, and to organise the political life of the country.

There was no intention whatever to interfere with the rightful liberty of the people. All must obey the law, but requests for delay in regard to complying therewith would be generously considered.

## [13] SEAPLANE'S FALL.

**NARROW ESCAPE FROM DEATH.**

**AVIATORS' EXPERIENCE**

Lieut. Mackintosh and Flying Officer Phillips, the two officers in the seaplane which crashed into the harbour off West Point early yesterday morning were both injured, one but slightly, the other sustaining injuries to his back.

Dragging operations which have been proceeding since shortly after the accident have resulted in the recovery of most parts of the plane although after its submersion and the effects of the fall from a height of from 800 to 1,000 feet it is not thought that anything other than the engine will be of much use.

At the time of the accident the seaplane with two others, all of the bombing type, were returning from escorting the s.s. "Kalyan" on which was the new Governor of Hongkong. His Excellency Mr. Cecil Clementi, C.M.G., and by some means or other two of the planes changed their relative positions with the result that the plane on which were the two officers mentioned got underneath and near to the other. The effect on the elements of a plane's passage is naturally at its strongest underneath, a not inapt comparison being that of the wake of a ship. The inevitable consequence of the change of positions was that the former plane side slipped and fell.

At the time the water was reached control had almost been regained by the pilot, the plane being almost righted after its vertical drop. The water was struck with terrific impact and a huge wave swept the decks of the s.s. "Tean" between which and the "Ninghai" the plane fell with not a lot of distance between each.

As communication by wireless when the 'planes are out of sight and by observation otherwise is always kept with H.M.S. "Hermes," the accident was noted aboard and a ship's pinnace and police launch were soon on the spot. Both officers were found to have freed themselves from their trappings, the emergency accomplishment of which can be achieved in one action. They both kept afloat and when the pinnace and launch arrived were found to be safe in sampans. They were conveyed to H.M.S. "Hermes."
