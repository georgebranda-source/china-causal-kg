## [5] WEDDING AT HOME.

**INSPECTOR D. MACDONALD MARRIED.**

**PRETTY CEREMONY.**

"Friends of Inspector D. Macdonald, of the Hongkong Police Police Force, who is spending his leave in Scotland and is due back next month, will be interested to hear that he was married on July 15 to Christina, elder daughter of ex-Baillie Norman Macrae and Mrs. Macrae, of Craig Road; Dingwall.

The ceremony was performed at the Castlehill United Free Church, Forres, by the Rev. Ronald Macdonald, M.A., of Dingwall Union Church, assisted by the Rev. R. G. Macdonald, M.A., and the guests were principally the relatives of the bride and bridegroom.

Beautiful wedding music was played by Mrs. Douglas, of Torres, the Second Paraphrase being sung at the close of the proceedings.
The bride, who was given away by her father, was handsomely attired in a gown of broche crêpe de chine and silver brocade, caught at left side with large diamente ornament. Her long bridal veil was edged with rare lace and crowned with orange blossom, and she carried a bouquet of white roses. She wore a long string of pearls—the gift of the bridegroom—and silver shoes.

Miss Atta Macrae was principal bridesmaid. She wore a frock of shell pink crêpe de chine embroidered in mauve and gold, with cape of mauve georgette, and large hat.

There were four dainty attendants—the Misses Grace and Mary Mackay (niece of bridegroom), Miss Jessie Macdonald, U. F. Manse, Dingwall, and Miss Mary Black, of Edinburgh.

The bridegroom's brother, Mr. Duncan Macdonald, of New York was the best man.

Luncheon followed at the Royal Station Hotel, where the toast of the young couple and the bridesmaids, was drunk, and they had a hearty send-off on their departure for Cornwall for their honeymoon.

## [6] PRISONER'S ESCAPE.

**CHINESE ELUDES INDIAN CONSTABLE**

**KOWLOON COURT INCIDENT**

A mild sensation was caused at the Kowloon Magistracy at about noon yesterday when it was announced that a Chinese prisoner had escaped.

The man, who had several previous convictions against him, had just been sentenced by Mr. E. W. Hamilton to six months' hard labour on a larceny charge, and was being conducted to the cell by an Indian constable. Before being placed there the prisoner obtained permission from his guard to enter the lavatory, which is on the ground floor on a side of the building. The constable's attention being attracted elsewhere at the main entrance, the prisoner jumped over the short wall on the other side and escaped. Seeing that the prisoner was a long time in coming back, the constable went to investigate but he was nowhere to be found.

The constable is now in the custody of the Police for the negligence.

## [8] STRIKE CHANGES.

**THE BEGINNING OF THE END?**

**GENERAL LI TAKES "OATH."**

Said to be commanded by Russian officers, Chinese naval vessels are patrolling the river and approaches at Canton. At night there is marked activity. It is not known if the precautions are a sequel to the recent Bolshevik coup.

While the “Canton Gazette” writes about the severance of steamer communication to and from Macao, in support of the boycott of Hongkong, a Canton vernacular paper, at a subsequent date, reports as follows:—As conditions in Canton are abnormal the Strike Executive has decided to give permission to all or any persons to leave for Macao or Hongkong, provided they give a written undertaking to return within a week.

This is in direct variance to the

policy professed all along by the agitators. Some think that it may be the beginning of the end. Having been so busy in the last week the Canton Government may be finding the strikers too heavy a burden to bear.

There is still little traffic from Chinese ports to Macao but military leaders in the vicinity are making fortunes by escorting trading junk, passengers, foodstuffs, cargo, etc., to Macao.

No change has been reported at Canton. General Li Fook-lam is stated to have taken the oath of allegiance to the Kuomintang, or rather the Red party. Hu Han-min is believed to have been removed from Whampoa to Bocca Tigris. Further inland than Kongmoon, the anti-Bolshevik troops of General Leung Hung-kai are moving southwards from the Cadets and it is predicted that they will eventually co-operate with Chan Kwing-ming's army in the outskirts of the province. Rockefeller Jr., has been very active in philantrophic work.

## [16] MR. E. F. L. STURDEE

**POPULAR SHIPPING MAN**

**LEAVING.**

The Colony will shortly be losing a well-known shipping man, Mr. E. F. L. Sturdee, the Canadian Pacific's General Passenger Agent for the Orient, who is leaving on transfer to the head office in Montreal owing to indifferent health.

Mr. Sturdee came to Hongkong in February of 1924, succeeding Mr. P. D. Sutherland who also left the Colony on transfer to the head office in Montreal.

Mr. Sturdee expects to sail by the Empress of Canada on September 4, Numerous friends here will wish him the best of luck and a speedy return to health in Canada's more benign climate.

Mr. Sturdee is being succeeded here by Mr. E. Stone, late General Agent, Passenger Department, Canadian Pacific, in Shanghai who has arrived here from the North.
Mr. Stone first came to the East in 1917, and after being stationed at Yokohama, returned to Montreal in 1919. He came out again at the beginning of last year.

## [17] NO GERMAN EXODUS.

**FACTS CONCERNING TUNG ON PASSENGERS.**

**RETURNING BUSINESS MEN.**

The return from Canton to Hongkong aboard H.M.S. Tung On of passengers of German nationality who were not taken up by that vessel has led to the assumption that there has been an exodus of German business men from Shameen.

A “China Mail” representative who made enquiries this morning was informed that the numbers of Germans returning to Hongkong from Canton was but few, and that in nearly every case they had previously gone up from Hongkong, by train or other means as in view of the responsibility that the naval authorities had to assume for passengers on the Tung On, it was difficult to get a permit to go up unless one had exceptionally pressing business. It was less difficult to get a permit for return at Canton.

A German business man who returned to the Colony from Canton yesterday assured a "China Mail" representative that German residents' safety and their businesses were affected in the same degree as British, but there was no talk of an exodus.

As regards the general position in Canton, the “China Mail” informant could say little “apart from confirming the news that Li Fuk-lam's forces had submitted to the Red regime which appeared to be established as firmly as anything could be said to be in Canton. There was still no communication between Shameen and Canton, he stated.

## [18] HOME CRICKET.

**COUNTY CHAMPIONSHIP DECIDED.**

**YORKSHIRE TOP AGAIN.**

As will be seen elsewhere in this issue, Yorkshire have won the county cricket championship again. This is the fourth year in succession that Yorkshire have been champions. On other occasions there was little doubt about their superiority but the competition has been very interesting this year.

Hongkong took an added interest because in cricket at Home was aroused by the wonderful feats of Hobbs, Sutcliffe and Tate in Australia during the winter and the feat of Hobbs a short while ago, of beating two world records.

After a great struggle Surrey are second. Lancashire were "tipped," early in the season by "Plum" Warner, a former England captain but they disappointed. Still they have the satisfaction of getting the better of two drawn matches with the champions—a feat not accomplished by any other county.

Notts and Kent started badly but recovered. Middlesex are the only county beat Lancashire twice but they fell to some of the weaker opponents.

Yorkshire are the only team not to have been beaten outright, truly an excellent record and ample testimony to their all-round strength.
