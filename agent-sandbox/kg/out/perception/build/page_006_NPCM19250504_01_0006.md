## [14] FRANCE AND MOROCCO.

Although to-day's cables from Morocco report that the Riffs have "infiltrated" into the French zone, the position need not be viewed with any due alarm. Marshal Lyantey is confident he can deal with the trouble with the troops at his disposal. The intentions and dispositions of the French Government, there is every reason to believe, are as pacific towards the Riffians as they are conciliatory towards Spain, and considerate of her susceptibilities. France, it is true, maintains her legal interpretation of the Franco-Spanish Agreements of 1904 and 1912 as investing Spain with the duty, as well as the right, to control effectively the Moroccan zone entrusted to her protectorate, i.e., to ensure by a military occupation that no troubles shall occur within that zone such as might entail any danger to the adjoining French zone. Juridically, therefore, France would claim for herself the right to occupy militarily any areas evacuated by the Spanish army with resultant difficulties to the contiguous frontier regions of the French Protectorate.

But this claim, which is contested by Spain on the ground that the latter's Protectorate can be legitimately exercised without necessarily involving a complete military occupation (as a matter of fact, within the French zone the military frontier does not everywhere extend as far as the political frontier), is to be upheld, for the time being, rather on abstract than on practical grounds. Sensible.

Shanghai—or a firm there—sets an example that might well be followed by business houses in Hongkong. Messrs. Jardine, Matheson & Co., Ltd., have decreed that during the summer months their business hours are to be 8.30 a.m. to 12 noon and 2 p.m. to 4.30 p.m. The summer is as trying in Shanghai as it is in Hongkong. An early start, an extended tiffin interval and an early cessation of work probably means greater productivity although the actual working hours are less. A good start is the main thing and a man is more likely to be eager for his work at 8.30 a.m., than he is at 9 a.m., be the difference only thirty minutes. It may be that productivity is not the thing aimed at, but simply a realisation that Europeans work at a disadvantage in the torrid, or humid conditions of a Far Eastern summer, a recognition of that fact, and the introduction of a system that aims at a condition of things which shall be to the advantage of the European and a means to strengthen his reserves of health. It is about time such a sensible system be adopted by Hongkong business houses.

## [16] May Day:

It is not clear why the organised forces of Labour have selected the first day of May on which to celebrate their existence and to voice their grievances—fancied or real. The Morris pole is, presumably forsaken, and processions of drabness and speeches of spleen, take its place. London's labourists march to the Bulgarian Legation and protest against the alleged persecution of Bulgarian workers—a persecution which is denied. Moscow's "joy" is more militaristic than Labouristic. There the garrison is reviewed, 50 aeroplanes participate, the while members of the Government and Diplomatic Corps look on. Canton provides the humour, although it is stud and sorry stuff. "Down with foreign religion," and "Down with capitalism" are the cries, whilst there is a word for the "progressive Soviet." The Cantonese dearly love a procession. The one at Canton was three miles long. There is something pathetic about these processions—in London, Moscow and Canton. We do not seem to have progressed very far even in China with its boasted civilisation, when a three-mile long procession can give vent only to parrot like cries the purport of which, it seems very likely, at least one and half miles of the procession does not understand.

## [23] ANIMALS AT THE OPERA

from a recent issue of the Straits Times:—

Sir,—We are alas, some what accustomed to lax manners in the audiences at the Victoria Theatre but I venture to suggest we have about reached the limit when animals are allowed inside at an operatic performance. The admirable performance of La Traviata was marred by the yapping of a pet dog carried by some one in the stalls, and the chattering of a pet monkey brought by a lover of music into the circle. In addition those in the circle were compelled to listen to the incessant buzz of conversation which hardly ever died away throughout the entire performance, and which emanated from one corner of the circle only.
In a London theatre one could complain to a stalwart attendant who would speedily amend matters, but in Singapore one can only suffer in silence.—Yours etc.

Come, gentle tripe, the hungry
carter's joy.
