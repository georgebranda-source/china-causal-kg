To those not fully acquainted with the Company, it may be explained that the chief business is the disposing of and acquiring small lots of shares for the interests of clients. The Company also negotiates the transfer of houses, land and property as will have been noticed by its various offers in the local Press. As the majority of investors are able only to purchase in small lots and as these small lots do not interest the ordinary broker of Hongkong the consequence was, that until the formation of the Small Investors in Hongkong, nearly three years ago, the investor in small shares—and this means the average person—had no broker or firm specialising in meeting his or her needs. To cater specially for the small investor the Company was commenced and it has had the pleasure of serving innumerable clients since that time with such satisfaction that many of the Company's clients have been the means of introducing also from one to five new clients. ## [13] Great Increase.

<sub>section under [12] HONGKONG COMPANY'S FACILITIES:</sub>

Since the above appeared in the "South China Morning Post" on February 8, 1924, we understand the Company's volume of business transactions for a recent month amounted to $245,000 and the number of clients has increased 400 per cent.; a fair proportion of these are in Macao. There are still a good number of investors for whom the Company has not yet been privileged to act. With the object of obtaining a larger proportion of these investors a local-limited company is now in the process of negotiation to be known as the Small Investors' Shares Real Estate Co., Ltd. The limited company will follow the procedure adopted by the old company for the last three years except that certain small lots of shares will be purchased for the Company's own holdings from a variety of sound local companies yielding satisfactory dividends and bonus. These profits will be collected by the Company and in turn paid over to shareholders. The great advantage of such a system is that instead of an individual buying a particular share which may not yield satisfactory returns he will, as a shareholder of the Small Investors' obtain dividends from the majority of companies in which the firm have invested. In other words all his eggs will not be in one basket so that if one particular basket drops he will still (given a hundred baskets) be 99 per cent. to the good. This method is long tried and successful as is proved by the success of investment companies in Britain. Moreover such companies are able to furnish unbiased reports, reference to any particular share, held by its shareholders and it is the intention of the Small Investors to furnish such reports.

Local shareholders will naturally, by doing business with the Company, be helping their own interests as the brokerage fees they pay for the acquiring or disposal of shares will eventually be returned to them in the form of dividends and bonus.

## [15] New Capital Required.

It is in consequence of the quickly growing clientele and in order further to facilitate the transfer of small lots to and from clients as stated previously (it is the future intention to purchase outright a large proportion of the small lots offered) that further capital is required. In the past the Company has not been in the habit of buying outright but has communicated with various of its clients until the particular lot offered has been accepted. Furthermore, as many small lots are frequently offered during the course of one day it has not been always possible to dispose of a lot on the same day that it was offered. As it usually happens that clients have urgent reasons for selling investments, such as a departure for Europe, or drastic need of cash, the delay in disposal frequently proves a hardship to the client; hence the desire to prove of greater service and assistance to all customers by purchasing outright small lots only; this will have the added benefit of enabling the Company to have always on hand small lots for ready disposal. Furthermore, the firm will continue as before to add the small lots together for disposal in larger lots. For instance, lots of 100, 150, and 250 could be disposed of as one lot of 500, which enables the obtaining of a better price for such a lot.
