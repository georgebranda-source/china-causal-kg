## [16] THE PIPE IN POLITICS.

The British Prime Minister knows an anodyne for the worries provoked by a brilliant Chancellor's silk stocking budget, the temperamental incompatibilities of members of the Irish race, the magical appearances of foreign communists in Glasgow and the falling French franc. Mr. Baldwin smokes a pipe. With fine fervour he praised it when he received the Freedom of Dundee. He said he had always found that pipe smokers had a wisdom, calmless of outlook and breadth of vision which were denied to cigarette and cigar smokers. This unwarranted reflection upon fellow-worshippers of "My Lady Nicotine" will be answered, no doubt, by the cigar smokers and the cigarette lovers. It is sufficient here to concede the philosophic importance of the pipe in politics. One can imagine Mr. Baldwin quoting, with a fine air of detachment.

If you climb the ladder of politics, where
Whoso ascends breathes difficult air;

## [17] *(no headline: column 3)*

And, being highest of men at the time,

Are slightly elate with your seat sublime,

A little apt at yourself to wonder,

And mistake your own bray for real thunder;

Think how rockets rise and how sticks descend—

Put success in your pipe and smoke it, friend.

Here is "wisdom calmness of outlook and breadth of vision." In that wide vision lies the soul of content, particularly in politics, since the name of an irritating heckler in Parliament can be substituted for that of King James in such adapted lines as these:

How sweet for me to know King James, who never smoked on earth,

Is smoking down below.

Probably the heckler likes his pipe, too, and sees similar pictures in the blue mist above the bowl. So Prime Minister and heckler wreath their vengeances in vapour—and their angers are dissipated in pacific puffs.

## [18] Honneur Aux Dames!

Men occupy the centre of the stage in the present production of what may be termed "Strike and Strike" with an indefinite run before it. The limelight is on the men and on the whole they are standing its glare very well. But the work that women are doing is not to be despised even if it is not patently apparent. The general outcry against servants is dictated, unconsciously or otherwise, by the extra-labour, worry and inconvenience, which the ill-natured actions of servants has thrown on the shoulders of many women in the Colony. Households have been left where there has been, and is sickness and where the needs of quite young children have been ignored by the abscending ones. It is in such cases as these that the boss of the house—the man—has risen to the occasion and seen to foodstuffs, and taken a hand in washing up, in addition to doing some piece of Volunteer work outside. Generally he has been heartened by the splendid uncomplaining fortitude of his helpmeet, where the contrary attitude might reasonably be expected. In the ranks of single women, the readiness to be up and doing has not escaped observation. It has been less spectacular than war work, but in that it is aimed at intolerance, selfishness and feelings that know no law of common sense, it is equally valuable. The Colony is not unmindful of the services that women, young and not so young, are performing so willingly, so ungrudgingly and so efficiently.

## [19] The Magnificent Nine.

"The European population increased by nine, the total" white population being 2,240, as against 2,231 for the previous year. During the same period the coloured population other than aboriginals decreased from 1,043 to 1,018." These doleful sentences, indicating Australia's "effective" use of an area of 335,116,800 acres, appear in the report for the last year of the Administrator of the Northern Territory (Mr. F. C. Urquhart). Thirty-four years ago there were 670 Europeans in the Territory; therefore, the average net increase over so long a period has been 46 Europeans yearly. Here is tragic evidence of neglect, a big chink in the armour of a White Australia. To the Northern Territory and to the other vast sections, east and west, of tropical Australia the world looks whenever high politics involve considerable spaces. If the North were fever-smitten and unhealthy like Sierra Leone and British Guiana, the Commonwealth might defend the dearth of Europeans as dictated by nature. But the far North is not unhealthy; it is merely unpeopled because the fleshpots tended in southern capital cities have proved too enticing. The Commonwealth has written "Thou shalt not" across the immensities of the North and, secure behind the defensive arm of Britain in the lists of the outer world, has built factories, made farms and perfected channels of commerce in the south of the continent. This condition of affairs cannot persist unchallenged for ever. Populations jostle one another in foreign countries and a title to unsued land is insecure. The Commonwealth Government's new proposals for developing the North will come before the present Federal Parliament. If these proposals have life and are not merely a grandiose reproduction of the policy which has laid a dead hand upon the Northern Territory they will be welcome as long overdue.

## [22] FATAL RAINS.

### -

Stubbs Road was blocked in several places by landslides early this morning but P.W.D. coolies were put to work and a clearance has been effected as far as Gough Hill (the lower side) where further work is temporarily held up by a large landslide.

A hundred men are at work there clearing away soil and debris and it is hoped that traffic will be able to get through later in the day.

The road has also been breached near the Peak Hotel (not far from the new flats) and the old Chamberlain Road has to be used, the lower road being impassable. Work of clearance is in progress here, too, and it is anticipated that traffic will be able to get through later in the day.

## [23] Railway Held Up.

<sub>section under [22] FATAL RAINS.</sub>

'New Territory. residents had the somewhat unpleasant experience of having to walk for some distance this morning in the course of their journey from outlying districts to Kowloon.

The Kowloon-Canton Railway was blocked over a distance of 200 feet owing to a landslide between Shatin tunnel and Ho Mun Tin and passengers had to walk round the gap and transfer to an emergency train (a small engine with a few carriages).

Feet Deep.

A large landslide at Victoria (old Jubilee) Road has necessitated the closing of this, too. The fall has occurred at the junction of the road with Sassoon Road (that running down to the Dairy Farm).

Another affected to a considerable extent is Shaukiwan Road, slides having occurred near Quarry Bay and Sai Wan Ho. Tramcars have had to stop running. In places mud and water are 4 to 6 feet deep.

Street Scenes.

Practically the whole of the roads in the lowest parts of the city were flooded at one time or another this morning. People going to work by tram were often caught while leaving or going to a pavement as inches of water had piled up at most corners. In places where steep gradients lead into the main arteries, the flow of water was similar to a rapid on a river, accumulated refuse, loose building material and other stuff being washed right down to the sea-wall and into the harbour.

On the tram-line side of the Central Market water stood higher than the pavements. Those who passed along Jubilee Street or Queen Victoria Street had to wade through water which rose above the ankle.

With a fairly strong south-west wind blowing sheets of rain could be seen carried along the main routes. Cataracts spouted from housetops and street crossings were like confluences of minor rivers. As late as nine o'clock motor vehicles and trams had to proceed with headlights as it was very difficult to see through the blinding rain which generally met one slatewise, full in the face.

That part of Queen's Road Central outside the Queen's Theatre was not nearly so badly affected as on previous occasions. Evidently, shopkeepers had been prepared for the deluge and they had kept the flood out by putting up their barricades.

This morning the road on the other side of Po-Hing Theatre, Yaumati, looked more like a small river than a road, the water rushing down the sides several inches deep, while near the Orient Tobacco Co., the water was half way across the road. The buses kept running with the water over their wheels in places.

Berlin, July 16.—The German draft of reply to the French Security Pact. Note was drawn up by the Cabinet yesterday. Strict secrecy in the matter has been maintained. The newspaper assert that the reply declares that Germany is ready to join the League of Nations on conditions especially referring to article sixteen of the League of Nations Pact, namely the right to march across Germany on request of the Council League, also the evacuation of occupied areas as provided by the Versailles Treaty. The reply emphasises that Germany is greatly interested in the acceleration of the security question but says that the French Note has considerably expanded the basis of the German memorandum of February 9, introducing various new juridical questions, for example, sanctions which require elucidation before the Conference can be held. The reply is to be published on July 21 simultaneously in Paris and Berlin. There will be big debate in the Reichstag thereon on July 22. Reuter.

## [25] LOCAL BOXING. SEARCH FOR RIVAL TO CARTLIDGE.

**WHAT ABOUT JOE COLE?**

-

Now that Andre Dupre has gone to Sun Francisco—and, incidentally, has been defeated by Jim Cartridge—another likely opponent for the local champion has appeared on the horizon.

When the strik is over, the Boxing Association may stage a "get-together" tournament even though the season is closed. Chances of match promoting will be enhanced if the major part of the Fleet is congregated in Hongkong.

Joe Cole of California is the man about whom tidings have been sent to Mr. J. Brook, the manager of the Hongkong Boxing Association. One of four young American pugilists who are fighting their way into the limelight and who arrived at Manila a short time ago, Cole is spoken of as a very promising boxer. The other three comprise Jimmy Darcy, a welterweight, Billy Leonard, a 140 lb. man with seven knock-outs to his credit and Harry Tunney with a record of 18 U.S.A. fights and only one defeat.

Cole has figured in 14 main events, some of which were at important California centres. On February 10 last, he decisively, bent Mike Denton at Oakland.

## [26] COAL CRISIS

**POLICE CALLED TO DEAL WITH STRIKERS.**

**GLAMORGAN COLLIERY INCIDENT.**

Five thousand strikers surrounded the Rock Colliery at Glynnenth, Glumorgan, where seven hundred men are still working and refused to disperse.

A hundred police who were summoned came into collision with the strikers.

Sixteen were injured.

The miners eventually decided to cease work and the colliery is at a stand-still.

Hankow, July 16.—In connection with the recent report in the vernacular papers that Wu Pei-fu had sent a military officer to interview Tuan Chi-jui regarding the various diplomatic problems resulting from the Shanghai incident and also the statement that Wu Pei-fu was thought to be proceeding to Hankow and when necessary to Tientsin to confer with Chang Tso-lin and Feng Yu-hsiang to discuss national defence against England and Japan, Wu Pei-fu states that such are absolutely groundless and likewise that letters and telegrams alleged to be sent out by him in connection with the Shanghai, Hankow and Canton cases are also fabrications.—Reuter.

Peking, July 16.—It is reliably reported that fighting has begun at Huayin and Shensi between forces belonging to General Sun Yueh, Civil Governor of Honan, and those of Wuh Sin-tien, the Tupan of Shenzhi. Tuan Chi-jui has wired instructing Sun Yueh to withdraw, but it is believed that the latter is unlikely to comply. The Honan Tupan Yush Wei-chun is reported to be supporting Sun Yueh with the object of getting him out of Honan.—Reuter. A message from Peking via London stated: Civil war has again broken out in Szechuan province where General Yang Han is attacking rival generals near Chungking.
