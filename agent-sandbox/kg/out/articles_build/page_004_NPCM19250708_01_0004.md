**LOTION**

An infallible remedy, affords immediate relief and effects a speedy cure.

Just Arrived

association of a lion with the tribe of Judah is, of course, as old as Genesis, but in other ancient lands, in Assyria, for example, and Mycenae, lions were ever emblems of royalty. That the ancient Kings of Scotland were descended from the Kings of Judah is doubtless as true as that England was colonised from Troy. Let us also believe the other legend that Charlemagne, who had nothing to do with either Scotland or Judah, conferred the badge of the lion rampant upon the Scottish Kings. But, although men have used beasts as their standards or totems from the dawn of time, we do not find our European Kings and Princess bearing arms till the twelfth century. The first English King whose great seal shows his shield emblazoned is Richard I. and he bears a lion rampant. The three lions presentгдант on the modern Royal Arms, which some of our revenues, like Napoleon, call "leopards," seem to have come from Normandy, Maine, and Aquitaine, one lion from each province. The Scottish lion rampant giles, armed and langued azure, was adopted a little earlier than our Richard Cosur de Lion by King William the Lion, who claimed the ancient earldom of Northumbria. The badge of the Earl was a lion rampant, and it has been thought probable that King William adopted this as corroborative evidence. But the theory of course does not invalidate the ancient legend. The Kings of Judah may just as well be connected with the Kings of Scotland through an Earl or two of Northumbria as by any other lumber mortals.

Thousands of Chinese owe their protection in the past and in the present to the Union Jack. There is no more benevolent colonising Power in the world than the British. The Empire has been rather slightly mentioned as the refuge of political exiles from outside: in that description may be found a*genuine compliment to the tolerance of the British—to their justice and to their fair-mindedness.

Propaganda

So long as an individual or a section is peaceable and law-abiding their nationality matters nothing. There will be the same degree of security, for him or it under the British flag as for the true-born Briton. It is as well that in a time of emergency this fact should be appreciated by the Chinese in our midst who are at present engaged in the performance of work of real service to their own and other communities. Their example must act as a powerful deterrent to their weaker compatriots. But—there is room for further propaganda, and no pains should be spared by those in authority to ensure that such propaganda is created and fostered. The ways of the intimidators are silent, and secretive. They can be countered by equally unobtrusive methods. Those who are weak and timid must have the truth of the position brought home to them with candour, always keeping in the forefront the prestige of the Colony and of the great Empire of which it is an important outpost. If that propaganda is wisely and discreetly carried out, it should then become an easy matter to separate the Chinese sheep from the Chinese goats—the loyalists from those who are ungrateful for the protection afforded them in the past.

Bolshevised.

Bolshevism, in common with other movements, had its own opportunity to justify itself. But its beginning was bad and its continuation in "ways that are dark" has deceived no one but the upholders of it, and in time will deceive those Chinese who seem to see in it that equality which levels everything. In all that it has touched, Bolshevism has defiled. Nothing is sacred to it. Ideals go by the board. Morals, ethics and religion are unknown, unless it be a brand emanating from the high priests of the movement. The work of men's hands and the fruits of their imagination are subject to molestation, even when the work is that of one who owns not Russia as his abiding place. ## [12] TROUBLE BREWING.

**POSSIBLE CANTON OUTBREAK**

**MILITARY RIVALRY**

That all is not well, internally, with the present Canton Government is evident from intelligence brought down by people who have left for Hongkong since the alleged attack on Governor Hu Hanmin.

It is an open secret that several of the leaders, civil and military, do not see eye to eye. General Hsu Shung-chi is said to have made overtures to one of Chan Kwing-ming's subordinates, rather than let territory slide into the hands of General Cheung Kaisek who commands the Cadets and to whom is attributed pronounced Soviet leanings.

As yet there is no trouble on the surface at Canton. Nor can it be predicted with any degree of certainty how events will pan out. Chan Kwing-ming's name is again prominent but gossipers probably mean his active lieutenants who are now in the field for themselves. The pro-Chan influence is stated to be widening in the hinterland of Swatow but Hung Leuk and Hung Shiu-lun are also alleged to have quarreled.

Taking all in.all, there may be cause for surprise in the not distant future as political agents are very busy at the moment.

YEARS AGO. probably would be able to tell off hand what feature of Pharaoh's throne would have been manufactured until exceedingly recently in practically the identical manner that it was 3,000 or more years ago. Yet the answer is simple, for the gold leaf which gilded that chair of state was obtained by virtually the same hammering out process as is the gold leaf hitherto used on the store windows of the twentieth century. But now electrochemistry has entered the field, a method having been invented to obviate the great amount of labour necessary heretofore. Thus, upon a ribbon of silver is electrically deposited a film of gold. This silver ribbon is then laid upon a celluloid band and immersed in a bath of nitric acid. The gold leaf is thus left on the band, whence it is blown by warm air between protecting sheets of paper. Though crude plans of manufacture may survive for centuries, it would seem that they must inevitably go down before the onward march of time.

## [17] THE WEMBLEY LION.

The House of Commons is much occupied with definitions. This zeal we applaud as conducive to an accuracy not hitherto found among politicians, observes a London paper. But there is a certain diffusion of energy. Not content with investigating what silk is and what, if any, is the limit to the number of ways in which it can be artificial, honourable members have gone off after lions. There was one who felt that his country expected him to inquire whether the Wembley lion was the lion of Judah. The bearings of this observation probably lie in the application thereof, as Captain Bunsby would have said; but we have no notion what mordant satire is intended. Another honourable, gentleman, however, was incited to declare that the lion of Judah is the Scottish lion, and the Minister who had to deal with a situation like that must have the sympathy of all humane people. Mr. Samuel boldly committed himself to the opinion that the lion of Judah is the Palestinian lion, such a creature, we presume, as Bensiah slew in a pit on a snowy day, and his information is that this creature is 'rounder and shorter in body and shorter and curlier of mane than the Wembley lion.' If this sort of thing satisfies those who believe that the Kings of Scotland were descended from the Kings of Judah and inherited the lion of that tribe, we shall be gratified but surprised. It seems safer to rely upon the undeniable fact that neither the lion of Wembley nor the lion of Scotland nor any other heraldic lion resembles an animal found in Nature whether among the faunts of Palestina or elsewhere.
