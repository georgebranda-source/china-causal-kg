## [14] CORRESPONDENCE

### Drenching Street Passengers.

(To the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir,—It is from personal and eye-witness experience that when any one passes along a Chinese street he is often subject to receiving a downpour of dirty water, some kind of dirt or spittle, etc.

But now I regret to state that this happens not only in the Chinese streets but also among European quarters. I have seen a man drenched to the very skin while he was passing along the Praya East at the building adjacent to the Royal Canteen and at the same spot at another time another man also was supplied with a plate of something like macaroni soup. There are many more cases, which, when put down, will fill many columns.

Personally I was once drenched at the Post Office Tram station and another time at the Butterfield & Swire Building. For all these instances the culprits seemed to get off scotfree, because if the sufferer were to go into the house or building to inquire he would certainly receive a hot answer or might even be charged with trespassing. For the welfare of the public I would like to call the attention of the government to inflict a severe punishment to those uncivilized culprits when caught.

Again I wish to point out that Hongkong is scarcely anything more than a granite mountain. Consequently, houses are generally built on terraces. Therefore masters and mistresses of those concerned should take more care of their servants and not to allow them to throw down any rubbish to a lower terrace, for that will not only give extra trouble to the servants, but also make the place very unsightly. At the present time many roofs are decorated with mango fruit stones, etc. If the fear of punishment by the Government or the horror of being trented alike does not force them to take more care of their servants, let common sense and humanity induce them to do so.

In conclusion. I'have the honour to thank the Editor for thus encroaching so much space in his valuable paper.
