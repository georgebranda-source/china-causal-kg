## [5] NEW DRY CHIEF.

An attempt to bring more order into Prohibition enforcement, by the appointment of a new head of a unified service, is announced. Colonel Lincoln Andrews becomes Assistant Secretary at the Treasury in place of a lawyer previously in charge. Andrews's job is to unify the Customs, Coast Guard, and special Prohibition organisation.

The nature of the fight before him is indicated by an instruction to the Commander of the "Dry Navy" at Atlantic City to "shoot first" and ask questions afterwards, when dealing with suspected craft refusing to heave to. Prohibition vessels have been painted "invisible grey" as a result of brushes with smugglers' motor-boats, which travel at over 40 knots.

Geraldine Farrar, travelling to Montreal in a sumptuous sleeping car, was held up on the Canadian frontier. Forty bottles of champagne and whisky were discovered inside a piano and behind ventilators. Miss Farrar remained in bed, and was allowed to continue her journey.

Men on the Atlantic coast, it is said, are fired on by boats at sea and from motor-cars on land, and are living practically in a state of war.

The substitution of a soldier for a lawyer as head of the Prohibition forces is not expected to mean more rigour, but a policy of concentrating on the big offenders, similar to that adopted in New York State in padlocking the large restaurants.

Another side of the problem is illustrated by a seizure of contraband liquor from a concert singer's private railway saloon.
