## [15] WORLD OF SPORT.

**TOKYO OLYMPIC.**

**1929 MEET TO BE IN**

Future Far Eastern Olympic Games will be held in September, not in May as at present, according to a decision reached at an informal conference between the Japanese and the Chinese athletes on their survival in Manila.

It was also decided that the 1927 Olympic Games should be held in Peking or Shanghai, and that the 1929 meet should be staged in Tokyo. After that year it was suggested that the Games should be held once in every four years, not in alternate years as at present and that therefore the first meet after the coming into force of this new rule should be held in 1933 in Manila.

Regarding the coming Games the conference considered the proposal offered by the Filipino athletes and agreed to assign to the Pentathlon and Decation contests one unit in the championship calculations, as distinct from other land games, to play the base-ball matches on the two-round system, but volleyball and basketball matches on the one-round system, and to make the fancy-diving contest an open event.
The Japanese delegates offered the proposal that, from the next Olympic Games land events should constitute three units towards the championship, namely track events one, field events one, and mixed events one. The Chinese delegates on the other hand counter-proposed the inclusion of the relays in the mixed contests, constituting one unit for the championship. No decision was reached on this point.

## [16] HOBBS'S BOOK.

In his latest article, in the "Daily News," "continuing his comments on the English team's Australian tour," J. B. Hobbs says: "Years ago I published a book, 'How to Make a Century,' which is now out of print. My own copy was sold on behalf of a charity fund, and I could not obtain another. On the third morning of the first test, at Sydney, however, a parcel was delivered at our hotel, addressed to me. It was a copy of my book, and inside the cover the anonymous donor had written: 'Study it well, J.B. You'll need it.' It seemed to be a direct challenge, and certainly put me on my mettle, for I made 115. I sincerely thank the donor of the book and hope that he reads this story.

## [17] FOUR JOANS."

**ENGLAND'S TENNIS HORES.**

Will England's "four Joans" eventually regain England's tennis supremacy?

That question is asked by Mrs. Lambert-Chambers in an article in the 'Standard.' She says that at least half a dozen girls are showing distinct possibilities of winning the international championship.
There was little to choose between Joan Lycett, Joan Fry, Joan Reid-Thomas, and Joan Ridley. She considered Miss Fry, after Miss McKane, the most consistent player of the younger generation in England. Naturally Miss McKane was 'every' body's first choice, but Miss Fry had plenty of pluck and determination. She also possessed a perfect match temperament. Her victory over Miss McKane was well deserved.

## [18] NO MORE 50-50.

**DEMPSEY AND KEARNS PART.**

Before leaving on a belated honeymoon, Jack Dempsey announced the end of his business partnership with Jack Kearns. They jointly own £200,000 worth of Los Angeles real estate, which will be sold during the next few months in order to divide the proceeds.

Dempsey declared that he is unwilling to fight again under a contract which gives Rearns an equal share with himself.

## [19] ATHLETICISM RUN MAD

**BOYS TREATED LIKEBULLFIGHTERS.**

Dr. H. M. Moran, of Sydney who captained the Wallabies' football team in England, has strong views, quite apart from the medical aspect, on the question of school athletics.

"Athleticism has run mad in the Greater Public Schools," he declared. "Indeed, so strong is the cult of athleticism in those schools that it seems to me that the schools are using the prowess of their boys as an advertisement." The worst thing that could happen to a schoolboy nowadays is to show a great talent for athletics. His devotion to school football and rowing is liable to interfere with the development of his character." We are rightly keen on athletics, but there is a wrong sense of perspective applied to them.

## [21] MASSAGE

Golfers don't as a rule go in for muscle massage and such artificial aids to physiological stimulation, as footballers, tennis players, and sportsmen in other strenuous fields. Maybe they are making a mistake in not doing so. At any rate, the inside history of V.T. England's victory in the recent Blackheath championship makes it appear so. Between rounds England's honorary trainer, Duncan MacMillan, got to work on his charge and pummelled embrocation into him for three-quarters of an hour—right up to within a few minutes of starting. The effect was magical, England played inspired golf, reached the turn-in 33, and finished the round one stroke over the course record, 73. His opponent was beaten, as much by surprise at the vigour of his adversary as by the par variety of his golf.
