## [13] WORLD OF SPORT.

**FOOTBALL**

**MISTAKES MADE BY RULING BODI**

We have somehow grown to regard Cricket as a game conservative and even aristocratic, and Association football as the most democratic of sports, writes E. G. L. in All Sports. But these words, so freely used, are political terms. They refer to the governing spirit of the sport, not to the people who play it and watch it. And in this light can we describe football as democratic.

Jack Sharp used to be a professional gricketer; he has reverted to his amateur status, and is actually captain of Launcashire. The same might, conceivably, happen to Jack Hobbs. But if Charles Buchan wished to return to his amateur status and finish his football life with Loyton, or Binks, of Huddersfield, desired to give up professionalism and lend South Bank again, do you think he could? This apart from the club's consent.

Would the Football Association situation it? It is highly improbable. Indeed, we have only to turn to the report of the last meeting of the committee dealing with reinstatement to discover that the difficulty of persuading these gentlemen that it is not undesirable is enormous.

The same committee, it may be observed, also refused permission to severall old professionals to "sit on the Committee of Management" of such clubs as Tinotype Football Club, Bass Football Club (Burton-on-Trenton), and to one to act as a director of Accrington Stanley Football Club.

## [14] CAN IT BE?

They think, apparently, that such a procedure would not be in the best interests of the game. Will they please tell us why? To us, the uninitiated public, who believe that some clients managers have more power and influence in football than some directors, it seems odd that an old player may be the former, but on no account the latter.

Is it the idea that a professional should not be allowed to contribute to the good of football unless he accepts payment for it? How odd!
The M.C.C. take some hard knocks for their pains and principles, but we remember that their committee for choosing the teams to play South Africa in the Tests here last summer had instructions to consult a professional, as well as the team captain, if they deemed it necessary, and that, in fact, Hobbs was consulted.

## [15] MISTAKEN POLICY

Have the Football Association Selection Committee ever consulted anybody beyond their own little coterie? Possibly some of them have—an officially and rather in the nature of "sounding" opinion. But would have ever admit it?
So recently is when Vivian Woodward, an amateur, was good enough to lead England, and, later, when Arthur Grimsdell, a professional, stood out as the player and captain of the year in all four first-class intelligence and experience invited to express an opinion as to the players they were to lead on to the field? Oh, no.

## [16] VERY HOLEY

We begin to find, now, that football is not so democratic as we had always thought. Indeed, a closer examination than this short article permits would reveal that the Football Association is something like a hierarchy, and as conservative a ruling body as there is in sport or elsewhere.

In fact, when football comes to be called democratic we find so many holes in it, and such big ones, that while we are finding one we stumble into another.
Taking it broad and large, the weak spots with the strong, the Football Association is undeniably efficient defensively. Very little escapes its net of surveillance. But when we see an old player denied the privilege of working for the common weal of football for nothing, simply because he was once paid for playing, it seems absurd to attach the tag "democratic" to the gentry of Russell Square.

## [20] BOXING

**DE LEON WINS AGAIN.**

Gavino De Leon, now labelled "the Filipino Terror," scored another victory in Sydney on April 25, when he knocked out Jack Green. Green weighed nine stone and De Leon 8.1L.

Green had a lead at half wry. He hit the Filipino with all sorts of punches, but could not stop him getting to close quarters, where De Leon scored heavily to the body. De Leon forced the pace in the eleventh and fought like a tornado in the twelfth, when Green was so plainly beaten that the towel came in.

## [22] BASEBALL.

**ATTEMPT TO POPULARISE GAME IN ENGLAND.**

An Anglo-American Baseball Association has been formed in London, and the members are hopeful of popularising the game in England.

At present there are four teams proposed under the association—Chelsea, Brentford, London Lions, and American Legion.

The season opened on May 10, at Stamford Bridge Grounds, will in game between Chelsea and the s.s. Levinthan, followed on May 17 by Chelsea v. Oxford University. Other games to follow will be against the s.s. Pittsburgh, and a series against a picked team from the Paris Baseball League.

Mr. B. G. Gildersleeve, who managed the Chelsea side, did the pitching for that team, and Mr. Charles Brooks, who was out of the game last year owing to business obligations, is now managing the Brentford team, and will pitch for them.

Every possible assistance will be given to either clubs or individuals who wish to play, on communication with C. E. Bates, hon. secretary, care of Old Colony Club, Aldwych House, London, W.C.2.
