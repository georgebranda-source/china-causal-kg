Those familiar fictional characters, Tom Sawyer and Huckleberry Finn, are making their appearance in a Moscow motion picture house. Mark Twain has always been a popular foreign author in Russia and "Tom Sawyer" was successfully dramatized and produced at the Moscow Children's Theatre.

Mrs. Lane, wife of Sub-Inspector Lane, was forced from her ricsha when approaching the Star Ferry, Kowloon, yesterday morning. Her aggressors were a party of Chinese and as the nature of their intentions appeared decidedly unfavourable it was fortunate that the police were near at the time. Kagawa Toyohiko, a Labour leader of Kobe, Japan, arrived in London recently to study the workings of the British Labour Party as an aid to organizing a Labour party in Japan. Mr. Toyohiko said the Socialist and Labour leaders of Japan had combined in efforts to further the movement and that while the Japanese Labour Party would be modelled after the British system it would function in some ways along the lines of the Social Democratic Party of Germany.

A new kind of "death ray," exceeding in its destructive capacity any previous device, rumoured or realised, has been reported to the Commerce Department Washington as the claimed accomplishment of a German inventor; Mr. William T. Daugherty, Trade Commissioner at Berlin, forwarded a summary of an article describing the invention, which is called "heliotneub," with the startling assertion that its mysterious waves are capable of paralyzing life for six hours over a distance of forty miles and to an altitude of more than 45,000 feet. Rome has for long been recognized as the city of fountains. These are always playing, and give the city a gay and lively aspect. At almost every corner, especially in the ancient part of the town, you are bound to find one, and their number has increased to such an extent that the claim that modern Rome has surpassed imperial Rome, at least where fountains are concerned, is fully justified. Besides the large and well-known fountains, such as the Trevi Fountain, or the Fontana delle Naiadi, or the one at the Janiculum, there are numberless smaller fountains in various parts of the city which are scarcely ever visited by foreigners and the very existence of which is even ignored by many Romans. Many of these are really gems of art, hidden in remote and obscure corners in zones now mostly inhabited by people of the poorer classes.

## [14] GENERAL.

Says an Australian paper:—An unprecedentedly long record of service in the overseas trade with Australia is shared by the steamers Taiyuan and Changsha, which have been voyaging continuously between China and Japan and Melbourne via Sydney for close on 40 years. The first worn-out cable in the Melbourne Tramway system was taken by the Taiyuan to Hongkong at an early stage of her career, the broken strands being converted by Chinese into nails for use in tea chests.

What must have been an amusing spectacle, though productive of a certain amount of anxiety, occurred recently in the Mersey River. A hopper with a crew of nine men was blown on to a sandbank during a high gale. A lifeboat put out and stood by the hopper till the crew decided that it was no place for them. They were taken off and tugs remained by the hopper to take her off at high tide. But this hopper was of an independent turn of mind and slipping off the sandbank she made for home—which was the Sandon Dock. On her way she passed no fewer than fifteen ships, wending her way across the bows of one and under the stern of another. The tugs which had been ready to tow her off had nothing to do but follow her. As she arrived opposite her dock gates, which were open at high water, onlookers thought she was going to bump into the dock wall, but avoiding this she sailed serenely into her own dock, where human beings once more took charge of her and tied her up at her berth.

## [19] DRAMATIC CRIME.

**BALKAN LEADER SHOT DEAD.**

**WOMAN'S VENGEANCE.**

The Correspondent of the "Chicago Tribune" writes: Vienna is discussing the most dramatic sensation it has had in recent years. There is only one topic amongst the Viennese, that is the extraordinarily dramatic murder in the Vienna Municipal Theatre. Before hundreds of spectators gathered to witness Ibsen's tragedy, "Peer Gynt," Balkan hatred suddenly blazed out from the darkness of a private box. At the very moment that Ibsen's Peer Gynt was simulating death on the stage a beautiful young Macedonian woman, Mencia Carniciu, rose from her chair in a private box where she was sitting with a party, drew an automatic pistol, and fired six shots into the dress circle a few feet below.

When the lights were turned up, Todor Dimitroff, a Macedonian secret agent, was found shot dead, his wife mortally wounded, and another person badly hurt. After a breathless moment the audience stamped for the exit. Police rushed to the woman's box and arrested her. She declared she had avenged treason against her countrymen by the act.

According to the story of Mencia Carniciu as hastily told to the police, the man she killed, Todor Dimitroff, betrayed two Macedonian comrades in Sofia several years ago. They were both executed, and she was appointed to avenge their death.

Upon searching the clothing of the dead man's wife and her companions in the theatre who were also wounded, it was found the wife was carrying a small revolver, and the men were all armed and well provided with ammunition.

Name. Dimitroff received a bullet through the back of her skull, penetrating through to the throat. She is in a desperate condition.

## [20] PRISONER'S STORY.

Mencia Carniciu presented a striking figure when she appeared before the Chief of Police to be examined. She is 26 years old and is the daughter of one of the richest families in Macedonia. She is a young woman of extraordinary beauty, tall and svelte, with jet back hair and blazing black eyes. She was utterly defiant in face of the murder charge. She told the examining magistrate she vowed two years ago to take the life of Todor Dimitroff as retribution for the lives of two Macedonian patriots whom, she declared. Dimitroff betrayed to their death, Telling the story quietly and proudly, she related how she came to Vienna and contrived, through a woman who knew the Dimitroff's, to arrange the exact seating for both parties in the Municipal Theatre for the performance of Ibsen's "Peer Gynt." Deliberately she told how she had visited the theatre and witnessed the performance earlier in order to judge the best moment to open fire.

"I knew he was always expecting attack, and always ready to attack, Mencis Carniciu declared," So I schemed to sit behind him, and as soon as the chance was right I fired"

Apparently Madame Dimitroff threw herself between her husband and the line of fire at the crack of the revolver, which explained how the second bullet found its mark in the skull of the dead man's wife. Dimitroff's companion, a man named Bagatinoff, had just been able to get his pistol from his pocket when Mencia fired again, killing Dimitroff.

The murder occurred at the moment when Peer Gynt was supposed to be drowned in the Sea. The Viennese actor, Herr Tressler, who was playing the part, emerged from the waves and came back to life, and addressed the audience from the stage, urging them to remain in their seats.

Police investigation establishes the political character of the dramatic murder. From the history of his past activities in Balkan politics the police have confirmed that Dimitroff was responsible for the death of two Macedonian leaders, Boris Sarafoff and Ivan Garbanoff, in Sofia seventeen years ago. Dimitroff, who was 50 years old, had been living in Vienna during the past few months at the apartments of a prominent Macedonian patriot, Philip Attanasoff, but the latter was recently driven from Vienna by the police. Recognised as a sworn enemy of the present Bulgarian Prime Minister, M. Zankoff, Dimitroff went in fear of his life. He was always accompanied by three or four Macedonians armed with automatic pistols, ready to protect him from assassins.

According to a number of Mencia Carnici's friends, an element of mystery in the case is now presenting itself. Her intimate friends declare she was madly in love with the man she killed. When the police arrested her immediately after the tragic murder she had a smoking pistol in one hand and a razor in the other. She admitted that she intended to commit suicide, but she was either too stunned or had no time in which to do it.

In the files of the Vienna police head-quarters officials have found an old Bulgarian extradition paper drawn up against Dimitroff, charging him with being a member of the Macedonian Revolutionary Committee.

The victim of the tragedy in the Burgtheater has now been ascertained to be the notorious Todor Panitza, the founder and leader of the Macedonian Federalist organisation, who twenty years ago murdered two leaders of the Macedonian movement, Sarafoff and Garbanoff in the streets of Sofia. Panitza then fled to the mountains in the Macedonian regions of Bulgaria, where he organised revolutionary bands and lived like a prince, though he was always in dread of assassination. He came to Vienna a few days before to adjust differences which had arisen between Bulgarian emigrants there. He was always attended by heavily armed companions to guard him against attack and murder, and his presence occasioned much irritation in Austrian political circles, which represent such abuse of the rights of asylum being extended to Bulgarian emigrants.
