**MR. BALDWIN REVIEWS SITUATION**

**GERMANY AND THE LEAGUE.**

*London, July 25.*

Mr. Stanley Baldwin, the Premier, addressed fifty thousand Lancashire and Cheshire Unions at Lord Derby's residence at knowsley Park. He said that the host satisfactory feature of the foreign outlooks was the evident desire of Germany to continue negotiations with both Britain and France. He wished that Germany would enter the League of Nations very soon, when existing problems, notably disarmament, would assume a new and better aspect.

## [2] OVERSEAS TRADE.

Mr. Baldwin continued that the chief factor in checking the growth of Britain's overseas trade was the great increase in productive and distributive costs. We were threatened with a conflict between the coal-owners and miners, each with a very strong case, but all the time the financial position of the industry was getting worse. These conflicts must be lifted on to a new plane by both masters and men. He hoped that the negotiations which were about to be resumed would result in carrying on an harassed industry over a year or two till it would be easier to negotiate permanent settlements.

## [6] BOYCOTT DENIED.

**SHANGHAI CHAMBER'S DESIRES**

**SPEEDY SETTLEMENT**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*Shanghai, July 25.*

The Chinese General Chamber of Commerce denies that it has decided on a British and Japanese boycott.

The Chinese General Committee of Commerce has requested Reuter to deny the report in the local Press to the effect that the Vice-Chairman of the Chamber, at a meeting last Sunday, announced that goods purchased after June 30 from British and Japanese sources would be seized and confiscated after discovery by the students and that the boycott should be maintained for a year. The Chamber states that the report is unfounded, as the meeting was convened exclusively to discuss steps to urge the Chairman, Yu Yah-ching, not to retire. Hostile foreign criticisms have here been aroused and various retaliatory measures are proposed in letters to the foreign Press, all calculated to do harm to Chinese and foreign relations. This Chamber wishes all foreign business men to understand that no action of an illegal nature has been or will be contemplated by the Board of Directors, whose only hope is that a speedy settlement on the lines of international justice of the May 30 affair, may be effected and the moral support of the foreign friends of China abroad is earnestly solicited and will be heartily appreciated.

## [9] RUBBER BOOM.

**REQUEST TO MODIFY RESTRICTION.**

*London, July 25.*

A manifesto emphasising the adverse effect to their interests of the legislative restrictions of rubber exports from Malaya and Ceylon has been signed by the Society of Motor Manufacturers and Traders, the Royal Automobile Club, the Commercial Motor Users' Association, and the British Cycle and Motor Cycle Manufacturers' and Traders' Union. The manifesto points out the enormous increase in the prices of crude rubber for immediate delivery and says that approximately six million cycle and motor users in Britain are paying and will continue to pay the price during the present restriction; describes the scheme as too inelastic to adjust the supplies with the necessary rapidity to prevent the maintenance of an abnormal price, and urges that it is the Government's duty to modify the scheme.

## [16] MITATION “CLOUDS.”

**METHOD OF TRAINING PILOTS.**

The loss of the sense of balance which is suffered by aeroplane pilots when flying within clouds or fog can now be imitated exactly by a particularly ingenious contrivance. This contrivance will enable pilots to continue their navigational training during clear summer weather.

A hood, similar to the hood of a peril-regulator, is fitted in front of the pilot's cockpit. The instructor, who occupies the observer's seat immediately behind, draws the hood back over the pilot's head directly he believes that a safe height has been attained. The pilot is then seated in a miniature dark room for the hood is fitted so as to exclude all light. Small electric bulbs glow upon his instrument and permit him to read the compass, altimeter, airspeed indicator, turn indicator, revolution counter, and "bubble" (a transverse spirit level). By these instruments he must set his course and fly perhaps 100 miles without sight of the outside world.
