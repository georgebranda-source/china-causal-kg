Summary

  • Coverage drives raw counts. Pages per month range from 16 to 75, so everything below uses shares of the month's claims.
  • Themes that clearly move (95% intervals don't overlap): "Recent disturbances occur in Shanghai" is 8% of claims in Q2 1925 (11% in June) and 1% or less in each later quarter. China Mail delivery appears only from June to September 1925. "A strike is taken" falls from 8% (Q3 1925) to 2% (Q3 1926). Suggestive only: the boycott is about 1% in every 1925 quarter and 1-3% in 1926, reaching 6% in Nov 1926; its intervals overlap.
  • Caution, May 1926: the strike spike (12%) mixes in the British General Strike (London printers, coal). The event node does not keep strikes in different places apart.
  • Strike as cause or effect: it is mostly a cause in every period (about 3-5 claims as cause for each claim as effect). As an effect it peaks in Jun-Jul 1925 and Jun-Jul 1926. It never shifts from effect to cause.
  • Similarity between months is low throughout (size-matched Jaccard 0.03-0.07). Adjacent months are only slightly more alike than distant ones (0.049 vs about 0.041 at 2-16 months apart), so there is no gradual drift. A small core of hub nodes recurs; the rest are one-off incidents. The low point in all four versions is Nov to Dec 1925, and December 1925 is unlike every other month. The fall at 17-19 months apart only reflects pairs involving Apr-May 1925. Exact edges almost never repeat (Jaccard 0-0.02).
  • Robust to clustering: monthly share series of the top 12 nodes correlate 0.63-0.97 (Spearman) across the four versions. Monthly similarity series agree 0.78-0.95. Two-month series agree 0.55-0.85. Quarterly similarity barely varies (0.135-0.151), so it cannot tell periods apart.
