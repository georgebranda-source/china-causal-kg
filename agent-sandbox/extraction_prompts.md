## 1. Extraction prompt (current)

System prompt:

```text
You extract causal claims from 1925 China Mail (Hongkong) newspaper text. OCR errors are expected.
Go through the text sentence by sentence. Extract a claim only when the text directly and clearly says that one named thing brought about another. Both the cause and the effect must be stated in the text. Do not infer links a reader might guess.

EXTRACT:
- a stated reason or result: because, due to, owing to, on account of, in view of, since, as a result of, in consequence of, the cause of, led to
- a named cause acting through a causal verb: "the meeting forced the Chairman to sign"
- an action taken to protect against or deal with a named threat: the threat is the cause, the action is the effect
- a protest, reply or demand about something: the thing it is about is the cause, the protest, reply or demand is the effect
- a reason a speaker gives to justify an action: the reason is the cause, the action is the effect
- "had X happened, Y would have followed" about real past events: keep X and Y as written
- claims by officials, speakers or reports, and hedged claims ("it is said", "reported", "believed"), if the causal link itself is clear
- any such claim, whether or not it concerns the strike

DO NOT EXTRACT:
- events simply listed or placed side by side, even if one seems to explain the other: "The strike continues and all Chinese have left Shameen."
- sequence alone: "After this", "then", "when"
- a causal verb with no named cause: "they were prevented from doing so", "the hotel was compelled to hire staff"
- predictions and conditionals about the future: "will probably lead to", "if these measures were adopted"
- denied links: "had no connection with", "was not the cause of"

If "this" or "these" refers to the cause or effect, extract the claim only if the sentence just before names it, and make the quote cover both sentences.

Return one item per claim, with the fields in this order:
reasoning: one or two sentences naming the words that state the link and which way the causation runs, before anything else.
quote: copied character for character from the text, the shortest span showing the claim.
cause: a short cleaned-up phrase, with OCR errors fixed and pronouns replaced by what they refer to.
effect: a short cleaned-up phrase, in the same style.
Do not use outside knowledge. If there are no such claims return an empty list.
```

<text>

## 2. Check prompt (not in use)

System prompt:

```text
You check one causal claim taken from a 1925 newspaper. Decide whether the quote itself directly and clearly states that the cause brought about the effect.

KEEP if the quote has any of these:
- a stated reason or result (because, due to, owing to, in view of, since, as a result of, in consequence of, the cause of)
- a named cause with a causal verb (forced)
- a protest, reply, appeal or demand about the cause. No causal word is needed: "protested regarding the firing" and "appealed to the Government to abolish the tax" both count, and the thing it is about is the cause.
- an action taken against a named threat
- a reason a speaker gives to justify an action
- "had X happened, Y would have followed" about real past events
Hedges such as "it is said" are fine. "This" is fine if the quote also names what it refers to.

REJECT if any of these apply:
- sequence only: the only link is "after this", "then", "when"
- the two events are only placed side by side
- a causal verb with no cause named in the quote: "were prevented", "was compelled"
- a prediction or conditional about the future: "will probably lead to", "if these measures were adopted"
- the quote denies the link: "no connection", "nothing to do with", "not the cause of"
- the cause or effect is not actually what the quote says

reasoning comes first: name the words that link cause and effect, and say which rule applies. Then give keep as true or false.
```

User message, once per claim:

```text
Quote: <quote>
Cause: <cause>
Effect: <effect>
```

JSON schema (`format`):

```json
{
  "type": "object",
  "properties": {
    "reasoning": {
      "type": "string"
    },
    "keep": {
      "type": "boolean"
    }
  },
  "required": [
    "reasoning",
    "keep"
  ]
}
```

### V1–V4: recall prompt

```text
You extract causal claims from 1925 China Mail (Hongkong) newspaper text. OCR errors are expected.
Go through the text sentence by sentence and list EVERY causal claim. News items usually contain several.
Count all of these as causal claims:
- a stated reason: because, owing to, due to, on account of, in view of, as a result, since, for fear of, therefore
- an action taken in response to something: X protested about Y; after Y, people did X; troops were sent because of Y
- a purpose: X was done to bring about or prevent Y
- a consequence of a strike, measure, shortage or event: prices rose, ships were held up, shops closed
- something a person or report claims caused something, including claims in reported speech, even if disputed
- a prediction or warning that X will lead to Y
- "had X happened, Y would have followed": keep X and Y as written
If a chain links X to Y to Z, give one item per link. Do not count events merely listed one after another with no link between them.
Return one item per claim, with the fields in this order:
reasoning: one or two sentences identifying the claim in the text and which way the causation runs, before anything else.
quote: copied character for character from the text, the shortest span showing the claim.
cause: the cause, in a short phrase.
effect: the effect, in a short phrase.
Do not use outside knowledge. If there are no causal claims return an empty list.
```

V2–V4 added worked examples, in `extraction_probe/recall_exp.py`. One of them taught "After this the
