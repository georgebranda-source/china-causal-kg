A whirlwind of intense velocity lasting only two minutes or so struck Tungshan and Taishatau (to the ef the Canton) about 10 45 last night, causing considerable damage and the death of two men by electrocution.

Huts and matsheds were carried away and a part of the German Consulate roof was torn off. Trees were uprooted and the sky was suddenly illuminated by a big red flare. Foreign residents who had just got back to their homes in this district were reading about the Hongkong storm when many were thrown violently about while windows came tumbling down. ## [5] THE RIGHT WAY.

**CRITICAL SITUATION CONTROLLED.**

**GOVERNOR'S FIRM DEALING**

On the morning of June 1, just as the students and labour unions of Changsha were preparing for their annual demonstration connection with the "Six-One" (June 1) movement, when it was proposed to commemorate and make demands in connection with the anniversary of the unfortunate affair on the Bund here in 1923 when marines from a Japanese gunboat, in their effort to clear the dock of a violent mob and permit the landing of passengers and freight from a steamer of the Nisshin Kaisha, shot into the crowd and killed a man, the news came to hand of the serious rioting on May 30, in Shanghai, writes a "North China Daily News" correspondent. News that the police had fired into the crowd and that nine students had died caused tremendous consternation and a great outburst of nationalistic feeling. Processions were started at once and for a day or two Governor Chao made no attempt to repress them.

## [6] EXCITEMENT HIGH.

By June 4, when word had come of later disturbances in Shanghai and of further shooting, the excitement was running fairly high. Schools that had been parading the streets in connection with the June first celebration now held meetings—that is, the Student Unions did—and other meetings of a violent sort were called in the hall of the Educational Association.

The leaders of the movement calling for retaliation soon organized what is to be called "The Tsing-Hu. (Tsingtao-Shanghai) Cruel Killing Snow Shame (Vengeance) Association." Included in the leadership of this organization are students and labour leaders as well. Every sort of radical proposition has been made, most of them copying the proposals made in Shanghai, for death penalties on the officers that did the shooting in Shanghai on May 30, for the removal of British consular officers; for a complete boycott against the British and Japanese; etc. In many of the schools agitated meetings have been held, usually ending in the taking of a solemn oath by the students body to work here after body, soul and spirit, for the sake of China; and for the enforcement of a complete and permanent economic boycott against Britain and Japan.

## [7] AMERICA DRAGGED IN.

Those who watched these developments in school after school came to believe that it would not be long before other countries would be mentioned by name and included in the attack. By the morning of June 6, when word came of an incident in Shanghai on June 3, when American volunteers were fired upon near the New World Theatre, and police action had again become necessary America was also proclaimed as one of the nations to be found guilty. The telegrams here all said that the American Troop had opened fire with a machine gun and had "killed numberless persons." All sors of rumours of impending violence then spread over the city. The governor had already, on June 4, put up a strong proclamation, condemning the shooting in Shanghai on May 30, as unhumanitarian, but urging students and all others to refrain from acts of violence, so as not to give the foreigners any opportunity for incrimination and for retaliatory acts. He proposed severe measures for those who should be found disturbing law and order. This proclamation had had a decided effect until the afternoon of June 6, when the news of the American volunteers reached Changsha.

Before long a girl was found at the gate of the Educational Association, weeping and calling attention to a printed poster on the walls, soon put up all over the city to the effect that an engineer, a foreigner, in the employ of the Canton-Hankow railway, had beaten a Chinese to death. Later in the evening a new rumour was started to the effect that another foreigner had killed a Chinese to the west of the city.

When we turn our attention inwards, we see a bewildering multiplicity of "I's" and are stunned by the clamour of opposing voices, all coming apparently from ourselves. But consciousness is a unit, and it appears in the outer world as many simply because it presents itself there through different media, as an electric current in platinum wire may appear as light, in iron as heat, round a bar of soft iron as magnetic energy, or in a solution as a power to decompose and recombine. As consciousness works through the intuitional, mental, astral or physical body, it becomes blinded by the material that enwraps it, and by illusion identifies itself with the craving of the senses, the reasoning of the intellect, and cries "I think," "I feel," "I want." As evolution proceeds, man learns to know himself to be life, not forms, bliss not desire, knowledge not thought, and then first he becomes conscious of unity, then alone he finds peace. Consciousness, at the present stage of evolution, has its centre normally in the astral (emotional and desire) body. We are held back by wanting to unite ourselves with objects transitory, mean and narrow; can we not, then, push ourselves forward by wanting to unite ourselves with the permanent, the noble and the wide? What we need is to cultivate the emotions and to direct them in a way that will purify and enable the character. The basis of all emotions on the side of progress is love, and that is the power which we must cultivate. The first condition of human goodness is something to love, something to reverence.
