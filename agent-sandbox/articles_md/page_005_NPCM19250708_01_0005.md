## [1] CORRESPONDENCE

(To the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir,—Some people do not care one way or the other whether the remaining river boats continue operations or not. Others do care. There are yet others who, by virtue of long contact with the crews, are able to throw light on one aspect of the trouble as the men concerned themselves are far too much scared to do anything.

Strike Executive are calling on the No. 1's of the crews. On introduction they take considerable pains to explain that they are volunteers, duly empowered by the Executive, the members of the latter having had to make themselves scarce. I have recognised one or two of these "volunteers" as former No. 1's on river and coastal shipping. As they are out of work themselves they are anxious to see all colleagues idle. After a brief hurangue on national honour, they emphasise the claim that if all shipping was to cease the trouble would soon be over. As is characteristic of the Chinese worker, the men approached put off the agitators by promising that if vital lines shut down, they will also stop themselves. In this manner, one has been waiting for the other and the service has continued till now. If a visit were paid to the ships an hour or two after arrival at the wharves here some of these "volunteer" delegates could be seen actually making their daily rounds.

With regard to the letter published in your issue of yesterday, from “An Americann Friend,” I beg to propose, with all deference, that the Government have the letter translated and re-published (1) in the vernicular papers which are still being issued and (2) as circulars as was the case with a speech of Mr. D. Lloyd George at a critical period of the last War. Such publication may do a great deal to commenter pernicious propaganda from sources beyond the control of the Colony.

About the question of reward for arrests and/or convictions of agitators. There are some of us who may think twice about performing a public duty because a wrong impression may be gained, viz. that of going after the monetary reward. Such people would consider a medal or other tangible remembrance a much more suitable reward.

Yours, etc.

## [2] WORLD'S SHIPS.

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, July 7.*

Lloyds Register shows—the world's shipping to be 64,641,000 tons compared with 64,023,000 in June of last year and 42,514,000 tons in 1914, Britain's shares respectively being 19,301,000 tons and 18,954,000 tons. The United States tonnage has decreased by 579,000 tons.

The world's sailing tonnage has fallen by 1,718,000 tons since before the war to 1,611,000 tons, of which 659,000 are owned by the United States.

## [4] HELD UP:

**INCIDENTS AT SHANGHAI**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

Shanghai: July 7.

The shipping situation remains the same and the concentrated intimidation of the last few days resulted in a number of tramwaymen failing to turn up this morning, but the Tramway Company is determined to carry on. Specials guarded the trams to-day. There was no incident. Otherwise, however, the situation is quiet, though forty thousand are out of work due to the cutting off of the bulk of the power supply of electricity. In many cases resumption is believed to be taking place owing to the inadequate dole which is being given out.

*Shanghai, July 6.*

The bulk of the power supply of electricity has been discontinued. A number of mills are installing their own motors.

Agitators are taking advantage of the discontinuance of the electricity supply and are furthering unrest, but no serious trouble has occurred.

The intimidation of tramwaymen is being carried on without abatement. Last evening, a crowd of a hundred, including a number of former tramwaymen, held up a car conveying thirty tramway fitters from the repair shops. They forced the fitters to accompany them to Chinese territory.

About half the telephone operators have resumed work.

The body of an American member of the crew of the s.s. President Pierce has been found in a creek, whilst a British blue-jacket has been missing for sixty hours and has not yet been found.

At the inquest on the man who was shot by the American seaman Dizik, it was stated that the deceased was shot by the sailor when the latter was protecting a Municipal officer. Two men who were arrested were charged at the Mixed Court, one for inciting to riot and the other for firing crackers. The first was remandied on bail, and the other nominally fined.

The Japanese community is raising subscriptions for the purpose of making a presentation to the Volunteers and naval landing parties in recognition of their services. So far, nearly ten thousand dollars have been subscribed.

## [6] Dosser Trial.

<sub>section under [4] HELD UP:</sub>

Shanghai; July 7..

The Mixed Court dismissed three of the four charges against Dosser who was allowed bail on Tls. 20,000 security (which the Soviet Trade Mission is alleged to be furnishing).

Dosser, was remanded for a week. All charges against his wife were dismissed. The remaining charge against Dosser is that he made preparations to openly incite disorder.

The Court dismissed the charge that accused was making preparations to commit a hostile-act against certain foreign states, because the text of an article in the Chinese Criminal Code, whereon the charge was based, meant "declare war" and not "hostile act." Two other charges were dismissed as they rested upon the above mentioned charge.

## [10] THE PARADE.

The naval, military, and Volunteer units made a brave show as they paraded the streets yesterday afternoon.

The regular naval and military units marched through the West Point district and the Hongkong Volunteer Defence Corps through the Wanchai and Causeway Bay district.

The Hongkong - Volunteer Defence Corps were at full strength, less the Reserve Company out on patrol and guard duties.

In the intervals, while “standing by,” all the members of the St. John Ambulance Brigade are engaged in public service, some in the Post Office, others at the Fire Brigade Station with the Motor Ambulances, etc., while others again are prepared for special sanitary service under the Sanitary Department. Arrangements made apply to both Hongkong and Kowloon.

Miss Helen Parkhurst, head of the Children's University School in New York City, and internationally known as the organiser and director of the Dalton Plan of primary and secondary school educational methods has been invited by the National Association for the Advancement of Education in China, to visit that country this summer and aid in the extension of her system in the Chinese schools.
