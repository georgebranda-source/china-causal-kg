## [7] COMING BACK.

**INFLUX FROM CANTON.**

**IMPENDING TROUBLE?**

Apart from the fact that the streets are strangely but pleasantly more quiet and that idlers, if there remain any, do not now burden will their presence Statue Square and other public places, Hongkong to-day presents no more different, appearance than it has done since the strike began and the colour of khaki sprang very much into prominence.

No further developments regarding the local strike situation have taken place but a feature of some importance is the influx into the Colony which has taken place during the last 36 hours. Two thousand returned from Canton yesterday. Naval authorities report everything quiet in Canton and decline to make any statement with regard to rumours of approaching civil war between the Red and anti-Red factions.

The increase in the numbers returning from Canton to Hongkong is significant, however, particularly as river steamers arriving today report that restrictive measures are in operation at Canton with regard to people wishing to leave. The only sampans that appear to be allowed to convey passengers to the boats are those flying a distinctive flag the significance of which is not quite apparent. Officers from the boats are not being allowed to land in Canton.

There has been a distinct falling off in the numbers leaving Hongkong for Canton, steamers on yesterday and to-day's trips taking about a third of what they have been during the past week or fortnight.

## [8] REGISTRATION?

The question being asked by many in the Colony is “what measures are being taken to ensure against undesirables and useless idlers from returning from the Colony?” Others go further and ask why all returning to Hongkong with the avowed intention of working should not be registered and their thumb prints taken in order that their movements could be traced.

With regard to the former question the Hongkong police are kept informed as to the character of the people returning and from every boat, that comes in, those considered in any way doubtful are taken direct to the Central police station and dealt with.

With regard to general registration a “China Mail” representative made enquiries as to the view of the Government and quoted the case of the Philippines where every resident has to have in his possession a schedule relating to himself and his position. His attention was directed to the fact that on an average something like 5,000 Chinese came into Hongkong daily by steamer, junk and rail or merely passing over the border on foot. The position of Hongkong was different from that of Manila where a supervision of ocean-going vessels was sufficient. Such supervision was already in existence in Hongkong but registration of every person, in some cases of people spending but a few hours in business here was impracticable and little useful purpose would be served. An enormous staff would be entailed by any such scheme.

## [9] STOLEN FIREWOOD.

At the Central Magistracy this morning, Mr. McElderry imposed fines of $5 or one week's hard labour on six women who were arrested by the police yesterday afternoon as they were leaving a plantation below Mount Kellett with 333 catties of wood.

Other gangs of copies had been busy on the hillside above Wongneichong village where extensive damages has been done by felling about 400 trees. A European police sergeant visited the place at 10 o'clock last night on hearing the sound of hatchets and arrested two men. They were charged this morning and sentenced to pay a fine of $25 each, or one month's hard labour.

The Hongkong-and-Shanghai Bank declare an interim dividend of £3 per share subject to deduction of income tax. This will be payable on and after Monday, July 10. This notification, also one relating to a temporary restriction of share transferals, are to be found in the advertisement columns.

## [10] Cintint

**POLICE RAID.**

**SEDITIOUS LITERATURE HAUL**

**OWNER JAILED.**

In a police raid carried out at 9 p.m. on July 1 at 15D Wellington Street, first floor, a quantity of seditious literature was found in a writing desk. A Chinese seaman of the Admiral Oriental Line, who lived on the premises with his family, admitted that he was the owner of the desk and he was arrested along with another man.

When charged before Mr. S. B. B. McElderry at the Central Magistracy this morning, the second accused was discharged as there was no evidence against him. The case against the principal accused was proceeded with, Mr. J. Gordon Leask appearing for the defence.

After hearing evidence, 'His Worship convicted the 'accused and sentenced him to six months' hard labour.

## [12] WORKERS WARNED.

### -

A notice similar to that posted up at the Naval Yard workshops when employees commenced to absent themselves has been issued by the Headquarters of the China Command. It draws attention to the consequences which would attend any defection on their part. The notice is in the following terms:
“Any employee of the War Department who absents himself without leave for more than six consecutive days during the present strike will be considered to have left his employment and will be struck off the books.”

"He will thereby forfeit all claim to gratuity in respect of his previous service, and pay for work performed since the last pay day."

Should any employee offer himself for re-employment after discharge, he will, if his services are required, be taken on as a new hand, and at the lowest rate of pay for his trade or calling.

## [13] A COMPLAINT.

A householder in the Wongneichong district has complained to the "China Mail" that the "boys" who have left the employ of Government servants at the quarters there are returning in the absence of the male members of the householders and in elegant silk stockings and furnished with fans with which they idly cool themselves are from a safe distance attempting to make fun of the ladies who are carrying out household duties. The police have been informed of the matter as this is obviously a case where the special powers invested in them by the Governor's proclamation relating to deportation should be exercised.

## [16] Hongkong's Send-Off.

<sub>section under [15] PRINCE CHICHIBU.</sub>

H.R.H. Prince Chichibu, second son of H.I.M. the Emperor of Japan, arrived in Hongkong on June 5 on board the cruiser Idzumo. The next day he embarked on the s.s. Hakosaki Maru for Europe.

“A slight figure in white, with topee raised above his dark wavy hair and with the sun catching his slightly tinted glasses, waved energetically to them (those who had gone to see him off) from the Captain's look out and kept time with his topee to the singing of the national tunes by Japanese Boy Scouts”—such was the main description by the “China Mail” at the time of the Prince's departure from Hongkong.

Prince Chichibu is 24 years of age and unlike his brother, the Prince Regent, has not done any travelling before. He is the first son of a Mikado to be educated outside of Japan and the telegram marks the stage prior to his arrival in Britain where he will acquire a knowledge of English and of European customs. He will be away from home for about two years and he may stay with an eminent British family in London, who are friends of the Japanese sovereign.

## [18] OBITUARY.

**MR. H. W. PETLEY.**

The news of the death of Mr. H. W. Petley, of the Mains Department of the Hongkong Electric Co., Ltd., came as a great shock to his many friends some of whom were talking to him as late as 5.30 p.m. yesterday.

Mr. Petley had not been in the best of health for some considerable time but as a Volunteer of some years standing he considered it his duty to mobilise together with his comrades and until the last hard-anticipated participating in yesterday's military and naval demonstration. He actually reported at Headquarters at the time appointed but owing to indisposition was exempted.

Death was due to heart failure. The late Mr. Petley, whose age was 40, had been with the Mains Department of the Electric Company for 20 years. His home was Whitfield Street, Quarry Bay, and he leaves a widow and one child with whom much sympathy will be felt.

Deceased had been a keen sports man and a cox of some standing.

## [31] WEAR.

**THE PATH TO REFORM.**

**VESTS SHIRTS DRAWERS COMBINATIONS TENNIS SHIRTS**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, June 7.*

Lord Birkenhead (Secretary for India) made an important statement in the House of Lords in regard to his conferences with the Viceroy (Earl Reading) on the Indian problem.

He stated that no decision would be taken before the government of India Legislative Assembly had been consulted. The Government would not be diverted from its high obligations to India by tactics of restless impatience. The door to acceleration of the reforms would not open to menace, still less would it be stormed by violence; but the date of the Royal Commission to review the reforms might be accelerated when responsible Indian leaders gave evidence of a genuine desire to co-operate in making the best or the existing constitution. Any constitution produced by the Government's critics in India backed by a fair measure of general agreement of the peoples of India would be very carefully examined.
