## [10] BACK TO NORMAL

**MORE SIGNS OF THE TIMES.**

**CAUSES FOR OPTIMISM.**

No further defections on the part of workers are to be reported this morning and although no general body or workers has returned to work, attempts to be reinstated, have been made by "boys" and amahs in many districts.

The more moderate estimates which are now being given regarding the beginning of the inevitable return of workers are supported by the reports of distress among Hongkong strikers in Canton. There are known to be 80,000 of these in Canton and the purse of the Government there has been so severely taxed that few of them are receiving the 40 cents a day. Meanwhile the food situation (with the restriction placed on exports from Hongkong) is on the way to becoming grave.

That trouble is anticipated at Canton by the Hongkong authorities is evident from the fact that no European lady is allowed to travel to Canton.

Meanwhile, Hongkong is still carrying on and the action of the Café Wiseman management in inaugurating from this morning a breakfast service from 8 o'clock and the anticipation of a return to service of the soda fountain marks yet another step forward in the return to normal functioning of benefits which, although they may hardly be considered vital, are things which make an enormous difference: in these times of stress.

Even more important still as a sign of the times is the statement made by Mr. N. L. Smith, Food Controller, to a “China Mail” representative this morning that the services of the army “killers” had now been dispensed with, owing to a return to more normal conditions. Beef was now available in large quantities in the more central markets, also pork, the difficulties in connection with the retailing of which had now been overcome. It was hoped to extend the market services to more outlying districts now that the situation was so well in hand.

## [11] SHAMEEN ECHO.

**QUERY IN HOUSE OF COMMONS.**

**NUMBER OF CASUALTIES.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

*London, July 13.*

In the House of Commons, replying to Mr. Will Thorne (Lab.), Mr. Austen Chamberlain (Foreign Secretary) said the number of Chinese casualties in the recent firing at Canton was not accurately known. They had been reported to be about 30 killed and 70 wounded but may have been more. The foreign casualties so far known were one Frenchman killed, four British and one French wounded.

## [12] "CARRY ON."

**SHAMEEN POSTAL SERVICE.**

**MORN TILL NIGHT.**

*Canton, July 11.*

Great credit and the thanks of all foreigners both in Canton and Hongkong is due to the Postal Commissioner, his deputy and two assistants, who have worked from early morning till night under adverse circumstances to clear both incoming and outgoing mail in the Shameen branch and have laboured cheerfully and smilingly to "carry on" with this service. Without their efforts the Shameenites would have missed much of the cheer that has come with their epistles and newspapers from Hongkong.

## [15] STRIKERS RESTIVE.

**ALLEGED PREFERENTIAL TREATMENT.**

**DISSATISFIEDSEAMEN.**

Employed at one time as an “excise officer” when the Opium Monopoly was farmed out years ago, a Chinese who was one of the last to reach Hongkong before the steamers ceased running to Canton, told a “China Mail” reporter that unrest was growing among the strikers on account of alleged preferential treatment.

This man came down with the express intent of warning his family not to leave Hongkong. He is now awaiting resumption of steamer traffic to return. In Canton, he said, funds had reached a very low ebb. The nominal strike chairman is said to have been a Hongkong Seamen's Union official at one time and he is accused of sympathy toward the idle seamen. Those dissatisfied state that seamen each get two meals a day and some of the favoured ones receive an additional twenty cents. Among those who have found fault are the cargo coolies, who belong to a guild strong in numbers. Somehow or other plans went wrong. The guild in Hongkong is believed to be very wealthy, but no money was forthcoming. Accordingly, the coolies had to draw rations from the strike executive, and they are complaining.

An attempt, the informant concluded, was now being made to float a loan or issue bonds. Meanwhile the strikers' pickets were trying to exercise strict discipline. Correspondence to individuals in strike quarters from up-country or Hongkong is held back and passes must be obtained to leave the dormitories for fear of the rank and file running away.

## [16] “WE WANT TO WORK”

**SHANGHAI'S UNPAID, ANGRY STRIKERS.**

**POLICEMAN CHEERED.**

*(Reuter's Service.)*

A thousand strikers this afternoon assembled outside the General Labour Union in Chinese territory and angrily demanded strike pay which, it was alleged was three days overdue.

The money was not forthcoming. The crowd grew very hostile to the Union officials who, on seeing themselves endangered, called the assistance of Chapel Volunteers. The latter dispersed the crowd with difficulty, levelling their rifles.

The crowd rushed into the Settlement followed by Volunteers. A Municipal policeman ordered the latter to leave the Settlement borders and they obeyed. The policeman was cheered by the strikers who were shouting "We want to work."

Five hundred Fengtienites are now keeping order on the Settlement borders.

## [17] BLOWN OVER.

**RED V. ANTI-RED TROUBLE IN CANTON.**

**YUNNANESSE QUIET.**

Evidently better.counsels have prevailed in Canton and the so-called Red v. anti-Red trouble appears to have blown over if only for the time being.

Business correspondence received by Hongkong Chinese firms yesterday indicated that the dispute seems to have been patched up, and that a political disturbance is not expected just yet: One letter says that but for the cessation of communications there might have been just a little trade before the mid-autumn season.

Anti-foreign feeling, writes the “China Mall” correspondent, is not so pronounced and non-Chinese have been able to go about in various parts of the city. Honam Island, on the south bank of the river, is very quiet. Agitators are still active on the Bund and on the River, “welcome home” deputations meeting strikers from Hongkong who have gone up overland, and pickets preventing the rank and file from getting out of Canton.

The Yunnanese invasion from Kwangsi has receded into the background, but an outbreak between competing militarists at Swatow will not occasion surprise.

## [18] STRIKERS FINED.

**TWO COURT. CASES IN KOWLOON**

At the Kowloon Magistracy this morning, a former amah in the employment of Mr. H. E. Scriven was charged with leaving without notice on July 6. She was subsequently found to be working at Armand Buildings and arrested.

The amah said that she was employed as a temporary servant on a wage of $12 a month and left as she had too much to do. She claimed that the regular amah was paid $20, and since the latter went on strike she (accused) had to do double duty.

His Worship observed that, although the legal aspect was quite clear, he could not think of a more tactless case, as the amah had only served six weeks and was required to do more than her legitimate share of work. The accused was ordered to pay $14 to the complainant as compensation, or in default three weeks' hard labour.

Another case tried by Mr. E. W. Hamilton was that in which Mr. J. H. Oxberry charged a bar boy at the Palace Hotel with leaving without notice: It was stated that the accused was engaged in April on a monthly wage of $11 and left with the other servants on June 23.

The accused pleaded guilty and was fined $25, or one month's hard labour, in addition to payment of $11 to the complainant as compensation.

## [19] BOY FRIGHTENED.

**ALLEGED INTIMIDATOR DENIES CHARGE.**

**ＡＧＡＤ**

A Chinese watchman employed at the Kowloon Drinks was charged at the Kowjoon Ministry this morning with intimidating a house boy in the employment of Mr. Marriott. Evidence was given that the boy was threatened by the watchman when using the gate and that he was found by his master on Sunday afternoon in a frightened state.

Accused denied the charge and stated that all the house boys at the Dock Company were not on friendly terms with the watchman.

The Rev. G. R. Lindsay, giving evidence on behalf of the accused watchman, said that the man was employed for 13½ years in the pantry of the Peak Hotel until obliged to leave owing to a change of comprudores. Witness assisted the accused in securing employment at the Dock Company as a watchman, and from enquiries he had made he considered that the man bore a satisfactory character.

His Worship adjourned the case for two days.

## [20] PARTNERS' DISPUTE.

**ALLEGED INTIMIDATION TO STRIKE.**

A partner in the Mow Ling Restaurant, Kowloon, appeared before the Magistrate (Mr. E. W. Hamilton), yesterday as a result of the complaint of a fellow-partner that he had threatened the latter- and attempted by intimidation to get him to close the shop.

No evidence other than the complainant's statement was forthcoming, although it was stated that a pantry boy had made a statement through the complainant. At the Magistracy, however, this lad stated that he had never heard the complainant being threatened, nor was he told to cease work himself.

The complainant's story was that his fellow-partner told him that the shop would be destroyed unless it was shut. On the complainant pointing out that the firm would lose money, and probably its licence, the defendant had said that by destroying the building they would be able to get $5,000 insurance.

A few days later his fellow-partner told witness that unless he struck he would have to buy a coffin.

In spite of cross-examination, the pantry boy adhered to his statement that he had never heard any of the conversation mentioned by the complainant. His Worship commented that it was obvious from his terrified demeanour that the boy had been intimidated himself and adjourned the case until tomorrow.

## [23] P. O. PROGRESS.

**CHINESE \ DLUNTEERS DO WELL**

"Things are moving much more smoothly here," said a busy official at the General Post Office to a "China Mail" man who inquired to-day, "although there has been a good deal to learn of Post Office work."

"Our volunteers are still with us; they are improving daily and have become more than efficient. One great help to us has been the fact that we have got one launch running again."

Through Chinese, volunteers coming forward there is now no necessity for native addresses in Des Vœux Road and other central localities to call for correspondence. A Chinese hospital at Wanchai is also arranging for the delivery of mail, and arrangements are being made to deliver all Chinese correspondence.

## [24] LOYAL WORKER.

**RECOGNITION IN COURT CASE.**

On the resumption of the case against Chanan Singh, accused of negligent driving of a motor lorry and colliding with a motor cycle ridden by Sergeant A. Nicoll, at the Central Magistracy yesterday afternoon, Mr. McEiderry said that he was satisfied that the accused was on the wrong side of the road and was responsible for the accident. In view of the fact that the accused was not used to driving this type of vehicle, and also that he had offered his services voluntarily as an emergency driver, His Worship imposed no penalty and discharged the accused.

## [25] STRIKE ITEMS

The “Wah Tsz Yat Po,” in an extra mentions the fact that a large number of members of the South China Athletic Association and the Chinese Swimming Club have volunteered for public service, and have registered with Dr. S. W. Tso, Assistant Labour Controller.

Considerable importance is placed by the Chinese masses.on the manner in which business houses, restaurants, and teahouses on both sides of the harbour have responded by re-opening their doors and catering for public wants as best they can.

Several Chinese-owned restaurants, supplying foreign-style food, in Yaumati have started business again, and others are following suit. It is expected that nearly all establishments of this class will be open as usual in a day or two.

Quite a number of nightsoil coolies of both sexes have returned to their employers and asked for work. As these are not Government servants but work in collaboration with contractors, no record can be kept.

A good many small barber shops have re-opened to-day, the staffs emerging as if from "nowhere."

## [30] GONE NORTH.

**TYPHOON CHANGES. ITS COURSE.**

Throughout yesterday the typhoon depression located at the north of the Luzon maintained the course which it had set direct for Hongkong and at 4.30 a.m. the No. 5 signal was hoisted. Later in the morning messages received indicated that it was proceeding in a more northerly direction, and the latest information to hand with regard to it is that it is between Formosa and Amoy (within 30 miles of lat. 24 degrees N; long. 119 degrees E) travelling N.N.W. Its intensity is still unknown.

Early this morning all the tied-up river shipping had left their wharves for shelter with the exception of the Paul-Beau and Charles Hardotin and the San Nam Hoi which were alongside their respective wharves. The Tung On sailed for Canton-flying the White Ensign and the Ko Chow left for Macao as a bit of private enterprise.

The following reached the local American Consulate General from the Manila Observatory:—

3.30 p.m., July 13:---Typhoon in about Long. 121 E. and Lat. 19 N., moving N.W.
