## THE NEW ERA

BRITAIN AND LOCARNO

TREATY.

IMPORTANT DEBATE.

USE OF COMMON

RATIFICATION

(Renter's Service

London: Nov. 18.

The Locarno debate opens to a crowded House of Commons. Mr. Austen Chamberlain, who was warmly cheered, moved the approval of the ratification of the Locarno Treaty. He regretted that the Opposition had notified an amendment, but pointed out that the amendment did not criticise the Locarno agreement and did not challenge the Government's policy of accepting the limited obligations thereof.

He emphasised that the Government regarded Locarno, not as the end, but the beginning of the work of appeasement and reconciliation. (Cheers).

Loearno would assist the Cour-

cil of the Assembly of the League

to bring the disarmament problem

to a successful conclusion.

He declared that Russia, according to his information, was not prepared to join the League of Nations on any terms, and emphasised that Britain remained the sole judge of what constituted the immediate danger of war.

Mr. Chamberlain regretted that it was impossible to confer with the governments of the Dominions and India before Locarno, but said they were kept fully informed of everything done.

He emphasised that their liberty of freedom and action was safeguarded specifically in the Treaty.

He concluded by appealing for a ratification of the Treaty, safeguarding peace, and laying the foundation of reconciliation and friendship with our enemies of a few years ago. (Loud cheers).

It was recognised that only their own government, acting with authority, and their own Parliament, could take for them the obligation that the Imperial Government asked the Commons to approve for Britain, but the Government hoped to discuss the matter fully at the next Imperial Conference which he hoped would not be too long delayed. (Cheers).

It was not thought possible to treat matters so important and covering so wide a field by despatch or cable. Personal contact and explanation were necessary for a true appreciation of the position.

Mr. Ramsay MacDonald severely criticised the recent speech of Mr. Ormsby. Gore, on which he said evil suggestions had been based that Germany had been brought into the new alliance against Moscow.

NOT AGAINST MOSCOW.

He asked was it the Government's view that Locarno was engineered for the purpose of uniting Western civilisation against Russia.

Mr. Chamberlain replied: "No," and "I constantly repudiated any idea of pursuing such a policy. (Ministerial cheers):

Mr. MacDonald said he was very delighted to hear it and congratulated Mr. Chamberlain on his successful efforts to create the peace spirit, but said unless the opportunity was taken swiftly to seize the chance thus opened up, the harvest would never ripen. He added that as regards the Dominions and India, he was unable to conceive a more calamitous system of conducting an Imperial foreign policy, than the method enshrined by the Locarno Fact.

## A LENIENT VIEW

BANK MANAGER'S

IRREGULAR CONDUCT.

$750 FINE FOR FORGERY.

On the grounds that in what he had done the prisoner had had no intention of defrauding the family of his dead friend or of defrauding the revenue, three of the charges of fraud brought against Wong Man-wai, a local business man described as the manager of the Ka Leung Bank, were at the Criminal Sessions this morning withdrawn.

To the charge remaining, that of having written a letter to the Hongkong Electric Company in the name of a dead man, requesting delivery of outstanding dividend warrants, well knowing the letter to be a forgery, the prisoner pleaded guilty.

Sir Henry Pollock, K.C., Attorney-General, said that the deceased man whose name had been forged was a great friend of the prisoner and on his death the prisoner had advanced sums of money to the family totalling between $50,000 and $60,000. Through delay in taking out letters of administration the prisoner seemed to have been entrusted by the family with certain scrip.

The Attorney-General referred to the very full statement which the prisoner had made showing all the transactions, also that he had come to an arrangement with the family for settling up accounts and with the revenue, estate duty in full having been paid with added penalties which brought the total sum paid out to $15,000.

Having regard to the circumstances of the case the Crown, although of the opinion that the conduct of prisoner had been both irregular and reprehensible, thought that the imposition of a fine would meet the case and that the power of imprisonment under the ordinance should not be exercised in his case.

Mr. F. C. Jenkin, counsel for the prisoner, referred to what he termed the “generous statement” of the Attorney-General and emphasised that the prisoner had not gained a certain form having mixed himself up in the business. He had at the request of the family of his dead friend looked after certain matters and the widow who was resident in Canton had given him the fullest authority.

Mr. Justice Wood emphasised the gravity, of the charge under which the prisoner made himself liable to three years' imprisonment but said that having regard to all the circumstances of the case he would impose a fine of $750.

### MR. AMERY SPEAKS

HONGKONG'S WONDERFUL

CONFIDENCE IN FUTURE.

### (Router's Service.)

London, November 18.

Mr. L. C. S. Amery is a speech at the annual dinner of the China Association declared the situation in China was the subject of anxious concern on the part of the British Government.

He mentioned "the wonderful history of Hongkong" and said he was glad to think they were able to give Hongkong the help for which she appealed. They found it not from the Imperial exchequer but in the patriotic and willing co-operation of the West Africa, Malay States and Hongkong, and Shanghai banks.

Mr. Amery was firmly convinced that Hongkong's difficulties were transient. He believed though the period of waiting might be anxious, the Chinese people's commonsense would never allow the present state of affairs to continue for ever, nor would the patience of the British Government witness for ever the impoverishment and retrogression of a great British Colony.

### DR. WU'S HELP

## HOPES OF ENDING THE BOYCOTT

MONEY EXPORT EMBARGO?

Dr. C. C. Wu (Wu Chao-chu) is now reported to be strenuously exerting his influence towards ending the present Canton boy-cott.

Among the. Kuomintang officials Dr. Wu is probably the "best. know and most respected diplomat. In the discussions between delegates of the merchants and the Canton Commission of Government Dr. Wu is reported to have used his good offices.

Another favourable report is that General Cheung Kai-shek's officers at Swatow are quietly supervising the strike pickets there and restriating them from any possible excesses.

Meanwhile the next step is eagerly awaited by Chinese merchants in Hongkong—the promised arrival of the Canton delegation to-morrow afternoon.

Passengers coming into Hongkong by train from Samchin state that the pickets just across the border have been exercising strict vigilance during the last two days in the matter of money and valuables. Those who take with them anything over $20 or excess jewellery have been sent back along the Chinese section of the Railway, or asked to deposit the surplus pending their return from Hongkong. No official notice has been given of any order by the Strike Executive regarding the export of money or valuables.
