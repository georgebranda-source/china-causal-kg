## MATTER OF PRIDE

TRIBUTE TO VOLUNTEER WORKERS

ST. JOHN AMBULANCE BRIGADE

Having proved their usefulness to the community more than ever this year in attendance on street resident cases, strike work and more particularly at Po Hing Fong where they were the first on the scene and rendered valuable aid the St. John Ambulance Brigade of Hongkong assembled at Government House this morning for the presentation of awards.

His Excellency the Governor, Mr. Cecil Clementi, stood at a table above the steps of Government House and the ambulance divisions, mostly in their khaki uniforms and the railway employee ambulance men in blue, were lined up immediately in front.

With the Governor were:—The Hon Mr. A. G. M. Fletcher, Colonial Secretary, the Hon Mr. H. T. Creasy, the Hon Mr. P. H. Holyoak, Mrs. and Miss Holyoak, the Hon Mr. C. G. Alabaster, the Hon Mr. Chow Shou-son, the Hon Mr. J. H. Kemp, the Hon Mr. R. H. Kotewall, Mr. Ho Kom-tony, Dr. W. V. M. Koch, Dr. E. Stuart Taylor, Dr. E. P. Minnett, Dr. B. C. Wong, Mr. A. R. Sutherland, Mr. L. G. F. Nightingale, the Rev. G. T. Waldegrave and Commissioner E. Ralphs. In charge of the ambulance brigade were Corps Superintendent, Mr. A. Morris, Division Superintendents, Messrs. J. Ralston (Kowloon division) and A. eleArculli (Indian division).

Commissioner Ralphs, in addressing His Excellency, said:—As you, sir, have taken a very active part in developing the work of the S.J.A.B. in Ceylon, it is not necessary for me to enlarge upon the value and importance of ambulance training and I shall touch only lightly on the work of the Hongkong Corps of the St. John Ambulance Brigade during the year.

## Brigude's Work

In June last, we increased our membership and formed two new divisions or companies,—an Indian division consisting entirely of members of the Indian community, in charge of Mr. E. Arculli, with Dr. Samy as surgeon, and the Kowloon division, consisting of Europeans under Mr. Ralston. At the same time the membership of the Chinese Y.M.C.A. was largely increased, as was that of the Kowloon-Canton railway, both very active and very efficient divisions.

The members of the corps have made every effort to become proficient so successfully that all—to the number of 100—have without exception been examined and passed as “efficient” for the year, and the examining surgeons have reported that a high standard has been attained. I may mention here that every member is obliged to take a course of instruction and pass an efficiency test annually, so that our uniform is a guarantee that the wearer is really proficient in ambulance work.

## On The Spot

The members are always ready to respond to a call to render first aid to the injured; as far as public duty is concerned they provided—as in previous years—a detachment for duty at the races, and took charge of the Government motor ambulances during July and August last, keeping a squad of men on duty, night and day, continuously in both Hongkong and Kowloon. Numerous accident cases have been attended during the year, the most serious being the Po Hing Fong disaster, when the full strength of the Corps was engaged for several days. I may mention here that while we were on parade yesterday a messenger arrived in hot haste to report that a member of a football team playing on the Sookumpoo ground had broken his leg. The requisite number of our men were told to fall out and render assistance. Twenty-minutes later they were back in the ranks having treated the case, under the direction of one of our corps surgeons, who was on parade, and conveyed the patient to an adjacent Hospital.

I would here pay a tribute to the many medical men in the Colony who have helped us and who are inviting in their labours in training our members in first aid work.

EUROPEAN LADY SUED AT KOWLOON.

WITHOUT NOTICE.

DISMISSA1

A Chinese coolie and an amah summoned Mrs. McKim, of Humphrey's Flat, for a month's wages at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning. ### “KING OF KINGS.”

A NEW RULER FOR PERSIA.

REMARKABLE CAREER.

(Renter's Service)

Teheran, Dec. 13.

The Constituent Assembly has unanimously appointed Reza Khan Pehlevi as King of Persia and has decided that the sovereignty shall remain the Pehlevi Dynasty.

Reza Khan Pehlevi at 49 has had a remarkable career.

Born of a middle class family he entered the army as a private at the age of 15, rising to be one of the senior officers in the Cossack division by 1921.

When he marched on and captured Teheran he became commander-in-Chief and War Minister.

He assumed the Premiership in 1923, when he desired to establish a republic but was opposed by the clergy, whose support he has now apparently gained.

He proceeded to subjugate insurgent tribes, from whom he exacted taxes.

For the first time, in half a century he dismissed all foreign army instructors: but engaged foreigners to reorganise the legal, postal and financial systems, and became head of the provisional government on the recent deposition of the Shah.

## TEA LEAF FIRE

ANOTHER WEST POINT

RIAZE

The Central Fire Brigade received a call at about 12.30 this morning from No. 7 Police Station to deal with a fire at First Street, West Point. Equipments from Kennedy Town and Wanchai also assisted.

A large quantity of tea leaves stored on the second (top) floor of house No. 86 had been set well alight before the master of the shop and an assistant, who were sleeping, realised that the floor was on fire:

The building was of more stable construction than the ordinary type of Chinese tenement houses, and this enabled the firemen to deal with the fire from the floor itself. A hose was also brought into action from the opposite house with good effect, the fire being extinguished within half an hour.

The stock of the tea merchant, Tung Chung, together with his belongings were destroyed. The goods were insured with the General Insurance Company for $5,000, another policy covering the furniture for $500. Some damage by water was also done to the belongings on the first floor, used as a dwelling, and an eating house on the ground floor. A portion of the woodwork on the second floor, which was partly of concrete, was also burnt away, but the damage to the building was not great.

A lighted cigarette carelessly thrown away is believed to have been the cause of the outbreak.

## PEACE MOVE

CANTON MERCHANTS OFFER?

LOAN TO END STRIKE:

"Extras" have been issued by a Canton vernacular paper, says a merchant who arrived here yesterday and was interviewed by a Hongkong Chinese reporter, stating that a large sum of money will be advanced by a Canton merchant—to be ultimately repaid by the Canton Government—to bring about a settlement of the strike and boycott.

After reviewing the steps already taken and the appointment of delegates, the publisher of the "extra" says that he understands that a certain wealthy merchant in Canton is willing to advance this large sum, which will be repaid in due course, after the strike is ended, by the Provincial Government.

Mr. Kan Kum-shek also arrived here yesterday, and stated that he came to ascertain when the Hong-kong "fraternal" party would leave.

Mr. Yeung Sai-ngzaam is expected to return to Canton early this week.

The list of names for inclusion in the party closed yesterday. According to present expectations the party may leave on Thursday or Friday.

Mr. Kan had nothing new to report in connection with the appointment of delegates by Canton.

## MOTOR ACCIDENTS

COOLIE INJURED. FATALLY.

TWO MINOR MISHAPS.

A Chinese earth coolie died at the Kwong Wah Hospital yesterday evening from injuries which he received earlier in the day in a motor accident at Tsun Wan Road.

The driver of the motor car in a statement alleged that the coolie was crossing the road and hesitated in front of his car. The front wheel struck the man down and he fell on his head.

In another accident near the “Star” ferry station yesterday morning, a European lady travelling in a ricsha was thrown out when a motor car coming behind collided with the vehicle and overturned it. The lady escaped injury, but the ricsha was slightly damaged.

On Saturday afternoon a Chinese girl received slight bruises when a motor car knocked her down at Queen's Road East near the Wanchai Market.
