# CORRESPONDENCE.

## Hongkong Education

(To the Editor of the China Mail).

## -

Sir.—This “certificated Teachers” controversy seems to get more futile every day. May I attempt to bring it back to sanity with a few points with which I think everyone will agree.

1. If a man who was not oriously unfit through his own fault, started giving advice as to how to keep one's health, he would probably be discredited. So too if a man says, "I don't hold it with this here education, but what I does say is……," I think we should take his opinions with considerable reserve.

2. It is dangerous to generalise. I remember suffering at the hands of a wrangler who could not explain conic sections, but that should not make me condemn wranglers as teachers. So too I know of the headmaster of a Board School who has written one of the most illuminating books of elementary literary criticism ever published, and who has obviously all the attributes of the perfect teacher. The only hope is to plump for the system that gives the most reasonable chance of success, talking things by and large.

3. I think that most local educational authorities in England would jump at the young enthusiastic Oxford honours man if they could get him at the price. It looks as though Hongkong were "offering a fair wage if we can get such", or perhaps the said hypothetical enthusiast is attracted by the glamour of the East and the chance of contact with an unknown civilization—if so, good luck to him: he will certainly be more likely to teach the Chinese something than the man who "is not here for his health".

Chinese, with their amazing educational traditions to which we have no sort of parallel in Europe is not the same thing as drumming the three Rs into the rising generation in England. Other things being equal, I think the only hope of achieving anything out here is to have even who have "read, i.e. not to remember... not to criticise... but to weigh and consider". (I quote Eacon from memory, probably inaccurately): men whose first and constant thought will be "What, if anything, have we to teach them? Is if worth while? How far rain the best in Chinese culture be combined with the best in European? Am I to aim it turning out typists or engineers or good citizen?"...and so forth and so on. I am far from saying that such a state of mind is incompatible with the holding of a certificate, but I certainly think that a long probation in educational technique in England might quite possibly be an actual handicap. No one gets into a cut sooner than a teacher if he does not keep a constant watch on himself. (No one in this controversy seems to have quoted our University, which offers the most illuminating comrades possible on the points now at issue).

## FOE OF BOLSHEVISM

## EORIS SAVINKOFF COMMITTS SUICIDE

### (Reuiter's Service.)

Boris Savinkoff has committed suicide in prison.

A few hours previously he wrote to M. Djerjinsky, head of the Police, bagging for his liberation. He received a hopeless answer and threw himself from the fifth-floor window, being killed instantly.

[Foris Savinkoff was a well-known anti-Bolshevik who was alleged to be the leading spirit of the most counter-revolutionary plots, in recent years, operating outside Russia.] He was arrested on Russian territory on August 20. He was tried and sentenced to death, but recommended to energy in consequence of a remarkable statement, in which he said that he considered all his counter-revolutionary activities were mistaken. He recognised the justice of the revolution and acknowledged that he was guilty of the charges brought against him, adding: I recognise the power of the Soviet and say, 'He who loves the Russian nation must recognise the Soviet power.' I do not ask for pity.'

## ARMS EVIL

## VALUE OF PUBLICITY AS DETERPENT

A GENEVA DEBATE.

(Reuter's Service.)

The Romanian, Polish and Persian delegates in particular displayed much anxiety during the important discussion of the general committee of the arms conference with regard to the obligation of giving publicity concerning supplies of arms.

They contended they should be exempted from affording information as long as neighbouring country, meaning Russia, was not party to the proposed convention.

Mr. Burton declared the United States in all circumstances would give the fullest publicity to arms shipments. The United States Government and people would be very disappointed if the convention did not sufficiently provide for publicity.

#### E. Discussion was adjourned. EARLIER CABLES

GENEVA, May 12.

The technical committee of the arms conference has adopted the British proposal to exclude warships and aeroplanes from the category of objects of which the export is not allowed. The decision must be ratified by the general committee. The legal committee pronounced in favour of the prohibition of the export of arms etc., to private individuals, but favoured its being allowed case of governments under certain conditions.

## HOW AMERICA STANDS

Official circles believe that the action of the American delegation at the Geneva arms conference in supporting British motion to exclude, warship and aircraft from the operation of the proposed arms treaty, represents a desire to facilitate the conference reaching an agreement rather than any particular attitude of the Washington government on the question immediately involved. It is understood that the delegation did not discuss the status of aeroplanes and warships prior to their departure for Geneva.

## EARLIER CABLES!!

While the bare announcement that the State Department is informally negotiating with France regarding the French debt is the only official information available, it is generally understood that increased pressure for the French settlement of the obligation was recently exerted in several informal ways. It appears that the French Ministers who recently conferred with the Under Secretary of the Treasury, Mr. Winston Churchill, on his visit to France, were clearly informed by the American government that it is desired that the French funding commission should be sent to Washington at the earliest moment in order to discuss the debt settlement with an American committee headed by Mr. Mellon at Washington. ## HIGHER TAXES

MEETING FRANCE DEFICIT.

(Reuter's Service.)

Submitting his financial proposals, M. Caillaux, the Minister of Finance told the Finance Committee that France's share of the Dawes annuities should be earmarked for the purpose of completing the reconstruction of the devastated regions and the redemption of inter-Allied debts instead of being included in the credits of the Budget.

He announced that the Government proposed to issue a loan liquidating the Dawes annuities. He estimated the deficit in the 1925 budget at four milliards, for which he was half a milliard in respect of departmental economies. Three and a half would have to be found and these would be raised inter alia by an increase in the indirect taxes on tobacco and alcohol and an increase in certain schedules of income, notably those affecting securities and agricultural profits, also by the creation of regions reportedly in connection with oil and insurance.
