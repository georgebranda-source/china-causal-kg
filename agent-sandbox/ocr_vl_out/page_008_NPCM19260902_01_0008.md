## RETURN TO CANTON

## CHINESE GRADUATE FROM AMERICA

DIPLOMACY AND FACT

## 一 

## 5511 

An interesting passenger by the s.s. "President Pierce" which arrived in Hongkong this morning is Dr. Tsun Kit-wong, a native of Canton who for eleven years has been away from his native land studying law in America.

In an interview reproduced in an American newspaper, prior to his departure, Dr. Tsun Kit-wong is credited with having stated that "militarism in China is dying a natural death and the Democratic principles" of "Government based on the American constitution are being firmly rounded. The military leaders in China are fast losing their power, while at the same time the diplomatic Government of Canton is becoming stronger every day. In a very few years Canton will be the seat of a Chinese diplomatic Government based on the American form."

The same paper states that "Dr. Tsun Kit-wong came to America" with the idea of carrying back to his people the methods of Government which have made this nation one of the world's greatest. His ambition is to draft such a Constitution to which it has devoted much study.

"Seen this morning by a China Mail" representative: Dr. Taun Kit-wong stated that it was an entirely new China to which he was returning. He had not studied the political situation sufficiently to express an opinion but it had come as a great surprise to him to learn that on returning after eleven years to visit his father in Canton, so many difficulties in the way of passports from various powers in Canton were necessary.

It is Dr. Tsun Kit-wong's intention, once having visited his father, to go to Shanghai where he intends setting up in practice. He graduated from Canton High School and went to America in 1916, entering the Stockton High School. He graduated from the Baldwin-Wallace College, Ohio and finally from the legal department of the De Paul University of Chicago.

Hongkong theatre-goers will be pleased to learn that our old friends—Mr. R. B. Salisbury's "Quaints"—are going great guns in their present tour of India.

## HEROIN TRAFFIC

SEIZURE ON FRENCH MAIL

STEAMER

JAPANESE PLEADS GUILTY,

As a result of the raid by revenue officers on the French mail steamer "Angers" yesterday, a Japanese passenger on the vessel was "charged before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning on two counts under the Dangerous Drugs Ordinance".

Johejiro Tanaka, 37 years of age, described as a tailor by profession, pleaded guilty to charges of unlawful possession of 8,200 ounces of heroin and importing the drug.

This record quantity of heroin, packed in cardboard boxes, was found in seven cabin trunks in the baggage room claimed by the defendant, who had booked from Marseilles to Kobe.

At the Home market price, the seizure would be worth more than $100,000, but out here about three times as much.

Mr. J. D. Lloyd (Superintendent of Imports and Exports) asked for a committal of the defendant to the Sessions on both charges.

The case was fixed for hearing at 11:30 a.m. on Saturday.

## INDIANS STORY OF GEMS & IDOLS OF GOLD

New York, July 23.—A weird story of a enve filled with idols made of the purest gold comes from Mexico.

It is related by the director of the National Museum in Mexico City, who has commissioned a party of explorers to seek for the cave, which is situated somewhere in the Santa Clara Mountains.

The director says that some time ago an old Indian visited his office and offered to lead him to the mysterious cave. The man explained that he was a charcoal "burner," and happened to come upon the treasure while seeking shelter from a storm.

Entering a long tunnel, he suddenly found himself in a cave, which was lighted by a ray of light from the roof.

The ray, falling on two huge idols, caused them to shine as the sun, and at first filled him with terror.

The cave, which was of large dimensions (so the man continued), was filled with golden images and armour of gold, set with precious stones.

On leaving the cave the covered the entrance to prevent its rediscovery, and had journeyed to the capital to enlist the assistance of the museum director, whom he offered to lend to the spot.

The director was unable at the time to accompany him. When later he sought the Indian he found that the old man had died.

### "WENT TO SLEEP."

## BRITISH SUBJECT FOUND ON SHIP

"LOOKING FOR UNCLE."

Victor Simpson, a British subject, was charged this morning before Mr. J. H. B. Nihill at the Kowloon Magistracy with having had a "free transportation" from Shanghai to Hongkong without the consent of the owners or master of the as. "Linan" of the China Navigation Company.

The defendant, in relating his story to the Magistrate, said that he came from Tsingtao en route to Hongkong via Shanghai with $500 and on arriving at Shanghai all his money was stolen from him. He made several attempts to see the British consul at Shanghai, but the latter postponed his interview several times, while he ran short of funds.

He sold some of his personal belongings to support himself, continued defendant. To continue

his trip to the colony, he appealed to the second officer of the steamer who asked him to wait. According to the defendant, he was so tired that he went to sleep underneath a life-boat while he

The master of the "Linan" gave evidence, corroborated the testimony of the defendant as to he circumstances under which he was found.

## BOBBED HAIR

## CRAZE ENDED IN BERLIN

Berliners, like gentlemen, "prefer blondes."

This is shown (writes the Berlin correspondent of the London "Daily Chronicle." under date of June 10) by the fact that Berlin has chosen a "blondine" as the city's "Summer Queen of Beauty." She is Fraulein Grete Reinwald, a pretty young firm actress.

Fashion journals are more interested in the "fact that the queen" has not bobbed or shingled air.

Little more than a year ago the short hair craze reached here. Almost in a night it captured the fair sex of Berlin.

It was exceedingly unpopular with the male population. Callous employers dismissed their women secretaries, clerks and typists for no other reason than that they had bobbed or shingled hair. Hundreds of engagements must have been broken off for the same reason. Yet, for weeks, there were queues outside the barbers' shops.

But Fraulein Grete's election is a sign of the times. There is little doubt that she was chosen because she had not yielded to the "un-womanly and un-German fashion."

The reaction against short hair has set in with that impetus with which the new fashion originally conquered Berlin. Actresses and film stars are flocking to lonely holiday resorts—determined to come back capable of playing Juliet or Marguerite without false hair.

Though the burber laments that the “ladiés” salon” is now almost deserted, he has begun to do n' roaring business in hair restorers!

## SOVIET CUNNING

## SIGNS A TREATY WITH AFGHANISTAN

NON-AGGRESSIVE PACT.

Moncov, Sept.

A Russo-Afghan Treaty of neutrality and mutual non-aggression has been signed with great ceremony at Pagman, the Summer residence of the King of Afghanistan, by M. Stark, the Soviet Plenipotentiary, and the Afghan Foreign Minister.

It provides for neutrality in the event of an armed conflict between either and third Powers, mutual non-aggression and abstention from entering into hostile agreements with third Powers, and mutual non-interference in home affairs.—Reuter.

##### ... FATAL FIRE

## NEGROES CUT OFF IN A TENEMENT

## LEAP FOR LIFE

Five negroes are dead and one missing as the result of a fire in a five-storey negro tenement, the occupants of which were cut off from escape owing to a deep trench, which was cut in front of the building for a new underground railway, which prevented the fire-men hoisting ladders.

Many of the negroes jumped from the windows and were more or less seriously injured.—Reuter.

## VIOLENT STORM

LIGHTNING'S HAVOC IN LONDON.

TRAMCAR ON FIRE.

Rugby, Sept. 1.

A brief but violent thunderstorm, which broke over London this morning, did considerable damage. "There were many narrow escapes, but no lives were lost.

The streets in the low-lying localities were flooded, and the electric railway service between the City and Kew was disorganised owing to the current being cut off through the flooding of the track.

The buildings struck included elementary school buildings and ten houses.

Two tramway cars, of which one was set on fire, were also struck, and owing to the floods it became necessary temporarily to change the route of certain bus services.—British Wireless Service.
