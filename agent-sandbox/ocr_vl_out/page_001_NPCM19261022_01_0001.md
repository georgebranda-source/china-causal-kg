## POST-STRIKE TRADE

Opposition at Wuchow Withdrawn.

OBEYING CANTON'S ORDER.

Prediction of Improvement by Next Week.

Instructions by the Strike Committee in Canton have resulted in the withdrawal of "boycott" restrictions at Wuchow.

Gratification is derived from the fact that, in response to a telegram asking for guidance, Anton replied that cargo from a Hong Kong ship could be landed without hindrance.

An expert on Canton, and river shipping predicts with a good deal of confidence that next week will show material improvement in the process of trade resumption with Hong Kong.

By then, he thinks, opportunities will be forthcoming for Canton traders to put out "feelers" and it is felt that there will be no cause to discourage them.

Latest reports follow:—

The g.s. "Fook On" was delayed several days at Wuchow before her cargo could be landed.

Cargo coolies and sampan men (members of the transport union) refused to work the "Fook On" at first until an assurance was cabled (in reply to a request) by the Canton Strike Committee that "the boycott was over."

As reported yesterday, surtax was not collected and expectations are that steamers will now be bringing down cargo freely from Wuchow to Hong Kong.

It is noteworthy that the one boat has left the Canton-Wuchow run to return to the Hong Kong-Wuchow trade.

Livestock, poultry, produce and firewood are shipped extensively down from Wuchow.

From a reliable source the "China Mail" learns that about 20 labourers, suspected to be strike pickets, have been active during the last few days.

The allegation against them is that they are attempting to prevent sampans and junks from intercourse with incoming British steamers, having even gone to the extent of cutting the ropes of the native craft when such were made fast to British steamers.

Both in Hong Kong and Canton, Chinese merchants have been inquiring about shipping cargo by the s.s. "Taishan" next week when she takes up the night run. In the past night-boats have carried nearly all the cargo, simply because they have practically all day in which to load and discharge.

In addition to strictly native cargo, big shipments of produce (such as silk) are carried by the night steamers, for re-export from Hong Kong.

Compradore departments and clerks (where re-engaged) are said to be working smoothly.

In some instances, Chinese servants have been asked to put up deposits to guarantee their good faith.

## NARROW ESCAPE

## CYCLIST THROWN UNDER TRAM CAR

EMERGES ALIVE.

A young Chinese riding a push bicycle had a miraculous escape from death opposite the Admiral Line offices at Des-Voeux Road about 8:30 this morning.

The cyclist, dressed in European clothes, was coming into town when his mine skidded on the tramway track with the watering of the road in the morning and was thrown in the middle of the track in front of tram car No. 17 proceeding to Causeway Bay.

The "motorman" promptly applied his brakes, but the car had already gone half its length over the youth before it was brought to a halt.

Passengers on the car and in another which had crossed shortly before the accident gathered round and were surprised to see the youth scramble out alive.

The youth had a cut over the eye and was bleeding through the nose, but beyond this he was none the worse for the mishap and was able to walk to the dispensary to have his wounds dressed.

The machine fell clear of the track and was slightly damaged. Two European schoolboys assisted in straightening the handle bar of the machine and the cyclist wheeled it along.

## NO PICKETING

Strikers Forbidden to Carry Firearms

As the result of extensive inquiries made in Canton city, a Chinese merchant assures the "China Mail" that there is no picketing whatsoever, nor are any agitators going the rounds of shops to promote a boycott.

There is no intimidation but workers who are members of unions are still afraid to handle British and/or Hong Kong cargoes.

The Commissioner of Police (Li Cheung-tat) has forbidden pickets to go about with arms.

This order is to prevent the possibility of trouble breaking out—"Wah Tsz Yat Po."

## TRADE INDICATIONS

Price Fluctuations of Various Commodities.

Prices of various commodities of trade have changed somewhat this week, partly because of the withdrawal of the blockade "but mainly on account of the severe drop in exchange.

Flour: Stocks are plentiful in Canton, because merchants there have been getting direct shipments from Shanghai and American (Pacific coast) ports. The drop in exchange had resulted in local prices appreciating about 10 cents per bag of 50 lbs.

Vegetables: Additional sources of supply being now available to Hong Kong through the resumption of shipping, prices have shown a fairly substantial drop during the last two days. Shipments are now coming here regularly.

Firewood: Prices are still high. Before the strike, Hong Kong received the majority of the firewood export from Wuchow. Although ships have come down from Wuchow, they brought no firewood.

The Diplomatic Corps has for several days past discussed the Canton surtaxes. It is understood that the matter was again referred to the home Governments yesterday.

It is believed that if unanimity is reached identical notes will be presented through the Consuls at Canton

Chinese official opposition to the surtaxes is growing owing to the belief that the proceeds will be used sooner or later for the war against the North.

Unofficial opinion in foreign circles is also increasingly stronger against the surtaxes fearing that if they are even tacitly admitted they will soon be increased to ten or twenty per cent, thus strangling trade almost as effectually as the boycott did.
