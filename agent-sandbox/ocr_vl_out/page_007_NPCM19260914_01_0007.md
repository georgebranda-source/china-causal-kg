## AT GENEVA:

## SEATS FOR NON-PERMANENT MEMBERS

## CHINA INCLUDED:

It has been decided that seats on the Council of the League will be allotted to Belgium, Sweden, Romania, Poland, China, Chile, Cuba or Columbia, Uruguay, and possibly Finland.

Belgium, Uruguay, and Sweden will be re-elected for one year; Romania, Chile, and Cuba or Columbia for two years; China and Poland, three years; and possibly Finland one year.—Reuter.

Genevi, Sept. 13.

The original scheme for the election of non-permanent members of the Council has been modified so that in the event of the resignation of a member there will be a by-election, whilst, re-eligibility will be decided separately by a secret bullet. Any member may ask the Assembly to direct that there be no discussion for re-eligibility.

Elections to the nine non-permanent seats will be held simultaneously.

The Business Committee of the Assembly will decide the procedure for fixing the number of years for a country's tenure of office—whether three years, two years, or one year.—Reuter.

## IN BANDITS' HANDS

## MEXICANS SEIZE RICH AMERICAN

## CAR HELD UP

Mexico City, Sept. 13. Federal soldiers are pursuing bandits who are holding as prisoner Mr. Jacob Rosenthal, a retired New York merchant, after holding up a chair in which he was motoring with a party (including three other Americans) 40 miles from Mexico City as the party was returning from the sensible resort of Cuernayaca.

The bandits levelled their rifles, ordered the car to stop, took all the money and jewels, seized Rosenthal and dragged him into the neighbouring woods.

The curious fact is that no ransom is demanded, and the authorities are puzzled to know why Rosenthal was specially selected as prisoner.—Reuter's American Service

## LONGEST TUBE

À GERMAN DELEGATION.

The extension of one of London's tube, railways was officially opened to-day by Colonel Moore Brabazon, Parliamentary Secretary to the Ministry of Transport.

Sir Alexander Muddiman, moving for its consideration, pointed out that the Bill attempted the creation of a united Indian Bar by removing the distinctions which existed in the statue between Vakils and Barristers.

Barrister members, headed by Sir Harf Singh Gour, vigorously opposed the inclusion of two clauses which sought to deprive Barristers of their long enjoyed rights of seniority and pre-audience over Vakils in Indian High Courts. Before the Bill, as amended, passed the third reading, Sir Alexander Muddiman emphasised that success in working the Act depended on the goodwill and not on the Trade Union jealousy of those leading Barristers and Vakils who would man the various Councils.

## LANDSLIP IN BURMA

## OFFICIAL REPORT OF CASUALTIES

Simla, August 28.

The Raj, in a communication regretting reports of the serious landslip at Tiger Camp, Bawdwin Minx, in the Northern Shan States, Burma, states that the landslip occurred on the evening of August 16, and devastated about seventy labourers' dwellings.

## Still Hopeful

That the whole present civil warfare affecting the provinces of Hunan and Hupeh amounts to anti-Red struggle is the contention of General Sun Chuan-fang as estimated to his officers and related to a local Chinese journalist recently at Shanghai.

General Sun is of the opinion that the motive which prompted the Allied troops to be engaged in a life-and-death fight is not one after private gains nor selfish ends, and for this simple reason he has sciemnly declared to adhere to Marshal Wu Pol-fu and render him assistance, both actual and constructive, whenever occasion demands.

However, General Sun still expresses the hope that the war will only be carried on in those two provinces, so that his territorial sovereignty along the Yangtze will not be encroached upon. Shanghai Times.

## SULTAN CAME BACK

NATIVES THOUGHT HE WAS A CAPTIVE.

Paris, August 11.—Believing that Sultan Mulai Yusef of Morocco (who has recently been visiting Paris) had been enticed into captivity and that he would never return from France to his native country, the largest assemblage of Moors that has ever greeted a ruler awaited at Rabat (Morocco) to-day to make sure that it was really the Sultan who was returning to his palace and not a substitute.

Ever since the Sultan left for his tour of France a month ago wild reports had been spread around the native markets that he had walked into a trap and that the French would hold him as a hostage and might even banish him to some remote island.

Mountaineers from the Atlas country and from the Riff, and white robed tribesmen from the scrub and the desert welcomed him. It was a gathering that, for size and diversity, Morocco had never seen before.

## IRISH CENSUS

## GREAT DECREASE IN POPULATION

London, August 23.

Surprising figures are disclosed in the Irish Free State census in April. The population since 1911 has decreased by 167,000 to 2,972,000. During the same period the population of Northern Ireland has increased by nearly 6,000 to 1,256,322.

There are 973 females to every 1,000 males in the Free State and 1,066 females to each 1,000 males in Northern Ireland.—Times of Ceylon.

## PEKING'S STEP

## ENDING A CUSTOMS TREATY

The "China Express and Telegraph" writing on August 19 says:

It is reported from Geneva on August 17 that China has taken the first step towards the denunication of the so-called 'unequal' Customs Treaties, despite the negotiations at Peking by officially 'notifying Brussels that tariff pacts of extr-territoriality will automatically be terminated on October 29.

Commenting on this, the correspondent of the "Morning Post" says: The action of the special Chinese Tariff Commission carries out threats made recently by both the Chinese Minister at Rome and the Chinese Minister to the United States. According to official information received by Chinese diplomats at Geneva and throughout Europe, Peking is preparing to denounce all existing treaties three months before the extension period arrives. The Chinese have refused the request of the Powers for delay pending the outcome of additional pourparters. The abrogation of the Treaty with Belgium will be followed by similar action in regard to Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch, Italian, French, British, and American treaties.

The Chinese are now offering Belgium the opportunity to sign a complete Customs Treaty on lines of equality and reciprocity, similar to those between China and Austria and Germany. If she refuses, the Chinese will extend the boycott now aimed at the British and Japanese to various Belgian exports. According to Geneva authorities, Peking, despite the claim that China has no stable Government, is within its legal rights in repudiating Customs' extra-territorial treaties. Significantly, however, the Chinese are offering Belgium the same reciprocal treatment accorded to the Germans, thereby permitting importations at the same tariff as levied on most favoured nations. Although empowered to false tariffs on German goods, China is extending the lowest duties in order to encourage trade competition with Britain, Japan and America, and thereby forcing Customs revision.
