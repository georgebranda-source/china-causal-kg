## WEDNESDAY, SEPTEMBER 30, 1925

## NEEDS OF THE PRESS

IMPORTANT EMPIRE

CONFERENCE

VISCOUNT BURNHAM'S VIEW.

(Reuter's Service.)

Melbourne, Sept. 29.

Lord Forster, Governor-General of Australia, opening the Imperial Press Conference, emphasised the immense importance of the Conference for the Empire and Australia.

Mr. S. M. Bruce, Premier, in a forty minutes' speech, dwelt on the Empire and the importance of the subjects to be discussed by the Conference.

He emphasised that Empire communication was most important as it was vital to maintain the communications of the Empire in order to promote better understanding between its component parts.

The Australian Government desired to see the cost of communications reduced to the lowest possible level.

Viscount Burnham was elected President amid acclamation.

He said the Conference, though unofficial, had the approbation and good wishes of all the Governments of the Empire, and likened the Empire Press Union to the League of Nations.

He recalled the work of the Union for the advancement of the Press, especially in obtaining adequate recognition of Dominion newspapers by the authorities at Home, and breaking down obsolete barriers, so that Departments of State and Houses of Parliament now received Dominion newspaper representatives on equal terms with the British Press; but the best work, he said, had been in cheapening and accelerating the means of communication, especially the recent reduction of the cable rate to Australia to sixpence, which was an earnest of better things in the future which the competition, between directional wireless and renovated cables was bound to produce.

He described broadcasting as possible real rival of newspapers

## MILL STRIKE

## A POLICY OF DRIFT AT BOMBAY

(Router's Service.)

In the absence of a serious attempt at a settlement the mill strike appears likely to continue indefinitely.

Only three mills are working, and numbers of hands have gone home.

The men's leaders are urging the Government to appoint a committee to investigate the position of the mill industry.

## LABOUR CONGRESS

MINES NATIONALISATION SCHEME.

ARMY RESERVE STORM.

(Reuter's Service.)

London, Sept. 29.

The vexed question of the recruitment of an army supplementary to the service which raised such an outcry among Trade's Unions early in the year was again raised at the Labour Conference when a resolution calling on all members of the Party to refuse to join was unanimously adopted.

The Miners' leader Mr. A. J. Cook announced that a joint committee of the Trade Union Congress and the Labour Party and Parliamentary Committee, were drafting a scheme of nationalisation mines for submission to the Labour movement subsequent to presentation to parliament.

The main principles were settled but the question of confiscation or compensation had not yet been decided.

## OPEN LETTER

## CHINESE ADDRESS TO SOLDIERY

THE CANTONESE PEOPLE.

Mr. Leo Chung-jun's second letter to the soldiers in Kwang-tung has been translated and sent to the "China Mail" for publication, inter alia, as follows:—

Since the arrival of Red Russians in Kwangtung through the invitation of Cheung Kai-shek, Wong Ching-wai and others, they are regarded as “wolves getting admission into the house.” The Cantonese version that Cheung and Wong are fond of Communism which would menace Kwangtung does not sufficiently describe their faults and disclose their secrets. Speaking truly, they are taking the country's goods to be changed for. Soviet Russia's money and they are under the shelter of Soviet Russia. They are obeying the order of aliens.

Though I am not a wise man, I cannot keep in silence. I have repeatedly applied to my elders and brethren for their prompt action to save themselves from the fire and the deep and have earnest expectation towards the forceful soldiers. I have urged them to expel the bad elements by united strength and to relieve the villages without taking discrimination.

Now the Kwangtung Army of Chiu Yeung and Mui Yuen is advancing westward. The dawn of light appears in the clouds of East Kwangtung and the evil mist will soon dissipate. Then the thirty million people of Kwangtung will come to life again. The Kwangtung Army will not hesitate to relieve their brethren from danger and the Kwangtung people will prepare to welcome them having for long hoped to do so.

My elders and brethren, you should get up quickly to follow them. Do not put your hands into your sleeves and look on. If every man can exert his ability, the campaign to expel Reds will be successful. It is the action of self-salvation and not helping others. My elders and brethren are aware of this idea, of which I need not say any more. What I want to state is that all soldiers in Kwangtung, whether hosts or guests, who are being supplied by the Kwangtung people, have to do their duty by protecting the people. Seeing that the people are in such a wretched position, how can you delay your operations to relieve them? Moreover the Reds are pressing forward to exterminate the dissenters. Can you bend down your head to suffer; the rough treatment of Russian officers? To fight for the Cantonese who will extol your merit is better than to serve under the bad elements who are hated by many persons. It is unnecessary to argue which is right or wrong. To relieve Kwangtung is to relieve your country and to help others is to help yourselves. Our military officers must have understood this point. I hope you will rise up quickly to help the soldiers in the East River and to expel the bad elements from the Five-Rams City. You may attack them from different directions or start the battle in an inner part. The present time is the only opportunity of settling the whole province of Kwangtung and it is also the day on which the Cantonese are applying for your relief. If you still take no notice of their supplications, you will not be allowed to keep your present position on the other hand you will be eventually exterminated by those whom you keep company with. Every person beags the responsibility of the rise and fall of his province. You cannot force the public to follow your opinion on your certainly can choose the way of popularity. I expose my views for your decision.

## Chairman's Speech

The reasons for the proposed amalgamation were placed before the meeting by the Chairman, who said:—

Gentlemen,—As you will have learned from the resolutions just read, it is proposed to amalgamate the Tug and Lighter Company with the China Provident Loan and Mortgage Company, Limited, for which purpose

it is necessary to wind up our Company and appoint a Liquida-tor, to fill which post it is proposed to appoint Mr. Sydney Hampden Ross. I now lay on the table for your inspection a proposed agreement for the transfer of the assets, liabilities and business of the Tug and Lighter Company to the China Provident Loan and Mortgage Company, Limited. The terms of this agreement are to the effect that in return for the transfer to it of the whole of the asse and liabilities of the Tug and lighter Company, including its profits to date, the China Provident Loan and Mortgage Company, Limited, shall allot to the shareholders of the Tug and Lighter Company up to 60,000 shares of the China Provident Company of the nominal value of $600,000, in the proportion of three shares of the Provident Company to five shares of the Tug and Lighter Company, to rank for dividend from January 1, 1925. This, I think, is a very fair arrangement for both parties, but I must tell you at the same time that I am also interested in the proposal as chairman of the Provident Company, and shall not therefore vote on the resolutions. You may ask why, if the Company is now working at a profit, we should sell out to the Provident Company; but we have only made money since the outbreak of the boycott when cargo boats were very scarce, and should only have to face a struggle with the Provident Company later on. when business resumes its norma course. In every way it seems to us that the arrangement is for the mutual benefit of both Companies and as such we put it before you

## HONAM DEATH

## SECOND ENGINEER IOST OVERBOARD

A EONCKONG RESIDENT

A distressing fatality involving the loss of the second engineer of the s.s. "Honum," Mr. David Mason, was reported on the return of that ship to Hongkong from Canton yesterday afternoon. It occurred in the early hours of yesterday morning as the "Honum" was lying at anchor off Shameen.

The Second Engineer had attended ashoro n. performance of the "Broadensters" and was at the time (shortly after 1 a.m.) about to make fast to the "Honum" the painter of the sumpan which conveyed him from the shore. He is stated to have lost his balance and fallen overboard, disappearing from view at once.

As soon as those on board were aware of the disaster the alarm was raised and gunbouts in the vicinity trained searchlights on the spot, without, however, resulting in any sign of the missing engineer who, it is presumed, was drowned.

Mr. Mason had not been long in the employ of the Steambolt Company but was an old resident of Hongkong and has many friends here. The deepest sympathy is extended to the relatives, who include Mrs. D. Harvey, of Kowloon.

Apart from this distressing mishap the voyage of the s.s. "Honum" was without untoward incident. The same conditions prevail as heretofore, namely the strike pickets continue to patrol with vigilance in the vicinity of the steamer and prevent passengers being taken off. Chinese merchants and others who had made their own arrangements were able to leave the ship but some of the poorer class Chinese passengers were unable to do so and were obliged to return.

22 Europeans and 61 Chinese were carried on the voyage to Hongkong.

## ELECTRIC LIGHT

## SUB-TENANT AND PAYMENT QUERY

MR. J. M. XAVIER SUED.

The price of current supplied by the China Light and Power Company, Ltd., to Mr. J. M. Xavier formed the subject of proceedings at the Summary Court this morning.

The claim was for $16.15 (amount used during July, August and September).

Defendant admitted liability for September but stated that for the other months the principle tenant, Mr. Britto, from whom he hired the room was liable.

The plaintiff's solicitor, Mr. D. McCallum, claimed that Mr. Xavier's letter of September 26 asking them to cut off the current and send in the account amounted to acceptance of responsibility for the whole.

Mr. Justice Wood found against this, however, and made an order for the payment by Mr. Xavier of $5.5 (amount consumed during September), costs being allowed plaintiff on the writ.
