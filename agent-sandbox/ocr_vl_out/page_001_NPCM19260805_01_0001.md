## OUR POST OFFICE

RECORD MAIL HANDLED IN JULY.

STAFF DIFFICULTIES.

Canton Strike Has An Indirect

A department of the local public service which receives little publicity except when the annual report of its working is published is that concerned with the Post Office administration. Yet, needless to say, the work of sorting, delivery and transhipment and the supervision and administration proceeds daily — sometimes nightly—with demands on the time and labour of the whole department particularly heavy at certain times.

A correspondent to-day refers rather "belatedly to delay in the delivery of newspapers arriving on Saturday morning and received on Monday holding that if the department is under-staffed steps should be taken to make up the deficiency.

A “China Mail” representative who made enquiries was informed that the delay referred to was on account of the staff having to prepare for an outward mail to Europe on the day in question as well as two inward Europe mails and other smaller mails both ways. As it was the end of the month, some 200 men had had to be paid, this also leading to a certain amount of interruption.

For normal times the present staff of the Post Office is sufficient, he was further informed, although the especial difficulties faced by men who were comparatively new to the work had to be taken into consideration. A large percentage of the clerical and outdoor staff consisted of men engaged since the strike and although they were doing their best it stood to reason that they were not yet as conversant with the technicalities as men who had been engaged on the work for ten years or more (as some of the men who left during the strike had been). A great deal of extra supervision work was required. A Record July.

Further interesting facts with regard to the amount of mail handled by the department were given to the newspaper representative. Hongkong acts as a sort of “Clapham Junction” in regard to transhipment of mail in the Orient and transhipment involves a considerable “and growing” demand on the time of certain of the employees owing to fewer ships going direct from Shanghai to Canton since the strike and the growing use of the postal service in regard to communications between those ports and between Shanghai and Yunnan.

This will be more fully appreciated when it is stated that during July of this year over 21,000 bags of mail were received here, this number being a record for one month within the memory of members of the staff who can go back over many years.

Considerable extra work has had to be undertaken as an indirect outcome of the Canton strike, mails which have been transhipped to boats for Canton having to be brought back in certain cases, unloaded and transferred to other boats. Although Shanghai has been cabled with regard to the impossibility of parcels to Canton being handled by the depleted staff there, a considerable number had had to be stored here which had left there prior to the notification.

The Siberian route, it would appear from further answers to the "China Mail" representative's enquiries, is being very extensively used at the present time particularly for the despatch of newspapers and periodicals, from Europe here, although, naturally enough, the quantity despatched from here via that route is considerably less. The opening up of the Tientsin-Pochow railway enabling mail for North China and Europe via Siberia to be forwarded to Harbin by the rail route had enabled the local authorities to make more regular air reliable connections in Shanghai.
