## COAL STRUGGLE

## GREAT COST TO THE COUNTRY:

London, Nov. 6.

The steadily increasing number of m era resuming (at present 300,000 working), together with the mild weather, has mitigated the effects of the coal stoppage during the past week, in which unemployment has diminished by 4,000 but nevertheless still exceeds 1,500,000. A disquieting estimate of the cost of the stoppage was given by Mr. Walter Runciman in a speech at the Chamber of Commerce, Liverpool, namely, between £216,000,000 and £270,000,000 of which textile accounted for £26,000,000.

On the other hand capital issues to October 31 totalled £205,000,000, which is £11,000,000 above last year.

Considerable hope attached to the re-opened negotiations owing to the appreciable assimilation of viewpoints of the two sides, although formidable hurdles remain to the negotiated.

It is understood that the miners are asking, firstly, for the coordination of district agreements, which represents a concession since district agreements are thereby recognised; secondly, a guarantee of a minimum wage; and thirdly, a fixed ratio between wages and profits varied according to the number of hours worked. The last-named constitutes a snag since it implies a national agreement which the owners have hitherto opposed.—Reuter.

To-day's resumption of the coal negotiations opened with the Trade Union Congress mediators hopeful and the miners' leaders non-committal.

The coal owners are reported to be determined not to sign any national document or form of agreement with the Miners' Federation.

Following a Cabinet meeting both owners and miners are expected to meet the Government this afternoon. The negotiations may be continued during the week-end if there is no break down to-day, and in that event the miners' delegate conference will be recalled to London on Armistice Day to conclude an armistice for a settlement. — Reuter.

Although the miners and mine owners have not yet met, and the Government statement is only of the briefest regarding to-day's meetings of the Coal Committee with the owners, and subsequently with the Miners' Executive, Reuter learns that the Government communicated to the latter the rational principles which it is believed the owners may accept. — Reuter.

## RUGBY'S WONDERS

## A WIRELESS TELEPHONE:

TALKING ACROSS ATLANTIC

### Rugby, Nov. 7

The Dominion delegates made an inspection yesterday of the high power wireless station at Rugby and were deeply impressed by its vital importance to the Empire as a means for the dissemination of general news.

Messages of greeting were sent to Australia by the Australian Premier, the Rt. Hon. S. M. Bruce, to which, within twenty minutes, a reply was received at Rugby by cable.

This served to emphasise the new intimacy which has thus been established between Britain and the Overseas Dominions.

Particular interest was displayed by the visitors in the wireless telephone transmitting apparatus, with which experiments in trans-Atlantic telephony are now proceeding so well that it is hoped to start a regular commercial service within the next couple of months.— British Wireless Service.

## FASCIST FERMENT

COLONEL GARIBALDI'S POSITION.

DID NOT BETRAY FRIENDS

Coronel Ricciotti Gurlbaldi has indignantly protested against the accusation that he betrayed his friends.

In an explanation of Lapolla's visit, Garibaldi has offered a semi-official statement to the effect that the former went to France purely for the purpose of taking precautions against entry into Italy of the suspected plotter, Scivoli, against Mussolini's life. ## ABROGATED

## CHINESE TREATY WITH BELGIUM

Peking, Nov. 6.

The Chinese Government has

abrogated the Sino-Belgian Treaty

of 1865.—Reuter.

Peking, Nov. 6.

The abrogation of the Sino-Belgian Treaty is considered in diplomatic circles as the most important event in China for a considerable time. It is pointed out that the abrogation has abolished Belgium's extraterritorial rights and places. Belgians in the same position as Austrians, Germans and Russians. M. De Warzee, however, is still treating the situation calmly, and is hopeful regarding the treatment of Belgians throughout China.

Foreign diplomats point out that the Waichiaopu's action is a serious breach of treaties as Belgium alone had the right to propose a modification or abrogation, and further that Mr. Wellington Koo, by declining to agree to Belgium's request to submit the matter to The Hague has violated Article 40 of the Protocol signed at Geneva by the Chinese and other Governments, wherein it was agreed to recognise the competence of The Hague Court, instituted in accordance with the Covenant of the League of Nations, and to submit thereto such questions. China's action, therefore, is regarded as flaunting the League.—Reuter.

Canton, Nov. 6,

The coercive measures again Chinese students in Belgium has provoked indignation in popular circles here. The Nationalist Government has received requests to issue a strong protest against the attitude of the Belgian Government in expelling students for their demonstration favouring negotiation of a new Sino-Belgian Treaty, and all local organisations are planning a mass demonstration on the late Dr. Sun Eat-sen's birthday, November 12.—"Canton Information Bureau," through Reuter.
