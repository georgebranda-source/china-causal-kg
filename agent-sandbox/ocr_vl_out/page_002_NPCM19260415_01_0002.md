## ECONOMIC CONFERENCE

## Britain's Polley Approved by America

Washington, April 5.—That Britain was wise enough to block the holding of the so-called Economic Conference proposed through the League of Nations leaked out here to-day through Russian propaganda telegrams which credited the “necucisation” against the British to the head of the Central Union of Co-operative Societies. Nothing but praise was heard from those expressing an opinion regarding the British stand.

The proposal to convene the International economic conference, it was stated, was brought up by Mons. Loueller, the French delegate, to a meeting of the League Just, December.

The purpose of the Conference, its promoters said, was to devise a way for international regulation of production, and distribution of the most important categories of raw materials, such as iron ore, coal, cotton, copper, petroleum and rubber. It also proposed to busy itself about and also to seek a solution of customs liabilities, preparations, loans, and the world's depreciated currencies.

As a result of violent opposition by the British delegate, whose country, he said, was not interested in the conference, it has practically "died a booming." About all that is left of the grandiose scheme is a "preparatory committee." This committee is not functioning as yet according to the Russian reports.

“This so-called Economic Conference is an outrage,” said a member of the Senate Committee on Foreign Relations, “and is merely a befalled attempt to get on record before the world and have the news disseminated that a plan is on foot to communicate the world's raw materials. The danger is that such a scheme if given wide publicity would furnish fodder for some of the bovine brains that are doing all they can to bumper the producers in favour of the slickers who would like to live off the community.”—“China Press.”

The report of the above Company for the year ended December 31 (presented at the annual meeting at Evatt and Co's Offices on Sunday, April 10), states:—

Tapping was continued for the whole year under contract, on terms which provide the payment to the contractor of a price per lb., the contractor undertaking to maintain the estate in clean condition, and to pay all costs of production and upkeep. The Company's standard of production to April '20, 1925, was 227,200 lbs. when it was increased to 243,200 lbs. The assessment allowed of the export during the financial year of 160,321 lbs. at the minimum rate of duty.

## South Africans Stirred

South African commercial opinion is somewhat stirred at the action of the South African Railways Administration in placing an order for the purchase of 100 grain waggons direct through the High Commissioner's Office in London, without affording local merchants any opportunity of tendering.

After the very emplastic assurances held out by the Railway Board and others that more business should be placed through South African merchants, surprise is expressed that orders of this character are placed overseas without the local merchant being in any way advised. As a matter of fact, the first intimation they had of the orders being placed were the newspaper announcements.

## DIRECTORS: INDICTED

Commercial opinion doubts very much the wisdom of an order being placed for so many grains. Waggons, in view of the present crop prospects.

Only in the most extraordinary circumstances, it is held, can the railways be justified in spending over £500,000 on grain waggons without calling for public tenders at all, and over £500,000 on locomotives, without giving any real opportunity for public tenders to be submitted.

## NEW CLUB

## 0

Efforts are being made to or gain the bankers' club in London for the exclusive use of active or retired members of the banking profession, and premises have been obtained in the West End where financiers may entertain their customers and friends. An institution of this kind was proposed several years ago but plans were laid aside on account of the war.

London bankers in the past have in their club affiliations been no Brahmin caste. They may be found on the membership rolls of the Union, Oxford and Cambridge. Athenæum, the Carlton, the Reform, the Marlborough, the United Service, the Pall Mall or a dozen other organisations. Special features proposed, apart from the usual facilities and comfort, of a club, will be the organisation of one of the finest banking and commercial libraries in the world; an information bureau capable of dealing with all points in banking and commercial matters, particularly law and accountancy; and an attractive home for country and foreign members visiting London.

## HONGKONG TIDE

The tide-table given below has been obtained by aid of the Tide-predicting Machine, which includes 40 components for the better prediction of tides, from the result of the analysis of the tidal observations, taken at the Kaulung tidal observatory under the direction of Dr. Doberck during the years 1887, 1888, and 1889.

The times and heights are given for Kaulung; but they may be used for the Victoria Naval yard and Aberdeen, the differences being very small.

The times of high-and low-water must not be considered to coincide with the times of slack-water and change of current, the two phenomena being quite distinct. April 9 to 15, 1928.
