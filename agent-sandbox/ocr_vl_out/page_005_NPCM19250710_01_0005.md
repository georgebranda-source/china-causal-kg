## FRIDAY, JULY 10, 1925

## NOT YET

ABANDONMENT OF TREATIES

UNSUITABLE

WHAT MUST BE DONE FIRST.

### (Reuter's Service.)

In the House of Commons, the Laborite Mr. Johnston asked whether Mr. Austen Chamberlain (Foreign Secretary) would advise British initiative for the abandonment of extra-territoriality in China similar to Britain's action to Japan. Mr. A. M. Samuel (Foreign Under-Secretary) replied that it was self-evident the present position in China as regards orderly administration, the safety of life and property compared very unfavourably with Japan in 1894.

The Government regarded as important regards China that the Treaty Powers concerned should act together in this matter. He said "we are ready to abandon the rights when conditions are suitable but they are unsuitable for so doing."

## Belgium Interested

### (Router's Service.)

The troubles in China were reportedly, amongst the subjects discussed by M. Briand and the Belgian Foreign Minister (M. Van der Velde) who is visiting Paris.

It is stated that Belgium is far from indifferent to the situation in China on account of her big interests in the Far East.

## Deudlock Continues

(Reuter's Service.)

A message from Peking states that deadlock between the Powers and China continues, the former insisting on discussion in the first place of China's demands relative to the Shanghai disorders, while China desires to discuss, simultaneously, the revision of the Treaties and other demands which the Powers consider require a larger conference.

## Washington Treaty

(Router's Service.)

Reuter understands now that the French Parliament has ratified the Washington Treaty as regards China; it is expected the ratifications of the various Powers will be shortly deposited.

The Washington Treaty will then come into force, clearing a way for the Tariffs Conference which the British Government regards as an opportunity for doing something to co-operate with China. As regards further Treaty signed at Washington providing a commission to enquire into the abolition of extraterritoriality, this commission is not yet appointed owing to request for postponement by China and other Powers.

## FIVE DEMANDS

## ORDER OF PRECEDENCE LEADS TO DEADLOCK

### (Reuter's Service.)

The situation continues a deadlock. While nothing has been officially announced it appears that the Chinese Government desires that negotiations regarding the last eight of the thirteen demands, as well as the revision of the so-called unequal treaties shall be taken up simultaneously with the first five demands. It is understood that the Powers insist that the five demands arising directly out of the Shanghai incident be discussed first. Meanwhile agitators continue to urge negotiations with Britain alone.

The Chinese Chamber of Commerce has hitherto handed to the bureau distributing strike pay $620,000 but it is believed that it is insufficient to last much longer.

Paris, July 9.—The Chamber of Deputies has unanimously adopted the Bill ratifying the Washington Eight Hour Day Convention Bill. It contains a rider that the Convention will not be effective in France until it is ratified in Germany.—Reuter.

## FORTY SAVED

JOHORE COAST

From Our Own Correspondent.)

SINGAPORE SHIP LOST.

Singapore, July 9.

Forty aboard the S.S. Ban Le Guan have been saved.

There is chance that the others have survived.

A message from Singapore published yesterday stated:—

The local steamer Ban Lee Guan has been lost off the Johore coast. There is only one survivor. It is feared that nearly fifty are drowned.

## RATE DOWN

BANK OF FRANCE

DECISION.

EMISSION SCHEME.

(Havas Agency.)

Paris, July 9

The Bank of France has reduced the Bank rate from 7 to 6 per cent.

The Chamber adopted this morning a scheme for the creation of an emission bank at Madagascar which will bring a reduction of 200 millions in the circulation of banknotes of the Bank of France.

Alike schemes in concern with banks in Indo-China and French Western Africa will come under discussion immediately at the re-opening of Parliament.

## CORRESPONDENCE

CHINESE SPORTSMEN

(To the Editor of the China Muit.)

Sir,—It is now perfectly clear that the agitators are out to cripple and, if possible, to destroy the business life of the Colony and that the incidents at Shanghai and elsewhere were used as a cloak to conceal their intent. Having served their purpose, these incidents are no longer referred to at all. This being the case, the path for the Hongkong Chinese to take is also clear. The peace of the Colony is threatened and the sporting thing to do is to rally round the Government to help to preserve law and order and thereby protect their own families. All the foreign sportsmen are doing essential service in some form and it is up to the Chinese sportsmen to fall in line. It is true that Dr. Tso and his helpers have gathered together about a thousand Chinese to volunteer but this number is disproportionate and the South China Athletic Association alone should be able to enlist a few hundred amongst its members to serve as Volunteers or Special Constables. This possibility should be immediately considered by Messrs. Li Yih-tong and Ng Szew-kwong, the energetic Chairman of the two most important Chinese sporting organizations of the Colony.

Are Chinese sportsmen cowards?

The next week or so should provide

mple opportunity to show that

hey are not.

Hongkong, July 10.

## JACKAL'S PREY

BABY CARRIED OFF.

CA),CUTTA TRAGEDY:

At Kalighat, a crowded Calcutta suburb, a jackal entered an occupied hut, and carried off an Indinn baby, which had just recovered from an attack of smallpox.

An immediate search disclosed traces of blood on the roadway. The child has not been discovered.
