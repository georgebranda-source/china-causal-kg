From Our Own Correspondent.)

Canton, May 22

After eight consecutive days of long sessions, the extraordinary session of the Central Executive of the Kuomintang came to an end today with a session which lasted from 9:30 a.m. until nearly 3 p.m. The surprise of the last day's meeting was the announcement that Mr. Wu Han-min said to begin Shanghai on an important mission, is in reality on a "long leave of absence," and that Mr. Eugene Chen has been given a temporary appointment to his office of Secretary of Foreign Affairs.

Mr. Chen, formerly editor of the "Canton Times," which has been suspended by the "Canton Gazette," is known as a forceful writer, but in his earlier journalistic days here was always markedly anti-British in sentiment. He has recently returned from the North having left after Peking had fallen into the hands of Wu Peifei and

The next session of the Central Executive Committee of the Ku-mintang will be held in mid-August. Normally that body meets only twice each year, but the meeting just closed was a special session to consider the status of the Communists.

#### C. C. Wul's Suggestions

The first declares that a definite policy and method must be found for the future development and standing for both the Peasant and Labour movements, and recommendations that such policy and method be worked out by the Standing Committee of the Kuomintang. This committee has nine members, and is almost continuously in session between meetings of the Central Executive Committee.

To-day's closing session of the Central Executive Committee of the Kuomintang was marked by the introduction of two resolutions by Dr. C. C. Wu, both of which were passed.

The other resolution sponsored by Dr. Wursays that there are "certain theoretical and controversial points concerning which the Kuomintang has as yet reached no decision, and that these should be referred to a special committee for study and for reporting upon to the next National Congress of the Kuomintang."

This National Congress, which will be held in November, probably in Canton, will attract delegates from all over China, from America, and from every other place where the Kuomintang has members in good standing. The committee will meanwhile study abstract questions like the "Class Struggle," and will hew out party platform planks or planks for submission to the National Congress for adoption, amendment or rejection.

## A General's Opium

War preparations go on apace, the last two days having seen the shipment of 1,100,000 cases of ammunition to Shiukwan. ## Trouble Over Sports

Threats of the students and pupils of many schools and colleges to quit the big annual inter-schools field meeting here, if the Canton Christian College was permitted to enter athletes, have come to nothing. To-day the Canton Christian College had all of its competitors there, and won many events.

Yesterday it was reported that if the prescribed team entered the grounds they would be stoned, and then eight large schools sympathising with the College threatened withdrawal from the meeting if conciliation were not effected. Such a withdrawal would have cut the attendance so seriously that the gate receipts would not have paid expenses, so the Canton Christian College, which usually wins the meeting, was permitted to enter its team under an assumed name. Opposition to the College entry was because of the recent expulsion of three radical students.

The meeting, which will continue until Wednesday, is the largest ever held in South China, 91 schools and organisations being represented by more than 1,100 young athletes, of whom about 400 are girls.

## OBITUARY

CHINESE JOURNALIST'S

The “China Mail” records with regret the death of Mr. Li Sumling, who was one of the first Chinese in Hongkong to enter the field of foreign journalism.

Mr. Li had been a translator on the "Wuh Tsz Yat Po" and subsequently occupied responsible position in the editorial staff of the "China Mail."

He proceeded to Peking, before the advent of the Republic and was a staunch supporter of Sino-American relations.

Afterwards he was in the sphere of politics, being appointed to a high post in the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.

He was engaged in Government work—diplomatic and financial—for about twenty years, recently going to Hankow at the request of Marshal Wu Pei-fu, who appointed Mr. Li as Commissioner for Foreign Affairs.

Mr. Li was of a ripe old age and had been in failing health for several months, succumbing on May 18.

COUPLE DE PORTED.

## DRAMATIC ESCAPE

SENTENCED SOLDIERS' 24 HOURS' LIBERTY.

ARRESTED IN KOWLOON

After over twenty-four hours of liberty, two Surreys' private who escaped from Murray Barracks guard room at midday on Saturday under somewhat dramatic circumstances were recognised yesterday afternoon in Kowloon by a police officer who arrested them. They were later handed over to the military authorities.

From the rather meagre details that have leaked out it appears that the three men had been duly tried by court-martial—one (Private Kebble) on a charge of alleged attempted suicide, whose sentence was six months, and the other two, (Privates Arnold and Barker) on charges alleging disobedience and striking their superior officer, they being sentenced to twenty-eight days' detention.

## Clever Ruse

At the moment when their sentences were communicated to them they were all in the guard room, minus hats and belts, but dressed in the ordinary khaki uniforms—awaiting escort to the detention barracks. One of them conceived the idea of asking permission to cross the compound to the library, which was granted, a solitary escort being sent with them. Inside the library Private Kebble, it is alleged, reached for a book off one of the shelves and instantly threw it in the face of the escort, who was naturally taken completely off his guard. The three privates thereupon lost no time in making a daring bid for freedom.

Kebble's career, however, was very soon checked, for the escort recovered from his discomfiture quick enough to give pursuit and capture Kebble in Des Vœux Road.

Exciting "Man Hunt."

The other two privates once they got out of the Barracks rapidly showed a clean pair of heels to their pursuers and all who showed an inclination to join in the "man hunt." Being, as stated, broad daylight, the incident might have been expected to culminate in the rapid "round up" of the two privates, but they seemed to know their way about exceedingly well. They were eventually lost sight off by their pursuers in the vicinity of the Exchange Building.

The Police were at once informed of the sensational incident and a description of the escapees was supplied them. The two men were seen in Kowloon yesterday at 2 p.m. by a police officer who recognised them by the officially issued description and arrested without difficulty.
