## PRAYA SMASH

RECKLESS DRIVING CHARGES FOLLOWS.

TRUCK STRIKES RICHSA:

As a sequel to an accident which occurred at 2 p.m. yesterday at Prayu East, the driver of a hand truck was charged before Mr. S. B. B. McElderry at the Magistracy this morning with having no licence and driving recklessly.

Mr. N. M. Bux, giving evidence, stated that he was travelling in a riesha from east to west when he noticed a loaded hand truck come out from Landale Street at a fast pace and stand in the way of a trum car proceeding to Causeway Bay, the driver of the latter narrowly averting a collision by applying his emergency brake and pulling up with a jerk. The truck people then turned the truck round so suddenly that the shafts and one of the wheels of the riesha on which he was seated were smashed and the coolie slightly injured.

Sergeant Roberts said that the damage to the ricsha was estimated at $20, but he was not pressing for compensation as it was found that the coolie had been using a private ricsha for public hire.

The accused stated that in attempting to avoid a collision with the trum car he swerved the truck round and in doing so struck the riesha.

Fines amounting to $15 were imposed on the two counts.

## DISHONEST COOLIE

## CAUGHT BY MARKED NOTES

## DOCTORS ROBRED

A Chinese coolie in the employment of Drs. Dalmahoy Allan and Strahan was charged before Mr. S. B. B. McElderry at the Magistracy this morning with theft of money belonging to his employers.

Dr. Leslie Thomas informed His Worship that small sums of money mysteriously disappeared from time to time from the drawer of his table. It was discovered that the thefts took place between the time of closing the office in the evening and the following morning. On Monday evening he left some marked dollar notes in the drawer, which were found to be missing the next morning. The Police were informed, and Detective Sub-Inspector Dorling found one of the notes along with other money on searching the quarters of the accused.

The accused pleaded guilty and was sentenced to six weeks' hard labour.

## PARTNERS' DISPUTE

LEADS TO CHARGE AGAINST EMPLOYEE.

The payment to an employee by a client of a shop in Centre Street of an outstanding account amounting to $47.16 led to the appearance of that employee at the Central Magistracy yesterday.

The complainant was a partner in the firm and he charged the employee with theft of the money in question. The defendant did not deny that the money had been paid by the client and said that he had, in fact, told the complainant that it had been. It had not been paid to the complainant because there had been a quarrel among the partners of the firm in question and defendant had been told not to pay him money.

As the complainant admitted that the defendant had told him the money had been paid, His Worship (Mr. S. B. E. McElderry) said the action of the defendant was not like that of one guilty of larceny. The defendant was therefore discharged.

## COLONY'S HEALTH

## THE PREVALENCE OF ENTERIC

Four deaths from enteric fever have taken place during the week ending August 29, according to the return of the Medical Officer of Health. This and a further return for Monday show that five other people are down with enteric.

Of the total notifications, eight were reported from the Victoria district and one, from the New Territories. British cases numbered two, of which one was imported, Chinese five, Irish one and Portuguese one.

One British case of diphtheria and one Chinese case of paratyphoid fever have also been notified during the week and a death has also taken place from influenza.
