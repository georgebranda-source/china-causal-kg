#### U. S. TENNIS

## NEWPORT INVITATION BOWL

JOHNSTON TO THE FORE.

(Reuter's American Service.)

Newport (Rhode Island). August 24.

In the final of the Newport Invitation (lawn tennis) Singles Bowl, W. M. Johnston (U.S.A.) beat B. I. C. Norton (South Africa) 6-3, 6-3, 6-3, winning the trophy outright.

In the doubles W. M. Johnston and C. J. Griffin (U.S.A.) beat B. I. C. Norton (South Africa) and Ray Casey (U.S.A.) 6-2, 6-1, 5-7, 2-6, 6-1.

[Note: Johnston is one of America's Davis Cup team and ranks second or third in the world's list. Norton plays at Wimbledon a few years ago and nearly beat W. T. Tilden, the world champion. Since then he has been resident in America where he has been in the front rank. Griffin excelled a few years ago and his name also appears in the national championship records. Casey is one of America's juniors; he was at Wimbledon this year where he played with credit.]

## OPEN LETTER

CANTON'S BOISENVEIK SVILB.

## ……

The following letter has been addressed to Mr. P. H. Holyoak by Mr. Chang Tui-piu, representing the Trade Maintenance Committee of Kwangtung Province:

Dear Sir,—As temporary residents in Hongkong, we wish to assure, through you, our friends outside Canton that the people of Kwangtung have not been unaware of the approaching dangers of Bolshevism and have in the last two years repeatedly, unceasingly fought them by voicing protests against all harmful acts, refusing to pay all unjust and unreasonable taxes whenever possible and, at least once, met the Bolsheviks arms to arms. In our earlier struggles we had not received any moral or material encouragement from outside.

Our people of Kwangtung real, be the close relationship, and mutual interest in inter-dependence on each other between Canton and Hongkong; are fully congizant of the fact that further delay in the return to normal trade will result in economic ruin in all parts of South China, are not blind to the treachery of Bolsheviks taking shelter behind the patriotism of the students and dissatisfaction within the workers, and clearly understand that the Bolsheviks intend to cover their violation of treaty spirit with the announcement that strikers have the privilege of refusing service to persons or parties they have reasons to oppose or disagree with.

We are afraid that Bolshevism will respect no diplomatic settlements and that the authorities at Peking and those in other capitals have not been thoroughly acquainted with the local situation in South China.

It may be of interest to you for us to point out again that the people of Kwangtung are not at all inactive in their desire to oust the evils and dangers of Bolshevism from their territory, that their seeming inertia has been forced by circumstances which they are still unable to overcome, and that, as soon as necessary and proper arrangements are perfected, necessary steps will be taken to recover the power and the government now usurped by a party- of Holshevists whom no responsible persons inside or outside

Your indignation over the acts of Bolsheviks in places where foreign interests as well as Chinese are affected and your desire to see an early restoration of peace and trade have our sympathy; appreciation, and gratitude and we shall not hesitate to assure you that we are not by any means slow in our plan and effort to get rid of a curse and danger now cursing not only China but all other parts of the world wherever Bolsheviks may be found.

Since Bolsheviks has become a world curse and is bringing suffering to all mankind, it should be destroyed as a common enemy to mankind wherever it may be found, and the question of removing a common enemy should not touch that of national, political, or territorial issues or jurisdictions.

Being likewise cursed by Bolshevism, the people of Kwangthong are watching with deep interest the manner the people of Hongkong will adopt to lessen evils now being spread by Bolsheviats in this part of the world.

Hongkong, August 25.

## STAR THEATRE

As will be seen from the announcement by Hongkong Amusements, Ltd., the Colony is to have the pleasure of welcoming back old favourites to the Star Theatre next Thursday, Friday and Saturday when Mr. Wallingford Tate and Mr. Jack Crichton will present the Broadcasters Co. in miniature revue.

Three members of this Company last visited Hongkong with that successful combination known as the Globe Trotters, and patrons of the Theatre are assured a bright performance.

Mr. Crichton will also be remembered as the principal comedian of the last Bandman Opera Co. that visited Hongkong where he made a big hit in Afgar, Irene, Bran Pie, etc. Mr. Tate's pianistic skill and character sketches are well known throughout the East.

This present Company has now toured the East for over eighteen months and is now passing through to play return dates at Rangoon, Calcutta and Bombay, so the Star audiences are assured of a really first-class entertainment.

Of the ladies, Miss Bobby Broadhurst, the well-known Australian Contralto, will again charm us as she did with the Globe Trotters, while Miss Lucina, who has recently been playing with the Dolly Sisters in Paris comes to Hongkong with all the latest American songs and dancers.
