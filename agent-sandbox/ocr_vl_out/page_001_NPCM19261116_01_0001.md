## LATEST OUTRAGE

#### PIRATES SEIZE B. & S. STEAMER

“Sunning” Set Ablaze.

REPORTED STERN RESISTANCE— “SOMEONE WOUNDED.”

Having been pirated and set afire by the pirates in consequence, it is thought, of the determined resistance put up by the officers, the China Navigation (Butterfield and Swire) steamer "Sunning," a coastal vessel engaged on the run between Shanghai and Hong Kong, was discovered in the early hours of this morning by H.M.S. "Blue bell" off Chelang Point, between Amoy and Hong Kong, in distress.

"Wireless messages briefly recording this fact and that two other China Navigation vessels are standing by to render assistance, in addition to H.M.S. "Bluebell," were received in Hong Kong this morning.

The “Sunning” was on her way from Shanghai to Hong Kong with European passengers on board and the usual coastal cargo. The vessel left Amoy yesterday morning.

No news of any casualties has been received, beyond the brief reference to someone having been wounded, nor is it possible to tell at the moment the extent of the damage.

When discovered, the upper works of the ship were afire, but determined efforts were being made to get the flames under control.

As soon as the news was received in Hong Kong this morning, the Taikoo Dock tug was despatched to the scene to assist and to tow the disabled vessel to Hong Kong, should it be found necessary.

The value of the haul made by the pirates is not as yet known—in fact, particulars of any sort as to the piracy are lacking, but it is thought that the pirates booked as passengers at Shanghai.

When the "Sunning" was discovered by H.M.S. "Bluebell," which was on its way to Swatow from Hong Kong, the pirates had made good their escape.

Later: It is understood that the flames have been subdued. Owing to the heavy seas running, which has rendered access to the "Sunning" by vessels standing by extremely difficult, also to the fact that the wireless-apparatus of the "Sunning" has been damaged, very few details can at present be given. It is understood authentically that "some-one has been wounded" but that no deaths have has yet been reported.

The information at present received has been by means of signals from the damaged vessel and that accounts for its bareness of detail.

The two B. and S. vessels standing by are the “Kaying,” proceeding northwards from Hong Kong, and the “Suiyang,” proceeding southwards.

The "Sunning" will not arrive in Hong Kong today. The scene of the piracy and fire is some 80 miles distance from Hong Kong and the heavy seas render towing impracticable at present.

## Shirping Boycott Ceases

## CARGO ON THE MOVE

Normal Relations Likely

The "new" boycott is rapidly nearing its end and complete for small relations between Cantons and Hong Kong should be restored within a few weeks.

The Canton Government, it will be recalled, promised to call the boycott off on October 10. Canton kept its word, and the pickets retired.

Then came the new boycott. This has never received official recognition nor—as far as is known—any encouragement or help.

Two bodies were mainly active in the new boycott but a third was “interested.” Ex-Heamen agitators who wanted their jobs back and the Transport. Union directed “attention to passengers and traffic while the Economic Severance Association made investigation as to stocks of British goods in Canton.

As foreshadowed by reports published in the "China Mail" on Saturday, and confirmed yesterday; better counsels have now prevailed. The Transport men now want to work and will handle cargo from Hong Kong. The ex-seamen pickets have been pacified and have abandoned their "peaceful blockade."

The merchants who supported the Severance Committee are not nearly so prominent as the more sensible who have argued that it is treason against the Koumintang to hinder the resumption of communications, that being now the policy of the Party.

From the outset, leading Chinese traders who have been in touch with the "China Mail" and whose views have been given prominence in these columns were confident that the strike, boycott and blockade would be entirely settled, as all parties had to retain the goodwill of the Nationalist Government and the Government was in favour of trade.

Although the amount of cargo going up this morning (following the cancellation of the transport boycott) was comparatively small, the "China Mail" can give only the same reason as before, viz. pessimism and the usual desire to wait a few days to see the outcome.

The thin end of the wedge has now been inserted by those who wish to trade, and there is no reason why all ships, including British, should not be working again at Canton in a week or two.

The attention of malcontents having been diverted from the Steamboat Co.'s wharves in Canton, it is now predicted that cargo-boats, coolies, etc., will not be reluctant to discharge and load British vessels at Canton.

Further news from Kongmoon is that the industrial dispute there was settled by the good offices of the Customs brokers. The coolies got one side of the business and the boatmen the other. Shipments are now moving freely between that port and Hong Kong and Kongmoon merchants are looking forward to a satisfactory settlement when the winter solstice comes round on Dec. 22.

## COAL HOPES

## Settlement Almost Certain

SOUTH WALES VOTES.

Change From United Front

The first votes taken in the coal-fields have been in favour of accepting the terms proposed by the Government for a settlement. Such decisions were reached at meetings of miners at Clydach Valo, Blaenavon, Garndiffalth, and Abertillery in South Wales.

The Miners' Councils of Yorkshire and Warwickshire have also decided by a big majority in favour of acceptance.

It is noteworthy that in South Wales the men have preserved a more united front than in any other coalfield, and that the South Wales representatives voted for rejection of the Government proposals at the Delegate Conference held in London last Saturday.

The favourable decisions now re-corded by the above mentioned South Wales districts may therefore, without undue optimism, be taken as indicating the probability of the settlement being accepted, not only in South Wales, but generally throughout the country.

Meanwhile, the number of miners returning to work maintains its steady daily increase. To-day's increase, is 7,442, bringing the total men at work up to 338,153.—British Wireless Service.

CONSTABLE WHO SLEPT ON DUTY.

MAKING AN EXAMPLE
