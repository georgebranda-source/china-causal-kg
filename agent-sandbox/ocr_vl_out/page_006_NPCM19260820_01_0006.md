In these distressing circumstances it would certainly have been futile to have kept up a mere semblance of negotiations without a practical Basis. And so, at the beginning of Autumn the whole country is faced with the possibility of the stoppage being carried on through the long cold Winter months with calamitous results to the women and children of the miners and of the families of men in other industries affected by the mining dispute.

The breakdown in the conference yesterday is to be deplored. It is not for "us at this distance to "reason why." The populace at Home, however, have a right to demand intervention by some means to save the mining industry.

try and to mitigate the uneconomic effects on the whole country. A month or so ago, Mr. Garvin, in the Observer, expressed the opinion that the only alternative to peace by negotiating was for the Government to call the Coal Commission together, and consult with that body as to the most feasible method of ending forth with a most deplorable situation. Since then the Anglican Bishops and the Free Church leaders have endeavoured to effect a conciliation between the coal owners and the miners, but even their proposals were turned down on a district vote of the miners.

For the moment there appears only one ray of hope. The miners in an important colliery in Notts have accepted the owners' terms—a 7½ hours week at the old wages—and are signing on immediately. The Derbyshire and other Notts owners have intimated their intention to re-open their pits on the same terms. This example will doubtless be followed by others, in which case it will be tested whether Mr. A. J. Cook's vitriolic eloquence fails against the cry of the miners themselves for work in order to keep themselves and their families from starvation.

A hint is given in to-day's cables of a probable development when the House of Commons resassemblies at the end of this month to sanction a continuance of the Emergency Regulations. But no great reliance can be placed on a cursory statement of this kind. The miners' leaders having failed to effect a peaceful settlement, it only remains for the "ment themselves to have a turn at saving themselves and the industry in which they are employed.

## LOST A WATCH?

## TWO WALTHAMS IN POLICE HANDS

POSSESSOR CONVICTED.

Arising out of a case before Major C. Willson at the Central Magistracy this morning, two American, Waltham gold wrist watches are now in the hands of the police awaiting claimants. One watch has the makers' name on the dial and is complete with a flexible band. The other, slightly smaller, which has hooks designed for use either as a wrist or pocket watch, may be the valued possession of some person, whose initials appear in woven characters at the back (decipherable as F.C.S.C.), the date April 10, 1917 on the inside of the back cover, and the makers' name and number (Waltham, U.S.A., 186755) on the works.

## CORRESPONDENCE

These watches were found in the possession of a young Chinese who visited a pawnshop at a time when Sergeant Brittain and a detective were there making investigations in another case. In answer to a charge of unlawful possession, defendant stated that he purchased the smaller of the watches in Shanghai and the other from a local shop. An employee of the latter shop refuted this statement in evidence.

His Worship was not satisfied with—the explanation—and sentenced defendant to three weeks' hard labour. Order was made for the waiches to be held by the police pending claimants.

BRITISH LEGION APPEAL BY

EARL HAIG.

FOR DOING GOOD.

[To the Editor of the "China Mall."]

Sir,—On this, the anniversary of the day upon which, twelve years ago, the British Empire entered the War, it behaves each one of us to pause and consider how far our obligations to the men who faced the fury of battle have been fulfilled.

In many ways efforts not unworthy of our national honour have been made to mitigate the privations of the thousands who returned—many of them maimed and shattered in health—to face another struggle—hardly—less severe. But it is to supplement governmental action, to fill up the many gaps that exist, and to give assistance in the many hard cases of unforeseen calamity that I ask for support on a generous scale for the British Legion Appeal Fund.

Quietly, but effectively, this Fund is bringing renewed hope and comfort to the many thousands of ex-Service men of all ranks, who through no fault of their own are in distress, to their dependants, and to the widows and children of those who gave their lives. As recently as last Whit Sunday the Prince of Wales referred to the British Legion as "one of our greatest organisations for doing good."

Cheques, made payable to "Earl Heig's British Legion Appeal Fund" and crossed Barclay & Co., should be sent to the Organising Secretary. (Captain W. G. Willcox, M.B.E.), Appeal Department, British Legion. '26, Eccleston Square, London, S.W.1. Yours, etc.

I believe that the great majority of British men and women keep fresh in memory the debt due to those of my old comrades who to-day are in distress, and that they are sincere in their desire to prove their gratitude. In this belief I appeal most earnestly for generous support, so that the debt may be liquidated.

London, Aug. 4. Field-Marshal.

## RENT TREBLED

## GOVERNMENT DISPENSARY ISSUE

## THE CONTROL PROBLEM

Sir,—I am very pleased indeed to see that others are taking up the cudgels on behalf of the public dispensary in Kowloon. If you, Sir, will kindly look at the register you will appreciate the work done by this Government institution. Many have recourse to it and will be sorry to see it go.

Supposing that it is argued that the Government does not mind a few hundred dollars more. Had a Fair Rents Board been established it will be the implied duty of the Government to appeal against the landlord's increase. There is a strong precedent. Some years ago, the landlord put up the rent of some premises at Wanchai, leased by the Government for housing sanitary coolies. Sir Edward Stubbs was so incensed that the Rents Restriction Ordinance followed.

Although you assure us that the dispensary may be incorporated with the Kowloon Hospital I strongly urge the Government to shut down on principle. If the house owner be so heartless there is no option. The public ought to be made to forego such a boon if it is too heartless to stand for rights. When such a situation arises, I have no sympathy for either the Government or the public.

, Yours, etc., DISGUSTED.

Hongkong, August 19.

## NIGHT RAID

### LOTTERY TICKET PRINTER FOUND. TWELVE ARRESTS

As a sequel to a raid carried out by Det-Sergeant Meadows and a party of police on a matshed hidden among trees on the hillside near Cape D'Aguilar Station, last night, twelve Chinese were charged before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning with being concerned in the printing of lottery tickets.

Three of the defendants were charged in addition with keeping a common gaming house for the purpose, but a question arose as to whether there was sufficient evidence on this point. Two of the men, however, were found to be old offenders, and were fined $100 each, or four weeks' hard labour, while the third, who was a proof reader and as such held a responsible position, was fined $50, or two weeks.

Two others were discharged, as they were merely servants, and the remainder received a fine of $25 each, or two weeks.

## EDITOR SHOT

## NO SEMBLANCE OF A TRIAL

## Peking, August 6

Lin Pai-swal, aged about 50, and for the past five years editor and owner of the "Shoh Hui Jih Pao" a paper noted in Peking for its trenchant and outspoken editorials, was arrested at one o'clock, this morning by Peking gendarmes under Commander Wang Chi, and executed at two o'clock at Tlenchiao.

The arrest and execution were carried out by orders of General Chang Tsung-chang. The immediate cause of the arrest and execution is said to be an editorial yesterday's issue of Mr. Lin's paper, which, severely attacked not only a well-known political protege of Chang Tsung-chang but also attacked a famous Chinese military leader.

Mr. Lin was already asleep at one o'clock when the gendarmes came to his gate. In answer to their battering, his servants came to the entry to be told that Commander Wang of the gendarmerie wished to see him at headquarters. No time was given him even to dress. The soldiers took him off in his sleeping garments.

Apparently he was not given even the semblance of a trial but was at once hustled off and shot. His remains were coffined and put in the possession of his family, which took them to Lung Chuan Ssu, a temple in the south city.

The following public announcement was made by Commander Wang of the Pekingendarmerie:

“The public is hereby notified that, upon orders from Tupan (Chang Tsung-chang), the manager of the ‘Shoh Hui Jih-Pao’ should be executed due to his confirmed connection—with—the enemy—army. Besides abiding by the order,” the public is to be told of the same. (Signed) Wang Chi, commander.

Wang Chj, commander, Metropolitan Gendarme Forces, August 6, 15th year of the Chinese Republic.

Mr. Lin was a native of Fukien. For many years he was in Shanghai. For the past twenty years he had been a journalist. His latest journalistic venture was over five years old. He also had been a member of the Chinese parliament.

The "Shoh Hui Jih Pao" was not as large as some of the other Peking Chinese papers, but it is said to have been widely read, due to the often sensational character of Mr. Lin's editorials. In Chinese circles it is stated that Lin was in no way subsidised and had been thoroughly impartial in his attacks, adhering to no particular faction but critical of all of them when circumstances seemed, to him, to warrant such criticism.

He is the second newspaper editor to be executed since the present military regime in Peking has come into power. The other editor executed was Mr. Shao Piao-ping, editor of the former "Ching Pao." He also was arrested at night and executed without trial within a few hours.—"Chung Mei."

## Another Editor Arrested

Chen, Shi-wu, proprietor and editor of the "Shih, Chieh W. n. Pao" and the "Shih Chieh Jih Pao," was arrested by the local gender-merie at three o'clock t'dis morning and is now held in the headquarters of General Wang Chi, Commander of the gender-merie. Efforts are being made by Chen's friends to save him from the fate which overtook Mr. Lin Pai-shu yesterday morning.

The arrest was effected by General Kang, Aide-de-camp to General Wang Chj. with the assistance of about thirty gendarmes; Chen had already gone to bed when the military visitors called at his house, but was wakened and carried to the headquarters in a motor car.

It is "not known what are the charges against him, as his two publications are rather friendly to the anti-Kuominchun cause.

Previous to the arrest of Mr. Chen, the gendarmies seiled the "Ming Li Wan Pao" and took all the employees of the papers into custody. They made a thorough-going search of the editorial offices

for Chen Chi-an, manager of the evening paper, but as Chen happened to be out, they failed to set hold of him. It is understood that Chen has fled to the Legation Quarters for safety.

The "Ming Li Wan Pao" was a recent addition to the family of evening papers in Peking and had a rather limited circulation. It was the first paper to publish the text of a circular telegram alleged to have been issued by General Sun Chuan-fang advocating peace. Whether this is the cause of the suppression of the paper remains to be seen.

The "Shih Chieh Jih Pao" and the "Shih Chieh Wan Pao" are probably two of the largest morning and evening papers in the capital. The evening edition may appear as usual this afternoon, as the military authorities did not suppress the two sister publications, although they have taken their manager into custody.—"Kuo Wen."
