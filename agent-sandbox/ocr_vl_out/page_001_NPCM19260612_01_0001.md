## CORDON LIFTING

SHAMSEEN GOODS CAN GET INTO CANTON.

STRIKERS' DISCRETION

Still A Boycott Against British

(From Our Own Correspondent.)

Indivisative of the growing renonableness of the boycott and strike leaders, and of their wish to make pence with Hongkong if a suitable formula can be worked out, is the action taken, to-day by the Strike Committee in regard to permitting transportation of goods from Shameen godowns to dealers in Canton.

Hitherto nothing in the way of goods on Shameen could be taken to Canton, unless it was smuggled through, but now, at the formal suggestion of the Canton Chambers of Commerce, the strikers have noted to permit opening of the Shameen godowns and the transportation to Canton of all stored goods, except British.

British goods, which the owners can prove were purchased and delivered in Shameen before the strike began last year, may be transported to Canton upon payment to the strikers' fund of a tax of 10 per cent. of the purchase price. All other goods will be taxed 1 per cent. of the purchase price by the strikers.

Certain exceptions are made, however. The strikers will permit the removal of no goods from the godowns of certain British firms in Shameen.

Pickets' Powers.

As to the removal of "kerosene from Shameen, the strikers voted that this be left entirely to the discretion of the Minister of Finance.

The strikers make a proviso that both the owners and the Strike Committee shall have one representative each at the godowns when the goods are removed, so that the date of arrival may be definitely established.

The Strike Committee also ruled that in future, country passage boats may not be searched by pickets unless there is reasonable suspicion that Hongkong goods or passengers are being carried, and that to make such search even then a special permit from strike headquarters will be necessary. This ruling was made in response to repeated complaint by country passengers. Any pickets disobeying the new order will be put into the strikers' jail for two months.

At Chan Chuen yesterday, the strike pickets caught two of their own number, convincing at the smuggling of contraband goods. The goods were confiscated, and the guilty strikers have been brought here for punishment.

Rule With Heavy Hand.

In other respects the Strike Committee is “ruling locally with a heavy hand. To-day an edict went forth for the arrest of all tailors and carpenters who have not yet joined their respective unions. When tailors were arrested to-day their sewing machines were taken first to the city police stations in the various districts, and were then removed to the warehouses where the strikers keep confiscated gods. The tools of the carpenters fared similarly, and the city police gave no protection.

The Nationalist Government has notified the owners of all country passage boats, and of the ferries from Canton to Honam, that the annual registration fees for their craft are due at once. After one week all boats not flying the new flags which denote that the tax fees have been paid will be stopped en route and brought back to Canton by gunboats and armed cutters.

Within the last four days remittances totalling "nearly $30,000 have been received by the Overseas Department of the Central Kuomintang as the result of campaigns in Siam, Singapore, Canada and the United States. This money was all contributed for the aid of the strikers.

The next Criminal Sessions will be held on Monday, June 21.

A memorial of re-entry by the Crown on Inland Lot No. 2428 has been registered according to law.

H.M.S. "Tamar" came out of dry dock to day, after undergoing her annual overhaul. She has been moored in her old place alongside the quay in the Naval Basin.

## TO-DAY'S FUNERAL

##### POPULAR P.W.D. OFFICER'S DEATH

##### MR. G. FALKNER, M.C

The funeral of Mr. Gilbert Falkner, M.C., engineer in the Buildings Ordinance Office of the P.W.D., took place at Happy Valley this morning, a very large number of his colleagues and other friends attending.

Doing took place at the Government Civil-Hospital-yesterday-afternoon and the news of it came as a considerable shock to his many friends. Mr. Falkner went to Hospital early in the week with influenza and complications later set in.

Mr. Falkner, who would have been 39 years of age next month, was a popular officer. He had a distinguished army career during the War, being awarded the Military Cross for gallantry. He took a keen interest in the local Volunteer Defence Corps and was Company Sergeant Major of the Reserve Company.

BIG HOTEL "THEFT" ECHO.



## $10,000 Case Defendant "Released

The unusual procedure of a pardon being granted to a man serving a term of imprisonment has just been given effect in Hongkong.

About four weeks ago, a Chinese was sentenced to four months' hard labour by Major C. Willson, Police Magistrate, the charge being the result of a loss of $10,600 in banknotes, by a resident at a leading Chinese hotel. Defendant was alleged to have been the man's friend.

After investigation, the money was recovered.

On conviction, Mr. D. McCallum applied for a case to be stated. With amendments, as asked for, the statement was filed at the Supreme Court for the consideration of the Attorney-General and the law officers of the Crown.

Interviews followed. Then it was indicated that a recommendation would be made to H. E. the Governor, to grant a free and unconditional pardon to the man in jail.

This "was accepted. The alternative, of course, was to fight out an appeal case in public, to have the conviction quashed. This might have meant a longer stay in jail, while the case was being heard. The free and unconditional" pardon returns the man's status quo, so that he had nothing to be given by an appeal.

The pardon was put into effect and the defendant is now at liberty.

(Note: — As is generally known, the Crown cannot be sued for damages for wrongful imprisonment, in the same way as an individual can be proceeded against.)

## A CHOP'S VALUE

## MUST BE REGISTERED TO STAND BAIL

OPIUM TRAFFIC CASE.

The “value” of a native firm's chop, for the purpose of standing surety in bail, was argued at some length at the Magistracy this morning by Mr. M. K. Lo (for the defence) and Mr. J. D. Lloyd (Superintendent, Imports and Exports) who was for the prosecution.

Lau Yu-leung and Yu Yau-heung were charged under a section of the Opium Ordinance (when this law was amended) with trafficking in opium. The amendment extends to all persons domiciled in the Colony, irrespective of whether the alleged deals were in or out of Hongkong.

Mr. Lloyd described the second defendant as the managing employee of the first. If there was no case against the first, there was none against the second, remarked Mr. Lloyd.

Bail was fixed at $3,000 in cash and $3,000 surety. Mr. Lo. contended that there were many firms in the Colony, which had been in business for years, and yet not registered at the Secretariat for Chinese Affairs. The chop of such a firm, not registered (ruled His Worship, Mr. R. E. Lindsell) was not good enough.

## CANTON VICTORIES

SMASHING RLOWS DEALT AT

WU PEI-FU.

MATERIAL OAPTURES.

(From Our Own Correspondunt.)

Cunton, June 10:

After General Tang Shang-chi's (he is an "ally" of Canton) victories over Wu Pei-fu's allies in Hunan, the field "general of the Revolutionary Army quickly consolidated his new positions, and on June 7 dealt new and smashing blows at the disorganised "enemy"—he reports in wires to Canton.

The Hunan Protection Army of 6,000 men, hurriedly raised by General Ho Yao-tao to aid the Wu Pei-fu forces, has been entirely routed in two battles at Hungmaio and Chinchow, Tang-Shang-chi's victorious troops killed 400 of the enemy the first day, and more than 800 the second day. The casualties to the Canton forces are reported at 122 killed, and nearly double that number wounded. Six field guns, 11 machine guns, and more than 450 rifles were captured. More than 1,200 of the "enemy" were surrounded when Yungfeng-size was captured. These" surrendered and were disarmed. Many have re-enlisted on Canton's side.

The "enemy's" shattered left wing is reported to have retreated to Pehkwo and Sinchoo.
