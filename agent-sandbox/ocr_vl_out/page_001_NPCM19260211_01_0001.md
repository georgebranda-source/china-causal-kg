### Sir A. Chamberlain Praises Hongkong

An important statement in regard to the anti-British strike and boycott in Canton was made in the House of Commons by Sir Austen Chamberlain, who referred to the recent negotiations for a settlement; expressed his admiration for the resource and courage of the British communities of Hongkong, Canton, and Swatow; explained why it is not advisable to despatch a Special Commissioner to Canton; and asserted that he was still giving the whole question his closest consideration.

SINISTER INFLUENCES.

(British Wireless Service.)

Rugby, Feb. 10.

Sir Austen Chamberlain, Foreign Secretary, made a statement on the situation in China in the House of Commons today. He said that His Majesty's Government was giving careful and constant attention to the serious problem arising out of the anti-British strike and boycott in Southern China. At the beginning of the year it seemed likely that the negotiations between Hongkong and Canton would be successful. They had, unfortunately, broken down in a manner which must make it clear to the rest of China and, indeed, to the whole world that the Government of Canton was for the time being under influences so blindly anti-British that they were not open to reasonable settlement.

Our Friendly Policy.

The position in the rest of China was different. The policy of the British Government, having been dictated by a sincere desire to promote friendly relations with the Chinese people, and being inspired by their legitimate aspirations, this attitude of patience and conciliation was bearing fruit in the slow but steady restoration of friendship and good will between the British and Chinese peoples. The trade conditions in the Yangtse Valley were now improving, and would be equally improving in North China if it were not for civil war, whilst order was being re-established.

There were many signs that these lawless actions were directly attributable to Communistic activities, which had disgusted all sections of the Chinese people, who were very far from approving the actions of the extremist section in Canton. This, feeling would doubtless spread, unless the intervention of the foreign Powers strengthened the extremists and thus united China against the aggressives.

Hongkong Prised.

“Meanwhile,” Sir Austen Chamberlain continued, “I must be allowed to express my admiration for the resource and courage with which the British Colony of Hongkong and the British communities of Canton and Swatow have faced so serious a situation. The whole question has recently been examined and now and most carefully by the Foreign Office in conjunction with our representatives in China, and I shall continue to give it my closest attention.”

## FERRY SUNK

## ILL-FATED "LEE FAT" LAUNCH

HARBOUR INCIDENT.

About 1.30 this morning the local ferry steam launch "Lee Fat" sank, close to the Praya, a little beyond the Wing Lok (Macao steamer) wharf, but no life was lost.

Recently the launch had been on the ferry service between Hongkong and Deep Day (Hau Hoi). She returned yesterday about noon and should have left again at 5 a.m. to day.

While secured by rope (to the bow) to the Praya wall last night, the coxswain was awakened by the crew to find water rushing in over the stern. It is believed that the wind carried the launch towards the seawall with the result that the bow was lifted up through touching ground, while the stern receded into the harbour.

There was a pronounced list to port. The coxswain ordered the bunker coal to be shifted to star board. When this was being done, the launch listed over to starboard and sank. The crew were rescued.

When a report was made this morning the launch was still secured by a rope, but was mostl under water.

Some months ago the "Lee Fat" was on the service to Cheung Chau (Dumbell Island) when she was pirated.

The fun of the fair was at its height yesterday in the case of a Chinese who was busy raking in the coppers with a novel form of price game in which cigarettes and sweetts were given away as prizes. ## CAFE INCIDENT

EUROPEAN ATTACKED WITH A KNIFE.

WAITER CONVICTED

Evidence was taken by Major C. Willson at the Central Magistracy this morning in a charge of assault preferred against a Chinese waiter of the Victoria Café, Des Voux Rond Central.

The complainant, Mr James Royston Stuart said that some dirty knives and forks were placed on the table when he went to have tillin along with his wife on Tuesday. Defendant took offences at a request to bring clean knives and attacked him with a knife in his hand. Complainant struck in self-defence and received a cut in his thumb.

Defendant pleaded that he misunderstood what was wanted and on asking complainant to repeat the order he was struck in the face. Complainant then tried to hit him with a flower pot. He denied attacking complainant with a knife.

A witness called by defendant stated that he was in the inner room and saw the complainant pushing the defendant away. Defendant demonstrated and was struck in the cheek. Witness took a knife away from defendant's hand, and there was a clamour by the waiters for complainant's arrest.

Defendant explained that the complainant attempted to get away without paying for his meal.

Complainant's wife stated in evidence that she did not actually see defendant pick up a knife nor did she see her husband strike defendant.

The Magistrate held that the charge had been proved and sentenced defendant to one month's hard labour.
