# Creating a Modern Vessel is Intricate and Complicated — M an y Classes of. Skilled Artisans Are Required —A Model of Craftmanship.

[By NORMAN C. McLOUDL]

When the modern steel ship is being built there is call for almost every type of skilled workers, from carpenters to plumbers, from steel erectors to electric inns, and from cuiklers to interior decorators.

There is no other class of construction in which as many trades are given active employment. The building and finishing of an ocean vessel is a task of widespread ramifications. Nowhere else is as much crowded into as little space. In no type of construction is there so much of industrial achievement.

The ship is a combination of hotel, warehouse, machine shop, power plant, office, building and agency of navigation. Every inch of space must be used to the best advantage. No part must be allowed to trespass on the rights and privileges of another. Each detail must be complete within itself and must be arranged in harmony with the demands of all other details and those of the ship as a whole.

Intricate Problems.

The intricacy of the undertaking is well exemplified in the precise system of adjustment required for the massive shaft which transmits power from the engines to the steel-bladed propellers. In adjusting the shaft line the conditions demand that the work be done when the sun is near the top of its day's journey—almost directly over head. This demand arises from the need for uniform expansion of the shaft metal under the influence of the sun's heat. In the early morning or late afternoon the heat will be one-sided, and the expansion will be uneven. When the sun is highest the expansion is almost uniform. Without this precautionary measures the shaft would be deflected, with results altogether unsatisfactory.
