## ALLEGED THEFT

A SALESMAN'S EVIDENCE.

## MERITED PROMOTION

APPOINTMENT OF HON. ME FLETCHER.

COLONIAL SECRETARY OF CEYLON.

News of the appointment of the Hon. Mr. A. G. M. Fletcher, Acting Colonial Secretary of Hongkong, as Colonial Secretary of Ceylon has been received by his many friends with genuine pleasure and the Colony of Hongkong joins in its appreciation of a well-merited promotion. The Hon. Mr. Fletcher well deserves the honour, being a most conscientious and able officer to whose energy and judgment successive Governors have paid tribute.

In addition to his official duties in the Colony, which extend over nearly a quarter of a century, Mr. Fletcher has specialised in a question of great concern to Crown Colonies like Hongkong which derive a large part of their revenue from the licensed traffic—that of opium suppression. He was Hongkong's representative on the League of Nations Opium Commission at Geneva and his able speeches there demonstrated the insight he had into all aspects of the problem.

A well-merited honour was conferred on Mr. Fletcher in June, 1922, when the King was pleased to approve his appointment as Companion of the Most Distinguished Order of St. Michael and St. George. Mr. Fletcher had three years previously appeared in the Honours List when he was created a Commander of the Order of the British Empire.

Mr. Fletcher first came to the Colony in 1902 during which year he acted as British Consul at Macao, whilst in the following year he was seconded for famine relief work in Kwangsi. In addition to numerous Government posts he was Secretary to the Military Service Commission, Chairman of the Vital Requirements and Shipping Control Committee and Clerk to the General Military Service Commission.

Mr. Fletcher, in his new post, will succeed H.E. the Governor of Tongkong (the Hon. Mr. Cecil Clementi, C.M.G.) who came from Caylon where he had been for some years. Colonial Secretary of Mr. and Mrs. Fletcher have Hongkong next Saturday.

## BURGLARY AVERTED

SUSPICIOUS CHARACTERS AT POWELL'S.

XMAS MORNING ARREST

The alertness of two Chinese detectives early on Xmas morning led to the prevention of what might have been an extensive robbery at the premises of Wm. Powell, Ltd.

Recent robberies (generally on Saturday nights or at holiday times when the robbers have had time to make extensive preparations) led the police to have extra patrols on the look out this holiday week-end.
