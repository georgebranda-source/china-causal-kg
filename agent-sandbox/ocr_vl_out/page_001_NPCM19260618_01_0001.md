## WHEN THEY GO

BOYCOTT ANNOUNCEMENT EFFECT.

SHARE MARKET "FIRM."

Pence Prospects and Revival

In Trude.

Although previous homes have not been realised, the consensus of Hongkong opinion is that peace with 'Canton is really in sight.'

The official statement by the Government, announcing the appointment of Hongkong delegates, had a beneficial effect in the share market yesterday afternoon, when there were hardly any buyers. Quotations did not move up to any appreciable extent but the absence of sellers was taken to forecast a rise this morning. As a matter of fact, so one of the lending stockbrokers informed the "China Mail," a very firm tone set in in the best investment stocks right away, such shares being those of the leading Banks, the insurance companies, Hongkong Electronics, Trams, "Star" Ferries, etc. When approached this morning, another broker said: "You won't be able to walk through Ice House Street for half price much longer; we are getting back to business."

Beginning of the End.

In native business circles it is also felt that the beginning of the end has come. A leading Nam Pak Hong merchant, however, expressed the opinion that there would be little material improvement just now, but increased confidence in the future would make up for a great deal. He also predicted that financiers would not be content to let their money earn 2 per cent., at current account, in the bank. The money market was easy to-day; when more money was released for trade, property, shares, etc., the trade recovery would make itself felt.

It has been felt that irrespective of the “strike” issue, the Rents Ordinance would not be continued after June 30. ## CANTON'S WAR

BIG NEIGHBOUR SUBMITS TO SOUTH.

IMPORTANT NEW ALLY.

(From Our Own Correspondent.)

Canton, June 16.

The Canton Government today officially announces an important new ally in the campaign against Wu Pei-fu's troops in Hunan, and a third province is to come under Kuomintang rule.

General Pang Han-chang, Governor of Kweichow province, has sent his envoy, An Chien, to Canton to offer Kweichow's submission to the Nationalist Government, and is already dispatching his troops against Canton's enemies in the North.

General Ho Lung is commanding the vanguard of Kweichow's troops sent to reinforce General Tang Shang-chi, and other Kweichow forces have already overrun ten of the districts of western Hunan.

The Kweichow forces have captured Yuanchowfu and Feng-hwanghsien, and will soon launch attacks on Lichow and Changteh.

This new alliance is likely to prove disastrous to the Hunanese forces, who now find themselves between the closing sides of a gigantic triangle and will have to break through one side or the other, or retreat precipitately northward.

Yesterday morning General Cheung Fat-fui left Canton for Shiu-kwan, where he has 4,000 troops. The General is under orders to bring his men up to the fighting front immediately.

The Nationalist Government has voted an appropriation of $3,000 a month to the Red Cross Association, and nurses will be sent North this week. The Central Kuomintang will also make a monthly Red Cross appropriation, probably of $1,000.

## OPIUM TRAFFIC

### CHARGE. AGAINST TWO CHINESE

ALLEGED LOCAL RING

The hearing of a case under the Opium Ordinance which was mentioned before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy on Saturday began at noon to-day when Mr. J. D. Lloyd (Superintendent of Imports and Exports) outlined for the prosecution.

Mr. M. K. Lo appears for the defendants in the case, Lau Yuleung and Yu Yau-heung, who are charged under a section of the Ordinance (when this law was amended) with trafficking in opium. The amendment extends to all persons domiciled in the Colony, irrespective of whether the alleged deals were in out of Hongkong.

In his outlining of the case, Mr. Lloyd said No. 61, Woosung Street, Yaumati, was suspected to be a depot for raw opium and a raid was carried out, but nothing was found. A card, however, was found in the possession of a man on the pr mises which gave the address of the first defendant. Investigations were then made by Senior Revenue Officer Watt and both, the first defendant and the second were found at a house where various accounts, books and documents relating to business deals were found.

Explaining some of the documents seized, Mr. Lloyd said that one inyoice related to seven tins of Yunnan goods, while another related to superior white rice. There were also letters relating to the suppression of the Swatow opium traffic and the possibility of obtaining a monopoly there. He (Mr. Lloyd) was unable to say how much of the opium came to Hongkong. The names of people who were known as dealers in opium were given all through the books. He said that the opium was bought in Tung Hing in the Kwangtung Province and then distributed to various places for sale.

Mr. Lloyd put in a list of the names of fokis which he had taken from the books containing the alleged transactions over many years, as he said these names would come up during the hearing of the case.

Chief Revenue Officer Watt was then called as the first witness and the case will be continued all this afternoon.

## CURBING LABOUR

CANTON GOVERNMENT'S RULING.

UNION LEVY TO GO:

(From Our Own Correspondent.)

Canton, June 16.

Trade unionism has finally become so bold and predatory here that it has been given a sharp scoring by the Nationalist Government and the Kuomintang.

The Mechanics Union here recently voted, merely because it needed money, to tax all factories using mechanical power at the rate of $1 monthly for every unit of horse power used. Some of the plants were terrorised into paying illegal tax, but others refused, and of the latter number nearly forty have repeatedly had their wires cut and have had pickets interfere with attempts to re-connect their plant with the lines.

This cutting off of power brought joint protests from the owners of idle factories, from several thousand workmen thrown out of employment, and from the Kwangtung Electric Company. The Canton Motor Factories Association also made a protest.

The Department of Agriculture and Labour, at the instance of various authorities, held an investigation. To-day Mr. Chan Kung-pok, Commissioner of Labour, gave out a ruling that the tax is illegal, and forbidding the Union from further cutting of wires or interference.

Row Over Teahouse "Tips."

To-day most of the tea houses of the city were closed by a strike of employees who maintain that their employers are not being fair in the division of the money from the sale of "leave-overs." Some time ago the union workers demanded a third of this revenue in addition to their pay.

Because many passage boats are being stopped near the Bocca Tigris forts, and people are being forced ashore by bandits, the Canton police force has been drawn upon to round up the outlaws. It is said that travelling labourers are often taken from the boats and sold into slavery, and that young boys are frequently taken and sold to childless people in the interior.

The Commissioner of Agriculture and Labour has ruled that hereafter pickets may no longer board passenger launches to search except with written authority from the Strike Committee. Several impostors have recently robbed passengers under presence of examining cargo.

## The Petitions

The Tenants' petition referred to submitted that a sudden discontinuance of all existing control over rents would have far reaching results in an increase in the cost of living and in a tendency to overcrowd.

Reductions in wages and increased cost of living was also stressed in the petition and comparisons given between the high rents in Hongkong and those of other places. It was claimed that the system whereby all the business firms in a certain trade congregate in one locality has made it possible for landlords to exercise "more than a fair control."

The landlords, on the other hand, in their petition, emphasised the building development which has progressed and claimed that economic forces were working for fair rents. The supply was now equal and in the case of some districts, in excess of the demand and a state of shortage which had led to the inception of the Ordinance could no longer be said to apply. The dangers of restricting capital investment were stressed.

Whether or not the removal of restriction will lead to an increase of rents remains to be seen.
