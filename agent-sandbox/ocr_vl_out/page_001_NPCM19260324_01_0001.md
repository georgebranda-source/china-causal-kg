## GENEVA CRITICS

BRITAINS HONESTY OF

PURPOSE

The debate on Geneva was opened in the House of Commons today.

Sir Austen Chamberlain, Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs, had a stern expression on entering a crowded and expectant House, which did not relax in response to Ministerialist cheers.

Mr. D. Lloyd George and Mr. J. Ramsay MacDonald, Liberal and Labour Leaders respectively, who opened the attack on Sir A. Chamberlain's conduct at Geneva, were the first members of the Front Bench to arrive.

## Attack Opened

Mr. Lloyd George initiated the debate by moving a reduction of the Foreign Office vote in order "to call attention to the proceedings at Geneva and the action of the British representative there."

Mr. Lloyd George regretted that Sir Austen Chamberlain had not seen fit to take the floor first as he did after Locarno and explain how Locarno was checked at Geneva. He declared that the Locarno Powers were responsible for the Geneva failure. The peace of the world hung on the action which the House of Commons took and directed the Government to take in the future. The events at Geneva undoubtedly antagonised the United States toward the League. Sweden, who took the right and courageous course at Geneva, represented public opinion, also of Great Britain, yet the Swedish delegate, M. Unden, was treated as a stubborn and irreconcilable pro-German thoughtful. He more to save the League than any one man.

Mr. Lloyd George said that they were entitled to ask whether Sir Austen Chamberlain knew beforehand that France and Poland intended to insist on the simultaneous entry of Poland into the League with Germany?

In the course of references to the United States, Mr. Lloyd George's mention of Senator Borahi was received with some laughter by the Ministerialists, at which Mr. Lloyd George asked what was the good of scoffing at one of the ablest and most influential men in America?

Mr. Lloyd George, continuing, said that, instead of using their influence to secure the admission of Germany alone, the Powers who were signatories to the Locarno Pact intrigued and even threatened in order to get somebody else in.

## Chamberlain on Lloyd George

Sir Austen Chamberlain, who followed Mr. Lloyd George, categorically denied that when he interviewed M. Briand on his return from Italy he entered into any engagement regarding the Polish claims or anything else; neither had he instigated or encouraged the Briand Press in the Polish claim.

Sir A. Chamberlain said that Mr. Lloyd George professed to come to the House as an open-minded and distressed enquirer, but he did not await even his (Sir A. DRUGGIST BUSINESS



Dissatisfaction among the partners at the lack of information as to the management and profits was the reason given at the Supreme Court this morning for an application in respect of the dissolution of the partnership. The firm was the Chai Tong Fung Kee, of 182, Queen's Road, trading in medicines and drugs.

Mr. Eldon Potter, K.C., applying on behalf of the partners for the dissolution, said that dissatisfaction had been expressed by the partners for a long time past as no profits had been shown and no account rendered since Chinese New Year, 1921. No payments had been made to them and defendant had avoided an explanation when pressed. The first defence filed was that the partnership had been an illegal one but this had since been withdrawn and no other had been filed.

The managing partner was not present and the application of the others was granted by His Lordship, an order being made for a proper account of dealings to be furnished and payment of amount found due to plaintiffs.

Mr. J. H. Seth was appointed receiver.

## Opium Seizures

The seizure of 190 tails of oplum hidden in the false bottom of a packing case, inside a safe and in the kitchen of a house at Lnichikok Road was mentioned by Chief Revenue Officer Watt when he charged three men with unlawful possession.

## GREATER COMFORT

The first of the new types of train car mentioned by the Chairman at the annual meeting underwent a trial trip this morning and demonstrated the improvements that have been introduced. The seat in the first class compartment are fitted with spring steel back supports and are shaped to the back. This combined with the longer wheel base and swing axle device which relieves the car from side shocks makes for complete comfort in riding.

It will be noticed that in the new car the first class seats downstairs are dispensed with. The problem of sufficient third class accommodation which had become increasingly acute has been considered in the new design and there is increased capacity in this department as well as greater standing room.

The entrance to the first class department is at the back and in the design of the stairs leading to the upper saloon, improvements will be noticed, the staircase having been widened and the angle made less acute. The lighting is also improved.

There is greater seating capacity in the upper saloon which has been lengthened and the seats at the ends are fitted with a view to comfort of passengers.

As in both the lower and upper saloons the clear height is only about 5/9", the centre of gravity is lower than on any other double deck top-covered car in existence. The car which was on the run this morning for instance, was made to lean to an angle of 22 degrees in the workshop and could have gone to a greater angle.

With regard to the mechanical side, the new cars are fitted with two ventilated 85 h.p. motors and the latest type of controller is introduced which enables the motorman to cut out a defective motor without opening the front cover.

On the trial trip, in the course of which a photograph was taken of the new carry the General Manager (Mr. L. C. F. Bellamy) was present, also the Assistant General Manager, Mr. A. K. Henderson, and Mr. V. Walker, the Workshops Superintendent.

### GOVT. CONTRACTS

## CONSTRUCTION COMPANY'S POSITION

Their position as regard the huge losses on work for the Government was reviewed at to-day's (fourth) ordinary yearly meeting of the Hongkong Engineering and Construction Co., Ltd., held at the offices of Messrs. Shewan, Tomas & Co.

Relief may be accorded, it was stated, other business proved profitable and hopes were he d out of expounding on certain lines.

Mr. H. G. Shewan (chairman and managing director) presided. He was supported by Mr. J. Scott Hurston and the Very Rev. Father L. Robert (directors) and Mr. S. Courtney Cook, A.S.A.A., (secretary).

Shareholders present were Messrs.:—J. P. Braga, A. W. Smith, J. Coulthart, A. A. Alves and Li Tung.

The secretary read the convening notice and the auditors' report.

Addressing the meeting, Mr. Shewan said:—

Gentlemen,—You will have seen from the report and accounts (which have been in your hands for some days and which, with your permission, I propose to take as read), that the results of our year's working have again been very disappointing.

This deplorable state of affairs is entirely due to the disastrous contracts made with the Hongkong Government, in 1923, under circumstances which I explained at our general meeting of last year.

## Estimated: Loss:

In our accounts for 1924 we estimated the loss on Government contracts, up to December 31, 1924, at $310,000 and I said at the last meeting that it was extremely "difficult to forecast" what the final outcome might be.

“During 1925 two of these contracts have been completed, resulting in the loss, shown in the profit and loss account, of $82,162.93, and two more were practically finished at the year end,” but on the fifth, which is the Chatham Road extension, work had to stop owing to labour troubles. In consequence of the strike with the resultant shortage of labour, and the higher rates we had to pay, I am sorry to say that losses on these contracts have still further increased beyond anything we could have anticipated, for in point of time alone all the contracts should have been finished, with the exception of Chatham Road extension, before the end of 1924.

"In the case of one contract, Beacon Hill catchwater, the exceptionally heavy rainstorm in July last caused a big landslide below Statue Rock which carried away a large section of the completed portion of the catchwater. Under the conditions of our contract we were required to make good all this damage, being paid for the extra work at the original contract rates, but as these were already much too low, we suffered a double loss, as well as loss of time.

By now the losses on these Government contracts have risen to more than $550,000, which amount has been provided for in the accounts. In addition to this, we estimate that there will certainly be a further loss of

$100,000 on the Chatham Road extension if the Government compel us to complete the work at the original contract rates, and this latter sum must be regarded as an item that may have to be charged in the accounts for 1926.
