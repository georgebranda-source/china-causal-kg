YESTERDAY'S POSITION.

Nasty Scenes Reported In Dock Land.

A review of the events of the first full day of the general strike at Home shows that milk and fish trains are arriving in London on scheduled time. The London underground railway and omnibuses are being run by volunteer workers. In the provinces railway services are at a standstill.

Nasty scenes were witnessed in dock, land, East London. hooligans coming into contact with the Police, who were reinforced and made several truncheon charges.

To counter-act the dislocation of the Press the Government has issued a penny paper—the British Gazette—to keep the public fully and correctly informed of all developments and to guide them in their action.

## London, May 4

An official broadcast message states that the London milk transport service is working according to scheme, and that long-distance milk and fish truins have arrived according to schedule.

The London Underground Railway and Omnibus Combine notify that they are endeavouring to operate their services with voluntary labour. The extent of working will depend on the number of volunteers.

The trunk railway companies hope to run passenger trains during the day.

Menntiane reports from the provinces show that the services are practically non-existent. The principal stations in Manchester are picketed, but one or two trains managed to leave London termin.

Hundreds of railway clerks and employees in supervisory grades resumed their ordinary duties in Hull after telegraphing to the Executive disapproving...of the strike action.

News from the provinces shows that the strike is being observed in the industries embraced in the Trade Union Congress order, but work is proceeding in others, including the Clyde and Sunderland shipyards, the Northampton boot factories and the Leicester hosiery factories.

A number of potteries have stopped owing to the lack of coal.

In Belfast business is being carried on as usual.

H. R. H. the Prince of Wales is proceeding to London by aeroplane to-night.

The Prince of Wales arrived by aeroplane at Croydon from Paris and quietly motored to London.

## A Firebrand

Saklutvala, the Communist M.P., was remanded at Bow Street Police Court till Thursday on a charge of making an inflammatory speech at a May Day demonstration in Hyde Park, in the course of which, according to a detective shorthand writer, he incited the Army to revolt and refuse to fight. He spoke contemptuously of the Union Jack.

Sir Travers Humphreys, prosecuting, said that this being the first case in the present situation, the prosecution only required accused to enter into recognisances to be of good behaviour.

Saklatvala was released on bail in two sureties of £100 each.

The Government is publishing a penny newspaper called the “British Gazette.”

Number one of the “British Gazette,” not exceeding 700,000 copies, published by His Majesty's Stationary Office, consists of four sheets, the inside pages being blank.

It explains its objects as a reply to the strike makers' plan to paralyse public opinion. The “Gazette’s” circulation will be raised by means of the unlimited resources of the State until it provides a sure and sufficient means of information and a guide for the action of all British citizens.

COMING TO HONGKONG.



Engine Trouble Causes Forced

Anxiety regarding the missing Spanish aviator has been dispelled. A forced descent was made in Chinese territory, about half-way between Hanoi and Macao, and he is now reported to be coming to Hongkong from the Portuguese Colony.

Last Saturday, Captain Gallarza and Captain Loriga left Hanoi (each with their own mechanics) in unfavourable weather conditions for Macao. Captain Gallarza arrived but Captain Lorign did not turn up.

A search was instituted, the Portuguese gunboats "Patria" and "Macao" and the motor launch "Demetriri" taking part. They were reinforced by H.M.S. "Marzion" and the submarine L5, from Hongkong. The "Marzion" returned to harbour to day, just before tiffin.

The French Consul General at Hongkong (who is also in charge of Spanish interests) received a cable from the French Consul in Canton this morning. It stated that Captain Loriga had wired from Suit Tung (in the southern part of Kwangtung province, and near the French Colony of Kwang-chow-wan), that he had landed at Tien Pak (a port of that part of the coast) and was safe. There had been engine trouble, and he asked for assistance as his motor needed repairs which he could not carry out by himself.

Accordingly, the Hongkong Consul immediately sent a radio message to Kwong-chow-wan asking if the French authorities could do anything. As far as the Consul (Mons. Dufauré de la Prade) knew, there were no aviation facilities at Kwong-chow-wan, but ships of the French Navy might be able to send help.

This morning, the Spanish Dominican Procuration (at Hongkong) received a telegram from Macno, indicating that Captain Loriga had joined Captain Gallarza there; and that they were coming to Hongkong to day to inspect the ground. They would then decide whether they would make their next hop from Macao, or Hongkong, and to Manila, or to Formosa.

As far as is known, the aviators did not arrive this morning by the s.s. "Kinshan" or the "Tung On."

In Macao it was mentioned that the aeroplane at Tin Pak would be brought to Macao by a Portuguese gunboat. Last night, the gunboat "Macao" was observed to signalling and her searchlight was at work. She put out during dark. As it is unlikely that the aeroplane (not being a seaplane) is to be "towed" to Macao—as one source put it—it is summised that the machine was left at Tin Pak, with a guard from the "Macao," which took Captain Loriga (and his mechanic) to Macao, and that the gunboat was going back to look after the plane till it could be taken or flown away.

To fly from Hanoi to Macao, one goes almost due East, following the coast-line of Kwangtung most of the way. After Kwong-chow-wan would come Tien Pak and other ports and then Macao. Yeung Kong is another of the better known places in the territory where Captain Loriga landed.

Some of the ports are linked up land-line telegraphs but the working during the last few years had been very unsatisfactory. Therein probably lies the explanation of the delay in the message getting through to Canton. The district and surrounding territory is under the control of the Canton provincial government and there are Customs establishments at various points. Matting and other products are the principal exports. In Tlen Pak, or Sui Tung, it is thought that Captain Loriga would have been able to get in touch with the local authorities as the places are by no means wilderness, although the names are not as well known in Hongkong as other seats of trade.

## EXCITING CHASE

TO-DAY'S EARLY MORNING

ROBBERY.

## SHOT IN THE NECK

Connaught Road, West was the scene of an exciting chase early this morning, a police officer finding it necessary to fire at a man running from the premises of firm which had been robbed. The man in question was shot in the neck and was taken to the Government Civil Hospital in a serious condition.

The shop in question is at 35, Connaught Road West, business of importing and exporting drugs and pharmaceutical wares being carried on there. It is thought that the value of the goods stolen is somewhere in the neighbourhood of $1,500 from the ground floor and the value of goods taken from the first floor has not yet been ascertained.

A foki of the firm was arrested by a Chinese constable in Queen's Road Central.

The visit to the firm's premises of men who were stated by the foki to be relatives is thought to have something to do with the robbery. Shelter was given them for the night at the request of the foki and it is alleged that these men participated in the robbery in collaboration with a gang which early this morning gained access to the premises and bound and gagged the inmates.

The robbers would undoubtedly have got away but for the action of one of the bound fokis who extricated himself from his bounds and gave chase to the man who was eventually shot in Hillier Street.

Four of the gang which entered were armed with revolvers and others with formidable knives.

## ASSAULT ON GIRL

SEQUEL TO A MOTOR ACCIDENT

EUROPEAN SUMMONED:

Mr. F. P. R. James, of the P.W.D., was summoned before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning for falling to report an accident and with assaulting a ten-year old Chinese girl living at No. 5, Prince's Terrace.

The complainant in the assault summons was represented by Mr. McCallum and Mr. A. O'Donoghue defended in both charges.

Prosecuting in the tram case Sub-Inspector Alexander said the accident took place at Caine Road at 2:30 p.m. on Thursday, but a letter reporting it was only received the following morning.

The accused explained from the witness box that he wrote the letter at 4 o'clock the same afternoon and gave it to the chief clerk to send to the traffic office, but the coolie had instead made calls on the way and did not deliver the letter until the following morning. Mr. O'Donoghue said that he was supplied with a copy of the letter shortly after it was written. Under these circumstances His Worship held that the charge could not be maintained and the summons was dismissed.

In the assault case Mr. McCallum said that the accused was riding a motor cycle and there was an accident. A little girl was badly hurt and ran into a shop. Accused followed her there and smacked her on the face for laughing at him. He thought it was unfortunate that, acting probably in anger, accused should have assaulted the little girl who was already suffering from the reaction of the accident.

After the evidence of the girl, accused, made a statement from the witness box. He said that he was proceeding at about ten miles an hour when the girl suddenly rushed across the road. He tried to avoid the accident by swerving, but she was hit by the front wheel. The girl got up and ran into a shop and he also went there to make inquiries. She then grinned at him and he merely flicked his fingers saying she had done nothing clever. He said that his fingers merely touched the girl's head.

Under cross-examination by Mr. McCallum, accused admitted that he wrote a letter to the girl's guardian claiming damages and that he also received a reply, followed shortly after by the summons. Answering another question he said he would have been justified even if he had assaulted the girl, as the accident was caused by her neglect. He had hurt himself, torn his clothing and damaged his machine.

Mr. O'Donoghue submitted that the case was very trivial and there was no evidence for a conviction. He thought the summons was an attempt to extort money by a mild form of black-mail.

Mr. McCallum strongly resented this suggestion and said that the accused did not cut a good figure in the box.

His Worship held he was satisfied that the accused deliberately smacked the girl and fined him
