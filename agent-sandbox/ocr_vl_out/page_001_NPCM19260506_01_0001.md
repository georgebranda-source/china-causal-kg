EMERGENCY PROSECUTION

Peaceful Picketing To Be Tolerated.

Both Sides are satisfied with the position in regard to the general strike at Home at the end of the second day.

A long struggle is being prepared for, and both the Trade Union Congress and the Government are marshalling all their forces.

A plea is still being urged by many for the resumption of the negotiations—so far without success.

## CONDITIONS DIFFERENT

Second Day's Impressions in London.

Although it was the second day of the strike all the roads to the city were crowded with traffic, chiefly an endless procession of private cars and food lorries, yet the conditions were most different. A twelve mile run yesterday took two hours and to-day one hour.

A number of omnibuses are working on the principal routes, including the London and General, besides independent buses manned by volunteers, even the stairways of these being packed.

There were distinct evidences of regular, although attenuated, train services, both long distance and suburban.

The tubes are partially working. Queues are waiting outside the stations, which yesterday were closed.

People coming to the City by long routes from the suburbs are most good-humoured in spite of the very dismal weather, drizzle, and mist.

A novel feature is the posting in windows of broadcast news bulletins, which are surrounded by eager crowds.

## Both Sides Satisfied

Both sides are satisfied with the position.

The Government Spokesman today broadcast that the position throughout the country was satisfactory. There was no sign of panic or even of great excitement, and no serious disturbance anywhere. Food supplies were normal, and 12,450 volunteers had so far been enrolled in London. Volunteers are flowing in all over the country.
