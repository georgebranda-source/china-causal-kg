FIELDS VARY IN SIZE:

Sun Comes Out; Going Still Fast.

[BY “PATROL.”]

Dew in the early morning developed into rain just before midday, but the sun came out later on for those who indulged in the Sport of Kings at Happy Valley to-day.

Known in previous years as the "off day," it was an opportunity for ponies which had disappointed to make amends. There were "upsets to form," with the result that "tote" dividends were, on the average, of not such small proportions.

The going was still on the fast side.

Most of the Shanghai crack jockey having returned more of the legal gentleman's riders had mounts.

Fields, varied between the large, and the smallest join the whole meeting. Honours were more evenly distributed.

Counter attractions had only a slight effect on the support for the fourth and last day of the Jockey Club's annual meeting. However, it may be that what appeared to be smaller attendance was due to the increased vigilance of the officials who, after complaints earlier in the week, put up a very good best—under the circumstances—to prevent overcrowding.

## DISARMAMENT

AMERICAN AMBASSADOR

(Reuter's American Service.)

## Washington, March 5

Mr. Houghton, the American Ambassador in London, has been requested to return to the United States to consult with Mr. F. Kellogg, Secretary of State, regarding the plans for the preliminary disarmament conference. Mr. Houghton sails on Sunday.

A Chinese coolie in crossing the road at Praya East with a load on his back was knocked down by a tram car and was taken to the Government Civil Hospital in an unconscious condition.

One out of a number of boys who were chasing each other at Leighton Hill Road was struck by the mudguard of a car and received injuries to the head and arms. The driver took the injured boy to the French Hospital where he was detained.

The front part of a Kai Tack motor bus was considerably damaged in a collision with an earth truck on the Kowloon City Road. No persons were injured.

## HAYLEY MORRIS

ECHO OF CASE AT OLD RAIN FY

SWINDLER SENTENCED.

London, March 5.

There was an echo of the Hayley Morris case at the Old Bailey where an engineer named Charles Gervais was sentenced to a year in the second division on a charge of attempting to obtain $400 on false pretences from the administrator of Hayley Morris's estate.

It was alleged that Gervais, in return for the money, promised to secure preferential treatment for Hayley Morris in Portsmouth Prison.

## BROADCASTING

IMPORTANT REFORMS AT HOME.

(Reuter's Service.)

## London, March 5

A most complete scheme for re-organising the management of broadcasting services in Great Britain is outlined in the official report of the Broadcasting Committee appointed to decide the nature of the administration after the expiry of the present Company's licence on December 31.

It recommends that broadcasting be made a public service; that a Corporation consisting of from five to seven nominees chosen by the Crown be appointed to take over the staff, contracts, and apparatus of the existing Company that the Commissioners be persons of judgment and independence and empowered to consult appropriate societies, organisations and advisory committees to ensure due consideration of all phases of broadcasting; that generous provision be made for experiment and research; that the Corporation operate the licence from the Postmaster-General for at least ten years; and that the Commissioners be invested with the maximum freedom, consistent with Parliamentary control through the spokesmanship of the Postmaster-General.

## LONDON STRIKE

ADVICE FROM ENGINEERING UNIONS.

"RETURN TO WORK."

(Reuter's Seriya.)

In an effort to avert a widespread dispute the Executives of seven Engineering Unions have advised the strikers at Hoel's factory to put themselves in a constitutional position and return to work.

[A cablegram published in our columns on Saturday stated: 'The Engineering and Allied Employers' National Federation has decided to post lock-out notices on March 5, to be operative on March 3, in all federated establishments in consequence of a strike by a section of London engineers and labourers over wages and a refusal to work with non-Unionists. The Engineering Union refuses to recognise the strikers' action. On Tuesday it was reported: The engineering lock-out has been brought a step nearer owing to a meeting of the strikers of the London firm of printing machine manufacturers, Robert Hoel and Co., which are the origin of the dispute, reaffirming their decision not to return to work.' Their attitude will be further discussed at a joint meeting of the Executives of the seven Unions involved when efforts will be made to avert a stoppage.]
