## WUCHANG'S FATE

CITY NOT YET TAKEN BY CANTON

BOTH SIDES' CLAIMS.

As is usual when something important is at stake in Chinese warfare, the opposing sides make claims which are greatly at variance.

Canton broadcasted on Sunday a despatch that Wuchang had been taken, that Wu Pei-fu's bodyguards had looted the Bank of China and Communications Bank in Hankow, and that he had fled on a Yangtze gunboat. On Monday, the anti-Canton section of the press was reticent.

Since then it has been established that although the situation is critical for the northerners, the stronghold of Wuchang has not fallen.

It is conceded that the northerners were still falling back. At the most they could only have held their line in places.

The southern Nationalists are pushing forwards steadily and may yet take Wuchang, the fall of which will mean the end of Wu Pei-fu's tenure in Hankow.

But a local merchant received only yesterday a cable from an agent in Hankow, asking him to hurry up shipments of sugar from Hongkong as the market was likely to go up.

An anti-Canton source hints that the present acting Premier in Peking has ordered the Chinese Navy in Fukien to attack Swatow. It is generally accepted that Marshal Sun Chuan-fang, who is Wu Pei-fu's ally in Shanghai and hinterland, has not taken the field on either side.

Reinforcements are being rushed southwards by Wu Pei-fu and a stand on the banks of the Yangtze River may be made with good effect.

The odds, at present, are still n Canton.

## TENANT SUCCEEDS

LANDLORD NOT FULFILLED

COVENANT.

GAS FITTINGS CASE.

Judgment was given for Mons Albert (proprietor of the Albert Cafe) in the Summary Court this morning in both claims and counterclaim in a case in which he was sued by his Chinese landlord for rent in respect of No. 6 Kennedy Road.

His Honour (Mr. Justice Wood) held that the landlord's claim could not succeed. There had been a conflict of evidence as to the consideration in respect of which a receipt for rent was given. It was not necessary for him in dismissing the claim with costs, said His Honour, to decide what was in fact the consideration accepted on the landlord's behalf.

His Honour found that when possession was given under the agreement, the covenant had not been fulfilled. His Honour also found that the expenditure by the defendant in making up this deficiency was reasonable. With regard to the claim of the landlord that certain fittings for which he was liable had been put in by the tenant without consulting him, His Honour found that this argument had no weight. Notice by the tenant which was necessary to create a breach by the landlord of a covenant to repair was not necessary to create a breach of a covenant which was to supply gas fittings at the commencement of the tenancy at a time when the landlord had full knowledge of his landlord.

The only obstacle to the success of the counterclaim by the defendant was the evidence of the landlord that compensation for this breach of covenant had already been given by the credit allowed of the sum expended in the account for rent. If the tenant's story was to be accepted he had not in fact received this compensation. The onus of proof lay with the landlord on this issue of fact and His Honour found that this had not been discharged in that he had not convinced His Honour that such compensation had been made.

In giving judgment for the defendant on claim and counterclaim, His Honour said that he had no reason to question the bona fides of the landlord's aims.

Mr. W. B. Hind appeared for plaintiff and Mr. L. D. Turner for the defence.

## THREE ROWDIES

## SQUABBLE IN EUROPEAN HOUSEHOLD

SERVANTS BOUND OVER.

Three servants employed at No. 3, The Albany, were brought before Major C. Willson at the Central Magistracy for disorderly conduct.

The first defendant, a house boy in the employ of Mr. E. R. Dovey, said that he was putting on his shoes when the second defendant, a coolie also employed there, flung a bottle at him which injured his hand whilst attempting to shield his face.

The second defendant alleged that he was assaulted without any provocation by the first defendant, who enlisted the aid of two other servants.

No questions were put to the third defendant, Mr. Grantham's servant.

Mr. Dovey, who was present in court, thought there had apparently been bad feeling between the servants as to who had been the informer in a recent raid carried out by the police in the servants' quarters.

All the defendants were bound over on personal bonds of $50 to keep the peace for six months.
