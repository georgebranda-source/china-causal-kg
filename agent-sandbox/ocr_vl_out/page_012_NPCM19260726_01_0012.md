## PRESS BLESSING

FOR "MINISTRY OF THE FRANC."

GENERAL SIGH OF RELIEF.

Few Cabinets have received such a general blessing as that beatowed by the morning newspapers on M. Polneare's so-called "Ministry of the Franc." There is a general sigh of relief that a Government has at last been formed which looks like weathering the storm and solving the financial situation. Politics for the moment are forgotten except for a few minor criticisms, such as the inclusion of M. Herriot and M. Painleve in the Cabinet.

The opinion, however, is in no way unanimous in forecasting the Government's financial programme.

The most startling intention in this connection is attributed to M. Polincare by the newspaper Le Journal (Democrat) that he intends to ask Parliament to agree to a prorogation for two years during which the Government will legislate by decree, the Premier's argument being that the franc cannot be cured overnight. It needs three or four years for a complete rest and relief from political strife.—Reuter.

The franc opened at 202 and improved to 200 to the £1.—Reuter. Closing Prices:

The French franc closed at 197% and the Belgian franc at 193½. Reuter.

Poincare's Programme.

Paria, July 24

Inspired quarters have lifted the veil of the Government's financial intentions sufficiently to indicate a two years' prorogation will not be adopted. On the contrary Parliament will probably sit until mid-August and vote on a comprehensive series of measures involving four to five milliards of increased taxation direct and indirect in which succession duties figure largely.

The Government has ruled out the capital Levy, the moratorium, and fresh inflation, and will honour debt agreements, though it will seek a modification of the Washington Convention, with a view to the insertion of transfer and safeguard clauses. Finally, the stabilisation of the franc will be left until the exchange position is remedied.—Reuter.

## MILLS WIN

## STRIKERS IN SHANGHAI GIVE IN

Shanghai, July 24.

Several hundred workers who went on strike at a Japanese cotton mill on June 24, agreed to return to work to-day.

The list of conditions presented to them by the Japanese management, which has been accepted, shows that the mills have won a distinct victory.

Super-unruly workers, "locked out" during the past four weeks have caused considerable disturbance in labour circles.

The acceptance by the workers of the management's demands is undoubtedly the result of the workers being starved out.—Reuter.

[A cable from Shanghai, dated June 24, stated:—There has been considerable unrest and agitation in local industrial circles, and lightning strike has been called in a Japanese cotton mill. Six hundred, downed tools, protesting against the arrest of a mill detective. The strikers set fire to the cotton, and when the brigade arrived they were attacked by the strikers with bricks, spanners and other heavy missiles. The Police drew their revolvers, but there was no firing. Twelve arrests were made, and the situation is considered most serious.]

## CLEARING OUT

## MEXICO NO PLACE FOR CATHOLICS

## LAWS TOO STRICT

New York, July 24.

Some of the Mexican schools intend to close down and reopen across the American border, where the children will be religiously instructed unhampered.—Reuter.

A. pastoral letter has been signed by all the Archbishop's proposing, as a protest against the religious regulations, which will be enforced on August 1, that all Catholic services be suspended and priests withdrawn from the churches on July 31.—Reuter.

An earlier New York enable stated:—The newspapers are considerably preoccupied with the troubles of Roman Catholics in Mexico, where the new law regulating religion becomes effective on August 1. A Mexico City despatch stats that President Calles has issued a proclamation prohibiting religious instruction and worship in all schools and forbidding any cleric to direct a private school. Telegrams from Nogales and Arizona report that the Archbishop of Mexico and the Bishop of Tobasco have been arrested on a charge of violating the law by calling on Catholics to exert economic pressure on the Government with the object of obtaining an amendment of the church laws.

## COMMERCE TREATY

## SIGNED BY BRITAIN AND HUNGARIAN

Sir Austen Chamberlain, Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs, and Baron Rubido-Zicby, Hungarian Minister in London, yesterday signed the text of the British-Hungarian Treaty of Commerce and protocol providing for continuance of a commercial status quo between the two countries until the new treaty has been duly ratified by the high contracting parties. The basis of the treaty is reciprocal freedom of commerce and navigation and mutual and absolute most favoured nation treatment between the two countries.—British Wireless Service.

## NO DIVIDEND

MARCONI'S SHAREHOLDERS DISAPPOINTED.

SHARES FALLING.

An unfavourable impression has been created on the Stock Exchange by the announcement of the Directors of Marconi's Wireless Telegraph Company that no dividend is possible on the ordinary shares in respect of the working for the year 1925, although the results of the trading show a profit.

The Board states that a further revision of the values of certain of the Company's interests in the associated and other Companies will become necessary owing to continued depreciation.

Meantime the annual meeting of shareholders has been postponed sine die.

The Directors state that they intend to secure profits on future operations and that henceforth these will not be diminished by losses incurred owing to possible future depreciation of investments.

The Company's shares have fallen—substantially during the past days and are now quoted at 17s. 6d.—the lowest for ten years.—Reuter.
