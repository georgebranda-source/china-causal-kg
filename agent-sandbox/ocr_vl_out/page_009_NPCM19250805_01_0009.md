The Duchess of York is reported to have been one of the biggest winners of the Royal family at the Ascot race meet. Gossip credits her with having won between £2,000 and £3,000. She celebrated her triumph by giving a smart little dinner at Claridge's, at which a tiny gold race horse was presented to every guest. Princess Mary did not have such good luck. Although she won a considerable sum the first day, the amount being swelled by the victory of her father's Rose Aloysia, she ended the week's betting several hundred pounds the loser.

"It is better to have a loving wife than to make fifty at cricket. It's better to have a loving wife than to make ninety-nine at cricket. I won't go beyond that." Thus cautiously spoke Sir James Barric—who was a cricketer before he became a famous author—to a Cotswold village cricket team of married men beaten at this game by a combination of bachelors. Several of his hearers had failed to score at all—their record being thus 0, or, in British-cricketing, parlance, "a duck's egg." Sir James Barrie was thus afforded an opportunity of which, in opening the village clubhouse immediately afterward, he availed himself shamelessly. President von Hindenburg has objected to the paintings hung by President Ebert in the rooms of the Presidential Palace. President Ebert, who was a great lover of nature, had decorated his rooms almost exclusively with landscape pictures. In his working room hung a large landscape, a woman's portrait by Rembrandt and two small water colours. President von Hindenburg, however, declared they were not cheerful enough for his taste, and has chosen several new pictures at the state picture galleries.

A Biblical ballet has been holding the attention of the Moscow public for some time. It is entitled "Beautiful Joseph," and depicts the incidents of the persecution of Joseph by his brothers, with his sale into Egypt and subsequent adventures there, through the medium of a rich Oriental ballet, with a weird, bizarre musical accompaniment by the modern Russian composer, Vassilenko. A "more conventional ballet entitled "Teodelinda," a story of bandits transformed into fauns, with musical accompaniment by Schubert, is given along with "Beautiful Joseph," and the double attraction has, for many weeks drawn large crowds to the Experimental Theatre, where it is given.

A French Commissary tried several points along the Belgian frontier; but each time the yellow Reds were returned to him (without thanks). He resolved to try Germany. From Strasbourg he took his proteges to the nearest point of the frontier beyond the Rhine, and told them to go straight on. They did not return. The new Chinese puzzle was solved.

Fifty Boy Scouts from Japan will go to China this summer to spend several weeks in camps there, says an American paper. At the same time some two dozen Chinese Boy Scouts will come to Japan for a visit. This plan of exchanging Boy Scouts with various countries has aroused considerable enthusiasm among Scout leaders in Tokyo. It was stated that the Boy Scout organisation of Hungary has begun negotiations with the Japanese organisation with the view of exchanging visits.

A Dutch shipowner has hit upon a new way of “showing the flag” which opens up rather dreadful possibilities. One of his steamers recently caused a sensation in a Danish port by arriving there flying the Dutch ensign embellished with an advertisement of “an oil engine manufacturing firm. Some nasty things were said in the Danish Press regarding this piece of commercial “enterprise,” and the Dutch authorities have administered a sharp reproof to those who thus degraded the national flag.
