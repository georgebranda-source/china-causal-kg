The recent advertisement that Lundy Island was for sale reminded a friend, whose passion is shipping history, of a little-known fact about the island—that it was the last place in the United Kingdom to maintain the service of his Majesty's mails with sailing craft. This went on as late as September, 1911, the vessel in question being a little cutter, the "Gannet," which ran from Instow with wonderful regularity. The mail subsidy was £50 a year, but at the end of 1911 a disagreement arose owing to Captain Dark, her owner, fitting a motor into her and demanding a higher subsidy. The Post Office could not agree, and so a forty-year agreement was terminated.

A Downside schoolboy, M.J. Turnbull, has scored 1,823 runs this season, averaging 94 per innings. He scored 46 at his first appearance with Glamorgan, in county cricket, against Essex, at Leyton.



A total of £175,260, has been paid for 237 pictures by John Singer Sargent during the auction of his works. This is believed to be a record for the sale of paintings by a single artist. Persons prominent in political and public life, fashionably dressed women, artists, members of the Royal Academy and others crowded the auction rooms. The highest price paid for a single picture was 6,000 guineas, for Sargent's copy of Velasquez's "Head of Prince Balthazar Carlos". A design in blue and gold for the Boston Museum reached 660 guineas.

A jewel of the Glasgow collection is Whistler's "Carlyle." This was acquired by Glasgow some thirty years ago for £1,000. It would fetch more than ten times that amount to day. Small as was the sum paid by Glasgow for the portrait of the Chelsea Sage, Glasgow bailies endeavoured to make it still smaller. They came to Whistler's Chelsea studio and then attempted to bargain. "Isn't it the case, Mr. Whistler," said one baille, endeavouring to get the painter to accept less than £1,000, "that modern pictures fade?" "No," replied Whistler, "modern pictures do not fade, and therein lies their eternal damnation."

The fortune of £61,725 left by Sir Rider Haggard, though large, is by no means exceptional: It is recorded in his biography that Rostand received in all £250,000 for one book alone; "Cyrano de Bergerac," while Daudet made more than £40,000 out of "Sapho." # SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

The Italian Premier, 'Benito Mussolini, has approved a plan for the transformation of the Palazzo Venezia into an establishment to house the offices of the Premier of Italy. It will be called the Government Palace. The palace for many years belonged to Austria and served as the Austrian Embassy.

A movement is on foot to introduce policewomen in Berlin according to the American and English example. The pioneer cities in this matter are Cologne and Leipzig, where permission and sympathy were only obtained after a hard struggle on the part of great-hearted women workers in the field of social welfare. In Berlin, although there is still some opposition, the majority of the city council are in favour of the innovation.

The opinion of Israel Zangwill as to the American actors and American plays that are sent to England was given by him at the meeting held in London not long since of the Theatrical Managers Association. After telling the managers they didn't know a really good play when they saw it, he switched to the subject of the American products with a characteristically impromptu epigram saying: "America sends us her stars—and tripe." Which is reminiscent of another Zangwillian retort. In his early days the novelist always signed his name "I Zangwill" and few lines which first name was "At an after-day's"

The Archbishop of Tuam, Co. Galway, Ireland, has delivered a smashing attack at evil literature from his pulpit. The Archbishop appealed to the Free State Government for protection against the flood of filthy and degrading literature which is imported into Ireland each year.

We are apt to make comments if we get three inches of rain in a week; in Zululand recently they got more than ten times this amount, and then there really was something to discuss. The new Tugela bridge, costing £32,000, was swept away, the railway bridge at Umfolosi was buried under four feet of sand, what was a good road is now a river of the black mud, with here and there a 10-foot hole, three new rivers have made their appearance, and the mountains sides have had huge boulders up to 50 tons weight riven from their sides.

The great game of Mah Jong, which was to oust bridge from favour in Society, is very nearly if not quite dead, and bridge remains very much alive. The Chinese invader has enjoyed a shorter vogue than most of the new sensations provided of late years, and the prophets have been falsified. A lending dealer in London bemoans that he is left with large supplies of Mah Jong sets on hand, for which there is now no demand. It is the fortune of war—or, rather of trade.

Irish Free State censors have banned the German film-story of the Life of Christ, which was recently blessed by Prebendary Carlile and other London clergymen, and shown successfully in London and elsewhere. The film was flatly turned down by the regular Dublin censor, and was then "referred to the Appeal Board: The chairman of this body" is Professor William Magennis, who recently came into the spotlight when he said: "What is good enough to show to hundred and twenty million people in America may not be good enough to show to three million people in Ireland."
