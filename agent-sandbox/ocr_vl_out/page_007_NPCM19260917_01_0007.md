## FRIDAY, SEPTEMBER 17, 1926

## COAL PARLEY

### MR. BALDWIN CONSULTS THE CABINET

MINERS SUMMONED

## ——

### Rugby, Sept. 16

The Prime Minister, who returned from Aix, les Bains last night, had a long consultation with the members of the Coal Committee of the Cabinet this morning.

In the afternoon there was a meeting of the full Cabinet, lasting two hours. It is understood that the Cabinet considered the whole inspection of the dispute in the coal industry, and endorsed the action taken by the Cabinet.

The Prime Minister has "summoned the miners' officials to move him at Downing Street to-morrow morning, at 10 o'clock and he will also to-morrow see the President of the Conf Owners' Association.—British Wireless Service.

Pending the Government's action in the coal dispute the drift back of miners to work continues. The mine owners estimate that, including safety men and outcroppers, at least 150,000 men are now employed and half a million tons of coal are being produced weekly.

## HOUSE ENTERED THROUGH SKYLIGHT

PROPERTY STOLEN.

A skylight which was left open provided access to a thief early this morning at No. 292, Des Vœux Road (Central), occupied by a Chinese married woman.

## THE WEATHER

POSITION OF TYPHQON OBSCURE.

The Observatory reports to-day as under:—

Pressure has increased moderately over Formosa and slightly at the majority of other, reporting stations.

In the absence of observations from the Japanese Empire no position can be assigned to the typhoon.

There appears to be a shallow depression over Indo-China, and an anticyclone over S.E. Mongolia.

Forecast till noon tomorrow: East winds moderate: fine to cloudy.

The gross cash receipts from sales of opium in the Colony last year totalled $14,541,757 the net revenue, excluding $72,438 derived from opium licence fees, being $12,476,028. Increased sales resulted from the influx of labour during the latter half of the year in consequence of improved conditions in the rubber market and the general atmosphere of prosperity. All the raw opium imported was Indian opium.

## AMERICAN KILLED:

## OUTRAGE BY FLEEING BANDITS

SOLDIERS SHOOT TWO:

Mexico City, Sept. 16..

Mr. Rosenthal was murdered by the bandits when Federal soldiers were pursuing them so close that they were able to kill two of the bandits.

It appears that "a ransom of $10,000 had been demanded for Mr. Rosenthal's release.—Reuter's American Service.

[A cable from Mexico City on September 13 stated:—Federal soldiers are pursuing bandits who are holding as prisoner Mr. Jacob Rosenthal, a retired New York merchant, after holding up a car in which he was motoring with a party (including three other Americans) 40 miles from Mexico City as the party was returning from the seaside resort of Cuernavaca. The bandits levelled their rifles, ordered the car to stop, took all the money and jewels, seized Rosenthal and dragged him into the neighbouring woods. The curious fact is that no ransom is demanded, and the authorities are puzzled to know why Rosenthal was specially selected as prisoner.]

## TIN BOOM

## SHARES RISE ON STOCK EXCHANGE

METAL UP £60.

Rugby, Sept. 16.

There is a small boom in tim shares on the Stock Exchange as the result of the rising price of tin, which to-day touched £316.12.6 per ton for cash.

Tin has gone up about £60 per ton for cash. Its unusual price is due to the increasing shortage in the floating supply owing to the consumption being greater than the production.—British Wireless Service.

## Pilots on Strike

According to a Wanhsien telegram of yesterday's date, General Yang Sen has concentrated his troops as well as cannon near Hungshachi in order to prepare for the reported fresh British expedition, and 70 per cent. of the citizens have fled and almost all houses are closed.

The same telegram also states that owing to the reports that General Lu Chin-shan will mediate between General Yang Sen's troops and the British war vessels and that Chinese pilots have gone on strike against British steamers, the excitement of the citizens there has been alleviated.—“Toho.”

Anti-British Agitation.

The Chinese are firing on all foreign vessels entering Hapkow reports the Shanghai correspondent of the London "Daily Mail." "A British gunboat was reported to have been hit but the damage could not be ascertained by the correspondent.

Meanwhile Peking reports that the British are leaving Szechuan province because of the anti-foreign feeling there. (Szechuan is west of Hupeh, the province in which Hankow is situated. Trouble at Wanhsien recently caused a stir in Szechuan). The Chinese are growing dangerous and the lives of white residents are believed to be in considerable danger.—" United Press.

## Tension Relieved

### Peking, Sept. 13

It is thought that General Yang Sen will be left to nurse his own troubles. Thus with hostility behind and front there is a general expectation that he will soon brought to heel.

The tension concerning the position in Szechuan has been considerably relieved by the further news that the local generals are very much shaken by fears of British reprisals.

No news has been received as yet of the Navy's plans, but it is reported from Hankow that the Consuls's representations, to the belligerents have had the effect of ensuring a cessation of the indiscriminate firing on British ships.—“N.-C. Daily News.”

## Admiral Sends Message to Navy Department

The Destroyers “Pope” and “Stewart,” at present stationed at Hankow, are being relieved by the Destroyers “Ford” and “Pillsbury,” now at Chefoo. The “Pope” will participate in target practice at Chefoo and the “Stewart” will proceed to Manila for overhauling.—“United Press.”

Culprits Not Identified.

Admiral Williams' message said that two men aboard the destroyers received "surface wounds," and that the vessels were struck several times.

The firing came from the south side of the Yangtze River 50 miles below Hankow and was not returned, as the destroyers were unable to locate the source of the shell. The firing on the steamer "Iling" was described as heavy and occurred five miles below Hankow.

A second cablegram from Admiral Williams dated yesterday said that the American gunboat "Palos," the destroyer "Stewart" and the mine-sweeper "Pigeon," defended themselves after being heavily bombardied with rifle and artillery fire by unidentified forces near Hankow.
