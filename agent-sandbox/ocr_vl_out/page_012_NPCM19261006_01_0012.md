### SIR A. COBHAM

## PIONEER PILOT OF THE

RECEIVES A KNIGHTHOO

London, Oct. 5.

Mr. Alan Cobham has been awarded a knighthood of the Most Excellent Order of the British

Sir Alan Cobham's companions, Sergenent ward and Mr. Capek have been awarded the Air Force Medal and membership of the British Empire Order, respectively.—Reuter.

### Popular Demonstration: "Rugby, Oct. 5

His Majesty has been pleased to appoint Mr. Alan Cobham a Knight Commander of the Most Excellent Order of the British Empire. Sergeant Ward has been awarded the Air Force Medal and, Mr. Capel, his other mechabic, has been made a Member of the Most Excellent Order of the British Empire.

The knighthood for "the famous airman and the distinctions for his companions" were announced by Sir Samuel Moore. Air Minister, to-day, at a luncheon in Sir Alan Cobham's honour given by the Air Council.

Sir Alan Cobham was greeted with tremendous applause.

Numerous speakers expressed admiration for his achievements, and the Air Minister said that it had brought appreciably nearer the day when London would be within a fortnight of every capital in the Empire. He said that there was no technical reason why that should not be achieved.

Sir Alan Cobham is only 31 years old, but he has already flown 300,000 miles. Among his exploits in recent years are his 12,000 miles flight through Europe, Northern Africa, Egypt, and Palestine in 1923. He made a 17,000 miles flight to Rangoon and back carrying the Director of Civil Aviation, in 1924. He flew 1,700 miles to South Africa and back in 1925. He flew 28,000 miles to Australia and back. — British Wireless Service.

## AIR CRASH

CAUSE A COMPLETE MYSTERY.

EXPERTS BAFFLED

Expert witnesses at the Inquest at Tonbridge in connection with the crash of the French aeroplane were totally unable to account for the cause of the fire. They declared that the pilot was most expert; that the machine was equipped with the most complete fire-fighting appliances; and that the wreck was so complete that it was impossible to ascertain even where the fire started.

The jury returned a verdict o

accidental death.—Reuter.

Public Enquiry To Be Made.

A verdict of accidental death, there being no evidence to show the cause of the fire, was returned at the Coroner's inquest on the seven victims of the crash of the French Air Union liner near Tonbridge.

An official of the Company said that all the latest inventions to ensure safety were fitted to their machines. He suggested that ignition of lubricating oil and not petrol may have caused the fire.

The wrecked machine had made 100 hours flight between Paris and London, and the pilot had flown 187,000 miles.

The Coroner said that a public enquiry would be held by the Air Ministry.—British Wireless Ser.

## DOCKERS' STRIKE

## WORK RESUMED AT TILBURY

London, Oct. 5.

The dockers and labourers, who struck at Tilbury yesterday, returned to work this morning. on the recommendation of the Transport Workers' Union, pending a meeting with the Port of London Authority, to discuss their grievances. Mr. Attwood has been permitted to enter the docks.—Reuter.

Meeting This Week.

Rugby Oct. 5.

Two thousand Tilbury dockers who went on strike yesterday returned to work today, and the differences that had arisen between the Port of London Authority and the officials of the men's Union, who had been forbidden to enter the dock gates, are to be the subject of a joint meeting later in the week. —British Wigless Service.

## BRITAIN & CHINA

CONSULAR INTERVENTION

NOT WANTED?

London, October 5.

The "Daily Telegraph," commenting editorially on China, says the argument against entering any kind of diplomatic intercourse with those unstable Governments which challenge the authority of Peking is unanswerable, and regrets that the British Government has to "some extent compromised its position by directing its Consular officers to intervene in more than one important case in the business of protecting British subjects and their rights," which should have been the exclusive sphere of action of the forces of the Crown."—Reuter.
