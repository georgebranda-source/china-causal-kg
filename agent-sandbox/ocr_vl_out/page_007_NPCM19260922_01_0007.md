### WENT ASTRAY."

PUISNE JUDGE REVISES

DECISION.

## ON A RENTS CASE

On the grounds that he "went astray" on a certain point, Mr. Justice Wood," in the Summary Court this morning, revised a judgment given on a claim by a landlord against Mr. Albert Guefat, proprietor of Cafe Albert, in respect of rent for his residence.

##  $ x_{1}=2 $

In a previous judgment His Honour found for defendants on claim and counterclaim (defendant alleged that he had paid the rent as well as had certain fitness put in the house for which the landlord was lable).

This morning, His Honour said that, with the help of the able argument submitted by Mr. M. K. Lo, an error in his original judgment had been shown. He had given judgment for the tenant on the landlord's claim for rent on the supposition that his defence to the claim was "release" in respect of it.

This defence had been supported by the evidence furnished for the landlord. "It seemed to me," said His Honour, "that the landlord was estopped by his agent's conduct from denying the'release.' "I gave judgment for the tenant, as I now think, on insufficient consideration.

"The distinction between'release', and 'payment' had been kept clearly before my mind. If the plea of'release' was to be relied upon, it was necessary" to prove that something, less than full payment was expected in satisfaction of the claim.

"On the other hand, a plan of 'payment' necessitated proof of payment of the child in full. This is a case where pleadings would have been of service in defining the real issues." "Release" was, in fact, never a part of the tenant's own case. It was suggested solely from the evidence of the landlord's agent. The tenant's case was "payment" not "release."

"I went astray," continued His Honour, "in assuming that it was material to find whether the Oxford's receipt was issued for cash or for some other consideration." "The question is material for the reason that the issue raised by the tenant's defence was 'payment' and nothing else, cash payment or no cash payment."

"On this issue, again, the landlord's case is not hampered by estoppel." The receipt given is a final discharge for the claim for rent, for the rent was paid in full and not otherwise. The landlord, having given the receipt to the tenant, is not thereby estopped from claiming a payment of rent from him, for the rent has not been, in fact, paid. The orus of proving payment is thereby on the tenant.

“As I have previously explained, I have not been satisfied that payment has been either proved or disproved by the evidence in the case. On these grounds, I now consider that judgment should be given for the landlord on the claim. The judgment on the counterclaim is reaffirmed each party to pay their own costs.”

## WEST POINT FIRE

MEDICINE SHOF ABLAZE AT MIDNIGHT.

$2,500 DAMAGE.

At 12:45 this morning the Central Fire Station received a call to deal with a fire which had broken out at No.438, Queen's Road West, ground floor, used as a Chinese medicine shop.

Apolliances from Kennedy Town and Central Station arrived simultaneously on the scene, followed by the Wanchai equipment on a "home call" sent five minutes later.

On the arrival of the Brigade, the contents of the floor, which comprised in addition to herbs a large quantity of bottled drugs, were well alight. With two deliveries working from the front and rear of the shop, the fire was extinguished in ten minutes.

Although the greater part of the stock was destroyed or otherwise damaged by water, the timely arrival of the Brigade and their quick work saved some portions of the fittings and confined the fire to the ground floor. There being no staircase inside the building, the fire did not spread to the two upper floors, which were practically untouched.

On "investigations · being made the fire was found to have started from burning joys" sticks placed under wooden shelves. These contained carboard boxes which quickly came within the range of the fire and in turn set alight the dry stocks of medicine leaves.

The contents of the medicine shop were insured for $1,100 jointly between the Heung On Insurance Company and the Sincere Company.

Including stock, fittings and fire damage to the ground floor of the building, the total loss is estimated at about $2,500.

## NO COERCION

## MINERS' VIEW OF NEW PROPOSALS:

REPLY TO MR. BALDWIN

The miners' executive yesterday and to-day discussed the letter from the Prime Minister to the Miners' secretary on September 18, in which Mr. Baldwin stated that the Mining Association declined to make a national agreement with the Miners' Federation but the Association declared that the 'con' owners in all districts were willing to observe the main principles which a national agreement would be designed to secure.

Mr. Baldwin's letter enclosed a memorandum explaining the National Arbitration Tribunal which the Government would establish—failing the conclusion of a national agreement—as soon as work was generally resumed. Though provisional settlements might be made locally the Tribunal might confirm or modify such settlements on appeal.

The Miners' Executive today replied that in the event of the stoppage being prolonged by an attempt to force back the miners on district agreements the responsibility would rest with the Government and the owners. The miners adhered to the principle of national negotiation and agreements. They desired an honourable settlement, but would not be coerced into accepting terms which they considered unjust. Reuter.

Nearly A Deadlock.

The coal conversations continued at Downing Street till one o'clock when it was officially announced that they are being resumed later to-day.

A flat rejection of the Government's proposals and the inevitable deadlock were narrowly averted during the two days' discussion of the proposals by the Miners' Executive. The leaders decided to ask Mr. Baldwin for an interview to explain what they regarded as a wide' divergence between Mr. Churchill's scheme—which the miners interpreted as confirmation by the Government of the principle of national negotiations and agreements—and Mr. Baldwin's proposals, which the miners contend are diametrically opposed to those of Mr. Churchill.

The miners are particularly suspicious of Mr. Baldwin's National Arbitration Tribunal. They suggest that if there must be one it should be established before the men resume work as a guarantee of good faith.—Reuter.

N.C.O. KILLED.

The newspapers report that serious disorders have broken out among the troops at Assen who were undergoing their annual manoeuvres. It is believed that the disorders were prearranged, as ball cartridges were recently missing.

A long route march in the sweltering heat yesterday was apparently the climax to the situation. On their return to barracks the men smashed the windows and wrecked the canteen. An N.C.O. was killed by rifle bullet, and there were other minor casualties.

## DUTCH POLITICS

### ECONOMIC RESTORATION. PROCEEDING

The Hague, Sept. 21

In the speech from the Throne at the opening of the States General, the Queen stated the situation of the country gave ground for gratitude in many respects. Although shadows were not lacking, the economic restoration was proceeding very gradually.
