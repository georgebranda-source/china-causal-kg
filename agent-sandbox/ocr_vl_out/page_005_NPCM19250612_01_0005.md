## MERRY SPEECH

THE PREMIER AND THE PRESS.

The Prime Minister, Mr. Stanley Baldwin, was the principal guest at the 85th annual dinner of the Newspaper Society at the Hotel Victoria, Northumberland Avenue, W.C. The society's membership is drawn chiefly from provincial news papers.

THE BIG UUNB FROM THE PROVINCES.

Mr. Baldwin said that some little time ago, had he been asked to attend a gathering of that nature, he should have come among them as a Daniel in the den of lions. But not so now. He spoke that night as a peer among his peers—he was an honorary life member of the Press Club. So far from being a Daniel, he had joined the majority, and he was a lion. (Laughter.) He had not yet learned to roar. That might come. (Laughter.)

Remarking that the Newspaper Society dated from very shortly after the great Reform Bill, he continued:

In those days—I am not going to comment on the fact, for this is a non-party meeting—there was a fourpenny tax on newspapers. Today we are apparently, if I may believe the newspapers, putting a fourpenny tax on business. (Laughter.) At the same time there was a 3s. Gd. tax on advertisements; I should have thought that a most admirable impost, corresponding very nearly to the income tax: after that amazing Budget of my colleague. (Laughter.)

In those days—largely, I suppose, owing to the want of facilities for transport—London monopolised much of "the power and wealth of the kingdom, and the provincial newspapers were largely dependent on London in the matter of advertisements and there was no common action among the owners of newspapers throughout the country.

So that and the agitation against the stamp duty on newspapers brought you together in self-defence. You formed a kind of union, which has lasted from that day to this, to promote and to protect the interests of the country newspapers.

## THEIR ROLE

I understand that during the last few years you have very kindly condemned to allow one or two emment gentlemen connected with the Press in London to attend your gatherings. (Laughter.) But do remember this; that your distinctive rôle is to keep London in its proper place—(laughter and choers)—to prevent either an interfering Parliament of London or a flamboyant advertisement of that city from corrupting "the simple morals of the provinces.

I am whole-heartedly with you, for I am a provincial heart and soul. I know there are persons who give themselves airs and use the term "provincial" in a disparaging sense, implying somehow some disability or limitation in our method or powers or manner of speech, some idiosyncrasy in the way we dress.

I prefer my Worcestershire tongue to what I hear in London. (Cheers.) I prefer the clothes I wear in Worcester to what I wear in London. (Cheers.) What would London be without the provinces? And where would the London Press be? One paper very well known which has recently presented itself in a new dress—the "Morning Post." (Cheers.) Where does the "Morning Post" go to get its editor?—Swansea. (Cheers.)

There is one paper which I feel it would be less majestic for me to mention—"The Daily Mail." "The Daily Mail" went to Portsmouth to get its great man—which accounts for the militant spirit of that paper—(Laughter)—and the facility with which it can handle every kind of artillery from the 15-inch to the pom-pom. (Lord Laughter.)

You have a gentleman of great renown and known to most of you, who has just written a valuable book for the layman, as I am, on journalism—Mr. Spender. Was he reared in London? Not at all. He came from a city visited since the times of the Romans for the celebrity of its waters, where what they are short of in what is palatable they make up for for the good they do. (Laughter).

The tide-table given below has been compiled at the National Almanac Office in London from the result of the analysis of observations taken by means of an automatic tide-recording machine in the Water Police Basin at Tsim Sha Tmi during the years 1908-9.

The zero of the table corresponds with the raro of the sounding in the Admiralty Chart, which has been found to be 4 feet 3 inches below mean sea level.
