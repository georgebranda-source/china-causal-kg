## Charge

Alleging that on the instigation of Mr. A. E. Murphy, of the Kowloon Tong Development Company, they had been wrongfully arrested, falsely charged and detained for two or three hours, six collies engaged on the work of levelling ground at Kowloon Tong claimed $1,000 joint damages at the Summary Court this morning.

Alleged Wrongful Detention and

Mr. D. McCallum appeared for the "plaintiffs and Mr. R. A. Wadeson for Mr. Murphy, the defendant.

The plaintiffs' case was that one of the six coolies was proceeding from work at 5 p.m. on July 27, carrying some cement bags, the property of his employers, the Tung Lee Company. He was stopped by an Indian watchman employed by the Kowloon Tong Development Company and accused of stealing the bags.

The coome was then taken to the house of Mr. Murphy at 47 Kowloon Tong and Mr. Murphy ordered his detention. The Tung Lee Company, said Mr. McCallum, had a contract with the Government for levelling certain ground at Kowloon Tong. Mr. Murphy was employed by the Kowloon Tong Development Co. which had nothing whatsoever to do with the Tung Lee.

## Workmen Protest

Certain of the fellow workmen of "the man who was detained by Mr. Murphy went to the house to enquire into the cause of his detention and to protest. They also, it was alleged, had been detained and abused "within degree."

Later, the police went to the scene, continued Mr. McCallum, presumably on information lodged by Mr. Murphy; the men were handcuffed, somewhat roughly handled, a revolver pointed at them and marched through the streets. "It was not until eight o'clock that they were released on the order of the police. " Subsequently, a letter was written to Mr. Murphy on behalf of the plaintiffs (by Messrs. Leo d'Almada and Mason) in which it was stated that the men were acting only in the course of their employment, that Mr. Murphy had obtained a revolver from his wife and, pointing at one of the men, had roughly pushed him towards his (Mr. Murphy's) office, at the same time directing the Indian watchmen to take the others to the same place. This, stated the letter, the watchmen had done, covering the workmen with their rifles in the meantime. After investigation of the charge the police had ordered their release. The letter concluded that it was difficult to understand such conduct and intimated that unless redress was made proceedings would be instituted.

To this letter there had been no reply, stated Mr. McCallum.

## Police Evidence

Inspector Stimson, in charge of Kowloon City Police station, said that it was shortly after 6 p.m. on the day in question that he got to the charge room. The six plaintiffs were there in the dock in charge of Indian watchmen. Mr. Murphy was not there and witness had difficulty in understanding what the charge against the men was. Some cement bags were produced which the plaintiffs were alleged to have stolen.

In reply to questions, Inspector Stimson said that the sergeant interpreter was in charge of the station during witness's absence and it would have been he who ordered them to be placed in the dock. So far as witness knew the men were not handcuffed when he saw them but it was the practice to take handcuffs off when placing people in the dock. Witness made an entry in the charge book.

Asked why he had done this if no charge had been formulated against the men, witness replied that he gathered from replies to questions put through interpreters that the men were charged with the theft of the bags.

## STONES THROWN

BRITISH LAND PARTY AT CANTON.

## WHARF INCIDENT

Armed British bluejacket and marines landed on the "two wharves of the Hongkong, Canton and Macno Steamboat Co., Ltd., at Canton, on Saturday, and ejected a party of strike pickets and others.

Stones were subsequently thrown, states a report which has reached the “China Mail,” but it is almost certain that the missiles were not directed at the landing party.

It is believed that in the pell-mell which ensued when the tres-passers rushed on to the western bund, a coolie carrying two-wine jar's was bowled over, and his aamshu lost through the containers breaking.

A "row" followed, a bully being jeered. Stones were thrown at the bully but not on to the wharf.

## Dining Room Upset?

There are two wharves, similar in appearance, on the Bund, which are the property of the company. The space in between is also British owned. Ropes have now been secured between the two wharves to prevent people landing there.

A correspondent states that strike pickets have used the wharf as a refectory. This seems to be borne out by the observations of an eyewitness who saw the caterer to the strikers rapidly evacuating cooking utensils, bowls, chopsticks and "chow" that was in course of preparation for the evening meal.

It appears that the British Consul has been in negotiation with the Canton. Foreign Office about the occupation of the wharves—discussed since last year when the steamers anchored in the stream prevent agitators "getting at" the crews, etc.

## "Row" on the Bund

The Strike Executive is reported to have issued orders for bidding their men to enter foreign buildings.

Till noon on Saturday, the trespassers were given to leave. About 2 m. p.m., several British gunboats steamed into position and parties were landed from armed launches. The sailors soon got to work and were in possession with little or no trouble.

Strike pickets were alleged to have fallen out with two pedestrians on the road. These men were taken by the police to a police station in the vicinity; from whence they are believed to have been forcibly removed to strike headquarters.

The “China Mail” learns that a man was subsequently found on the street with grievous wounds in his abdomen. It is said that the man was so badly cut that his organs fell out! He is surmised to have been mixed up in the “row” among the Chinese, on the bund, during the confusion.

This incident, lacks confirmation and is only given for what is worth.

## YANGTZE TRUTH:

## British Gunboats in Action

## INCIDENTS EXPLAINED

How B. and S. Steamer Came to

Details regarding the seizure of a Butterfield and Swire teamer on the Yangtse River, and the movements of certain British warships, have reached the "China Mail," throwing considerable light on the situation.

- Wan Hsien, where the steamer was seized, is far up the Yangtse River—beyond the war zone—in Szechuan province, which is ruled by General Yang Sen, nominally a subordinate of Wu Pei-fu.

The boat was surrounded by sampans at Wan Hsien, troops of Yang Sen trying to get on board the steamer. The ship was fired on and she then stopped in the river. The sampans containing the soldiers then collided and sank.

## Gunboats' Adventures

General Yang alleges that the steamer ran "down the sampans and thereupon ordered the vessel to be seized.

H.M.S. "Cockchafer" proceeded to the scene but ran aground. She was subsequently fired on, presumably by troops from the banks.

Then H.M.S. "Widgeön" was sent along to do what was necessary.

H.M.'s Consul at Chungking has also gone along to Wan Hsien to settle the matter if possible. (Chungking is the last big "port" on the upper reaches of the Yangtse, also in Szechuan province)

As regards H.M.S. "Scarab," which was reported by Reuter to have been in action with southern troops for two hours, disconnected messages have reached Hong Kong. As far as can be made out, firing was opened at the gunboat from the shore and was replied to, as has been done on other occasions.

## MARITAL TROUBLE

### QUEER DEFENCE BY A HUSBAND. THREAT TO KILL

An extraordinary defence was put forward by a Chinese when he was charged before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning with causing grievous bodily injuries to his wife. He denied the assault, but stated that he had a Bamboo pole in his hand.

His Worship: And the bamboo pole happened to fall on her head? Defendant readily agreed to this suggestion.

Divisional Inspector Blackman in a statement said that the woman was apparently the defendant's wife, and they had had a dispute between them. It was alleged by the woman that she was attacked with a hatchet, but he thought this was hardly consistent with her wounds.

Defendant was fined $5 and bound over to keep the peace.

On leaving court the woman informed the Magistrate that she had been threatened with death since his arrest. She was assured of protection by the police.
