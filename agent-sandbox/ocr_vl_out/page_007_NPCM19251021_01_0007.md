## LORRY DEATH

## NO BLAME ATTACHED

## ROAD WIDENING UROED

The inquiry into the death of a Chinese woman who was killed by being struck by projecting beams from a motor truck whilst she was travelling in another lorry at Talpo Road was continued at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday with Mr. E.-W. Hamilton sitting as Coroner and a jury composed of Messrs. R. M. Omar (foreman), Poon Ping-kong and J. J. V. Remedios.

The truck which carried the timber belong to Sir W. G. Armstrong, Whitworth & Co., Ltd., and was being driven by an Indian.

Mr. R. W. Pearson, of this firm, said that he supervised the loading of the lorry and took full responsibility. The beams had been properly fastened without any projections over the side, and witness was of the opinion that the deceased was leaning out of the lorry when passing the other vehicle.

After the evidence of the Indian driver of the lorry, the jury retired and brought in a verdict of death from a fractured skull as the result of a pure accident, no blame being attached to anyone. They added a rider that in view of the number of big lorries using that portion of the Taipo Road (near the Junction of Laichikok Road) it was desirable that the road should be widened as few feet as soon as possible.
