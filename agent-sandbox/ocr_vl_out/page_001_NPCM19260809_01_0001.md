## MAGISTRACY ITEMS

EXHIBITS IN COURT.

More than the usual number of assault cases and lareniess made up the charge sheets at the Central Magistracy this morning before Mr. R. E. Lindsell.

A blacksmith, from Shaukiwan with a wounded head was the complainant in an assault charge against a boat builder, the wound stated to have been inflicted with a wooden pole when complainant went on Saturday morning to collect the balance of an old debt amounting to $1.55. Complaintant alleged that he was attacked by the defendant and three others.

Defendant was fined $3 and ordered to sign a personal bond for future good behaviour. He was further ordered to pay $2 as compensation to the complainant, thus satisfying the debt.

Two scavenging coolies were involved in another case, the aggrieved party appearing with a wounded leg. An iron bar was brought to prove the assault.

Complainant said that they had a dispute yesterday over a broom and he was attacked at Leighton Hill Road this morning.

Defendant attempted to prove that he was also assaulted, but he had to pay a fine of $5, or go to prison for seven days.

Sergeant James. of the Aberdeen Police Station, charged two women and a man with assaulting another women who was in hospital with a deep wound in the thigh.

The injured woman was unable to say how she received the wound, and Sergeant James thought it possible that she fell over an anchor which was found there, as alleged by the defendants in their statements to the police and in court.

Defendants were remanded to bail for one week.

In explanation of a charge of unlawful possession of a bundle of copper wire and metal, a Chinese boy told the Magistrate that he found them in a lane at Wanchai.

Div.-Inspector Blackman said that inquiries had been made since Saturday morning to trace the owner, but without success. The boy received eight strokes with a light cane.

An apprentice-failed to get away with a bucket containing ten pounds of tin soldier, the property of the Taikoo Docks. He was charged with theft.

Defendant, who pleaded that he was pressed to commit the theft owing to starvation, was sentenced to four weeks' hard labour.

## ARMS IN BOOTS

PIRACY PREVENTED?

Five Chinese passengers of the s.s. "Suiyang" who trited to put into practice a stratagem one sometimes sees recorded in a cinema plot, were all arrested and charged this morning before Mr.

## MIDNIGHT BLAZE

YAUMATI MEDICINE SHOP DESTROYED.

BRIGADE SAVES BUILDING.

A fire broke out in Kowloon a few minutes before midnight, last night, in the ground floor of a three-storied building, the second block in Shanghai Street near the junction of Public Square Street, Yaumati.

Two appliances left the Kowloon Fire Station, followed by one from the Mongkok Station on the "home call" being given. Three deliveries tackled the fire from different angles, one of them being brought in from a lane at the rear of the building.

The efforts of the Brigade confined the fire to the ground floor, used as a herbalists' shop, where the stock of medicine leaves was destroyed. The fire spread to the ceiling and affected part of the first floor planking, but this was checked by the firemen taking a hose to the floor by ladder. The contents of this portion of the building used as a dwelling suffered water damage.

The appliances returned, to their Station 44 minutes after the receipt of the call.

## Narrow Escape

Although the impact was considerable the pillar shows little signs of the collision. This is the second false fire alarm-within-two months from the Hongkong Club and is believed to have been due to defective wiring. The Club is connected up direct with the fire station and as soon as the indicator falls the brigade immediately gets busy. The falling of the signal does not; however, open up immediate telephonic communication with the Club and as seconds may be precious the fire station on both occasions has immediately sent the machines away without confirming an outbreak.

## Second False Alarm

In June of this year a new alarm was put in at the Club and when the last false alarm occurred a glass cover which was being made for the push bell had not been completed. It was thought that the last alarm might have been due to this push bell having been accidentally touched. The push bell is now, however, under-glass-and-the-possibility-of-accidental contact is therefore removed. It is thought that a crossing of wires may have something to do with it and the Club authorities are having the connections examined.
