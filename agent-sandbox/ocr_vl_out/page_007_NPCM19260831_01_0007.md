## MINERS' EXECUTIVE MOVE

### Hint of. Climax Being Reached

The Miners' Executive met this evening to discuss fresh proposals for ending the dispute. At the close of the meeting they proceeded to the House of Commons to confer with the Parliamentary Labour Party Executive.

Sir William Joynson Hicks, Home Secretary, moved a resolution for the continuance of the Emergency Regulations, and his speech was much interrupted from the Labour Benches, one member, Mr. Jack Jones, being suspended after refusing to accept the ruling of the Speaker.

Referring to the business before the House of Commons M. Ramsay MacDonald suggested that instead of proceeding to discuss the general situation in the coal dispute before the discussion on the renewal of the regulations, the general debate should be taken to tomorrow as certain conferences were being held to day and certain explorations were being made regarding the situation.

This course was adopted.

Sir W. Joynson. Hicks said that the chief value of these regulations was in their preventive effect. On the whole the mining disغriets had remained quiet and orderly, and he paid a tribute to the excellent behaviour of the great emission of miners most of whom were suffering under what they considered a great sense of grievance. During last month only 64 cases had been taken under the provisions of the Emergency Regulations; and the bulk of those concerned in such cases were not miners.

Mr. Ramsay MacDonald contended that no case of continuing the regulations had been established. The country was peaceful, and there had been nothing in the history of this or any other country of such a quiet and orderly prosecution of an industrial dispute. The worst that had happened could, he said, be dealt with adequately under the ordinary law.

The debate is proceeding. British Wireless Service.

Sir Austen Chamberlain replied that the message was not sent through the Ambassador but, "with my knowledge and full concurrence." (Conservative cheers).

The Speaker asked Mr. Jones to withdraw.

Mr. Jones began his exit at a snail's piece, stopping at intervals to abuse the Government benches. He was finally suspended by 235 votes to 53.

Sir H. Slesser (Labourite, South-East Leeds) moved to terminate zero of the emergency regulations - one dealing with nets likely to reuse edition and the other with public meetings and processions.

Dr. Stacy A. Ransom one of the best known men in Shanghai and certainly one of the oldest residents among the American Community at Shanghai died at 8 o'clock last night (August 26) in the General hospital from Bright's disease at the age of 59 years.

DR. STACY A. RANSOM

## OBITUARY

Dr. Ransom had Been a sufferer from the disease for some considerable time past, but it was not until last week while on a holiday in Peitain to that the symptoms grew worse necessitating a quick return to Shanghai and an immediate entrance into hospital.

Born in Washington D.C., Dr. Ransom was educated at the University of Georgetown and at the age of 26 graduated in Medicine.

Prior to coming to Shanghai in 1902 Dr. Ransom served with the colours in the Spanish American War.

Immediately on reaching Shanghai Dr. Ransom was appointed Quarantine and Medical Officer to the American Consulate General and since then has been the United States Public Health Officer in Shanghai.

A popular member of all the principal clubs, sporting and social, the late doctor was also well-known and esteemed for his public works the chief of which were President of the Shanghai Medical Society. President of the American Association, Chairman of the Board of the Shanghai General Hospital and member of the Shanghai Municipal Council.

In 1903 Dr. Ransom was appointed second in Command of the Shanghai Volunteer Corps which rank he held at the time of his death, and in 1906 he organised and founded the American Company which he commanded for 14 years and which is to-day one of the foremost companies of the Corps.

A crack shot with the rifle Dr. Rainsom was for a considerable time, Chairman of the Executive Committee of the Shanghai Rifle Association and helped the local team considerably time and time again in a interport rifle matches.

At one time Dr. Ransom, who was a Shriner, was District Grand Master of the local Masonic Lodge.

Dr. Ransom leaves a wife who is now in Shanghai and one son who is at present serving with the American Infantry having graduated from the West Point Military Academy. To both of the bereaved the sympathy of the community will be extended.—“China Press.”

## ANIMAL COURTSHIP

### PROFESSOR J. HUXLEY'S LECTURE

London, August 10.—One of the most attractive evening lectures before the British Association for the Advancement of Science was delivered by, Professor Julian Huxley on animal 'courtship in which he asserted that Darwin's theory of sexual selection was essentially right, though wrong in many details. The first actions to which the name of courtship must be given were those of a few crabs and most spiders. The male fiddler crab had a huge claw, weighing possibly as much as the rest of its body, and often brightly coloured in the mating season. when the female passed the males assumed a tiptoe attitude and the big claw was held rigidly aloft. If the female did not notice this gesture, the males hurried after her again and struck a statuesque pose.

One type of hunting spiders offered the female a nice fly neatly wrapped in silk like a bouquet.

Web-spinners, which were almost blind, vibrated a thread in the female web in a special way different from the vibration with which they trapped prey. Otherwise they might be eaten by the female who was much bigger than the male. Sometimes the male was eaten after the act of mating. Times of Ceylon.

## CATHOLIC CONGRESS

CHICAGO ECLIPSED BY MADRID AND VIENNA.

Rome, August 13.

While the Eucharistic Congress of Chicago was gigantic in dimension and emblematic of America in its democratic setting and "biggest in the world" spirit for magnificence and pomp, it would be hard to surpass the congress held in Madrid in 1911 and Vienna in 1912.

A special solid silver monstrance, wolching seven hundred pounds, placed on a state carriage laden with roses and jasmin and attended by King Alfonso featured the congress of Madrid. In Vienna, the papal legate, Cardinal Van Rossum rode in the golden carriage of the Empress Maria Teresa, drawn by eight pure white Hungarian steeds, followed by the "Emperor Franz Josef in his state coach with emblazoned outriders, accompanied by the Archduke. Ferdinand and the entire court. Ninety thousand persons participated in the procession.

In the course of the final meeting of the Madrid Congress of 1911, King Alfonso made an important speech in which he recalled the historic claims of Spain as a Catholic country. The King and Queen of Spain got down from their state coach to receive the Eucharistic procession when it reached the portals of the royal palace.

The procession which terminated the Viehna Eucharistic Congress, of 1912 was, especially magnificent. More than 8,000 priests, 2,800 officers of the Austrian army, 3,500 state employees, 92 deputies, 260 bishops and 12 cardinals took part in it.

The first Eucharistic Congress was held at Lille in 1881; the second at Avignon, 1882; the third, Liege, 1883; the fourth, Frieburg, Germany, 1885; the fifth, Toulouse, 1886; the sixth, Paris, 1888; the seventh, Antwerp, 1890; and the eighth at Jerusalem in 1892.
