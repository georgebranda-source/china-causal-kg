## MERCHANTS BANK

Little has been heard of the Chinese Merchants Bank, Ltd., which suddenly closed its doors some time ago. It is now stated that order has been evolved and a first dividend may be paid. So much has been intimated as creditors have been asked to register their claims by the end of this month.

According to rumours in business circles, about 20 per cent. is expected in the first dividend. Anything further will depend on the results of recovery from the Bank's alleged debtors.

It will be recalled that the Bank was interested mainly in the rice trade, with offices at Shanghai, Canton, Saigon and Singapore.

The Canton accounts have not been handed over to the liquidator, it is stated. Representatives of depositors (or creditors), of that branch, together with leading merchants are said to have been recognised—it is not known by what process or authority—as a committee to wind up the affairs at Canton. Therefore, that branch will not come into the general scheme of liquidation.

Popular opinion is that about $800,000 owing to Hongkong creditors—or rather by the Hongkong office—will rank for dividend.

## SUMMING UP

Proof Of Criminal Agreement

Necessary.

In his summing up at this morning's hearing of the case, as presented for and against the two prisoners charged with criminal conspiracy to defraud the Wing On Company of $50,000. His Lordship made mention first of the fact that the two had been called upon to answer jointly, at least in regard to the first count. It may have seemed to the jury something in the nature of a hardship, said His Lordship, but he thought he could assure them that in fact it had not caused either embarrassment in the presentation of their case.

With regard to the first count there was an element in the evidence of alleged conspiracy peculiar to it, viz. consisting in an alleged agreement between the alleged conspirators. As a general rule if a man was brought up on a charge it was necessary to prove that he did certain criminal acts, in the case of stealing that he took and carried away an article with the intention of converting it to his own use. But the peculiarity of conspiracy was that an agreement had to be proved between two or more to commit a criminal offence or unlawfully by criminal means. The first fact the jury had to bear in mind as necessary for the proving of the constitution of an offence and for a verdict of guilty to be in their minds established was that two persons should have been involved in the series of acts which went to make up the alleged offence.

## Agreement Necessary

For instance, supposing they found that the first prisoner had done the alleged forgeries they must bear in mind that the charge involved the agreement in a conspiracy of others. Mere agreement was all that was necessary to constitute the offence but it was in fact very rarely in which - mere agreement alone was proved. If people, for instance, were to enter for criminal purposes into a written criminal agreement the chances were that no evidence other than the proving of the document and of the genuineness of the signatures would be adduced. As a general rule, however, facts going to prove criminal intentions were produced.

But persons acting with criminal intent did not as a rule enter into a written partnership. What one usually found in a case of this kind was an allegation that conspiracy had been formed and a series of facts produced in evidence from which the jury were asked to judge whether or not the inference could be drawn that the acts were committed in the pursuance of common criminal purposes. Such was the nature of the charges in this case. It was stated that the prisoners formed an agreement and entered into a conspiracy to defraud and that in pursuance of that agreement they did certain acts from which agreement itself could be inferred.

Another feature of the case of conspiracy to which His Lordship addressed the special attention of the jury was that supposing they were in fact satisfied that conspiracy was proved against number 1 prisoner acts by 1 in furtherance of criminal purpose would be evidence against the others. The same applied if conspiracy was proved and acts were proved against 2 in furtherance of criminal purpose. The proof would be evidence against 1. But the jury must first of all be satisfied that conspiracy had been established.
