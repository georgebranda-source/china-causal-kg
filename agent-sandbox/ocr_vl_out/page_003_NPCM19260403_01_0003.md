## CHING MING

CHINESE FESTIVAL OF "PURE BRIGHTNESS."

ANCESTOR WORSHIPPING

“Ch’ing Ming” festival (Pure Brightness) this year falls on Easter Monday, when the Chinese will worship their ancestors' tombs.

Europeans will have already noticed the approach of the festival by the applications from their staffs and servants for leave to return to their native villages, should such be anywhere close at hand.

There will be large numbers who will pay their respects to departed ancestors. Most of the Chinese cemeteries are in "the neighbourhood of Pokfulam and Aberdeen and it has been stated that extra buses will be put or to cope with the increased traffic.

The day will be observed as a partial holiday by Chinese: in Hongkong, without any special celebrations.

In most cases, the worshipping consists of the usual burnt offerings, and meditation.

Ching Ming, as one of the greater Chinese festivals, is the fifth of the twenty-four solar periods into which the year is divided. It is sometimes named the Festival of Tombs, for special thought is given to the dead, while graves are put in good order. It was anciently the day for the re-kindling of fires, which had been extinguished three days before. It is also a time for picnics and excursions into the country, and houses are decorated with foliage. Various things connected with this festival remind the Western student of the Christian Easter.

Han Shih (meaning "cold food") is a Chinese festival held immediately before Ch'ing Ming. In olden times, for three days no fires are supposed to have been kindled; hence the name of the feast.

The feast is said to have its origin in the death of Chieh Chih-t'ui, but this is only a more modern explanation.

## PAPERS LONDON POLICE SEIZED

"NATIVES OF ORIENT."

There has been considerable discussion of late (says a London paper in mail week) on the advisability of the Home Office disclosing more of the discoveries made by the police in the raid on the Communist Party headquarters last October, which resulted in the conviction and imprisonment of twelve of the Communist leaders.

The haul was one of the largest of the kind ever made by Scotland Yard.

Detective-Sergeant Renshaw, of the Special Branch, stated during the court proceedings that a quarter of a million items of documents, books, files, and the like were seized.

They made 12 van loads, and their removal occupied seven hours.

At the trial only a few documents were rendered as evidence, and the impression was given that the Attorney-General had decided to produce the minimum number thought necessary.

Subsequent events, such as the implication of university undergraduates and natives of the Orient in Communist propaganda, have suggested that discoveries of a serious nature, other than those revealed in court, were made.

Only certain facts relating to the extent to which the British Communist Party has been subsidised from abroad since 1920 were disclosed to the jury.

SIR C. CLEMENTI PRESENT

Major R. Melville Smith submitted the toast of the guests and spoke of the honour the Company felt at the presence of His Excellency, Hon. Colonel of the Corps. In the course of a humorous speech, the speaker referred to the references which had been made locally with regard to the application of H.M. King George's wish that British troops should be supplied with the "best" beer.

He didn't quite like to infer that the Engineer Company was composed of beer specialists, said the speaker, and history did not relate how far their R.E. collaborators were responsible for the shortage. But he was sure that the Company would like to turn the suggestion into an invitation, and he could assure His Excellency that he would find in the Canteen two British beers and one German. The speaker had every reason to suppose that the quality was all that it is supposed to be.

## POMPÉII FIND

BRONZE STATUETTES OF STREET VENDORS.

Four characteristic and highly interesting statuettes have just been brought to light at Pompeii. All four represent a "Placentarius" (pastry vendor) and are executed in a most artistic manner in bronze covered with silver patina. While keeping to the character of an ambulatory vendor, the artist chose the type of a Jew, bald and bent, perhaps one of those street amblers who earned a miserable living in the markets of Pompeii and Naples by selling eatables, such as snails, honey cakes, and other popular dainties. The figures hold on high large rectangular trays. The right hand grasps the throat, and they smack their tongues with evident glee, just like the pastry cooks of our own day in Naples, who call out in dialect! Cheste a lava & luglio ca to reerea o'stomaco, to signify that no one else sells such delicious cakes.

## ARCTIC FILM

Locked in the Ice, a British ship, carrying fifteen people, two of them women, will brave the hardships of the next Arctic winter off Devon Island, a Canadian Island north of Baffin Island, and six hundred miles south of the North Pole.

This expedition, organised by Mr. Victor Dines, a fur-trader. Is to make a fila based on a story specially written by Miss Winifred Graham. Short interest films of whaling, polar bears, and so on will also be taken, and the vessel, which sails under Commander F. A. Worsley, chief navigator of Shackleton's "Quest" will start for home in the summer of 1927. The entire enterprise will be British, and U.C personnel is to include four British film actors.

## RELIGIOUS WORK

EVANGELISATION IN NEW TERRITORIES.

ANNUAL MEETING

The conditions in the New Territories, in the various Churches this year, have been very good, states the annual report of the Hongkong and New Territories Evangelisation Society.

The report goes on to state that the members baptised have increased and the contributions have not decreased. The preachers and members of Committee in the various places have been sincere and faithful, and have shown resource in dealing with affairs, and the evangelistic work in the villages has often far exceeded expectations.

The accounts showed a total income for the year of about $8,400, raised in fairly equal proportions by European and Chinese contributions.

The annual meeting at which the report was presented was held in the lecture hall of Union Church, the Rev. J. Kirk Maconachie presiding.

Addresses by the- Society's Chinese workers followed, the Rev. H. R. Wells translating.

The superintendent evangelist, Rev. Li Shu-kwal, who, together with Mr. Wells takes general oversight of the work, made some interesting references to the fact that in spite of all the troubles in Kwangtung and Hongkong, no difficulties had appeared in the relations between foreign and Chinese elements in the whole course of the year's operations. The reason for that, in his opinion, is that in Christian work and principle, goodwill and consequent unity are achieved, and there lies the hope of the future.

Amongst those present were the Revs. Dr. Pearce and J. H. Johnson, Dr. (Mrs.) Hickling, Dr. (Miss) A. Sydenham, Dr. Ashton, Dr. Early, Dr. Hollings, Capt. W. Davison, Messrs. A. S. D. Cousland, E. B. Cubey, A. H. Mackenzie, J. L. MacPherson, C. C. Hickling and many Chinese ladies and gentlemen. Mrs. Ho Shu-lau, Hon. Secretary, and Mr. E. R. Dovey, Hon. Treasurer, were also present, the latter pointing out that with more funds the work could be much extended.
