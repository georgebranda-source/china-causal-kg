## CITY FIRE

## BLAZE AT WELLINGTON

A MORNING CALL

The third floor of No. 162, Wellington Street, used as an enting house, was gutted in a fire which broke out on this part of the building this morning. The flames had secured a good hold when the alarm was received at the Central Fire Station, but the efforts of the fire-fighters to quell the outbreak proved successful after considerable labour.

The inmates of all the floors look to the street on the alarm being given and there were no asualties.

Besides the third floor, which was left with the bare walls standing, the flames worked into a part of the second floor used as a restaurant. The contents of the floors below were damaged by water.

The extent of the damage has not been assessed: It is understood that the building was insured for $4,000.

## ACCIDENTS

## TWO MOTOR CASES AND A DEATH

Driving a motor car at Coronation Road yesterday afternoon, Mrs. Geldart, of Torres Building, Kowloon, ran into a Chinese girl who came from the rear of a bus.

The girl received injuries to the face and was taken by Mr. Geldart in her car to the Kowloon Hospital and later to the Kwong Wah Hospital. On inquiries being made by the police after a report was made, it was found that the girl had been removed by her people after receiving attention.

At 2.30 this morning a motor bus ran into two ricsha coolies at Gascoigne Road, necessitating the removal of the men to the Kwong Wah Hospital. The driver of the bus has been detained.

While sitting over the edge of a Taikoo Sugar Refinery lighter lying alongside the wharf at North Point, a member of the crew dropped into the water at 7 o'clock this morning.

The coxswain heard the call "man overboard" and promptly threw a rope. An attempt was made by the unfortunate man to reach the rope, but failing in this he was carried away by the strong current flowing at the time and lost.

### PROTEST "STRIKE."

HOIHOW MERCHANTS AGAINST PICKETS

BOYCOTT BOYCOTTED

A boycott has been declared by the merchants of Hoihow against the pickets, who are maintaining the anti-foreign boycott at that port.

As a matter of fact, the commercial interests have suspended operations against what they consider to be the high-handed action of the local boycott committee.

This Monday, a foreign steamer (said to be the "Mingsang") was in port. A junk, loaded with poultry for export, was making for the ship when pickets—vigil

ance having been increased on the steamer's arrival—gave chase. Rather than let the goods fall into the hands of the pickets, the shippers' fokis threw most of it overboard. As the birds were in baskets in crates, some of the

abandoned poultry was retrieved. At any rate, one foki was arrested. He was sentenced by a "committee of cargo, investigation-tion" to be marched, in shame, around the town, as a lesson to others.

The mercantile bodies considered the punishment too harsh. Business organisations decided that doors should be closed in protest.

Several hundred men are also reported to have marched to local government offices to petition redress.

A subsequent incident was the capture of one of several lecturers (or street speakers) sent out by semi-official authorities. He fell into the hands of a crowd but was rescued by the action of boycott pickets, who took into custody a bad character.

## TWO DISCHARGED

Two out of the three Chinese arrested in connection with the discovery of alleged bomb material at No. 24, Yee Wo Street, Causeway Bay, were discharged by Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning when Sub-Inspector Field had no evidence to offer against them.

Mr. A. E. Hall, defending, offered no objection to this course.

## RICSHA MISHAP

## WHEEL AND AXLE PART

COOLIE INJURED

A peculiar accident occurred at Bonham Road yesterday as Dr. W. B. A. Moore, of the Victoria Hospital, was riding in a ricsha. One wheel of the vehicle dropped out and the coolie was knocked down to the ground by receiving a prod on his side with a portion of the shaft.

Dr. Moore was not hurt and escaped with a mere shaking. The coolie was struck in the region of his spleen and also received abrasions to the right ankle.

A unique occasion in that it was a triple ceremony was observed today by the 1st Battalion, the East Surrey Regiment, which assembled about 700 strong on the Murray parade ground this morning.

It was a parade typical of the solemn pageantry of the British Army. Those who could get away from business and a number of ladies gathered in the stand, and were duly impressed.

First, H.E. the Governor (Sir Cecil Clementi) arrived, with Lady Clementi, and was accorded a Royal salute.

H.E. the General Officer Commanding, Major-General C.C. Lund, then came on the ground, accompanied by his A.D.C., and inspected the battallion.

The old drums were trooped off, passing through the whole of the lines (drawn up on three sides of a square).

The Rev. G. F. Stopford, C.F., pronounced the blessing on the silver drums, which were placed on a stand in front of the saluting base.

Lt.-Col. F. S. Montague Bates, who commands the battalion; then addressed his men.
