London, October 12.

While the miners of the respective districts are considering the proposals of the Miners' Federation Conference to call out the safety men from the pits, the latter themselves are taking action.

A deputation of the Federation, Colliery Deputies and Firemen's Associations representing 30,000 safety men were received at the Ministry of Labour, and discussed the situation with Sir Arthur Maitland to whom they submitted their position. The deputies were refused unemployment benefit during the stoppage.

Simultaneously the National Executive of this Federation met and urged that the time had come to reach a settlement. It is understood that the Federation adheres to its policy of advising the safety men to remain at work.

## SLAVERY IN NEPAL

## COMPLETE ABOLITION

Allahabad.—The Pioneer's Nepal correspondent states that an official statement issued at Katmandu shows that the Maharaja's anti-slavery campaign has triumphantly concluded with the manumission of 57,889 slaves, and that slavery no longer exists in Nepal. The emancipation started early last year. It was carefully planned and proceeded smoothly, the owners co-operating in a, notable manner. Out of a total of 15,719 owners the large majority favoured immediate release, and under 500 of them opposed abolition. Over 1,200 offered liberation without compensation, and actually freed 4,651 slaves without any cost to the Government.

The Maharaja's humanitarian policy has cost the Nepalese Government over 86 jakhs of rupees (about £240,000), the rate of compensation averaging seventy rupees per slave. The scheduled rates ranged from fifteen rupees ten annas for a male baby under 8 to a maximum of 100 rupees for an able-bodied woman aged 18 to 40, the female price averaging over 80 per cent more than the male.

With the object of providing a livelihood for the manumitted people cultivable lands have been thrown open and reclamation and clearance work has been started in Terai. Arrangements are in hand for the issue of sufficient advances to enable the newly-freed people to earn their living.
