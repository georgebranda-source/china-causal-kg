## Northumberland Ballot

The ballot of the Northumberland miners resulted in 16,288 for and 6,251 against the acceptance of the settlement terms. The men have been instructed to resume work immediately.

The Scottish collieries have resumed operations on agreements providing from seven and a half to eight hour shifts at pre-strike wages, but many pits are able at present to employ only a section of the men until they can be got into working order.

The North Staffordshire miners federation have authorised their members to resume work immediately, pending negotiations for an agreement.

## STREET SCRAP

## A DISPUTE OVER DRY OYSTERS

A dispute over dry oysters led to three shopkeepers being charged before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning with fighting in the street.

"You ought to know better than to fight in the street," said the Magistrate in fining the defendants $2 each. His Worship also ordered the defendants to sign personal hands to keep the peace for six months.

## INDIAN ELECTIONS

## No Absolute Majority Swarajists

HOPES FOR THE REFORMS.

## Further Continual Deadlocks Impossible

Delhi, Noy. 28.

The general election returns confirm the enriger forecast, inasmuch as the Swarajists, while forming the largest single group in the all-India Assembly and the Provincial Councils of Madras, Bengal and the Central Provinces, can now here command an absolute majority.

Thus, notwithstanding the fact that their refusal to accept office will mean the formation of somewhat precarious coalitions, there is every hope that the reforms will now work all over India, even in the Central Provinces and Bengal, which on the whole recorded a setback for the Swarajists because they will be unable, any longer to produce continual deadlocks, while as regards Bombay, the United Provinces and Punjab, the Swarajists' proportion of seats will be negligible.

A striking feature of the elections is the change of outlook regarding the election of the Speaker since the Speakers of the All-India Assembly and the Provincial Councils of Bombay, the Punjab and the United Provinces have been returned unopposed. The Swarajists challenged the Speakers in the Madras and Bengal Councils and both lost their seats.—Reuter.

## NOT DEAD YET

## LLOYD GEORGE AND LIBERAL PROSPECTS

Rugby, Nov. 28.

Mr. Lloyd George is optimist in regarding liberal prospects, in spite of differences in the Party which have been further emphasised by the refusal of the Asquithian section to accept the conditions on which Lloyd George's Fund should become available for Party purposes.

He said yesterday that the Liberals would dominate the next Parliament and Labour could not come into office without Liberal help. That would not be forthcoming without definite guarantees from Labour as to the policy to be followed. British Wireless Service.

### LATE M. KRASSIN

Without a service and with little ceremony, the remains of M. Krassin were removed from Chesham House and cremated at Golders Green. The ashes are being taken to Moscow where official rites will be given.

A crowd of 5,000 walked in the procession from Golders Green Station to the crematorium, mostly wearing red and black armlets.

The ashes of Krassin are expected in Moscow on December 1, and will be immured in the Kremlin wall, in the Red Square, opposite the grave of Frunze, former chief of the army.—Reuter.
