## AGROUND

MISHAP TO DANISH SHIP

"DANMARK,"

OFF CLOCKTOWER.

Stein Caught And Current Does

Ferry passengers crossing the harbour after nina o'clock this morning were amazed to find an ocean-going vessel apparently at rest with bows pointing in the direction of the shore and only a short distance from the Clock tower. Ferries were able to pass without difficulty.

The ship hardly had the appearance of being aground and it was only by the sight of the anchors still abroad that it was to be gathered that such in fact was the case.

The ship is the s.s. “Danmark” of the East Asiatic Co., Ltd., of which John Manners and Co. are the local agents. It was entering the harbour at nine o'clock with, of course, a pilot aboard when the stern caught on the sandbank which stretches some distance from the shore. The set of the current turned the ship until she came to rest in the position now occupied.

## Sandbank Silted Up

The sandbank in question was dredged some time ago to a depth of over thirty feet but is continually silting up, this being plainly apparent from the fact that the mean draft of the “Danmark” is 28 feet.11 inches.

No damage was sustained by the vessel which came to rest gently and is only on soft mud. The grounding took place two hours or so before lowwater and at the time of writing it was thought that she would be floating shortly after one o'clock.

“The ‘Danmark’ is a comparatively new vessel this being her second, run from Europe to the Far East and the second time of entry into Hongkong harbour. Her gross tonnage is 8,391 and nett. 5,342.41. She has 760 tons of cargo for Hongkong.

Although it is not likely that damage has been done in view of the fact that the ship rests only on the surf mud surface of the bank, it is interesting to note that cables are on this bank and that communication has been interrupted in the past as a result of similar mishaps.

## Rains Act Adversely?

The report of the Danmark to the Harbour Office gives the following additional particulars:—“grounded in 25 feet of water at 9 a.m.” Crew of 39 Europeans, 3 passengers.

A few months ago the m.v. Tonkin, belonging to the same line, was aground on the same spit of sand. Other vessels have also been in that position. It is thought that the recent heavy rains may have had an adverse effect:

"In addition to the 760 tons of cargo for Hongkong there are 11,000 tons of general cargo for further ports. From this it will be seen that 'the Danmark' was fairly heavily loaded.

While waiting for the ship to float off the agents are discharging some of the cargo (for this port). The object of this is to save time rather than because lightening of the vessel is absolutely necessary to free her from her position.

The agents consider that there will be no damage nor material delay in the departure for Shanghai. The Denmark was to have gone to number 5 Wharf at Kowloon.

SPRAYING WATER ON THE

GARAGE MAN FINED.

In prosecuting an employee of a motor garage at Caine Road for unlawfully wasting water, Mr. J. T. Ewing, overseer of the Waterworks Department, informed the Magistrate this morning that the man had connected a hose to the service pipe at the garage and was straying the water on the road.

This, he pointed out, was done at a time when every effort was being made to conserve the water supply especially on the higher levels.

Asked by Major Willson, why he should do such a thing, defendant explained that he wished to make the roadside cool, as the sun had been 'too hot on Saturday after noon.

A fine of $20 was imposed.

COMMANDING GENERAL SAYS "MUST STOP."

A VAGUE REPORT.

Disobedience to National Government

A castle received by the "Wah Tsz Yat Po" makes the rather startling announcement that Canton's present military chief intends to deall drastically with strikers on the ground that strikes contravene the rules of the Revolutionary Government.

On General Chang Kai-shek leaving for the Northern front, General Li Chai-sum was appointed commander of home defence. The Brief statement as cabled does not say whether General Li refers to strikers strictly of Cantanban, or the so-called "Hongkong strikers." The message is clear to the extent that military force will be used in putting down strikes.

Post and Press Strike.

Advice from Canton is that the postal strike continues and that all (native, subordinate?) employees were to have come out during the week-end. It being the end of the month.

The Commissioner is quoted as having stated, in reply to a request from the employees' union, that the "demand for an increase of pay must go to the chief commissioner in Peking.

It is interesting to note that mails which were expected here from Canton on Saturday and yesterday have not come to hand, ostensibly through delay in the post.

The end of the newspaper strike has not been reported. On the other hand, it is believed that proposals have been advanced for a settlement and these...it is stated...

## Hero Welcome

Detailed accounts have reached Canton about the arrival of General Chang Kai-shek in Shikwan and Lokcheong, on his way to the battle front.

Four Russian advisers, a big staff and several aeroplanes are with the commander-in-chief. The people of the various towns accorded General Chang a very cordial reception.

One report is that the mere fact of his personal arrival has caused order to be restored and outlaws to disappear from haunts where they exacted toll from the populace.

The Hunan Provincial Assembly is reported to have denounced General Tang. One accusation against him is that he has levied from the wealthy classes, 50 percent, of property and 20 per cent. on rental of fields. Non-payment will be severely punished, states the report.

General Tang Sang-chee, the pro-Canton commander in Hunan, has announced that another advance will be made immediately, from Changsha, into Wu Pei-fu's territory. Ping-kiang is stated to be his first objective.

The Hunan Government is said to have been remodelled on the style of the Kwangtung provincial government, with General Tang as chairman of the military department.
