## RUSSIAN HOLIDAY

## CIGLEBRATED BY SWATOWRADICALS

Swatow, Nov. 8.—The Ninth Annual Versary of the Russian Revoluion was celebrated here yesterday. The celebration included a public open air meeting with a fairly large attendance, the majority being of the Labour classes. The students were in the minority. The programme was made up largely of Communistic propaganda with the most decilled "Red" sentiment that has yet been expressed in Swatow. One young Russian spoke and about twenty Russians were on the platform and sang the Red National song. They then marched through the streets yelling the usual radical yells.

However, the day passed off without any violence and business seems to be progressing. The strike trouble with the employees of the medicine shops has been settled and the men are back at their work. The British boycott is off and the British boats are coming and going and are getting and unloading passengers and some cargo. The pickets are no longer working openly at least and one is not molested in the least in handling incoming or out-going cargo.

The schools and other organisations are celebrating Dr. Sun's "birthday this week and it promises to be a lively week for most of the people.

From the best report we can get the Southern forces have pushed right on into Fukien and will not stop until they have taken the province. In the meantime things seem to be settled here in Swatow and there seems to be no uneasiness whatever that there will be a change within the near future.—“China Press.”

## NEW AIR SERVICE

### Philadelphia, Nov. 20

The establishment of aerial transportation between this city and Norfolk, Va., was announced to-night by the Philadelphia Rapid Transit Company. Announcement also was made that the company would increase its schedule, between Philadelphia and Washington from two to three trips a day and that its air express service also would be extended to Norfolk.

Rates for passengers are $15 from Philadelphia to Washington or $25 for the round trip; Washington to Norfolk, one day, $20; round trip $35; The flying time from here to Norfolk will be three and one-half hours.

## SALT FIGHT

## HISTORIC EXEMPTION DE: FENDED BY VILLAGERS

Huangyen, Chekiang, November 12.—An ugly incident occurred in this district a few weeks ago. While salt is an object of taxation in most parts of China the districts of Huangyen and Taiping on the Chekiang coast are, and have been exempt from this tax from time immemorial. "Attempts have been made by the authorities from time to time to collect this tax, but in every ease it has been the signal for a miniature rebellion of the peasants and salt workers. The last attempt was made unsuccessfully about 60 years ago."

Just a few weeks ago, another effort was made this time under the guise of measuring some land just recently reclaimed from the sea, but still too salty to be of much value. The peasants in this area are still feeling pretty sore because the supineness of the gentry (not to call it an uglier word) in neglecting to have a floodgate built (for which funds were in hand) has caused the destruction of a lot of crop this year. And now come along the officials, trying to impose this new burden of salt taxation. Perfectly legal, but most important. A clash occurred between the peasants armed with hoes; spades, etc., and the soldiers with their rifles. The honours of the day rested with the peasants, six soldiers dead and four peasants. The city magistrate went out to investigate but, it is reported, even his life was threatened! The whole countryside threatened to rise and all the pirates flocked in to the cause of the peasants, who removed live stock and provisions out of the reach of the military. Never! never!! would this district submit to a salt tax. The officials, not liking the look of things, called a palaver. The peasants won; the tax is not to be imposed. The gentry have had to pay an indemnity to relatives of deceased; it is said $600 for a soldier and $400 for a peasant. Surely China after all is a democratic country.—"N.-C. Daily News."

STUDENTS' STATEMENT TO CHILD OF POLICE.



The Shanghai Chinese Student's Union on November 14 sent the following letter to the Chief of the Woosung and Shanghai Constabu-lary:

This Union is a public body working for the welfare of students. The activities have been undertaken for the benefit of students only, and it has never committed any lawless acts. Since the May 30 incident this Union has been guiding the students and advising them not to resort to extreme measures. This can be proven and must be within your knowledge as well. Now the general situation is very critical. People in various walks of life have expressed their views, and we have also to voice our opinion in order to show that students are keeping in close touch with the general situation of the country. Unexpectedly, on November 9, a large batch of police suddenly called out the office of our Union, sealed it up and arrested two officers of the Union who are still in prison. The people know that you are doing your utmost to maintain peace and order in this locality, but it is not proper for you to punish those innocent of any crime. Since this unfortunate incident the students of the various local educational institutions have become restless and their minds are greatly disturbed. Despite the advice of this Union, the attitude of these students is in no way reassuring. Thus your measures to maintain peace and order have had just the opposite results. We, therefore, most earnestly request you to order the removal of the seal from the office, the release of our officers and the return of the documents seized so as to show your broad-mindedness. It would be unwise of you to reject our request, because it would lead to constant disquietude. We hope you will give this matter your careful consideration.

NO MORE CABINET MEETINGS?

___

Peking, November 18. — Dr. Wellington Koo's Cabinet has resolved to resign en bloc on the ground that it is very difficult to control the political situation. The resignation of the Cabinet is expected to be announced after the Cabinet meeting which is to be held either on the 20th or on the 23rd.

Resignation was contemplated before the Tientsin Conference was opened, and the Ministers have finally agreed to day.

It is generally observed that after the wholesale resignations, no Cabinet meetings would be held, that it would therefore, be impossible for China to open negotiations as to the revision of the Sino-Japanese Commercial Treaty; and consequently negotiations would be deadlocked pending the stabilisation of the situation.—“Toho.”
