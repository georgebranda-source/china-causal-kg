## CANTON MISSION WORK

NESTERDAY'S INTERESTING CONFERENCE DEVISION.

A 'WITH DRAWAL-TEMPORALITY'

The decision come to yesterday at a Conference held in Hongkong to consider the immediate future activities of the U.M.S. in Canton and throughout Kwangtung, marks an epoch in the history of missionary work in China.

Reuter has already cabled with regard to conferences held throughout China recently the opinion that had been expressed by missionary bodies that schools and missions should be handed over to the Chinese at any rate temporarily, because, in the first place, parents of pupils would hesitate to expose their children to possible insult at mission schools, and, in the second, that it would give an opportunity to test whether the time was opportune for Chinese effort alone to carry on with the work.

The Chinese masters had in the great majority of cases noted splendidly at the various missions and schools, said the "China Mail" informant, and the Mission had every confidence in them. A few, left but merely as a result of intimidation and not out of sympathy with the forces of disorder.

Missionaries whose sphere of activities have been at Foochow and Pakhoi are awaiting consular instruction before returning

The schools at Canton affected by the decision come to yesterday are Holy Trinity, St. Andrew's Hall and St. Kildn's Girls' School.

The decision at the Conference held here yesterday was that the schools in Canton should re-open as under the aegis of the C.M.S. but with no foreign teachers, and as it is the first actual decision which has been reported to the "China Mail," its results as an experiment will be awaited with interest. An important member of the C.M.S. body at present in Hongkong who conversed with a "China Mail" representative this morning said that it should be made clear that the foreign teachers and missionaries were shirking nothing. In leaving and abstaining from visiting the scene of their work they were acting merely in accordance with consular instruction and they were prepared to return at any moment if it were possible and if it were thought that such action would serve a good purpose.

## ARMED ROBBERS

STORY OF BEING ENTICED BY A WOMAN.

COMMITTED TO SESSIONS:

Three Chinese were charged with attempted armed robbery before the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday, one of them having an additional charge against him of unlawful possession of a dagger.

The story of the prosecution was that the accused went to the doorway of a house where a woman was seated and ordered her to remain silent. This was overheard by the woman's husband who was in the cockloft, and he blew a police whistle. One of the men was secured after a chase, and on information given by him two others were arrested later.

Two of the accused explained their presence in the house by saying that they were enticed to the house by the daughter of the complainant, which statement, however, they failed to prove.

The accused were committed for trial at the Sessions.

## SAFETY RULES

WHEN IT IS FATAL TO IGNORE THEM.

DRAIN COOLIE'S DEATH.

Non-observance of the regulation was alleged at the enquiry yesterday to have led to the death of a coolie employed on work connected with the drainage department. If the regulations were adhered to, accidents would be impossible, said Mr. D. S. Edwards, of that department, who gave evidence at the enquiry.

Under the regulations in question, which were all explained to the coolies, said Mr. Edwards, they were told that it was dangerous to descend a manhole unless more than one of the holes were opened. Sewer gas would tend to accumulate and rise and when the manholes were not opened daily the gas would escape only when they were opened.

The coolie foreman who was present at the time that the deceased went down the manhole gave evidence at the enquiry. He said that before the deceased went down, another hole in Canton Road was opened and adhered to this statement, although the Coroner asked him how he could account for the fact that the drain in question in Canton Road had been examined by expert witnesses who found dirt still round the edges and were prepared to swear it had not been opened.

The regulations also emphasised the imperative necessity of a lamp being lowered before entry and the Coroner put it to the foreman that there had been one there on that day. Witness affirmed that there had been and that he had been there and saw it lowered the first time. He did not know what had happened when the deceased went down the second time. Witness had gone to shut the hole opened in Canton Road, he said, when he heard a shout that the deceased had become unconscious. On going down to pull the deceased from the hole, he himself had slipped and fallen, said the witness. They were both pulled to the surface by another foreman who arrived on the scene. The lamp which witness affirmed had been used was not to be found after the accident. As he was away at the time he could not swear that the deceased was lowered into the hole the second time with the rope around him as provided in the regulations.

Medical evidence to the effect that the death of the coolie was due to asphyxia, and that when admitted to Hospital, he had all the signs of sewer gas poisoning was given and the enquiry was adjourned until Friday.
