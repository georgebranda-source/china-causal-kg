## TRIBUTES TO SIR PAUL

COUNCIL REFERENCES

## ～

Stirring tributes were paid, at this afternoon's meeting of the Legislative Council, to Sir Paul Chater and the Hon. Mr. P. H. Holvoak.

H.E. the Governor (Sir Cecil Clementi) said: "Gentlemen,—Last night Sir Paul Chater passed away at his home in this island and it is difficult for me to speak without emotion of the grievous loss the Colony has suffered through his death."

His Excellency referred to the time when he first landed in the Colony as a young cadet. "He found a very successful scheme which was revised by Sir Paul to reclaim land from the sea. At that time Sir Paul was a member of both the Executive and Legislative Councils of the Colony. In fact, it was forty years ago when he first sat.

His earliest recollections, said the Governor, as Clerk of Councils, were associated with memories of Sir Paul.

"His sound advice, his wonderful foresight, and his unbounded confidence in the future," said Sir Cecil, "had a marked influence on the decisions of both Councils, and on the policy of the Hongkong Government."

Yesterday, His Excellency stood at the bedside of Sir Paul and the latter's last words to him concerned the good of Hongkong, even after his death. Sir Paul be-queathed to the Colony:—

His house (Marble Hall),

His marvellous collection

porcelain.

His historical pictures of Hong-

kong in its infancy, and, said His

Excellency, development schemes

of great magnitude—some com-

pleted and some in course of com-

pletion—which even changed the

appearance of the whole land.

These schemes were for the content and happiness of the public. "So long as Hongkong and Kowloon endure will his work remain as an imperishable memorial."

The Hon. Mr. Holyoak.

"Yesterday also," continued the Governor, "brought us the sad news of Mr. Holyoak's death in England. Like Sir Paul Chatter, he was a member of the Executive and Legislative Councils."

The loss of two most valued Councillors was a heavy blow to the Colony. Both were in heavy harness.

Although Mr. Holyoak was absent on leave, he was charged with business of high importance which he hoped to transact on behalf of this Colony, while in England.

"Mr. Holyonk, at the time of his departure, was the chairman of the Chamber of Commerce. Throughout the critical period of the boycott, he held that position with distinction."

The Colony, especially the mercantile community, owes Mr. Holyoak a deep debt of gratitude, said His Excellency, for his services—during that time of strain and stress—and we all mourn his death—not only as a personal and public loss—but also as a sacrifice of unremitting labour on behalf of Hongkong.

His Excellency then moved that a motion be recorded in the minutes of the Council, of the distinguished services of these two members, and that the Clerk of Councils be instructed to convey to the bereaved families, their dearest挚alty.

## Colleague's Tribute

Sir Henry Pollock, the senior unofficial member of the Council, was visibly moved when he rose to second. At several times in his address, Sir Henry's voice was obviously affected by the grief he felt. Just before his conclusion, Sir Henry spoke with the utmost difficulty, but concluded without wavering.

Sir Henry seconded the motion, on behalf of the unofficial members of both Councils; adding that it was difficult for him to express what he felt.

Within the brief space of twenty-four hours, two unofficial colleagues had passed away, colleagues who had taken part in matters affecting the legislation and public work of this Colony, said Sir Henry.

Sir Henry referred to Sir Faصل as the "master builder" possessed of a "master mind." No matter how small, he attended to every detail of any project he engaged in.

Speaking of the time when the Hongkong, Kowloon Wharf and Godown Co., Ltd. was formed, Sir Henry said that Sir Paul left nothing to chance. He went out day after day, in a sampan, with a sounding lead to satisfy himself by personal survey, whether the water at Kowloon was sufficiently deep.

Sir Henry mentioned this incident as illustrating the fact that his success was not due to mere luck, but to "that species of genius which has been described as an infinite capacity for taking pains."

Sir Paul Chatter had to his credit the main share in two large reclamation schemes. The Prayer reclamation of 1889-1897 gave a large area of flat building land from West Point to the Naval Yard. The eastern reclamation scheme, from the east end of the Naval Yard to East Point, would be completed in the next few years, and would give valuable building land.

In connection with building schemes, SIF Henry mentioned that Sir Paul was the moving spirit in the Hongkong Land Investment Co. and three other concerns—the Kowloon Land and Building Co., the West Point Building Co. and the Land Reclamation Co. Sir Paul also performed services on the boards of various public companies, giving shareholders the benefit of his ripe experience. He was a pioneer of building development in Kowloon.

Of Sir Paul's private life, Sir Henry said that he was possessed of an encyclopaedic memory which made his talk on every subject most interesting. By his death the Colony has lost a many-sided man of rare ability. It is hard to lose such a man, especially at the present time.

Not less conspicuous than his public services, were Sir Paul's generous gifts to the University, his munificent donations to churches in the Colony and other public institutions, in addition to his many acts of private benevolence.

"Brave Spirit Butching."

Of Mr. Holyoak, Sir Henry said in was difficult indeed to express his feelings. It was over ten years ago when he proposed Mr. Holyoak as a successor to the late Mr. Hewett, on the Council. He predicted that Mr. Holyoak would be a worthy successor and this had been amply fulfilled.

On many public occasions since, and in this Council, he had been proud to be Mr. Holyoak's lieutenant, said Sir Henry, and to second Mr. Holyoak's strenuous efforts.

Expressing the opinion that Mr. Holyoak would be hard to replace, Sir Henry said that his was a brave spirit battling gallantly against odds. " He was a man of wide and noble vision.

Sir Henry paid a tribute to Mr. Holyoak's grasp of trade and shipping, even in abnormal and unprecedented conditions. In him the Colony has lost a great and patriotic citizen, a loyal and a true friend.

The Hon. Sir. Shou-son Chow (senior, Chinese member of the Council) said that in the name of the Chinese community, his colleague and he expressed deep sorrow and associated themselves with the words of His Excellency and Sir Henry Pollock.
