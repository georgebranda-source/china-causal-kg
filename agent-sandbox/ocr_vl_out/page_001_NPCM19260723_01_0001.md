Danger is practically all over now from the typhoon which passed Hongkong yesterday — the second of the current season.

Early this morning it was reported to be filling up after moving it back.

At first it was thought that Macao would suffer but the Portuguese Colony has "nothing to report." As a matter of fact, the barometer there was higher than in Hongkong.

The s.s. "Pawnice" has been sulved.

Normal shipping movements were restored this morning. So far no loss in local small craft has been notified.

6 a.m.—The typhoon is filling up to the North of Pakhoi (on the southern Kwangtung coast). It went inland between Macao and the French port of Kwang-chewan.

The lowest, at sea-level, on this side of the harbour was 29.18.

The highest wind velocity was 85 miles an hour, at 8.54 a.m. yesterday. In the 1923 disaster, the windgauge registered much over the 100 miles and hour mark. Force 10 (about 70 miles) an hour was returned from Gap Rock.

## OUTRAGE SEQUEL

COMPENSATION FOR VICTIMS' FAMILIES.

HUNG HỒM INCIDENT.

The Government propose compensating the family of the Chinese_workman killed as a result of a bomb being thrown at Hunghom on March 10 of this year in the sum of $2,000 and to make appropriate payment to the eight injured.

This is in accordance with the notification of the Government in June of last year in which assurance was given that such compensation would be made in cases of death or injury arising directly out of the boycott. The outrage is held to be a direct outcome and in the votes to come before the Finance Committee at this afternoon's meeting provision is made for the expenditure of $3,290 on compensation to the nine families.

## Police Launch Stranding

The *stranding of the No. 4 Police launch on July 2 involved considerable damage to the vessel. The votes before the Committee to-day include the expenditure of $1,700 on repairs and stores for the launch and $1,100 on salvage costs, including the hire of the "Henry Keswick" and grant of honorari in respect of special services rendered in connection with the salvage.

## Rescue Tug Vote

The Government having agreed to the recommendations of a Committee appointed by the Chamber of Commerce to advise on the subject of the rescue tug "Kau Sing" that certain alterations should be made, to-day's votes provide for the expenditure of $2,715 on closing in the upper-bridge, altering the position of the search light, the supply of a double ended whaler, the fitting of storm shutters to the lower wheelhouse, the stuffing of the mattresses with Kapok and the supply of 50 life-belts and 10 lifebuoys.

The cost of the personnel of the officers and crew of the vessel is $3,180 more than anticipated in respect of the period. July 1 to December 31, this being due to the replacing of the proposed Chinese master by a European master and to the additional appointment of a Chinese quartermaster and six Chinese sailors. A vote for this amount is also included in the supplementary estimates.

## Kowloon-Canton Railway

The original '1926 estimates in respect of replacing damaged and worn rails to the Kowloon-Canton railway was $300 as it was not expected that the Fanling branch line would be running and it was hoped to continue the policy of strict economy in regard to replacing of rails. The running of the Fanling branch line and the results of an examination revealing the necessity for immediate replacement of certain portions of the track has necessitated an additional vote of $2,850.

## Volunteer Firing Courses

The cost of carriage to and from Stonecutters of Volunteer units firing courses on the range there has proved greater than anticipated and an extra vote for $500 is asked for.

For work carried out in England on the motor car used by the Colony's Commissioner to the British Empire Exhibition before exportation for use locally a vote for $852 is before the Committee.

Expenditure on local secret service work has been greater than was anticipated owing to political troubles and an additional sum of $8,000 is asked for the year.

Owing to the outbreak of Rabies additional Coolie Hire for dog catching has raised the monthly expenditure to $720 and $745 in April and May respectively, and at this average the sum of $4,500 now asked for is required to supplement this vote.

## NO LIGHTS AGAIN

SHIP CAPTAIN ROUND OVER.

ANOTHER PROSECUTION.

Another prosecution of local sea-going vessels for failing to exhibit regulation lights was heard at the Marine Court this morning, when Captain A. W. Hall (master of the "Pheumpenh") was bound over by Lt.-Com. G. F. Hole, R.N. (Marine Magistrate) in a personal security of $5 to come up for judgment when called upon to do so.

The offence occurred at 11:30 p.m. on June 29, when the vessel was lying at No. 7 buoy.

On Captain Hall entering a plea of "not guilty." Lee-Sgt. Goldsmith gave evidence as to boarding the ship; meeting Mr. Goldberg (officer on watch) who subsequently upbraided the native quartermaster, and to the absence of a light on the bow.

Captain Hall admitted that the light was not burning and His Worship recorded a finding of "guilty."

## Police Court Staff

In addition to supplementary estimates for 1926, there are before the Finance Committee today certain supplementary estimates for 1925, these including a sum of $2,050 for salary of extra staff and promoted officers at the Magistracy. The staff at the Central Magistracy was reduced when a separate Magistracy was established in Kowloon. Experience, however, showed that too great a reduction had been made and that the appointment of an Indian Interpreter and a Court Usher in addition to the staff provided for in the 1925 Estimates was necessary. Two clerks received promotion to higher classes during the year and received salaries in excess of the amounts provided for them in the Estimates.

$170 is also asked for in connection with Police Expenditure (cost of 1,000 sets of vehicle plates).

On account of the moulders going out on strike, the cost of production of these number plates was increased and the sum asked for is to cover the difference between estimated and actual cost (from $2 to $3).
