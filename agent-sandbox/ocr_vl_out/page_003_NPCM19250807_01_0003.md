### PARSEE'S MURDER. THREE MEN TO DIE

Bombay

The notorious Malabar Hill murder case concluded at the Sessions Court, when, of the nine men accused of complicity in the murder on January 12 of Mr. A. Q. Bawla, the wealthy Parsee merchant, of Bombay, seven were found guilty and two acquitted.

The charges against the whole of the accused included assaults on Mumtaz Begum, a twenty-two-year-old dancing girl, and Lieut. Saegert, a British officer who was one of a party which gallantly went to the rescue when Dawla and Mumtaz Begum were attacked by the accused. Lieut. Saegert was wounded by a knife thrust.

Sentence of death was passed upon three of the accused, namely, Shafi Ahmed, who was a rialdar (native officer) of the Indore State-Mounted Police; Shami Rao Dighe, a captain of the Indore Air Force; and Nate Pande, a high official of the Maharajah of Indore's household. Four others, including Anand Rao Phanee, Adjutant-General of the Indore Army, were sentenced to transportation for life.

## NATIVE DANCES

BASIS OF A NEW HEALTH SYSTEM.

Native dances as done by native men and women in North and South America, Australia, Africa, and other parts of the world form the basis of a new health system which a New Zealander, Mr. F. A. Hornibrook, and his wife are urging the civilised world to adopt.

Forty-six doctors and a number of eminent scientists attended as demonstration in London, when, after a paper read by Mrs. Hornibrook on the scientific side of the theory, a Maori from New Zealand gave examples of the original dances, and Mr. Hornibrook demonstrated exercises based on them and adapted for civilised men and women with little time to spare.

The strengthening of the abdominal wall is the end to which the system is adapted, and it is claimed to have exceptional results in the treatment of intestinal troubles.

"These native dances are really a system of physical education which, judged by its material results or judged by its hygienic effects, is far in advance of anything which the Europeans now have."
