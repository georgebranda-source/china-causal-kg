## STUDENT STRIKERS

CHANGE IN OFFICIAL

ATTITUDE?

"OFFENCES. EXPIATED."

It is significant that coincident with the moves that are being made in the direction of closer cooperation between Hongkong and Canton certain proposals have been mooted for a modification of the edict barring from admission to Government and Government aided schools students who were absent from study on certain dates.

The period of this suspension, or expulsion as it was then generally regarded, was not mentioned when the attitude of Sir R. E. Stubbs was outlined by the Chairman of the Education board and it is understood that the penalty paid during the period that has elapsed since their having been de-arred from entrance has been advanced to the Government as sufficient for the students to have expiated their offence. The end of the school year has been suggested as the date when such stringent measures might no longer apply.

The Government attitude is expected to be announced shortly.

It may be recalled that at the Education meeting at which Sir R. E. Stubbs' attitude was endorsed, nearly all the members who spoke were in accord with that attitude but a few thought it was too severe. Father Valtorta thought that temporary suspension and postponement of examinations would meet the case. Mr. J. M. Alves suggested the formation of a "humiliation" class of strikers in each school would be sufficient punishment and Dr. Wan spoke at length or "the need for taking a more liberal and considerate view."

Dr. Pearce thought that the balance had been struck between justice and generous consideration. Now, if ever, was the time for emphasising the importance of discipline. The matter should be left to the heads of schools. No one else could do anything in the matter.

The Chairman's final remark was that he thought that the matter could be safely left with the headmasters who, he was sure, would be sympathetic in their consideration.

The “China Mail” also understands that the Government has under consideration the proposed raising of fees which was not the outcome of the strike but revision which was considered necessary in any event. Representations have been made that the present is hardly opportune for an extra financial burden to be imposed on parents and it is likely that the Government may postpone or mitigate the increases.

## WORLD'S COTTON

## BRITAIN TO SHARE PROSPERITY

The great reason for it existing to-day than for several years, was the bountiful cotton crops of the world, and the world was assured of the most adequate supply.

An optimistic view of the future of the cotton industry was taken by Mr. Richard Brooks, in his presidential address at the annual meeting of the Manchester Cotton Association.

Britain had 57,000,000 of the world's spindleage of 160,000,000. America's spindleage was 38,000,000 largely engaged in supplying domestic requirements, so it would be strange if we failed to receive our due share of any improvement in world buying.

The organised curtailment of the output had undoubtedly saved the industry from a serious financial crisis. Even now, despite the most optimistic outlook, curtailment was still essential to avert excessive over-production and curious prices.

The question of the "futures" market for Manchester was seriously engaging attention.

## CUTTING TREES

## OFFENDERS HEAVILY FINED:

A SHAUKIWAN RAID.

Heavy penalties were imposed by Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning on a number of illicit woodcutters, the defendants being all women of the coolie class.

A large gang were engaged in the wholesale destruction of a plantation at Shaukijwan West yesterday afternoon. Guards from the Forestry Department, assisted by officers from Divisional Inspector Blackman's Station, pounced on the culprits and arrested some, whilst others made their escape. In a subsequent raid on huts in the vicinity, large quantities of freshly cut wood were seized, the total making up a full lorry load.

Five of the defendants were fined $50 each, or in the alternative four weeks' hard labour whilst others were fined $40 and $20, or imprisonment in default.
