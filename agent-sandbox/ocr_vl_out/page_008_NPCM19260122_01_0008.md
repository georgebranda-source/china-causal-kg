# LOCAL AND GENERAL

The Shu Tau Kok branch of the Kowloon railway is temporarily closed.

The s.s. "Tilawa," which left Singapore on the 18th instant, is due here tomorrow.

Whilst the tourist ship "Belgenlund" was at Yokohama, the Y.M.C.A. Seamen's Club arranged special tours for the crew.

Trips to Soochow and Hang-chow from Shanghai, for the "Belgenland's" tourists, had to be cancelled because of poor support.

Easton Lodge, Dunmow, Essex, which has been used as a Labour "Chequers" for a year of two, may become the first Labour University," It has been offered by the Dowager-Countess of Warwick, herself a Socialist, to the General Council of the Trades Union Congress, who have the proposal under review.

The Report of the Hongkong Horticultural Society for the year 1025, being the twentieth annual report, mentions "that the ordinary membership of the Society fell off considerably during the year and now stands at only 175, but it is hoped that interest will be revived during the coming season. The annual meeting is announced for Thursday next.

The most teamful meeting ever held by Manila Roturs undoubtedly was that staged at the Manila Hotel when following Luncheon, Major Gibson, chief of the chemical warfare division of the Philippines department, gave a practical demonstration of the efficacy of tear gas, to the vast discomfort of several score Rotarians who had foregathered on the lawn near the hotel pavilion. Major Gibson addressed the members of the club on his branch of the service and bespoke its support. The major pointed out that the use of gas in war was really more humane than the use of explosives, in that the victims of gas in almost all instances is only temporarily incapacitated and suffers no permanent ill effects.

One British case of diphtheria was reported in the Colony on Wednesday.

The steamship "Angiolina" has arrived at King George Dock, Hull, with a cargo of 10,165 tons of cotton seed from Alexandria, the largest cargo ever imported into Hull docks.

Visitors to the Zoo to the end of November this year numbered 1,796,470. Gate money was £57,871, a decrease of £10,537 compared with the corresponding period last year.

The Shanghai Municipal "Gazette" gives the figures of the foreign population of the Settlement taken at the recent census. The outstanding feature of the census is an increase in this population of over 6,000 as compared with 1920, while turning back to previous years the striking growth of the Settlement is well illustrated. In 1900 the foreign population was under 7,000, and it was not until the 1920 census that it went over the 20,000 mark. Up to 1910 the British led the foreign community in numbers, but from that time onwards they were surpassed by the Japanese. The ever greater number of Japanese there is a striking point about the census figures, for while they were a community of 736 only in 1900, they now head the totals with 13,804. Their increase during the last five years is practically 40 per cent.

A big fire in the heart of Bangkok destroyed about fifty houses, most of which were small ones. Several fire brigades combined to suppress the conflagration, which was witnessed by over 10,000 people among whom was the King of Siam.

Glowing tribute to the progress made by the Philippines Islands since American occupation was paid by Bishop Charles Bayard Mitchell, bishop of the Methodist Episcopal church of the Philippine Islands, in a recent sermon at the first Methodist Church in Los Angeles.

Everybody who knows him, either in Siam or Malaya, will rejoice at the high honour recently bestowed upon Mr. W. A. Graham by the King of Siam, says the "Straits Echo", Mr. Graham is not only an administrator of outstanding ability and one of the men who has helped to create the new Siam during a career of nearly thirty years in that country and its dependencies, but an author of distinction, a thorough sportsman and a most genial companion whose interests and knowledge cover an unusually wide range of subjects.

# SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

The Hongkong Police annual ball takes place this evening at the City Hall.

Prior to their departure on transfer to Kunla Lumpur, Mr. and Mrs. Gordon Brown. District Engineer, Gemas, and Miss Brown, were entertained to a farewell tea by the sub-ordinate staff of the Engineers' Department, Gemas, who presented Mr. Brown with an illuminated address as a mark of the appreciation and esteem in which they have held him during the time he has been with them. Mr. and Mrs. Gordon Brown and Miss Gordon Brown were all garlanded, and a most enjoyable evening was spent.

During an interval at the Lower Deck dance' on H.M.S. "Hawkins" on Wednesday night, Capt. Lake made the presentation of a silver rose bowl to Petty Officer Tilbe, as a token of appreciation from the ship's company of the manner in which he had carried out the duties of Secretary to the Entertainment Committee during the "Hawkins" two and a half years' commission.

The Shanghai Rotary Club entertained visiting Rotarians from Belgium who are passengers in the tourist ship "Belgenland".

The Chief Justice, S. S., Mr. P. J. Sproule, had to resort to the use of crutches in entering and leaving the Singapore Supreme Court as the result of a sprain sustained while playing tennis.

Learning of the wish of the Denishawn dancers, to see him, Mei Lan-fang sent word that just as they desired to see him, so he likewise desired to see them. Therefore he proposed that he attend the theatre where they were performing, watch "their versions of Occidental dances, and then take the same stage and "do his stuff" in Chinese style. This programme was duly carried out to the pleasure of all concerned. Incidentally, the audience voted the show that particular night was considerably more than the money's worth, since ordinary glimpses of Mei Lan-fang are infrequent and usually entail a long wait through a series of noisy Chinese plays until Mei cided to put in an appeal around midnight or early morning.

In Bangkok, on Christmas Eve, the temperature was below 60 deg. Fah.

Expenses of being an M.P. were given as follows by Mr. J. C. Gould, the Cardiff shipowner, in the London Bankruptcy Court: Three elections.....£1,300 each. Expenses as M.P., £2,000 a year. Total for six years.....£15,900 Mr. Gould was M.P. for Cardiff Central from 1918 until 1924. He said that a company known as Gould Steamships and Industrials, Ltd., was formed in 1920 with a capital of £3,000,000. Despite the slump it made a profit of £500,000, but had now been forced into voluntary liquidation by bad trade. The hearing was adjourned:

Railway relics, including 258 books on railroads of all countries, realigned £385 at Sotheby's.

Mrs. Leacock, wife of Professor Stephen Leacock, of McGill University, the well-known humourist died in Liverpool. She was brought across the Atlantic in the hope that a cure for cancer might be found in the new lead treatment, but she was too ill to undergo the treatment.

At Blackburn, it was stated in the case of one defendant that he found it too hot and stuffy to follow mill employment in summer and too cold to leave his bed in winter; and of another defendant that he declined to attend the court unless a four-wheeler was sent for him!

In an address at a luncheon at the Yokohama Bankers' club in his honour, Dr. John R. Mott, General Executive Secretary of the International Y.M.C.A. praised the spirit of the people of Yokohama and elaborated upon the programme of the Y.M.C.A. in Japan as well as throughout the world.

Miss Florella Pedley, daughter of Dr. and Mrs. Hilton Pedley of Kyoto, was married December 19, in New York City to Mr. Conrad Van Hyning according to cable information received in Japan. Miss Pedley was a member of the faculty of Kobe College three years and left Kobe for the United States August 25.

Mr. W. A. H. Thomas, Dairen manager of Messrs. Samuel & Co., Tokyo, and acting Vice-Consul for the Netherlands, and Mrs. Thomas expect to leave for England late this month. Mr. W. H. Winning, who is acting as Swedish Consul at Dairen, is expected to take over the Dutch Vice-Consulate also, while Mr. G. B. T. A'Beer of Tokyo will be in charge of the Dairen branch of Samuel Samuel & Co.

At the last meeting of the Shanghai German Women's Club officers for the coming year, were elected to take the place of members who will not be in Shanghai during the next season. New Officers are Mrs. A. Hartmann, secretary, Mrs. G. Rochreke, executive board member, Mrs. E.H. Thiel and Mrs. E. Krueger, Social committee members, Mrs. F. Thiel, the wife of the German Consul General was welcomed back as honorary president of the organisation following a year's absence in Europe.

Mr. Henry Brooks, J.P., of Blackpool, Lanes, who rose from the position of a circus acrobat and gymnast to that of theatre manager, and who became Mayor of Blackpool last year, left £35,840.

Mr. J. H. Kortright, of the Chartered bank of Yokohama, will leave Japan early next month for England where he will join his wife to spend a six month's leave.

The Rev. H. Parsons, who worked in Singapore at St. Andrew's Cathedral, has been appointed Priest-in-charge of Warwick, Bermuda and expects to arrive in that Colony about this time.

Earl Jellicoe, in unveiling at Portsmouth the portrait of the late Dame Agnes Weston, said the Mother of the Navy was a noble woman who built up a work for which naval men revered her memory.

Mr. H. O. Wusser, assistant manager for the Tor Hotel in Kobe, has taken the position of manager of the Astor House in Shanghai and will leave Kobe in February with his wife and two children for China. Mr. Wasser has been with the Tor Hotel in Kobe four years. His successor has not been announced.

H.E. Sir Laurence Guillemard, Governor of the Straits Settlements and British Agent for North Borneo and Sarawak, has kindly consented to the suggestion that one of the roads in Sandakan be named after him, in commemoration of his visit here in 1924, says the "British North Borneo Herald." The road running round the Recreation Ground is accordingly to be renamed Guillemard Road.

Mr. Andrew C. Fleming of the Robert Dollar Company in Tokyo has been appointed passenger agent in Kobe for the Dollar and Admiral-Oriental Steamship Lines, to succeed Mr. Ralph H. Norris whose resignation from the Dollar Line is effective January 1. Mr. Fleming was manager of the Robert Dollar Company offices at Chunking in China four years and six months ago and was placed in charge of the Robert Dollar Company office in Tokyo. This office is being discontinued from January 1. Mr. Norris, whose position Mr. Fleming is taking, expects to leave for the United States in a few weeks as the representative in America for an exporting firm in Japan.
