## NEW CANTON TAXES

## REVENUE TO SETTLE THE BOYCOTT

Powers Told About Procedure.

DOMESTIC LEVY: BUT CUSTOMS CO-OPERATION WANTED.

〣〣〣〣〣

Further concrete evidence is available as to Canton's intention to terminate the boycott, it being now revealed that the new taxes will be collected unless the Powers intervene.

The Nationalist Government chooses the obvious date of Oct. 11 for the levy to commence.

Reading between the lines it appears that Canton is particularly desirous of working through the Maritime Customs. But should facilities not be forthcoming, the Government will use their own organisation.

The Canton Information Bureau has issued an important communique through Reuter:—

The acting Minister for Foreign Affairs has addressed an identic note to each of the local representatives of the Foreign Powers whose nationals are in trading relation with Liang Kwang provinces.

“I have the honour to communicate to you the following translation of a mandate issued by my Government on October 4:—(1)—The Ministry of Finance is hereby instructed to levy a temporary internal tax on the consumption or the production of such articles as are the subject of trade between the Liang Kwang provinces and the other provinces in China and foreign countries.

(2)—The rate of taxation shall be equivalent to half the usual Maritime or Native Customs tariff (as the case may be) on general articles and to a full tariff on articles of luxury such as silk, silk-stuff, toilet articles, fur and leather articles of decoration, 'gems' and precious stones and similar goods. Cigars, cigarettes, imported wines, kerosine and gasoline, which are the subject of other special taxes, are exempt from this tax. (3)—The Ministry of Finance, for purposes of convenience, may collect such taxes at or near the various Maritime and Native Customs barriers and is instructed to make detailed regulations governing the collection of the said tax.

(4) —Any person selling or buying or otherwise dealing with articles on which the said tax has not been paid shall be liable to a term of imprisonment not exceeding three years and/or a fine equivalent to the times the value of the article or articles which shall also be confiscated.

(5)—This mandate shall come into effect on October 11, 1926.

"It seems desirable to emphasise the fact that the new tax is in principle an internal tax to be distinguished from the imposts levied as Customs duties under the Chinese Maritime Customs system. There is, of course, no intention to interfere with the latter as at present administered, though possible misunderstanding and friction would be avoided if Maritime Customs co-operation should be available in the collection of the new tax by fiscal authorities to be appointed by my Government."

## Reply to Customs

That Maritime Customs cooperation is desired is indicated in a letter addressed to the Commissioner of Customs by the Chinese Superintendent of Customs who states:—Further with reference to the collection of the new consumption and production tax, I wish to reply to your specific questions as follows:—

(1)—A tax will be levied based on the Customs Tariff unless for any technical reason such would prove unsatisfactory in which case an effective 2½ per cent. and 5 per cent. will be charged for general articles and luxuries, respectively.

(2)—The Ministry of Finance would prefer if possible to borrow a room or rooms in the Customs building for the use of a small staff to carry on the collection of the new taxes which would facilitate the work of both the Ministry and Customs administration.

## SHOT HIS BOLT?

## Genius of Cantonese Vanguard

FLANK ATTACKS BY ALLIES.

Sun Chuan-fang in a Race

[By Our Special Correspondent.]

Has General Li Chung-jen (Can-

conese 7th Army) shot his bolt?

After taking Te-an, he marched up the Nanchang-Kiukiang Railway with a comparatively small force and threatened Kiukiang, the headquarters of Sun Chuanfang.

Evidently there has been a race for time. Before other Cantonese detachments —including picked divisions said to be led by Chang Kai-shek in person—could attack Nanchang and start a debacle, the northern allies moved up reinforcements and the genius of the southerners' vanguard has had to retire.

The Kiangsi front has now been considerably shortened. Instead of having columns moving out to menace Hunan, Marshall Sun Chuan-fang brought back several mixed brigades to make a determined stand on this important railway.

Cables from the war zone indicate that the northerners attacked Li Chung-jen from two sides, pressed back his two flanks and caused him to retire a few miles, thus relieving immediate pressure.

It appears that the reinforcements that Sun Chuan-fang withdrew from the Yangtse banks have been sent down the railway to continue the counter-attack.

The northern troops who pushed back Li Chung-jen were brought up from Nanchang and other districts to the south of Tein.

Both sides are now throwing the greater part of their strength into the campaign. It is predicted that a big battle will be fought parallel to the railway—possibly the deciding engagement of the war.

In the south of Kiangsi, the rival armies are content to watch each other. In the north (i.e., along the Yangtse, between Kiujiang and Wuchang) hostilities have decreased considerably and it is understood that General Tang Sang-thee (commanding the 8th Cantonese Army) has come to terms with Sun Chuan-fang.

Colour is lent to this sudden trend of politics by a cable, reported to have been sent by Sun Chuan-fang to the Fengtian Party (in power at Peking) recommending an alliance with Tang Sang-chee (who is to be given the territory he, now holds) against Chang Kai-shek!

Wu Pei-fu is doing very little except, possibly, consolidating his small holding in Honan province.

Fukien is still talking about attacking Swatów but that is about all. Admiral Tu Hsi-kwei (ex-acting Premier) is reported by a Chinese cable to be returning to Amoy to take charge of warships there in a sortie on Swatow.

To-day the prospect is muc brighter for the northern allies.

The southern coup almost succeeded but the allied counterattack has driven the Cantonese from a dangerous position. Rapidity in getting up to the new front may prove the deciding factor in the next few days.

#### OUR G.C.H

## Alterations Considered Uneconomic

## MUST BE REBUILT

Reasons for Choosing Adjacent

Relative to the Government's decision to rebuild the Government Civil Hospital on the lawns adjoining the present site, a "China Mail" representative had an interesting interview yesterday with Dr. J. B. Addison, Principal Civil Medical Officer.

Our representative asked Dr. Addison whether, in view of the expense which would be incurred in erecting a new building, the present building could not be adapted to the changing needs or, in the event of a new building being necessary, was not a site in a more reserved and less noisy neighbourhood preferable?

It appears that the present building is considered by the Public Works Department to have "come to the end of its life" and that, to continue to pay out on alterations, renovations and other maintenance items would in the long run be an uneconomic policy. Similarly, from the medical point of view, it is felt that the only permanent solution to the problem of providing adequate accommodation and facilities is in the erection of a new building.

As regards the vicinity of the Hospital and the question of whether it was a desirable one, Dr. Addison said that undoubtedly it was extremely handy for the poorer classes of Chinese with whom they were very largely concerned. Again, the Hospital was in a far more convenient place as regarded the University for students of which the Hospital provided a valuable training ground.

By utilising a site in the vicinity for the new Hospital there would be a considerable saving in other ways, it was further pointed out. The enlarged sisters' quarters would still be available as would the present "A" block, which would be re-adapted as servants' quarters, and the present operating and X-ray room which would be the new stewards' quarters.

The site of the present Government Civil Hospital will be used partly as an open space or gardens and partly for providing sites for residences.

The work of building the new Hospital is calculated to take three years. It will have greater accommodation all round, including more European wards; and will be designed to meet the more pressing

needs of the Colony since the present Hospital was erected, some 60 years ago.
