# LOCAL AND GENERAL NEWS.

Nearly all the Malay residents of Johore Bahru—people who have lived there for many years are leaving the place, moving back, upcountry, to the kampongs. Johore Bahu town is now more or less over-run by Chinese and Tamils, and the Malays are leaving the place for this reason.—M.O.

Ipoh, July 8.—A new departure has occurred in local administration. The members of the Kinta Sanitary Board have been invited to a private conference with the Resident of Perak at Ipoh where they laid their grievances before him and a friendly discussion, lasting two hours, took place.

Interviewed by Chinese correspondents in Tientsin, Marshal Chang Tsō-lin expressed the opinion that Nankow is not as impregnable as many think and the attack on this section of the Kuominchun is not worrying him very much. The fall of the place is simply a matter of time, he said.

The "Singapore Free Press" correspondent at Jesselton says: This part of the world as well as Singapore is supposed to enjoy a reputation for the thirstiness of its people, a reputation earned mostly at the expense of entertaining visitors who, as a rule are not backward in the art of absorbing local colour.

Peking, July 3.—Admiral Tsi Ting-kan, Director General of the Customs Administration and a delegate to the Customs Conference yesterday handed in his resignation of both posts. No specific cause is said to have been given for his determination to resign other than his general pessimism about the present political situation. It is rumoured that Pan Fu has indicated a desire to get the positions which Admiral Tsai seeks to give up.

Washington, July 8. — The Government is without power to make regulations governing the radio in the absence of specific laws from Congress, the Secretary of Commerce, Herbert Hoover, was advised to day by the Department of Justice. Secretary Hoover has maintained that unless definite authority was provided by Congress to regulate radio communication the whole broadcasting machinery would be placed in an uncertain status.

The Hongkong Realty and Trust Company are calling up $2.50 a share on 170,000 shares. Such call will be payable at the registered offices of the Company on August 4.

On behalf of the Chinese General Chamber of Commerce, Mr. Yu, Ya-ching has transferred $8,500 to the Chinese Seamen's Union for distribution amongst the sailors who suffered losses during last year's strike. A further sum of $14,500 will be similarly distributed shortly.

Riga, June 18.—The Soviet has mobilised 300,000 Moscow workers to protest against the Anglo-Soviet note. The authorities started an attempt to penetrate the street occupied by the British Mission headquarters and ostentatiously frustrated the crowds intentions by the employment of the military.

The death is recorded of Mr. Peter Cronin, Chief Detective Inspector and Court Inspector at Malacca, which occurred on Wednesday afternoon (July 9) at the early age of forty and after a period of illness. A former member of the Royal Irish Constabulary, the late Mr. Cronin joined the Straits Settlements Police Force some twenty-six years ago and rose from the rank of constable to the position he held at times of his death. Mr. Cronin went to Malacca from Singapore in 1925. He was a much liked and a very popular officer and leaves many friends.

Rome, July 5.—The Pope vigorously protested to-day against the attitude of Mexico toward Catholics. In an address to students he expressed his sorrow over the persecution of Catholics which he said was continuing in Mexico. He declared that he desired to protest violations of Catholics' rights and conscience.

Jesselton, June 29.—According to a story that, has reached us here, the cooks of Miri recently went on strike over some grievance or other. The people of Miri have a delightfully novel way of dealing with strikers, "The cooks, who thought they were going to enjoy the pleasure of watching "boys" and "tukang ayers" wrestling with the Tuans' meats suddenly found themselves rounded up and hustled, about 100 of them, on Board a steamer. There they were told that any who wanted to come back and work could do so and thirty of them capitulated.—"Singapore Free Press."

Paris, June 7.—A crusade against low necks, transparent and sleeveless garments for women, has been started on the Riviera by the Bishop of Nice. The Sacrament of the Church will be refused in future to all women who appear in abbreviated garments. The bishop not only extends his campaign to those actually kneeling before him, but orders that ceremonies shall be suspended when any member even of a marriage party is “indecently” dressed.

## SOCIAL AND PERSONAL NEWS

The smallness and inadequacy of the Japanese Consular jail in Shanghai, together with the recent cases of prisoners escaping and committing suicide, have led the Japanese Consul-General there to telegraph the Minister of Justice in Tokyo for permission to repair and enlarge the jail premises. The present jail really consists of little more than a couple of flats. It is understood that the Tokyo authorities have sent officers to Shanghai to investigate.

At the Palace Hotel, Kowloon, the final in the recent billiards handicap will start at 9 p.m. on Monday. The finalists are F. M. da Cruz and G. Moore, both owe 200 points.

Simla, June 29.—At to-day's sitting of the Punjab Legislative Council three important Prison Reform Bills were passed. Two of these give effect to the Lumsdon Report regarding the appointment of new jail officials in place of the old jailers, held to be generally corrupt, and the release on probation of prisoners who will work on special farms. The third one is to train in Borstal Institutions adolescent prisoners segregated from those of mature age. It also empowers the Local Government to transfer to jail all inmates found to be incorrigible, or who have been punished for escape from an institution, or have been held guilty of a major Borstal Institution offence.

Unemployment in France is the lowest in Europe, according to the latest Government statistics, which show that in the whole of France only 409 people (22 of them women) are drawing out-of-work benefits. This year's figures are the lowest registered since the war. The highest were in 1921, when there were 91,225 unemployed. Last year there were 705.

London, June 28.—A big decline in membership is shown in the report of the General Federation of Trade Unions for the year ending March 31, which states that membership for the year was 735,000, a decrease of 94,000 compared with the previous year, and 847,000, compared with 1921. The decrease is attributed chiefly to amalgamation and also to the inability to pay contributions owing to bad trade.

Simia, June 29.—The monsoon has extended into Gujarat and the Central Provinces. A message from Gujarat says that the holding off of the monsoon is causing great anxiety. If heavy showers do not fall in a few days a very serious situation is apprehended. The prices of foodstuffs are already rising.

- Berlin, July 7.—Former Reichs chancellor Luther has been appointed to the board of German State railways.

Kolhapur, June 16.—The Maharaja of Kolhapur has passed a law prohibiting child marriages in his State. It provides that whoever becomes the guardian of a girl who has not completed 10 years or a boy under 14 years of age shall be deemed guilty of a crime punishable with fine up to Rs. 2,000.

The friends of Mrs. Frances Darbishire will be grieved to learn that a cablegram was received notifying her death at Home on July 4. Mrs. Darbishire was the daughter of the late Sheriff Davidson of Fort William, and was formerly engaged on the staff of the Raffles Girls' School. She married the late Mr. C. Warbishire in 1905.

Singapore, July 9.—We deeply regret, says the “Malay Mail,” to announce the death, of Mr. Ian Henderson, of Jemima Estate, F.M.S. Rubber Planters Estates, Ltd. We understand that Mr. Henderson sustained a broken leg as a result of a motor cycle accident on Sunday and that he died under an anaesthetic while the limb was being set at the Seremban Hospital, on Monday morning. Mr. Henderson, who was aged 23, was the son of Mrs. M. H. Henderson, of 80 Earl's Court Square, London. He arrived in this country at the end of last February.

The contracting parties were Sir Richard Waldie Griffith, aged 76, and Alice Maud Pearson, of 251, Cromwell Road, London, S.W., aged 40. The bridegroom, who is well known figure in his native land, is on holiday in Malaysia and the bride arrived in this country about six weeks ago. Dato Khatib Koyan is a Sumatra Malay by birth, but has served as a Penghulu for over 36 years. He has shown himself faithful and hardworking, and has earned the goodwill of all who knew him.
