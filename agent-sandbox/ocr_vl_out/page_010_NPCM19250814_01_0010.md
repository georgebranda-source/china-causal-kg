SHANGHAI CHINESE CITY OVER-RUN.

(Reuter's Service.)

Bands of striking wharf and godown coolies this morning overran the Chinese city creating a riot during which they looted the foodshops.

Shanghai, August 13.

The demonstrations were the result of strike pay not being forthcoming.

The police were helpless. They fired once, wounding one man.

The afternoon was quieter. A mob of a few hundreds started for the Settlement and demonstrated at the Chinese Chamber of Commerce. They were pacified by labour officials. The municipal police prevented trouble.

## POLICE AT DINNER

In the House of Commons on June 29 Miss Wilkinson (Lab.-Soc., Middlesbrough, E.) asked the Home Secretary if he would state on whose authority and for what reason two police officers disguised as waiters attended a private dinner given to an ex-Cabinet Minister and several other honourable members of that House at the Boulogne Restaurant, Soho, on May 14.

Sir W. Joynson-Hicks: Two police officers dined in a public room of the Boulogne Restaurant on the night in question. They did not attend the hon. members' dinner disguised as waiters. (Laughter.)

Miss Wilkinson: Has the Home office been supplied with any notes from these two police officers as to any speeches that were made in that restaurant?

Sir W. Joynson-Hicks: I certainly am not going to give any answer as to any rates of any kind supplied to the Department over which I have the honour to preside. (Hear, hear, and Lab.-Soc. cries of "Why not?")

Miss Wilkinson: I did not ask for the contents of these notes. I simply asked whether it was a fact that any such notes were supplied. Sir W. Joynson-Hicks: I told the hon. lady that the two police officers dined in the public restaurant. I knew nothing about it till recently, when I made inquiries. They were not disguised as waiters, and did not attend the hon. lady's dinner. What they went there for it is not my duty to tell the House. (Ministerial cheers.)

Mr. W. Thorne (Lab.-Soc., Plaistow): They went there to spy.

Miss Wilkinson: Is it the policy of the Government to exercisé surveillance over their political opponents?

Sir W. Joynson-Hicks: No. Had I known the hon. lady and her friends were dining there these police officers would not have been allowed to go, and they will not be allowed to go.

Miss Wilkinson: Thank you.
