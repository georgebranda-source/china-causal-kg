THANKSGIVING DAY

QUIET OBSERVANCE IN HONGKONG

## MEANING OF THE DAY

No official function marked the observance among Americans locally of Thanksgiving Day but several business houses were closed, also the American Consulate.

Americans commemorate to-day, has no fixed date and commemorates no particular event—it is rather the expression of a sentiment and hence has not always been observed on the same date or even in the same season.

The harvest home idea—the appropriateness of rendering thanks after the crops are garnered, has made the autumn season, however, the preferred one. The last of November having been chosen by President Johnson, this selection became the precedent for all subsequent years.

Thanksgiving Day is a universally observed national holiday, the head of the Government issues a proclamation and business closes down entirely. As it has to do with the safe reaping of harvests it is observed more in the country districts than in the cities.

In a Thanksgiving address at Nanking, Judge Lobinger gave a historical sketch of the national occasion and said in conclusion: "Our Thanksgiving Day as we have seen, sprang from various faiths. It is no longer sectarian. It is a product of ideas originating in different lands and has outgrown sectionalism. It needs only to pass the bounds of narrow nationalism to become a day of humanity. And it is one of the most gratifying features of this happy occasion that its participants are of various nationalities."

## OBITUARY

CHINESE DEATH AT 86TH BIRTHDAY

WELL-KNOWN METALS DEALER

## ( )

Fifty-seven years a resident in Hongkong, Mr. Chin E-tin, the well-known metals merchant passed away at his residence, No. 23, Wing Woo Street yesterday at the ripe age of 86.

By a strange coincidence yesterday was the late M. Chin's birthday and it was after receiving congratulations from his many friends and relatives that he expired peacefully.

The funeral takes place tomorrow afternoon, and the remains will be deposited at the Tung Wah Hospital repository. The cortege passes the Yat Pit Ting, Kennedy Town, about 1.15 p.m.

When Mr. Chin first arrived in the Colony he founded the firm of E. Hing & Co., now one of the oldest and most respected hardware firms. Many of the older members of the mercantile community will recall Mr. Chin, who from a very humble origin, rose to great wealth by integrity and shrewd handling of his business.

YUNNAN BRIGANDAGE

MOST RIFE

(From Our Own Correspondent.)

Yunnan at the present time is enjoying peace, as far as military operations are concerned, General Fan Shek-sang (the Yunnanese pro-Sun mentenary chieftain), having been driven out, and is understood that he has returned to Canton in the hopes of finding a more lucrative undertaking than that of attacking Yunnan.

But in spite of the lack of military operations, there never was a time when brigandage was more rife. Travelling is a risk which few will undertake. When undertaken, only a military escort will provide a chance of safety, so that no one thinks of travelling without it. As far as foreigners are concerned, no Consul will allow his nationals to travel otherwise.

I have just received news that trouble is expected at Talang, to the south-west of Yunnanfu, and the missionary residing there has been told to clear out as soon as possible, as the authorities cannot promise protection.

The city seems to be full of soldiers, and one cannot help thinking that their time might be well employed in suppressing brigands instead of kicking their heels here.
