## BAD TO WORSE

POSITION OF GERMAN

TRADE.

DYERS REVIEW

Berlin, September 3.

The general depression in German industries and their inability to meet their increasing demands for raw materials unless there is a considerable expansion in export trade was emphasised by Herr Duisberg, Chairman of the Association of German Industriallsts and Chairman of the German dye industry, in speech at a general meeting of the Association at Dresden.

Herr Duisberg, who has just returned from a world tour, finds the position in the country generally worse than when he left Germany. He said that the engineering industry was in a worse position in July than in June. The position of the iron and steel industry was extremely bad, as was evidenced by the number of unemployed, whilst the dyestuff industry, which was the backbone of the chemical industry, was very unsatisfactory, the business being under 50 per cent. of the average turnover.

Hierro Duisberg accused America of dumping competition. He said that although the export from the mining and iron industry had rather improved as the result of the British miners' strike the home demand for coal remained very unsatisfactory.

Herr Duisberg said that he expected the impending conclusion of the iron and steel impact to bring to an end French and Belgian dumping sales in South Germany.

The position of the electro-technical industry was generally satisfactory, but paper and textiles were not prospering with the exception of artificial silk, velvet, and silk. He concluded by recommending the strictest economy and an all round reduction it imports to the minimum.—Reuter.
