## NEXT CHINA WAR

CANTON'S AMBITIONS.

Only the next few months can tell whether Marshal Sun Chuan-fang was wise in delaying material aid for his professed superior, Wu Pei-fu.

Gossips say that Sun Chuanfang purposely deferred entry into the Act, that if Wu Pei-fu wasгоff and he could act for his own benefit. If, on the other hand, Nationalists were vanquished, he could not have been drawn into the Act. It has to be admitted that if he disposes of the Canton Party, his power will be greater than that of Wu Pei-fu at his zenith.

But the question now is whether Sun Chulan-fang, on his own resources, can crush Chang Kai-shek. That a new war will be in progress there seems little doubt as the southerners are now branching out.

Although Marshal Sun nominally rules five provinces it cannot be taken for granted that all the generals and people therein will rally to his standard, especially at a time when Chang Kai-shek has proved a formidable foe.

Should Chang Kai-shek respect Sun Chuan-fang's declaration of neutrality, the only concern then will be one of balance of power.

Then there is Marshal Chang Tso-lin and his Fengtien Party to consider. Eighteen months ago, he suffered a check by Sun Chuanfang. The Manchu partisans had extended an arm to Shanghai, to the rich area around. When Sun Chuan-fang struck, they hastily withdrew northwards.

Perhaps Chang Tso-lin will be satisfied to rule the North.

Admiral Tu Hsi-kwel, who was nominated by Wu Pei-fu for the acting Premiership in the absence of a President, is now without support and the Fengtienites are putting forward the name of Chien Yun-peng as the new Premier.

In Canton's Favour?

One considered opinion is that Fengtien will be contented to leave the territory south of the Yellow River to Sun Chuan-fang.

The consensus of opinion is that Sun Chuan-fang has moved too late; and that Chang Tso-lin will mark time until necessary—when he will fight the final with the survivor. Their old animosity is a factor in favour of the Nationalists.

A great deal hinges on what are Canton's ambitions so far. They are not pursuing Wu Pei-fu (who still has a large army in "Honan province") but appear to be diverting their attention to consolidating the "South first.

## ALLEGED GRAFT

AN EX-ATTORNEY GENERAL ON TRIAL.

CHAMPAGNE DINNER STORY

New York, Sept. 10

At his trial Henry Daugherty, ex-Attorney-General, said he suggested giving King, the intermediary, something for aiding in securing the reimbursement for the sale of the Metals Company. Later, at King's request, he gave him a cheque for $50,000 at a champagne dinner in New York attended by Miller, King and Smith. Herr Merton on the same occasion ordered $391,000 worth of Liberty Bonds to be delivered to a local bond broker.
