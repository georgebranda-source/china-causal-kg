## IN OXFORD

RUGBY PLAYER'S STORY OF ARREST IN FRANCE

"I suppose my trousers brought suspicion on me," said the member of the Esher Rugby Club, who was the victim of mistaken arrest when the team recently played Havre.

“Though I was knocked about a bit,” he added, “I did not want to complain to the consul as we were returning next day. It was best to treat the matter as a joke.”

The player is one of the forwards in the Esher Fifteen and on the evening when the team landed wore a pair of trousers of Oxford bagginess. He was followed by gendarmes to a café.

Suddenly, he went on, eight gendarmes came round me and, shouting "He is an apache," arrested me, and, putting a chain round my wrists tightly, marched me to the station, searched me, and then knocked me across the room. Neither my passport nor the efforts of my friends, who tried to explain the reason of our visit, made any difference.

I was thrust into a cell that resembled a stable and locked in for the night. They gave me a punch under the jaw in parting.

I was given nothing to eat or drink and was not released till I had been there five hours.

Have won the match by 3 tries to nil.

## DERBY SUBSCRIPTION

NOVEL TERMS OF STOCK EXCHANGE SWEEP.

Novel conditions appear on the tickets of "The Derby 1925 S/E Mutual Subscription Fund," which is the name of this year's Stock Exchange Derby Sweepstake. These are the terms of subscription:

The fund will be distributed by the organisers as to 10 per cent, less all costs and expenses incurred by the organisers, among charities; and as to 90 per cent. in such manner as the organisers in their absolute discretion deem fit.

All subscriptions are made and received upon the basis that the organisers shall be under no legal obligation whatsoever, whether directly or indirectly, to the subscribers, but they honourably pledge themselves to distribute the whole of the 90 per cent. of the fund by means of allotments to such of the subscribers as they may determine.

The fund is limited to members of the Stock Exchange and “the list will be closed at the discretion of the organisers.”

Each ticket bears the signature, printed in facsimile, of the two members of the Stock Exchange who are honorary organisers.

## AUTO SUGGESTION

At a certain Scottish golf club the whisky is as popular as the game, and as freely indulged in, One day a member became seriously ill and had to be removed to a home after noticing that his niblick had turned a pale blue colour shot with yellow stripes. Months later, when he had returned and was playing in a foursome, an adder moved out of the rough and took its snaky course over one of the greens. The convalescent turned pale and stood stock still for a second, then he observed with a shaky laugh, "I know you fellows all think I saw a snake there, but I didn't."

## IDEAL PARTNER

HELPFUL SUTCLIFFE.

THIRD TEST INCIDENT.

Continuing, in the “Daily News” his comments on the last English cricket team's Australian tour; J. B. Hobbs says:—

"No more united band of cricketers ever left England in quest of the ashes. Those members who were left out of the various matches took their disappointment like bricks.

The most consistent batsmen were Sucliffe and myself. Sucliffe's wonderful pe formances justify the expectation that he will be my successor. He possesses the right temperament for a great occasion. Is always a cheerful and encouraging partner, and never suffered from swelled head when Australia was riaging with his praises. I recollect that, in the third test, when we got together later than usual, the wicket was tricky, and Mailey was bowling wonderfully. I felt at sea, but suddenly, at the end of an over. Sucliffe approached and said: Look here, old man; it stands to reason that he cannot bowl like that for long. Just stick to it, Jack! I did, and made another century.

## RACING STORIES

"SAUCE FOR THE GOOSE"

A certain noble punter having had a very good week and not having received his cheque, on the following Monday, write to his commission agent to ask the reason. The reply came by wire, "Plenty of money; run out of cheques."

Later on, in the season the punter had a very bad week and omitted to put his account in order. On his bookmaker writing to remind him he replied (also by wire), "Plenty of cheques; run out of ink."

## HIS CHOICE

In a public house in Dublin there is an assistant who is very free with his “tips” that are seldom successful. On the morning of a recent Baldoyle meeting a friend called there and on seeing him the assistant exclaimed rather excitedly. “Look here, look here, I have a horse for you!”

Amid general laughter came the reply. Thanks, keep him for yourself. I am going down by train.

## WIMBLEDON

The “Nil Desperandum” Cup has been presented by a member of the Nil Desperandum organisation with “knock out” match play competition, club handicaps to court, for players with a par handicap of 20 and up. The matches will be for 18 holes with 36 holes for the final. Entry lists have been placed in the club house and in the Yokohama United Club. The lists close on May 31. It is hoped to give contestants three weeks for the first round and two weeks for the second but in any event players must complete their matches within the times stipulated. Closing dates for each round will be posted on the notice board in the club house.

ANDERSONS CHANCES.

J. O. Anderson, Australian champion, has left for England to take part in the British championships at Wimbledon. As the entries for these events will include all the leading players of Europe and some of the Americans, probably including Richards, Australians will be looking forward to the results with more than ordinary interest. There are many who think that Anderson will not only carry off the British title, but will produce form far superior to anything shown by him in America before. Since he last played in the States his backhand strokes have improved wonderfully, until now he is practically as deadly on a high ball on his backhand as he is on the forehand.
