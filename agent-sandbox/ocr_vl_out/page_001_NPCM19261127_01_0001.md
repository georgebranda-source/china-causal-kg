## RIGHT TO SEARCH

Two Hong Kong Yessels

Held up:

"TARANTULA" TO RESCUE.

Canton Soldiers Removed on

West River.

In attempting to enforce the unrecognised right of examining all craft on the West River, a detachment of the Canton Government's inspection Corps met with H.M.S. "Tarrantula" a few days ago, with unexpected results.

This British gunboat was escorting two lighters (tbwed by a steam-launch) from Wuchow to Hong Kong.

At Do-sing the "Tarantula" put on speed and patted company. The inspection Corps has a depot at this port, working from a pair (pontoon) in the river. No effort was made at the time to intercept the lighters for foreible examination.

About 12 miles further down river, an armed launch from the depot caught up with the tow. The cowswain was told to bring his launch and both lighters back to the official pontoon. This, he pointed out, he could not do upstream, ignist the current.

The British Flag.

Accordingly, the Inspection Corps told him to anchor one lighter, being the other up, and then return for the second.

Having given these orders, the armed launch returned to Do-sing. But the "Tarrantula" came up river again and found three armed soldiers in charge of the launch and lighters.

These soldiers were removed, in a motor-launch, to the British gunboat which told the coxswain of the tow to proceed on his journey.

With the three soldiers and their gifts, the "Tarantula" steamed back to the Inspection Corps' pontoon at Do-singe where the officer in charge of the Government depot received back his men and their arms, but not the launch and the two lighters.

It was explained that neither the depot's armed launch nor the three Chinese soldiers had any right to hold up vessels under the British flag.

Foreigners Asked To Pay.

Lighters and tow-boats controlled by the Standard Oil Co., have been convoyed from Canton upwards by armed launches of the Nationalist Government. This procedure has meant that craft under foreign flags have been "searched" by the Nationalist examiners at Samshui, but very few have submitted to the demand.

Requests by foreign merchants at Canton, to the Government, for Chinese Naval convoys, have brought about comical situations. Officials from the escorts, or from the Canton, Naval Department, have asked applicants if they will supply the necessary bunker coal for a round trip, or pay for the services in a sum practically equivalent to arrears of wages owing to the crew. Such suggestions have, of course, been turned down, but the inference is that if traders want protection they will have to find the funds with which the Canton armed launches can operate.

## TAI O FIRE

AN OLD MAN LOSES HIS FORTUNE.

OVER 300 PIGS KILLED.

Official figures of the big fire at Ta' O, 'Lantau Island' (in the Colony of Hong Kong) on Thursday evening, are 300 huts gutted and about 50 or 60 saved, 1,200 people homeless and damage at approximately $30,000.

Damage was confined to the several rows of matshed homes, built on stone piles in a series of creeks facing the main street on

the fire occurred) by a stretch of water 100 yards wide.

About 7 p.m. the alarm was raised. Owing to the combustible nature of the huts, the blaze soon spread to adjoining creeks and finally only one row was saved.

Police and other volunteers wud: in mud waste deep to check the ames.

Thought Pirates Had Come!

As Tal O is about three hours from Hong Kong, No. 3 fire float (the biggest in the Colony) did not reach the scene until about 9 p.m. when the fire was practically over. The shallow water surrounding the locality also hithered effective work.

An old man who owned one of the huts, on hearing the alarm, thought that pirates had raided the village as happened a few years ago. He forthwith concealed a wad of banknotes—his savings—in a crack. On rushing back to get his money, he suffered slight burns in the face. At present it is not known if he was successful in his quest.

Most of these fisher folk keep a few pigs in their homes. In the darkness and confusion about 200 of these pigs were not liberated in time and they perished in the flames.

In addition to actual loss of property, about 1,200 souls will have to seek temporary accommodation. It costs about $100 to build a home such as those destroyed.

Over 40 years ago, Tai O. village as it then was, was wiped out by a disastrous conflagration.

Some of the present unfortunate victims'still remember the event, which was worse than that of Thursday night.
