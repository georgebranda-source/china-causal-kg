A great stir was caused by the attempt made on Vistount French's life on December 19, 1919, while he was acting as Viceroy of Ireland, a post to which he had been appointed owing to the troubled conditions in that country.

During the King's visit to the Front in December, 1914 he conferred the Order of Merit upon the Field Marshal and the next year he was the recipient of France's highest military honour, the Medaille Militaire. On relia-quishing command in December, 1915 he was created Viscount French of Ypres and on his return home became Commander-in-Chief of the Home Forces.

His period of association with Ireland as its Viceroy was not the only one during which his name was intimately connected with the country for he was one of the military leaders who tendered their resignations in March, 1914 in connection with the threatened movements of troops from the South following Lord Carson's threat regarding arms running.

His early career centred chiefly round South Africa and his name is immemorially associated with

the. relief of. Kimberley of February 15, 1900, and the Battle of Diamond, Hill in which he bore the brunt of the whole Boer attack.

He was appointed to command in South Transvaal in November, 1900 and on his return to England the next year he was given the command of the first Army. Corps at Aldershot, his next steps up to the appointment as Field-Marshal

of Forces (1907-11) and Chief of Imperial General Staff (1911-14). Prior to his career in South Africa, French did good work in connection with the Nile Expedition being present at Abu Klea, Gabul and Metarmneh. He also commanded cavalry with Sir George White's force in Natal. He was born at Ripple, Kent, in 1852, the navy being looked upon by his parents as his future sphere. He in fact served in the Navy from 1866-70 and did not enter the Army until 1874.

## SILK DUTIES

THE DRAWBACK RATES ANNOUNCED.

### (Reuter's Service.)

The following are the drawback rates on silk articles produced in Great Britain and North Ireland from material on which customs or excise duty are paid.

Silk in an article of soil yarn 17d, per lb, throwa yarn undischarged 45d, per lb, thrown yarn and spain yarn wholly or partly discharged 49d, per lb, tissue undischarged 51d, per lb, wholly or partly discharged if proved to the satisfaction of the commissioners to have been produced from imported undischarged tissue, 93d, per lb, soil tissue, made from imported nails 19d, per lb, any other case 66d, per lb.

Doubled or twisted thread advanced beyond the stage of singles yarn 19d, per lb, tissue made from staple fibre or other waste, 11d, per lb, tissues proved to the satisfaction of the commissioners to have been made from other forms of artificial silk 21d.

In the case of articles made wholly or partly of silk or artificial silk which are proved to the satisfaction of the commissioners to have been charged duty and which are shown to be in such form and state that if duty had not been paid they would have been liable for life same rate of duty as already charged, then the drawback will equal the duty paid.

In the case of made up articles consisting wholly or partly of silk or artificial silk; if exported in the form and state in which imported, then the drawback will equal the amount of duty paid, and if manufactured in Great Britain or North Ireland from silk or artificial silk the drawback will equal the amount payable as drawback in respect of such quantity like silk or artificial silk as the commissioners opine has been used in the manufacture of the article.

## NOT THE WAY

## UNEMPLOYMENT BILL REJECTED

(Router's Service.)

## LONDON, May 22

The House of Commons rejected by 216 to 118 votes the second reading of the Prevention of Unemployment Bill introduced by the Labour Party providing for the establishment of a national employment and development board with a State grant of £10,000,000 a year, the board to consist of Ministers of the Crown and to be empowered to make advances to dominion or colonial governments, local and public authorities to be spent anywhere in the Empire for purposes calculated to provide employment.

Mr. Lloyd George declared that nothing could be done under that Bill which the Cabinet was not able to do without it.

A Conservative motion for the rejection of the Bill declared the Bill afforded no real solution of unemployment and would further handicap industrial revival by placing a new burden on taxpayers.
