# LOCAL AND GENERAL

The cup presented to the Golf Club by Shanghai visitors is on view in Maksra. Falconer's window

Singapore residents have been warned against wasting water. A water shortage is being experienced at Salgon.

The Golf Club has ordered a new Fordson tractor with five cutter units. Several bullocks be-longing to the club had to be killed. The tractor takes their place.

One of the London illustrated papers has a photograph of the handsomely carved chair presented by the Shanghai St. David's Society to the Welsh Eisteddford. The chair is a beautiful example of Chinese craftsmanship.

One of the latest novels—"The Uncharted Sea"—has Hongkong for its scene. There is a loveless marriage with a Taiwan of Hongkong's "machine-like, business-loving Englishman." We are told that "a great strike breaks out," in which the husband plays a die-hard part, being killed by the strikers.

Last year the time ball at the Royal Observatory (Kowloon) was dropped successfully 664 times. There was one failure, on December 16 at 10 a.m., owing to an injury sustained by the coolie, when hoisting the ball. It was dropped successfully at 11 a.m. The ball was not raised on June 26 at 10 a.m. owing to high wind, and on July 14 at 4 p.m., during a severe thunderstorm.

Referring to the “strike,” the Hongkong Observatory’s annual report says:→On “leaving the Observatory on June 26, the printer and his assistant, were assaulted by intimidators, so failed to attend on June 27 and 28. In consequence the weather reports for these days were not issued until June 29 when the printers returned to duty. This was the only interruption caused by the strike, so far as the routine work of the Observatory was concerned.

The "Soul Press" announces that a Japanese "birdman" has invented "a safe, practical aeroplane." The machine is a combination of an aeroplane and a helicopter, has sixteen wings, and is warranted to fly in any direction.

In connection with the alleged leasing of land in Mexico by Japanese the Tokyo Foreign Office states that it has received no information whatever. No Japanese residents in San Pedro, it is stated, are rich enough to undertake such a big enterprise and the truth of the story is doubted.

On the 31st ultimo, as the Hamburg-Amerika liner Oldenburg was passing the Akashi strait on her way to Kobe, she sighted a motor boat in distress, the men on board the boat, which was not under control and had a heavy list, waving their arms to attract attention. Captain Lueck slowed down, and took the men on board, tiving the motor boat also to safety.

A large-size submarine (2,000 tons), which was built at the Kawasaki Dockyard, Kobé, and taken to the Yokosuka Naval Port in the latter part of last month, is claimed by the Japanese Navy to be unique, embodying all the lessons of the European War, and her fighting efficiency is a match for ten submarines of the existing types. Submarines to be laid on the blocks are to be designed to this type in future. Admiral Kato, Commander-in-chief of the Naval Station, has inspected the submarine.

The Chinese Legation building in Seoul has been sold reports the "Osaka Jiji," which vouches for a price of ¥750,000. The building stands at Meji-cho on a lot covering an area of 7,500 tsubo an attempt was made several years ago by the Mukden Army to realise the estate it more than two million yen to supplement its chest, but the plan failed. Since then a large number of estate brokers have been interested in the property, which has now been sold. The proceeds will not be utilised for war purposes, it is understood, but go to reinforce the depleted treasury of the Peking Government.

Osaka Chauffure, dissatisfied with their treatment, recently went on strike.

Since the Armistice 2340,000,-000 has been spent, without service rendered, on unemployment.

Five thousand settlers are expected in Canada this year under the scheme for establishing families on the land.

The Gaumont Graphic at the Queen's Theatre shows pictures of the recent golf-archery contest with victory going to the Archers.

"What Happened to Jones" at the Queens is a first rate comedy which convulsed big audiences yesterday. The picture is being screened to-day and to-morrow.

From his London residence: Sir Evelyn Murray, secretary of the G.P.O., has spoken to America by telephone, but it is not certain whether it is commercially practicable.

The Government of Quebec is preparing to tax the heavy eater. A Bill introduced into the Legislature provides that a restaurant meal costing more than 4s. 2d is taxable at five per cent.

On March 1 the cost of living, says the "Ministry of Labour Gazette" was about 72 per cent. above that of July, 1914, as compared with 73 per cent. a month ago and 79 per cent. a year ago.

A well-dressed man coming out of a tea shop near the Temple pulled a banker chief out of his pocket, and at the same time dropped a sixpence. He walked on unaware of this, but a little girl standing near by picked up the coin, ran after him, and handed it to him. He rewarded her with half a crown.

Twenty guineas (8 offered by the National Farmers' Union) for the best pound lond of bread made entirely with flour milled from wheat grown in England and Wales, at the Bakers' Exhibition this year.

# SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

Mr. and Mrs. H. Birkett, Miss E. T. Birkett and Mrs. D. C. Miller left by the "Empress of Asia" yesterday.

F. M. Burian, former chief steward of the s.s. "President Cleveland" and for a month or so manager of Goulette's cafe, Manila, is in a serious condition from a bullet wound received when highwaymen attempted a raid. A stray bullet struck Mr. Burian Mr. Burian and Miss Candida Fanlo were married in the Manila Hotel about three years ago.

The Lawn Tennis Association have barred the German Champion, Frau Neppach, from further play in Germany, because she participated in the Riviera tournament.

Mr. Gishiro Kimura, proprietor of the "Kimura-ya Bakery on the Ginza, Tokyo, has died. Mr. Kimura was one of the pioneers in the bakery business in Japan, and has agents throughout the country, which are doing business under the name of Kimura-ya.

A successful operation severing tissue connections of the abdomen of twin boys born some time ago in a village near Nagoya, was reported from the Nagoya Medical College. Dr. Sato and Dr. Kawana were the surgeons who performed the difficult operation. The twins are expected to be able to leave the hospital within a week.

Mr. W. E. Gooch defeated Mr. R. J. Graciani in the final match of the Kobe Club Handicap Billiard Tournament, the play being witnessed by a large number of the members. Mr. Graciani, with a handicap of minus 150, had scored 128 points when his opponent, with a handicap of minus 250, had scored 250 points.

Prince Chichibu of Japan has presented the Kandahar Ski Club, of Murren, with a challenge cup to be known as The Prince Chichibu Challenge Cup. In accordance with the express desire of the Prince, who is a member of the Club, the cup will be awarded to the winner of a straight race open to all members of the Club.

MRS. CARTO'S BAGUALDO

Leonora Hughes, the famous dancer, who retired to marry Carlos Basúaldo, a wealthy South American, says that her entire future will be devoted to her home, which has now been blessed with the arrival of a son and heir.

Admiral Earl Jellicoe has just been elected a member of the Court of Assistants of the Mercers' Company. The Mercers is one of the richest of the City companies, and has an annual income of £111,000.

The great South African statesman, General Smuts, has occupied his leisure in writing a book on Philosophy, which Messrs. Macmillan are publishing in the early summer.

Mr. Sadao Suzuki, noted billiard player who recently toured America, has been elected director of the All-Japan Billiard League, with Rear-Admiral T. Sagawa of the Japanese Imperial Navy as the president. Mr. Suzuki is planning a tour of Japan for exhibition games.

Learnt his Greek Testament in waiting-rooms and trains. Went to Madagascar in a sailing ship in 1863 to build four churches for the London Missionary Society. Shipwrecked once, and once nearly killed by a cyclone. These are among the varied experiences of the Rev. James Sibree, F.R.G.S. now of Bromley, who celebrated his 90th birthday this month.

Prince Henry is to become president of the National Association of Royal Clubs.

At Ashby-de-la-Zouch, Mothering Sunday was "observed" for the first time since the Reformation.

General Booth's 70th birthday will be celebrated by the inviting of 70,000 poor people to a banquet.

Beef is the favourite joint of the English people, according to the " Review of the Frozen Meat Trade.

Mr. William Bradshaw, of Eccles, Lanes, son of the founder of "Bradshaw's Railway Guide." left £39,768.

Major-General William Vesey Brownlow, who commanded the mounted force in the first Boer war, has died in his 85th year.

South Africa's youth has been congratulated by the King in winning his trophy in the miniature rifle shooting match, 2,008 girls and boys competing.

The American ambassador to England thinks the "growing" (i.e. grumbling) is the pet prerogative of Britishers.—A popular pastime: "No one need sit up o' nights worrying about England" he says.

Sir Philip Watts, the naval architect who evolved the Dreadnought type of battleship and the Invincible type of cruiser, has died. Of the 34 battleships and battle-cruisers, which were present at the battle of Jutland, 29 were designed by Sir Philip.

Major Lionel Bettinson, of the National Sporting Club, has been nominated an honorary member of the German Boxers' Association in recognition of his efforts to strengthen the sporting connections between the two countries (says the Berlin correspondent of the Ex. Tel. Co.).

John Bull, of the Cheshire Cheese, Fleet street, is dead. Looking like that typical English-man of the cartoon, he walked into the tavern during the war and asked for a job as waiter. Asked his name, he replied: "John Bull." His appearance was so genuine, whatever doubts there might have been about his real name, that he was engaged on the spot, and ever since he has entertained customers from all over the world with his wilt.
