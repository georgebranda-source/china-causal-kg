### “UNFORTUNATE.”

HIS LORDSHIP ON EARLY DEFENCE.

THE ATHERLEY CASE.

TENTH AND LAST DAYS "HARKING"

At this, the tenth and last day of hearing of the charges of misappropriation of company moneys against James Edward Atherley, former manager of Getz Bros. (of the Orient) Ltd., His Lordship referred to the reservation of defence of Mr. Atherley at the early police court proceedings.

In the course of the case, said His Lordship, a statement was made by counsel for the prosecution and also by counsel for the defence as to the fact that in this case accused had had more than one opportunity of making an answer to the charge and had not availed himself of that opportunity. Mr. Zeitlyn had protested that whether accused should or should not have made an answer or given any idea of what his defence might be was a matter wholly for the accused and his representatives.

I fully agree with Mr. Zeitlyn said His Lordship. But, gentlemen (addressing the jury). I sometimes think that gentlemen who appear before us like the accused do not recognise the advantage of a statement made by an accused person as the earliest available opportunity. For instance, supposing when the accused is charged he gives an explanation and you find that the defence which has been adumbrated in his explanation is the defence adopted at the trial, a very strong argument can be based upon that statement.

It can be said that his defence has been consistent all along; that at the first available opportunity he stated what his defence was going to be; that all he stated in the witness box is to be believed and the facts as he stated are to be corroborated by the statement originally made by him. Now, take this case as an illustration. Supposing immediately he had had an opportunity the accused had said "it is true that I received these moneys but they were loaned to me by the compradoge. I did not take them knowing them to be the moneys of my employers and treated them as belonging to the compradoge" I think you will see at once how greatly strengthened the defence put up afterwards on behalf of the accused would have been.

However, as I have said, it is a matter solely for the expression of the accused and his advisers and the most you can say in a case where there has been complete reservation of the defence until the case comes on for trial in this court is that it is unfortunate that a statement was not made at once because any defence put up afterwards would have been greatly strengthened by a clear statement of what the defence was at the first available opportunity.

His Lordship then proceeded to sum up and was still summing up when this edition went to press.

Final Speeches.

In the final speech for the defence yesterday, Mr. Zeitlyn emphasised that Mr. Atherley had persisted in statements which demanded a large measure of credulity. When a man did not accept an explanation which would be better for him, and persisted in a statement which was harder to believe, was it not better to believe in a man. For the purposes of the defence it would be the easiest way to put it that moneys advanced by the compradore to Atherley were moneys from the Company. Atherley would not take that view of it. He persisted with determination that the true aspect of the case was that the compradore came to him with L.O.U.'s and O.K.'d bills and these together made a total for which he drew a cheque and handed it to the compradore. Continuing Mr. Zeitlyn said they had seen the compradore in the box. He was a man of considerable williness; he was not a gullible person but one who would not readily assent unless the truth was forced out of him. They would recollect that he (Counsel) had questioned him on five or six occasions about the officers of Getz

Bros. He had asked him what was the position of Mr. Kelly, and he had said he did not know. They would consider the mentality of such a man. And considering that, was it not probable that the story told by accused was the correct one? Was it not easy to believe that the compadore had taken the moneys of the Company as his own? And when moneys were paid back was it not probable that he took O.K.'d "bills and I.O.U's which totalled the sum handed over to Atherley? Surely that was not an improbable explanation. In the light of the character of the man in the box, this true explanation could well carry more confidence in the minds of the gentlemen of the jury for that reason.

Were They Loans?

Proceeding, Mr. Zeitlyn said that there was positive evidence that the transaction between Atherley and the compradore was in the nature of loans. He would deal with them under three heads—handwriting evidence, verbal admission and written evidence. Was it not strange that throughout all the books there was not one entry, showing that payment had been made to Atherley on behalf of Getz Bros.? Outside that exhibit D, there was not a single document, not a single book, not a single line which would show any payment to Atherley on behalf of the Company. They would recollect that their foreman had asked the compradore why there was no entry, and he had replied that having handed the moneys over to Atherley, he had finished with the matter.

Then there was the compradore's admission. He (Counsel) could not conceive of anything of greater support to the defence than an admission made by the compradore, that he looked to Atherley for repayment of the money which he had advanced to him. And there was even a stronger admission. He had said, "I had it always in mind to turn these I.O.U.'s and bills against the Company, if Atherley did not pay." And they would remember that a very wary, "wily man had made that answer.

Prosecution's Case.

Mr. Jenkin, Counsel for the prosecution, said that the true solution was whether Atherley knew in fact that moneys of the Company were going into his account, and in refunding cheques did he know he was refunding them to the Company. He (Counsel) held that he did know, and his learned friend had tried to get out of it by bringing in suggestions of fraud.

Having regard to Atherley's evidence entirely, he submitted that it was an affront to the intelligence of six men to ask them to believe that from start to finish, he did not know where the moneys came from, nor the true purpose for which every cheque was drawn by him. A deposit slip for May 28 showed that the totals collected were exactly the amount of a cheque drawn by Atherley. That was for the purpose of refunding moneys he had previously taken. They would remember those examples of intricate calculations, and Atherley would have them believe, that he did not know what they were for. Can you believe that this man, of whose probity and integrity, so much has been made, is telling you the truth?

Dealing with Mr. Atherley's banking account, Mr. Jenkin asked whether it was a coincidence that into this "most peculiar account for a man on this man's salary," money was poured at a rate which made even members practising at the Bar envious? Was it a coincidence that the account was opened on Settlement Day—on the eve prior to the day when he had to pay over certain sums? Was it a coincidence that on this day the Company's money went into this account, and not into the Company's account? Was there any necessity for Mr. Atherley to take a wad of the Company's shares and put it into this other account? In his own judgment, he had considered that the circumstances of this case were so strong that he did not wish to weary them with several details.

## A BIG BLAZE

FREE MATSHEDS GUTTED QUICKLY.

SCENE FROM HARBOUR.

Giving the appearance at first of being a big blaze in the eastern part of the city, a fire this morning destroyed three matsheds covering an area of 100 feet by 60 feet square, but did little other damage.

People who crossed the harbour from Kowloon after nine o'clock could see, from the, ferry, the flames reaching up.

At 9:37 a.m. Central Fire Station was called. A smart turn out resulted under Superintendent H. T. Brooks and Station Officer P. Condon.

Two motor pumps were brought to use but the outbreak had secured a firm hold. At 10.28 a.m. the order to cease work was given.

## WOMAN STABBED

ARMED ROBBERS VISIT

YAUMATI.

SURPRISE KNOCK ON DOOR

Whilst offering incense to “joss” on the verandah of No. 493, Nathan Road (Yaumati) yesterday morning, a Chinese woman was stabbed by armed robbers who had previously entered the house. The robbers were under the impression that she had gone to raise the alarm.

After binding the inmates, the gang collected loot valued at over $200 when an agent for sewing machines knocked on the door. The robbers took fright, dashed into the street and knocked the agent over in their hurry.

In the excitement the inmates stated that “two boys had been “kidnapped” by the robbers (for ransom). Later in the day the lads returned from school and it was found that a mistake had been made.

Yesterday, the delegates appointed by the District Commercial Guilds of Hongkong, sought the leaders of the local Chinese community for advice as to the advisability of going up to Canton to ask for "an "armistice" of a week or fortnight, before China New Year.

It is a fact that four delegates have been selected to make representations to the authorities concerned for permission to in-augurate a period of unrestricted travelling. Whether the mission will leave just yet remains to be decided.

In yesterday's report, the following paragraph was included:—

"This week there has been talk of steamers being allowed to run for a week," free of interference, but to carry no passengers.

The main argument to be put forward is that it is imperative for Chinese business concerns to make their final trade settlement before the end of the year, hence the necessity to go to and from Canton.

## POWER SCHEME

## RIME MINISTER OUTLINES PROPOSALS

## CHEAPER CURRENT

(Reuter's Service.)

Birmingham, Jan. 15

The Government proposals to promote a great national electrical scheme, in order to afford a cheaper supply of current, were outlined by Mr. Stanley Baldwin, Prime Minister, who pointed out that Great Britain would be seriously handicapped industrially if she continued to lag behind other nations.

The consumption of electrical energy in Great Britain was 200 units per head, compared with 900 units in Canada and over 500 units in the United States. He expressed the opinion that the latter figure should be reached here fifteen years hence by cheapening electricity through large scale production and distribution. He declared that the present system of generation was too parochial although most efficient.

The Government proposed to establish a Board, to be managed by practical men like the Port of London. Authority to control the whole supply of electricity throughout Great Britain. " The Board would co-ordinate existing stations, build new ones, raise their own capital, and work on purely commercial lines. The surplus would go to, reducing the cost of plant, when the interest and sinking fund were paid off. The Government would initiate a scheme with a guarantee. It did not consider that a subsidy was necessary.

## CABLE SHIP

LARGEST IN THE WORLD

SAILS.

A MAMMOTH UNDERTAKING.

(Reuter's Service.)

The “Colonia,” the largest cable ship in the world, has sailed from Greenwich this afternoon to lay the Eastern Extension, Australia and China Telegraph Company’s duplicate cable, over 1,800 nautical miles, between the Cocos Islands and Fremantle.

The cable, which is of the new loaded type and the first British owned and manufactured loaded cable to be put down, is designed for a speed of 2,100 letters a minute compared with 145 letters in the present line.

The question of further developing the lines between Fremantle and Adelaide is now being considered.

The "Colonia" is expected to arrive in the Cocos Islands in the middle of February and to complete the laying of the cable at Fremantle early in March.

## CHASING PIRATES

CANTON NAVY WANTS 100 LAUNCHES.

PROTECTION FOR RIVERS.

—

A report current is that the Canton naval authorities are preparing a plan to accord protection on the three rivers of the province and to clear same of pirates. The suggestion is said to concern the assembly of a hundred launches for patrol work. Such launches, it is stated, will be chartered at first.

At present there are not sufficient launches in Canton and the naval people are turning their attention elsewhere.

## WORLD COURT

OBSTRUCTION TACTICS IN SENATE.

"WASHINGTON'S FAREWELL."

(Reußen's American Service.)

### Washington, Jan. 15

The Senators who are opposed to the American adhesion to the World Court have begun to obstruct a resolution for adherence, keeping up a constant flow of oratory in the Senate, including Senator 'Please reading Washington's farewell speech' to his General's in which occurs the classical advice to avoid "entangling alliances."

Senator Blease, when bantered, told the Senators, most of whom knew the speech by heart, that he would not consider it discourteous if they proceeded with other affairs whilst he was reading.

MURDERS OF COMMUNIST OFFICIALS.

AUTHORITIES QUIESCENT.

(Reuter's Service.)

Riga, Jan. 15.

It is reported that much unrest prevails among the peasantry in various parts of Soviet Russia and particularly in Ukraine, White-Russia, where armed anti-Bolsheviks are active.

Murders of over-zealous. Communist officials are reported, chiefly in the Bobruisk district, where the militia and the local authorities are alleged to have refused to act against the rebels and are even co-operating with them.

TREATY BETWEEN SWEDEN AND DENMARK.

(Reuter's Service.)

Copenhagen, Jan. 15.

The new Swedo-Danish Arbitration Treaty has been concluded. It is similar in character to the recent Swedo-Norwegian Convention, and prohibits the employment of armed force for the settlement of disputes.

The Treaty will operate for twenty years, and be"renewable unless two years' notice is given.
