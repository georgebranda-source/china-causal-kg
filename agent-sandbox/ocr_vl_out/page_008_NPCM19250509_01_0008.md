## SAFETY FIRST?

Figures can be made to prove anything. The figures contained in the Colony's statement of assets and liabilities on December 31 last show balance of assets over liabilities to be $13,107,549.29. Proving prosperity? As far as figures can be made to do—yes! The Crown Agents hold $5,314,426.96—a large sum surely; whilst "perishable" assets include railway, L.W.D., and coal stores of over $800,000. If the Crown Agents Deposit Account bears interest on the sum mentioned, then that should comfortably pay the interest on the Government's overdraft at the Bank shown as $1,417,388.38. If not, then obviously it is not good finance. By now this overdraft will have been increased for the liabilities show $2,220,493.91, "Shipping Control Account" now donated to the Home Government for the Singapore Base Scheme. Investment Account is a simple sum of $8,972,105.70: Any criticism of the nature of this item is somewhat hindered by the fact the nominal value of the investments is £1,275,308, 3s. 8d.; the actual cost was £1,051,187.11s. 1½d.; whilst the market value is £1,142,631-18s-0d-But it does not escape notice that practically the whole of this money is invested in Colonial securities in which the highest interest earned is 5% and the lowest 3%. The difference between the actual cost and the market value of these investments seems remarkable;

but an examination of the figures proves that in only one instance is there a notable increase and that is in the value of the Funding Loan which has a market value of £749,412.10. 0d. against an actual cost of £662,991. 14s. 9½d. whilst the nominal value is placed at £835,000. 0s. 0d. Can these investments be said to meet the cry—"Safety First"?

## Homeless Boys

The promoters of a scheme for building on Stanley Peninsular an industrial home for poor Chinese children have completed most of the preliminary arrangements including the provision of a site by the Government and particulars of the scheme and of the attitude of the Government are given elsewhere in this issue. The main point that emerges is that it is essentially a private concern and that the institution must in no way be looked upon as a reformatory to which the bad boys of the Colony will be sent, although the adoption of such a course by a magistrate under suitable circumstances and with the permission of the management may come to be looked upon as an accepted thing. It will certainly be a help to the police who are faced with a difficult problem when it comes to dealing with homeless lads. They may be potential if not actual cargo pilferers or bag-snatchers but the mere fact of their homelessness is not sufficient to justify their incarceration in prison where if they not already entered upon such a life they are almost bound to be influenced by those with whom they come into contact. A school where they may be taught to become useful citizens will meet a considerable need and the sponsors of the scheme deserve the thanks of the public for their kindly forethought, also the Government for having done all in its power to make the task of preparation easy. Organisers of charitable schemes such as this should take note of the attitude adopted by the Government and be encouraged to come forward with concrete proposals such as were made in this instance for it is obviously to the advantage of the Colony that provision should be made for the homeless rather than that they should have to be shipped and re-shipped to their places of origin.

## Boxing Decision

(To the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir,—I must admit that “One of the Boys” talks sense even if he is misguided enough to claim that Cartlidge won on points or that the verdict of “draw” was fair to either of the contestants.

I also admit that a lot of what he says is information to me. He knows a good deal about boxing criticism even if he is "biased" and not "unbiased."

He says that Cartlidge told friends that he had won with the exception of three rounds. My contention is that Cartlidge had very little advantage at the end of the twelfth round and that Dupree went ahead and scored enough to give him the verdict.

I regret what I wrote about Cartlidge's record. On his record he should have put up a still better show and not left the decision in doubt. That is my point.

As regards holding, I must ask "One of the Boys" to find out from Jim what made him clasp Dupree's left with both gloves in the last round.

His orgy of queries I must.leave unanswered as only Cartlidge and Dupree can answer them properly.

Dupree can answer them properly.

Since it is not likely that a return contest will be put on, I gladly avail myself of your correspondent's acceptance. Mr. Editor, you have both our names and you will be doing us a favour by passing on the names. Open space or ring, I do not mind backing up my claim to a knowledge of the manly art but I plead "not guilty" to being judged by my pocket as I had nothing on the bout, not being able to get the price and amount I wanted.

### Hongkong, May 8. REFORMER

## British Income Tax Refunds

(To the Editor of the China Mail).

Sir.—For the benefit of any persons who may be unaware that they are entitled to recover a considerable sum of money should they derive any income from English registered companies, may I be permitted to point out the following salient facts:—

(1) Every person of British nationality is now entitled to recover either the whole or a proportion of any British Income Tax deducted at source from his dividends.

(2) This includes dividends which are stated to be paid “free of tax,” or even when no deduction for tax is shown on the dividend voucher in the case of a British registered company.

(3) No liability on income not derived from Great Britain can be incurred by putting in these claims.

(4) The amount recoverable varies from a small amount to hundreds of pounds, depending upon the amount and source of the income.

(5) These claims can be made in respect of income received from April 5, 1920 to date.

I shall be pleased to give further information regarding these claims to any of your readers in doubt if they will write me at 13, Buckingham Palace Gardens, London, S.W. England.

WILFRED T. FRY.
