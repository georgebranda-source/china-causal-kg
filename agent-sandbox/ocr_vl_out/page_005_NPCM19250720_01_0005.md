## MISSION WORK

TO-DAY'S MEETING

About 150 missionaries from the province of Kwang tung gathered at St. Paul's College Hostel this morning to review their position in view of recent developments in China as a result of which they were obliged to leave the sphere of their activities. Local missions were also represented.

The Press were not invited to the meeting but a "China Mail" representative who conversed after the meeting with its Chairman (Mr. G. H. McNeur, New Zealand Presbyterian Mission, Canton) was informed that no definite policy had been decided upon. The meeting had been called with the object of interchange of information as to the situation and its relation to the position of the chirebes represented.

"The aged for a united policy had been felt, said Dr. McNeur and there had been no distinction of denomination or nationality" in the representation at the meeting. The general feeling had been that the Chinese Christian churches were in a position now to stand by themselves if they so desired and if they wished missionary activity to cease the missionaries were prepared to leave.

A small committee had been authorised to go into the position, said Mr. McNeur; meanwhile there would be no question of sending missionaries home unless forlough was outstanding.

The Committee appointed with power to co-opt were Dr. Creighton off the True Light Middle School (American Presbyterian). Rev. W. W. Clavson (London Missionary Society, Canton) and the Rev. C. Sheep (United Brethren Mission, Canton).

## THE HARBOUR

STILL MORE SEAMEN AVAILABLE.

HOPEFUL SIGNS.

The s.s. Tung On. which arrived back from Kongnoon yesterday afternoon, sailed for Canton this morning with more supplies. Her trip to Kongnoon was uneventful except that she was aground for some hours on the Wangmun Bar and was not re-floated until H.M.S. Moorhen rendered assistance with the supply of a lighter for emptying the bunkers. Six European passengers were brought from Kongnoon.

Owing to the registration of numbers of seamen, the shipping situation has considerably improved and there will shortly be nothing to hinder ships with cargo to carry, moving from their anchorage. The Hai Ning, of the Douglas Steamship Company, has already done so and is now at her wharf taking on cargo, chiefly American Naval stores.

The harbour is gradually assuming a more normal appearance and many more launches are noticed to be in operation. Engineers and coxswains for the harbour launches have been engaged in considerable numbers and others have applied to the Police for employment. The War Department launches have maintained a service since the beginning of the strike and by an unfortunate coincidence there were mishaps to both this week-end. As a result of going aground at Green Island the Tommy Atkins sustained damage to her bows, part of the stem piece having been torn away. The Umphali has been refloated after having been aground for some days.

## MACHINE-GUNNERS' MEMORIAL

DUKE OF CONNAUGHT'S UNVEILING

The-Duke-of-Comnaught unveiled the Machine Gun Corps memorial in the open space between the Mall gates, and St. George's Hospital, Grosvenor-place, W. on May 10. The memorial, sculptured by Mr. F. Derwent Wood, R.A., shows the bronze figure of David, more than Sft. high, leaning on Goliath's sword. The figure stands on a base of Mazzano stone, and on each side are shown laurel-wreathed machine-guns.

The Duke chatted with four members of the corps who had won the Victoria Cross—Major J. R. N. Graham, Captain A. E. Kerr, Lieutenant W. A. White, and Corporal A. H. Cross—and inspected the guard of honour formed of former members of the M.G.C., now in the Royal Tank Corps.

The Duke recalled that 1,120 officers and 12,671 other ranks of the corps were killed in the war, 2,881 officers and 45,377 other ranks being wounded, missing, or taken prisoner. The casualties were more than a quarter of the total strength.

As the National Anthem was played, it was sung by the large crowd. Many women cried, and several relatives of dead members of the corps were helped into St. George's Hospital in a fainting condition.

Hundreds of men and women pressed forward to lay their wreaths besides the larger one sent by the corps, and the police had to link hands to regulate the movement.

## RIFF WAR

ATTACKS REPULSED WITH HEAVY LOSS.

AIR FORCE BUSY.

(Router's Service.)

Fez; July 19.

Marshal Petain has arrived and begun work with the General Staff.

Reports from Fez state that enemy pressure on the centre, northern front is palpably slackening.

New attacks at Ainnastouf and Ainaicha posts were repulsed with heavy loss. There were no French casualties.

Generally speaking, the various districts are not meeting with great difficulty.

The Air Force is very busy reprovisioning stations and compelling assailants to flee precipitously.

The first detachments of the Moroccan division from Rhineland have arrived at Oujda.

French Lose Posts.

A communique says the French have-lost posts at Babhosein, Wedbaumrin, after a violent Riff attack in which the Riffs lost heavily.

## Earlier Cable

Marshall Petain met Marshal Lyautey at Rabat and later had an audience with the Sultan of Morocco. M. Naulin, interviewed before going to Morocco applauded the decision to send Marshal Petain to Morocco, as his presence there would cheer the troops. M. Naulin said there were at present 125,000 troops in the Riff region and he believed this force was adequate to achieve the French aims. Reports in the French Press of Riff activities in the direction of Fez are confirmed by a message from Madrid which describes Violent-Riff attacks on Ainicha and incessant aeroplane bombardments of the enemy lines.

## TRADE TREATY

SOVIET AND GERMAN

NEGOTIATIONS.

(Reuter's Service.)

'Moscow, July 19.

The Ganetzky, head of the Soviet delegation which is negotiating the Russo-German trade agreement, interviewed by the Rosta News Agency, said the negotiations are now almost ended. Only a few questions are unsettled.

He declared the Soviet agreed to make important concessions but the German Government demanded further concessions while not making a single concession itself; but the appointment of Count Brockdorf Frantzau to represent Germany seemed to prove Germany's realised importance of immediate conclusion of the treaty.

He was sure Germany would soon make concessions and with goodwill the treaty might be signed in a few days.

## LISBON REVOLT

CRUISER DECLARES FOR REVOLUTIONARIES.

SURRENDER BEGINS.

(Router's Service.)

Lisbon, July 19.

A military revolt, headed by a number of officers who were arrested after the outbreak on April 18 and who escaped from the fortress of San Juliao, broke out early in the morning.

The cruiser Vasco da Gama de-

clared for the revolutionaries.

The Government took prompt measures to suppress the revolt, he leader of which is Captain Baptista. Four other officers have already surrendered and have been put in jail.

The submission of the Vasco da Gama is momentarily expected.

Other revolutionaries are encamped near Bélem but the movement is considered already to be suppressed. A large force of troops is precautionarily guarding the President's Palace. All is quiet in the city.

Later.

All revolutionaries surrendered this morning after much shooting. Martial law has been established all over the country. The Government threatened to bombard the Vasco Da Gama if it held out after which the cruiser surrendered unconditionally. In the afternoon the situation is otherwise normal.
