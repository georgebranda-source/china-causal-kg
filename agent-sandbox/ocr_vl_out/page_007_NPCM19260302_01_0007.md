## LEAGUE COUNCIL

SHEAF OF QUERIES IN THE COMMONS

DOMINIONS VIEWS?

(Router's Service.)

London, March 1

In the House of Commons the Marquess of Hartington (Conservative, West Derbyshire) asked whether communications had been received from the Dominions expressing their views on the proposed increase in the permanent membership of the League of Nations Council?

Mr. L. S. Amary, Secretary, of State for the Dominions, referred to Sir Austen Chamberlain's reply on February 17. He added that it was impossible to make a statement as to the nature of such communications which were of a confidential character.

Mr. J. H. Thomas (Labour, Derby) requested that the correspondence be submitted to the House before there was any debate.

Mr. Amery replied that he was not sure that he would be able to do so. He said that it would largely depend on the views of the Dominion Governments concerned.

Mr. Thomas asked if it was not essential that the House should know exactly the Dominion view-point before, the debate?

The anxiety of the members of the House of Commons on the League Council issue was reflected in questions by three Liberals and one Conservative asking whether a decision had been reached as regards the instructions to Sir Austen Chamberlain at Geneva and whether the Dominions would be consulted before Great Britain was committed to any change?

Mr. Stanley Baldwin (Prime Minister) in a joint reply requested the House to await a statement which he hoped to make later in the week, probably on Thursday.

Mr. J. Ramsay MacDonald (Labour leader) requested a statement on Wednesday.

The Prime Minister promised to his best.

Rugby, March 1.

Mr. Stanley Baldwin, Prime Minister, announced in the House of Commons that he hoped to be able to make a statement on Thursday, or perhaps Wednesday regarding the instructions to be given to the British delegates to the League of Nations Council on the question of the enlargement of the Council beyond the admission of Germany.

A number of questions was addressed to him on the subject, and he requested that these should be postponed until he was able to make his statement. If, on consideration, it should be thought desirable to have a brief discussion on that statement, the opportunity of doing so would be given.

## Sear Commission

The attention of Sir Austen Chamberlain, Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs, was called to the fact that the agenda of the League Council has been arranged so that the question of the Saar Commission will be brought up first before Germany has been admitted to the League.

Sir A. Chamberlain was asked whether the Government would endeavour to secure that a matter so closely concerning German interests should be postponed until Germany can be represented?

Sir A. Chamberlain replied that it was the usual practice of the Council to decide matters relating to its procedure, and he had been unable to anticipate the Council's decision on this occasion. As to obtaining a postponement of the Saar question he did not think that the interests of peace and international understanding were served by members of individual Governments saying what they would or would not do at a meeting of that great world Council—the Council of the League—and he did not see how an agreed decision could ever be reached if any Government announced its policy beforehand and was unwilling to make concessions.

## WAR DEBTS

FRENCH MINISTER FOR LONDON.

LAST YEAR'S BARGAIN

(British Wireless Service.)

Rugby, March 1

It is expected that the French Minister of Finance, M. Doumer, will come to London next week to take up the negotiations with the Chapcélor of the Exchequer, Mr Winston Churchill, regarding France's war debt to Great Britain.

The provisional agreement reached in last year's discussion between M. Caillaux (then French Minister of Finance) and Mr. Winston Churchill was on the basis of an average annual payment of £12,000,000.

Replying to a question on the French debt in the House of Commons last Thursday Mr. Winston Churchill said that the British Government regarded the bargain made with M. Caillaux as binding on both parties.

## INDIA'S BUDGET

REVENUE SLIGHTLY HIGHER THAN EXPENDITURE.

NO FRESH TAXATION.

The Budget was presented in the Assembly today. It shows that the revenue for the past financial year was 131.35 crores of rupees and the expenditure 130.05 crores of rupees.

The Estimates for next year show the revenue to be 133.43 crores and the expenditure 130.88 crores.

There will be no fresh taxation. The repeal of the cotton excise duty would cost the Government 175 lakhs (a lakh equals 1,00,000 rupees).

## WAR GRAVES

EXPENSES OF THE IMPERIAL

COMMISSION.

FIVE MILLIONS WANTED

In the House of Commons, to-day, Mr. Ronald F. McNeill, Financial Secretary to the Treasury, in moving a vote of £48,700 for salaries and expenses of the Imperial War Graves Commission, explained that last Summer all the Governments of the British Empire, including that of British India, had agreed to accumulate a fund until it should reach a capital sum which would provide in perpetuity an income sufficient to maintain the British war cemeteries in the different parts of the world in the manner that the nation desired that they should be £5,000,000, and it was estimated that the annual sum necessary would be from £200,000 to £250,000. It was in order to provide this country's first contribution that the Government asked the House to vote £50,000, less some small savings, for the purpose this year, and to vote the same sum for the next two years.

It was hoped that, at the end of 15 years, by the accumulation of the interest on these grants, with the contributions which would come from the Dominion Governments, the sum of £5,000,000 would be obtained.

The vote was adopted.
