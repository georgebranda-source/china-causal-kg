##### MR. D. K. BLAIR'S DEATH

His War, Strike And Sport Work.

The death is announced in a brief cable which gives no details. of Mr. D. K. Blair, late Secretary of the Hongkong Chamber of Commerce. Death took place yesterday.

LATE MR. D.K. BLAIR.

<div style="text-align: center;"><img src="imgs/img_in_image_box_771_1279_1547_2314.jpg" alt="Image" width="21%" /></div>


A studio portrait of Mr. D. K. Blair, taken shortly before his departure for Home, after many years' residence in the Colony

Whilst it will be a matter of grief to his many friends in Hong-

kong, to those who saw Mr. Blair at Home in the summer of last

year, the news will not come as a great shock.

Mr. Blair left Hongkong early in April, 1924. He had been in indifferent health and it was on medical advice that he left for Home.

He had served the Colony for many years and in different spheres and his judgment, discretion and long acquaintance with local conditions enabled him to fill with credit the responsible post he held when he left the Colony.

For his excellent work on behalf of the Colony, the late Mr. Blair was presented by the Government with a silver salver engraved with the Colony's emblem "as an indication of the appreciation of the Government of Hongkong of the services you have so willingly given on various occasions."

A sum of money was also voted to Mr. Blair by the Chamber of Commerce in order that he might purchase some memento as a mark of appreciation of his services.

When the Peak Hospital was handed over to the Government, Mr. Blair was appointed honorary secretary and treasurer. Mr. Blair reorganised the Hospital with such success that the Government was moved to express its warmest thanks "for" the very creditable manner" in which he "voluntarily carried out the arduous work." He also received a letter from the Colonial Treasurer expressing his sincere gratitude for all he had done, "in putting the Peak Hospital on a satisfactory basis both as regards efficiency and business matters."

Apart from his official work, Mr. Blair was best known as the Honorary Secretary of the St. Andrew's Society. The following tribute was paid to him by that Society on the occasion of his departure:—“As Secretary of the Society, you have placed the organisation at a high water mark of efficiency, and your personal interest and able management have been outstanding features in our Scottish celebrations.” Mr. Blair was a keen mason, being deputy master of Lodge Eastern Scotia, Number 923, of which he was also for many years a past master. His impending departure brought him a letter of sincere appreciation for his “many services and unequalled generosity;” and the lodge also presented him with suitably inscribed emblems of the craft as a souvenir.



During his residence in Hongkong, Mr. Blair took the keenest interest in yachting and together with Mr. Frank Smyth his name will long be remembered as prominently associated with the progress of the Royal Hongkong Yacht Club. For many years Mr. Blair was Sailing Secretary and he did splendid work in connection with the Club's annual regattas. It was in recognition of his hard work as an official of that Club that he was made Vice-Commodore and later Commodore, which position he held at the time of his departure from the Colony.

Before he left, Mr. Blair was the recipient of a handsome solid silver cigar and cigarette box, the gift of prominent members of the Yacht Club whose signatures were inscribed on it.

Mr. Blair was a Justice of the Peace for Hongkong

Mr. Blair was a native of Irvine, Ayrshire. He first came to the East in 1889 and was originally in the service of the Eastern Extension Telegraph Company serving Shanghai and North China as well as Hongkong. During the Boxer rebellion he was in the North and helped to maintain telegraph services at Tientsin and Taku forts during those hazardous times.

The late Mr. Blair came to Hongkong as Assistant Secretary of the Hongkong General Chamber of Commerce. Having acted as Secretary on numerous occasions, he was appointed Secretary in 1921.

During the Seamen's Strike, tribute was paid by the Government to Mr. Blair's excellent work "which had been of the greatest assistance" in conducting to the smooth running of the services.

Another branch of his earlier activities was that in connection with the raising of a Signalling Section of the Hongkong Volunteer Defence Corps during the War when he trained many residents in the art of telegraphic signalling. He remained with his section until it was desbanded.

Much sympathy will be felt with the late Mr. Blair's family.
