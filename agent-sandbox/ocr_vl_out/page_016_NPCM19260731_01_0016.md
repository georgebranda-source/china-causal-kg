### $8,000.000 DAMAGE

## EFFECT OF HURRICANE IN BAHAMAS

In the Bahamas, Nassau bore the brunt of the hurricane. The damage is estimated at least at $8,000,000.

Many families are homeless and, beautiful drives are sittered with broken glass and masses of debris. Nevertheless, the inhabitants eagerly joined in the search for the picturesque sponge fleet, consisting of 200 small, sleops and schooners, among which most of the 1500 missing persons were believed to have been drowned. There were sixty fishing smacks arrayed in the harbour before the storm. Only three survived.

The other islands suffered considerably, in one instance eight rum-running boats being hurled on the beach.

One ship was smashed to atoms. The crew having previously rowed as here, there were no casualties.

� Resolution and death engulfed what only last week was a favourite pleasure ground for wealthy Americans.

The blow fell toward midnight last Sunday. The hurricane approached from the north-east and struck Providence Isle, rooting up trees and shattering the Power Station. The island was plunged in darkness.

Six hours later the storm reached a velocity of 130 miles per hour, the harbour being churned into a boiling rage. Gigantic waves swept over the land, wrecking destruction and annihilating shipping.

The Government mail boats Brontee and Albertine with crews of 20 and 40, respectively, are missing.

Nassau was damaged.

Vessels are stranded 70 feet above the water level. One steamer is wedged in a liquor warehouse.

A miraculous escape was effected from a pleasure boat which was torn from its moorings and swept out to sea, where it rammed a barge. The crew, and passengers' all leapt into the boiling waters and reached the shore. Reuter.

## AERIAL PROGRESS

FIRST LONG DISTANCE CRUISE

To EGYPT AND BACK

Rugby, July 30.

The first long distance foreign cruise by Royal Air Force flying bouts was completed to day at Plymouth when two per-marine Southampton aircraft under the command of Saitdron leader Livock returned from a round flight to Egypt and Cyprus.

The flight was undertaken as ordinary service, exercise to a set time table, a crew of four and a full service, load being carried by each boat.

The total length of the flight was approximately 6,000 sea miles equivalent to nearly 7,000 land miles.

Starting from Plymouth on July 1 the route followed on the outward journey was via Bordeaux, Marseilles, Naples, and Malta to Benghazi, on the North African coast, and thence to Aboukir. From Aboukir the flying boats made a flight by way of Haifa to Eramagusta, in Cyprus, and back.

On the return journey a different route was followed during the earlier stages. Leaving Aboukir on July 18 the aircraft proceeded to Sollum and thence to Athens, a halt being made at Suda Bay, in Crete, for fuel. Corfu was reached on July 21. Thence the final stages were as on the outward journey via Malta, Marseilles, and Bordeaux.

## No Hitch

No trouble, whatsoever was experienced either with the aircraft or with the Napier Lion engines, with which these machines are fitted. The aircraft are equipped with wireless apparatus and constant communication was maintained throughout with the Royal Air Force and other wireless stations.

The machines were moored out to buoys at each port of call, one member of the crew remaining on board each night for watch-keeping purposes. The aircraft were re-fuelled by petrol taken on board by dinghies.

The result of this cruise clearly demonstrates the feasibility of composing a time table and programme, and adhering to it throughout without being impeded by conditions of weather, unless extreme. It also proves that these flying boats are perfectly capable of long distance flights independently of their bases or of their parent ships.—British Wireless Service.

## TRAIN CRASH

TWENTY CASUALTIES IN FRANCE.

Paris, July 30.

A mail train, going at run speed, was derailed between Noisy le Sec and Rosny.

Four were killed and 16 injured.

--Reuter.

The mail train took the points at seventy-five instead of the regulation thirty-five kilometres per hour for trains exclusively composed of mail vans.

The casualties were among postal employees.—Reuter.

## RUBBER

QUANFERLY AVERAGE

The "Rubber Trade Association"s official quarterly average is 21,000 pence. The market has now closed until Tuesday.—Router.

The Colonial Office announces that the percentage of rubber exportable at the minimum rate of duty from Ceylon and Malaya for the quarter beginning August 1 will be 100.—Reuter.

It is officially announced that 100 per cent. of the standard production of rubber will be maintained as the quota which may be exported at the minimum rate of duty from Ceylon and Malaya during the quarter beginning August 1.

Considerable doubt had been entertained in the rubber market whether this quota would be maintained in view of the fall in the price of spot rubber.

The average price for the quarter ending to-day is a very small fraction of a penny over 1.9. Under the Stevenson restriction schemes the export quota would have been reduced to 80 per cent. if the average price had been below 1/9.—British Wireless Service.
