# LOCAL AND GENERAL.

Two of the public bathing beaches, those at Stonecutter's and Kennedy Town, are to be closed at the end of the present month. Launches will not be available to these beaches after Wednesday evening. September 30, and the matsheds will be dismantled. Other public beaches remain open.

At a dinner given at Wellington to delegates of the Empire Press Union, who are visiting New Zealand, Sir Harry Brittain mentioned the increasing fact that New Zealand's 1,250,000 population did more trade with Great Britain than the 400,000,000 of China. This must not however, be taken as underrating the importance of certain branches of China trade to England and of China as a field for British manufactures.

Malaya and Hongkong "are both represented in a large Empire's delegation which is visiting Hull to see for themselves how comprehensive are the quays for handling fruit, the warehouses for storing grain and meat, the sheds for holding wool, and how expeditiously, with the aid of the London and North-Eastern Railway, can large cargoes be unloaded and transported over the greater part of northern England.

At the annual meeting at Auckland on August 4 of the New Zealand Insurance Company, whose local agents are Messrs. Holyoak, Massey and Co., the shareholders were informed that the directors felt justified in recommending an increase of the rate of dividend, and they accordingly recommended that a sum of £118,750 should be distributed by way of dividend for the year, being equivalent to a dividend at the rate of 1/7 per share. In order to meet this increase in dividend it would not be necessary to draw on the underwriting profits for more than £7,154, as the income derived from investments was only short of the dividend by that amount.

The case against a Chinese revenue officer, who is charged with attempting to obtain by threats a sum of $100 from a distiller, was continued before the Kowloon Magistrate and again adjourned. A scheme was alleged to have been suggested to the distiller by which he could defraud the Revenue Department.

That there is an intense desire on the part of Asiatics to acquire a knowledge of English is proved by the numerous schools in Singapore, and the latest candidate in the field is the newly established institution at Serangoon Road, with the euphonious title, "The Practical Double-Rate English School, where English and Chinese classes are held every evening."—"S. T."

Arrangements have now been completed for the opening of the trooping season at Southampton. Six vessels are preparing for a busy time, which will last until the spring. The “Derbyshire” will make the first sailing. She was to leave Southampton on September 2 for Bermuda and Jamaica. Returning to Bermuda she will make for Malta and Port Said, and then on to Colombo, “Hongkong, and other Far Eastern ports. On her homeward passage she will call at Bombay, Aden, Port Sudan, and Gibraltar, and the voyage lasting over four months will end with her arrival at Plymouth on January 11. During the coming winter the artillery noives to India will be made. When the 13th Heavy Battery leaves Hongkong it will go to Karachi.

Since the recent unpleasant incidents due to the appearance of sharks and crocodiles at favourite bathing resorts, the increase in the number of bathing pagars at Port Dickson is very noticeable. Nowadays scarcely any bathers are seen in the open sea. A very striking addition to these pagars is that adjoining the fine new bungalow recently erected at the fifth mile on the Coast Road, by a well-known Pasir Panjang towkay.

Among interesting items referred to in an article in the "Ice Cream Trade Journal" are the following: If the ice cream consumed in the United States in one year were put into one cone, it would tower over the Washington Monument; that more than 700,000 cows supply the cream, and that most of the eggs used in the trade come from China. All of which is highly interesting; comments the "N. C. Daily News," but are we to draw the conclusion from these latter facts that honest-to-goodness cream and eggs really enter into the composition of everything sold as ice cream? Alas, we wish we could believe it.

The cruiser “Diomede,” Captain C. E. Kennedy-Purvis, arrived at Portsmouth on August 29 from China, where she has been serving since her completion in 1922. The vessel was commissioned for the first time for service on October 2, 1922, so that by the time she is paid off her term will have extended to nearly three years instead of the normal two-and-a-half. The place of the “Diomede” has temporarily been taken by the “Concord,” Captain A. R. W. Woods, D.S.O., from the Mediterranean, until such time as the large cruiser “Vindictive,” Captain G. F. Hyde, R.A.N., arrives on the station. The “Diomede” will be transferred to the New Zealand Government, which a year ago offered to maintain a second cruiser of the same type as the “Dunedin,” already out there.

# SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

Joseph Conrad is the inspiration for the new library and reading room in the annex to the Seamen's Church institute at 25 South Street, New York. The memorial room will cost approximately $100,000 (G.) It is planned to make it a shrine for all Conrad lovers.

Mr. F. J. Benton, formerly of the E. E. A. and C. Telegraph Co., Ltd., in Singapore, is now settled in Sydney and is a director of the Rural Estate and Development Co., Ltd., and the Federal Building Loan Co., Ltd. He writes to say that if any Straits men require information regarding real estate in Australia he will be pleased to answer letters addressed to the offices of the companies, 251, George Street, Sydney.

Three men who in the last few years have been unofficial advisors to Herbert Hoover, Secretary of Commerce, and the Administration at Washington regarding Russian affairs, are going again to Soviet Russia. They are James P. Goodrich, formerly Governor of Indiana; William N. Haskell and Dr. F. A. Golder of Stanford University. Colonel Haskell, who directed the American relief administration in Russia, already has sailed from New York.

Mr. A. H. F. Edwards, who was Commissioner of Customs, at Canton at the time of the attack on Shameen, and was shot in the knee, has been undergoing medical treatment at home. It was found inadvisable to extract the bullet which was in the centre of the bone, but success was attained in the difficult and painful process of joining up the severed portions of a tendon which had been shot away. Mr. Edwards is shortly returning to the Far East via Siberia together with Mrs. Edwards, but his future sphere of activities is not yet known.

A wedding of especial interest to the younger set was solemnized at St. Joseph's Church, Shanghai, when Miss Marguerite Tahaney, of San Francisco, became the bride of Mr. Lewis Jackson Peoples, of Townsville, North Carolina, who is Han River manager of the British Cigarette Company. The bride was given away by Mr. Otis D. Terell, and Mrs. C. F. Wolsiffer attended as matron of honour, while Miss Ramona Gray acted as brides-maid.

The Very Rev. Dean Symons has been elected an honorary life member of the Shanghai Football Association. Dean Symons has always been a keen patron of sport, and on most Saturday afternoons is to be found at the Race Course, watching the games.



One of the leading exponents of advertising in Great Britain, Sir Charles Frederick Higham, who has many friends in America and managed the huge India Tea campaigns there, was married to Miss Eloise Rowe Ellis in London. Sir Charles is 49 and was Coalition member of Parliament from South Islington from December, 1918, to October, 1922.

An address by King Boris was a feature of the fiftieth anniversary celebration recently of the Aprilov Gymnasia, the first institution of higher learning established in Bulgaria. The King, who spoke for half an hour, devoted his remarks to the significance of the event as evidence of the beginning of Bulgarian culture.

The engagement is announced between James Geoffrey Hart, only son of the late Mr. James Hart, of the Chinese Maritime Customs (brother of the late Sir Robert Hart), and Mrs. Arnold Ellert, of Inverness Terrace, Hyde Park, and Elizabeth Marie Christine; only daughter of Count and Countess Carlo Zanadi Landi, of 20, St. John's Wood Park, and Piacenza, Italy.

A movement has been started in England to raise a fund for Mille. Aimée Dostoievsky, the daughter of the Russian writer Mille. Dostoievsky, left penniless by the Russian Revolution, has been ill for months, and in a letter to the London Times recently Hugh Walpole wrote: The smallest sums contributed to this fund, addressed "Dostoievski Fund," c/o Comptoir d'Escompte de Genève, Rue du Simplon, Vevey, will be thankfully received and acknowledged. I think that everyone who has known some of the finest and noblest moments of inspiration when reading "The Brothers Karamazov," "Crime and Punishment" and the other masterpieces will wish to help the daughter of so great a genius. If is the only fashion in which we can repay something of our debt. Dostoevski never received a cent for any translations of his books, as Russia was not in the Copyright Convention.

Mr. J. H. Smith, chief officer of one of the Ho Hong Steamship Co.'s boats, of Singapore, created what must have been a record recently. He left Singapore on August 31 returning on September 17, having in the meantime travelled to Hongkong, where he underwent the necessary examinations and returned to Singapore a master mariner.



Sheila Kaye-Smith has reached the first edition pinnacle, the goal of novelists. A recent list of wanted firsts issued by a well-known London bookseller includes Conrad and Moore as leaders with Gissing third. The other writers in demand are W. H. Hudson, E. M. Forster, James Stephens, H. M. Tomlinson, Norman Douglas, Lytton Strachey and D. H. Lawrence.

Princess Mary and Lord Lascelles may visit shortly the latter's Irish estate, Portumna, in Galway, one of the most beautiful spots on the West of Ireland. Ever since her marriage her Royal Highness has wanted to see Portumna Castle, though until now times have been unproprious. Now the long anticipated visit may take place at no distant date. The vast estate Lord Lascelles inherited, together with a big "cash" fortune, from his eccentric great uncle the last Marquis of Clanricarde. Some of the Irish property has been sold, and Portumna Castle wants practically rebuilding.

A copy of the Gutenberg Bible has recently been purchased in Austria by Edward Golston, a London bookseller, for a sum in excess of £10,000. It is in two volumes quarto and is said to be a perfect specimen of the early Gutenberg texts which were printed in 1455-56. Upon Mr. Golston's arrival in London he announced that the Austrian authorities objected to the books going out of the country, but he managed to rush them through as soon as the purchase had been completed. It was first reported that the treasure was in Cologne, but when Mr. Golston arrived there he received word that it was to be found in a town "twenty hours distant." His next stop was Vienna, and the books were soon located. It was then necessary to make a return trip to London to complete the arrangements for the large sum involved. There are only six other copies of the book extant.
