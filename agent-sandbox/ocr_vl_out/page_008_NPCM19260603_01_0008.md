# LOCAL AND GENERAL

$1 per Copy

5, Wyndham Street,

Hongkong.

On Tuesday, no case of notifiable disease was reported to the Medical Officer of Health.



Canton's largest public park, called the First Public Park, has been officially renamed the Chung Yeung, or "Middle Empire's park.

With buildings costing over $100,000, a college has been opened at Sunning city. The mayor of Kongmoon attended the inaugural ceremony.

Two Chinese were remanded for one week at the Magistracy yesterday on a charge of using two printing presses for which no registration had been obtained.

On a charge of damage to vacant land opposite King's Building in Nathan Road by removing soil, a contractor was fined $4 at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday.

A Shantung constable, which was caught sleeping "whilst on duty was fined $15" at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday. Defendant had been fined departmentally on "three previous occasions".

Owing to the illness of Mr. M. K. Lo, who represents Mr. and Mrs. J. S. Gill in the summons arising out of the Star Theatre incident, further hearing of the case has been adjourned until tomorrow afternoon.

The case in which a probationary interpreter of the Sanitary Department is being charged with obtaining money by false preferences has been adjourned for hearing at the Kowloon Magistracy on Monday afternoon.

In an armed, robbery at Yaumati yesterday morning, a Chinese amah was struck on the head with a chopper for raising an alarm. The robbers escaped with a pair of bangles. Another gang armed with choppers visited a house at Kowloon City in the afternoon and got away with property to the value of $86.

Tientsin, May 20.—A general strike of merchants here is expected because the Chihli and Shantung authorities, despite their promises, do not meet the requirements of conversion of the Mukden military notes. It is reported that Mr. Pan Fu will be appointed Governor of Chihli Province in order to adjust the situation.

The Water Police received a report yesterday that a sumpan belonging to the Netherlands Harbour Works, North Point, had drifted away between Tuesday night, and yesterday morning. It has not yet been recovered.

Tokyo, May 24.—It has been decided that the sum of Y.2,500,000; the balance of the bounty donated by the Imperial Household for the relief of sufferers of the earthquake disaster of 1923, will be applied to the fund to be utilised for social welfare in the earthquake stricken area.

The number of snipe and golden plover who meet their nirvana by coming in contact with the wireless aerials on the Tawuu Golf Course, North Borneo, has struck a correspondent, who adds that for months they have not been seen by daylight, though the shot guns of the nimods are certainly less effective than the wireless aerials.

At the end of May Lieut.-Col. C. H. Harington, who joined the British North Borneo Company's service in 1896, with Mrs. Harington and their children, left for England. Though Col. Hardington has completed 30 years service in Borneo it does not appear to be certain whether he is yet retiring. He will be relieved at Jesselton by Major H. S. Bond. Captain W. Cole-Adams goes round to Sandakan to take over Major Bond's duties and Captain Rice-Orley goes to Jesselton as acting. Adjuntant. Mr. P. A. Marriott has gone to Sandakan to relieve him. Mr. S. G. Holmes, Assistant Commissioner of Customs, has gone Home on leave.

Estates on the east coast of Sumatra have experienced considerable damage, done by elephants. Recently a batch of 18 or 20 elephants invaded Tandiong Poetri estate located on the slopes of a batak mountain and totally destroyed large areas planted up with tea. The manager of the estate mustered his force of coolies and, summoning necessary help from a nearby village, drove the herd of elephants off the property.

Passengers departing for Europe yesterday by s.s. "Antenor" included Mr. G. H. Stitt, the retiring manager of the Shanghai branch of the Hongkong and Shanghai Bank, Professor Hinton of the H.K. University, Mr. and Mrs. O. H. Blackburn, Mr. and Mrs. H. Bailey, Mr. and Mrs. J. W. Humphries, the Rt. Revd. Bishop Trollope, and Mrs. A. and Miss Pearl Ogilvie.

Weltevreden, May 25.—The police have raided a Chinese hotel in Batavia where they arrested twenty Chinese who appeared to be leaders of the Extremist Kuomintang Java Branch, many of whom had travelled from afar to attend the Communistic conference. It appears that the agitators are in regular communication with Kuomintang headquarters at Canton.

It is well known to the leaders of the Eurasian Community of Penang and to a few connected with Volunteering in that island that a good deal of anxiety is felt at headquarters of the Penang and Province Wellesley Volunteer Corps and by the members of the Advisory Committee in regard to the dwindling in the number of members of the Eurasian Volunteer Company and the lack of keenness in attendance at parades.

A special Masonic Service was held on Sunday evening, May 23, at the Union Church, Shanghai. The service, which was conducted by the Rev. John Darroch, D. LITT., O.B.E., District Grand Lodge Chaplain, was attended by some 50 Masons of the Shanghai lodges.

It is stated that the present mayor of Kongmoon, Mr. Cheung Foo-man, will be substituted shortly by Mr. Cheung To-yat, former magistrate of Sanwui.

Mr. Sun Fo has been named acting chairman of the Kuomintang Provincial Council, vice Ko Ying-fan, who is on a leave of absence because of the death of his father.

Bombay, May 12. — The Governor or Bombay has directed that Government servants should not accept presidents or other similar posts involving executive control of institutions which are meant for the benefit of one particular section of the community.

# SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

Yokohama, May 28.—The Yokohama Gendarmerie are investigating the behaviour of A. F. Anderson, an American, who is alleged to have taken photographs in the strategical zone whilst touring the South Sea Islands, now under the mandate of Japanese Government.

Washington, May 26.—The Andrews scheme to appoint “dollar-a-year” men to enforce prohibition is believed a ready solution. Andrews declared he intended to teach the plan in California first, but California prohibition officials have been objecting strenuously.

Washington, May 26.—It is understood that South American Governments generally intend to assign a special police guard...to American Embassies and Legations to prevent bombings similar to that at Buenos Aires which is believed part of a radical programme of terrorism in revenge for the conviction of Sacco and Vanzetti by the Massachusetts courts.

The Governor of San Sebastian, Spain, is conducting a vigorous campaign against kissing, which he considers indecent. Kissing both in private and in public is prohibited, and the majesty of the law in Spain now has the task of blotting out this moral cancer.

According to the native press, the construction of the motor road between Hankow and Ichang, known as the Han Motor Road, will commence shortly. It is understood that Chinese merchants have raised a capital of over $500,000 for the organisation of a motor service between the two leading Hupeh cities.

Tokyo, May 24.—A despatch from Rome, dated May 22, reports that the Aviation Bureau of the Italian Government has a plan to include in the estimates for the next fiscal year seven hundred million hiras for aviation. The money will be expended in the construction of 182 military aeroplanes and 6 airships to be equipped with 182 officers and 24,000 privates.

San Diego, May 26.—Three amphibian planes departed on an Alaskan mapping trip designed to plan mail routes and study defence possibilities of the Aleutian Islands.

The engagement is announced of Dr. A. B. China Doray, of the Jchore Medical Service, and Dr. (Miss) L. N. Henry, of the F.M.S. Medical Service, now stationed at General Hospital, Seremban.

Harbin will shortly have a "very fine Confucius Temple which is to cost 200,000 dollars. Of this amount 125,000 dollars have been promised by the Chinese population and the remaining amount is being asked for from foreigners. It is understood that foreign houses have already subscribed over 80,000.

Mr. A. H. Preece, senior partner of Preece, Cardew and Rider, the electrical consulting engineers for the Singapore Municipality in connection with the new St. James's power station, left for home by the "Kashgar," after a short visit of inspection of the works. He will break the journey at Ceylon for a few weeks and then proceed home.

Washington, April 20.—Associate Justice Johnson of the Philippine Supreme Court visited the Senate insular committee to day during hearings on the Philippine auditor bill. Mr. Justice Johnson is touring the United States in an automobile with his wife. He arrived here from Michigan, en route to New York. Mr. and Mrs. Johnson expect to drive to the Pacific Coast in July, and board a ship for home.

Joseph L. Travis, or "Joe" Travis as he was known to his friends in Manila, died in Miami, Florida, on April 16, according to advices received from Mr. E. F. O'Brien, former editor of the old "Manila Freedom." Mr. Travis will be remembered by many old-timers in Manila, as he spent several years there. He was first in the employ of the "Manila Freedom" and later with the "Sunday Sun" of which Mr. O'Brien was also editor, Mr. Travis left the Philippines about 1909, and spent some years in India and the Strait Settlements. At the outbreak of the war in 1914 he was in Singapore, where he was conducting a street car and outdoor advertising business.

Mr. and Mrs. Douglas Robertson, who left Bangkok for Home on May 16, have had to fairly "eat their way out of Bangkok" at farewell dinners.



The "Pinang Gazette" records the death of Mr. J. S. Shearer at Greenwich. The late Mr. Shearer was a very well known resident of Malaysia. He was formerly chief engineer on a vessel operating between Singapore and Penang, and was afterwards employed by the A.P.C. being in charge of their installation at Butterworth.

Peking, May 20. — General Chang-Chih-chiang and Lu Chung-lin, leaders of the Kuomin-chun troops, who have been silent since the withdrawal of their troops from Peking, have jointly issued a circular telegram declaring that they would not interfere in politics in future and that they would devote themselves to the maintenance of peace and order within their sphere of influence.

Mr. S. S. Dickson, Secretary to the American Legation at Bangkok, leaves early this month for Europe. He will stay over in Egypt for a brief spell, will spend some time in France and then cross the Channel to the United Kingdom. After a holiday there, Mr. Dickson will proceed to America. He will not be returning to Siam, having been appointed to the Foreign Office at Washington.

London, April 15.—Mr. John Barrymore, the American actor, has signed a life-contract to appear in films. The term "life contract" is understood to mean that the producing company guarantees to take films from Mr. Barrymore for as many years as he cares to act in them. The contract was completed by Mr. Joseph M. Schenck, the multimillionaire film magnate of Hollywood, on behalf of United Artists' Corporation, where players are remunerated on a "sharing terms" basis. Assuming that Mr. Barrymore makes only two pictures a year, his annual income from this source will be not less than $250,000. His recent film-earnings have averaged $280,000 a year.
