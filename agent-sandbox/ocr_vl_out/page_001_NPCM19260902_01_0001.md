## PEAK WATER

ENGINES KEEP PACE WITH DEMAND.

A few weeks ago, it will be remembered, restriction in hours of water supply service to the Peak were removed, the Water Authority notifying that if the consumption was reduced to something like 75 per cent of what it was in the normal course, such full supply could be continued; otherwise that it might be necessary to cut off supplies without warning, if there was a danger of the tanks being seriously depleted.

Upon enquiry this morning, the "China Mail" was informed that the two engines in operation had been able to keep pace with the demand and it was hoped—providing consumers continued to eliminate waste—to continue the service.

The Peak and higher levels supply throughout the winter will be dependent on the two engines in question and if either went out of action, restriction would have to be continued. The main engine broken up by the boulder crash would be repaired before the hot season next year.

## ISLAND OF KISSES

“UNCHINESE” VILLAIN

"Made up" as a Chinese villain, one of the screen actors in the George Jacoby Co. of Germany, now in Hongkong, was the hero in the eyes of the crowds of Chinese who gathered in Queen's Road Central this morning to witness the unusual sight of taking a film here.

Not many props were in use and not many "of the spectators understood what "out on location" meant" till they spotted the two machines in the distance.

A motor 'car with 'public vehicle' on the licence was drawn up outside a 'goldsmith's establishment and a street ricsha was standing near.

Herine "Lost" Here.

As explained in Tuesday's "China Mail," Mr. George Jacoby (who "directed, "Qno Vadia!") is making two pictures for Universal Pictures Corporation, "with scenes in Hongkong and Shanghai. They are "Island of Kisses" and "The Woman Without a Name."

In the “Island of Kisses,” the heroine becomes “lost” and is discovered in Hongkong.

This probably accounted for the fact that when a "Mail" reporter reached the spot there, was no lady there ready to "do her stuff."

The location is that part of Queen's Road, in the Chinese business centre, just beyond the old fire station.

What the Stars were Doing.

High cheekbones, “villainous eyes” and prominent, protruding teeth a la Lon Chaney were the principal features in the make-up of the man in the car, with the Chinese chauffeur in his seat behind the wheel.

"Shooting" had not actually begun and the actor—unlike any Chinese—was listening to an exhortation by another member of the company.

Another “star” sat nonchalantly in the ricsha awaiting developments. He gave the appearance of an ordinary non-Chinese foreman in workaday clothes.

A gentleman in shirtsleeves, apparently directing operations, was examining the inside of the car.

Just where the China New Year bazaar "begins" the two camera men were posted with their machines on the tripods.

Around them clustered two hundred Chinese, one of whom was heard to remark: Oh! why hang about when these P.W.D. surveyors are taking measurements every day.

This sort of chatter was general but there were not a few who understood what the business was about.

## MISSION HOSPITALS

## CANTON ORDERS INSTANT RETURN

TO PROTECT FOREIGNERS.

After a year or so of interference with foreign missionary institutions in Canton and elsewhere, an unexpected announcement has been made by the Nationalist Government which indicates that rights will be recognised.

One sequel to the “strike” has been the closing down of mission hospitals on account of intimidation by the pickets.

The communique to hand also refers to foreign buildings occupied by the military.

It will be recalled that statements have been made previously—not necessarily by any “inspired” body—that the control of certain institutions, by Chinese, would be aimed at.

The Canton Information Bureau issues the following through Reuter:—

Canton, Sept. 1...

## KWONG HING CASE

## WERE THEY AGENTS OR PRINCIPALS?

JUNK HEARING AGAIN

The question as to whether the Kwong Hangs acted as agents or principals in the shipment of goods to Mui Kong San Wui (Kwangtung) "which did not arrive at their destination owing, it is alleged, to a piracy, came before Mr. " Justice Wood again in the Summary Court this morning.

The Ho Yuen firm claims $996.36 as the price of goods entrusted to defendant and monies on account of freight customs dues and coolie hire in respect of the shipment which the defendants failed to deliver or return to plaintiffs.

Mr. M. K. Lo, on behalf of the defendants, maintains that they acted throughout as... agents and not charterers.

Mr. J. A. Gordon Leask appeared for plaintiffs this morning, notifying a change of solicitors since the previous hearing.

So Chun, partner of the plaintiff firm, was cross-examined by Mr. Lo this morning. He maintained that the Kwong Hing had given an undertaking in respect of the safe delivery of the goods. The plaintiff firm, he said, had paid to the defendants 20 per cent. increase on the Customs, dues for guarantee of safe arrival in view of unsettled conditions caused by the boycott.

Plaintiff further stated that all the arrangements with regard to the taking on board of the cargo had been directed by the Kwong Hing firm although the junk owners had done a certain amount on their instructions

Mr Lo referred to the evidence which plaintiff had given in a case at the Supreme Court concerning another shipment of goods on the same junk when it had been held by the Chief Justice that not sufficient evidence had been made out for him to hold that a piracy had taken place.

Mr. Lo referred to what he termed "inconsistencies" in the plaintiff's evidence at that hearing and his evidence now.

His Honour said that he did not agree. There were no materials inconsistencies.

After hearing further evidence, His Honour found that the Kwong Hing firm (defendants) had only acted as agents in collecting goods. He gave judgment for defendants.

## BAD BEHAVIOUR

## HOTEL AND CHARGE ROOM INCIDENTS

EUROPEAN FINED.

Charges of disorderly conduct and breaking a door handle were preferred against a ship's captain, E. Bentley, at present unemployed, before Major C. Willson at the Central Magistracy this morning.

Sergeant Pears gave evidence of defendant's misbehaviour at the King Edward Hotel yesterday afternoon where he had gone for drinks and quarreled with the bar boys and the management.
