##### V. R.C. SPORTS

Yesterday the events proper in the V.R.C. annual aquatic sports —including the Colony's swimming championships—began at the club's bath.

AQUÁTIC CHAMPIONSHIPS OF COLONY.

The quarter mile championship was decided in the harbour outside the Club, the course being from the boathouse steps to the Praya wall.

Of the eight entrants, Private Edwards did not start and all completed the distance with the exception of Razavet.

Leading most of the way; May was a good first, while Cooke had little difficulty in keeping E. A. Irodie from second place. Ramsay was fourth.

###### C.S.C.C. MEETING

NEW OFFICIALS FOR NEXT YEAR.

Sir Claud Severn (President) presided at yesterday's annual meeting of the Civil Service Cricket Club, held in the club house, Happy Valley.

In proposing the adoption of the report, and accounts Sir Claud referred to the Club winning both divisions of the cricket league last season. The Club was grateful to Messrs. Gipson and Wood for their work on the ground, and to Mr. Squibbs for work on the green.

The balance in hand was $1,436 as against $105 last year.

The following officers were elected. (Cricket) Captain of first XI, Mr. F. J. de Rome; vice-captain, Mr. B. D. Evans. Captain "A" team, Mr. W. H. Edmonds; vice-captain, Mr. F. Squibbs.

Cricket representative, Mr. F. H. Holdman; Bowls representative, Mr. R. Smith; Tennis representative, Mr. R. R. Wood; Steward, Mr. Gregory.

Hon. Secretary, Mr. R. S. Vergette; Hon. Treas., Mr. B. Maughan; Greenranger, Mr. H. Rose.

General Committee, Messrs. Deacon, Lamble, Alderman, Brawn, Harrison, Longbotham and Strange.

Proposed by Mr. Maughan and seconded by Mr. Vergette, a motion was passed authorising the committee to draw on fixed deposit account up to $2,000 for repairs and alterations to the club-house.

Sir Claud presented Mr. Gregory and Mr. Rose with inscribed silver cigarette cases in recognition of their services in looking after refreshments while the club boys were on strike.

Referring to the impending departure of H.E. the Governor (Sir R. E. Stubbs), Sir Claud suggested that a letter be sent to him, as Patron of the Club, thanking him for the great honour he had bestowed and the work he had done while in the Colony. The meeting expressed approval and then Sir Claud mentioned his own departure, speaking of the pleasure it had been for him to be associated with the Club.

Mr. E. W. Hamilton spoke of Sir Claud's services since his arrival here in 1912. They were grateful for all that he had done and trusted that Sir Claud and Lady Severn would be spared to enjoy many years of retirement. Mr. Hamilton said he hoped that among Sir Claud's many recollections of Hongkong not the least pleasant would be his connection with the Civil Service Cricket Club. Replying, Sir Claud said that later, he would take a more formal leave of them.

Proposed by Mr. Alderman, the usual honorarium was voted to the hon. secretary, treasurer and steward, a member having raised the question at an earlier stage of the meeting.

Mr. James and Mr. Williams were re-elected auditors for the year. Votes of thanks were passed to outgoing officers and to Mr. Maughana.

The following will play in the second round of the Kowloon Golf Club summer competition: K. S. Robertson v. F. X. Ramsford, R. Meldrum v. E. A. Beaumont; A. Brookshank v. E. A. de Blanc, F. Oliver v. A. W. Brown.

MATCH WITH "HERMES."

The Volunteer team was, mainly, the Hongkong Hockey Club first eleven but the form shown by the winners was not quite up to Club 1st XI standard, play being a shade patchy at times.

Winning by 5 goals to 2, the Hongkong Volunteer Defence Corps took another point yesterday afternoon in their Olympiad with H.M.S. "Hermes."

L/Cpl. P. W. F. Mills (Armoured Cars); Pte. A. P. T. Farquharson (Scottish Company), Pte. D. H. Sherman (Armoured Cars); Sapper A. A. Dand (Engineer Co.), 2/Lieut. E. J. R. Mitchell (Infantry Co.), captain, Pte. E. W. L. Martin (Infantry Co.); Cpl. H. Owen Hughes (Infantry Co.), Pte. H. J. Armstrong (Infantry Co.), Pte. G. P. Lammert (Infantry Co.), Pte. G. R. Vollers (Armed Forces)

Lammert (Infantry Co.), Pte. G. R. Vallack (Armoured Cars), Pte. D. J. Price (Infantry Co.)

Starting late and with several men short, the Volunteers found it difficult to get going. On the whole their game was sound enough and in the second half, when at full strength, they were superior to the men of the mystery ship.

The airplane carrier took the lead in the first half and Lammert equalised. "Hermes" got their second goal and Lammert levelled up again. The score at half-time was 2 all. Lieut. Sillitoe, R.M., playing centre forward for the naval team, gave an extraordinarily good display, being very fast. The second goal for his team was scored by him and was a real beauty.



In the second half Lammericored two more goals and/allack one. When the final whistle sounded the score was -2.

Owen Hughes played very well in the Corps forward line but had to retire for a while. It was during his absence that Sillitoe scored the second "Hermes" goal. Lammert, on finding himself unable, to get what he wanted from his colleagues, relied on his great pace. The majority of his four goals came from his going through on his own. The Corps half-back line was quite good. Sherman was the better of the two backs as Farquharson was not up to his usual form.

## FLYWEIGHT HONOURS

Los Angeles, Aug. 24.

Fred La Barbara won the world's fly-weight title from Frankie Genaro by a decision in a ten-rounds contest.

<div style="text-align: center;"><img src="imgs/img_in_image_box_2150_884_2492_1760.jpg" alt="Image" width="9%" /></div>


Frankie Genoa.

[La Barbara won the fly-weight title in the Olympic Games in Paris last year. In the first series he very narrowly defeated E. Warwick, the British flyweight champion, and in the final he outpointed the Scot, Jimmy McKenzie, who has since turned professional.

La Barbara will not be recognised in this country as flyweight champion of the world any more than was Genaro, who was “presented” with the title on the death of Pancho Villa, who deprived Jimmy Wilde of his crown.

This performance of Barbara, however, stamps him as a particularly good boxer, as Genaro, who, by the way, won the Olympic fly-weight championship in 1920, held two decisions over Villa, who refused to meet him again in matches for the championship of America. This was before Villa beat Wilde.

Unless it had been specially agreed that the bout should count as a championship, Genaro would not even lose his American title in a bout over ten rounds.

## 600 MILES RACE

HOME YACHTS' OCEAN

EVENT.

In the Ocean Yacht Race of 600 miles from Ryde to Fastnet and back to Plymouth, Mr. E. G. Martin's cutter, "Jolie Brise," was the first to finish, arriving at Plymouth at 2.45 p.m. on August 21.

The race was sailed for the most part in calms and light winds, but on the night of August 20 the weather was so wild that Jolie Bries's "marisat" had to be lowered.

## MOTOR CYCLE RACNL

STRENUOUS 6-DAY TEST AT HOME.

THRILLS BY LADIES

The most strenuous six-days' motor-cycle trial over organised—the international test of motor-cycles—concluded at Brooklands on Saturday, August 22. For over a thousand miles the chosen riders of England, Australia, Germany, Holland, and Norway have traversed mountain paths, hills, lanes, and roads, embracing all the hazard which can confront the tourist.

As a result the British rider and the British machine stand supreme. In the international trophy contest, which is confined to riders and machines which must be representative of one country only, Germany challenged Great Britain and while the German team was eliminated early in the trial the three British teams all finished intact.

In the international vase contest England won first, second, and fourth places, with an Australian team on English machines occupying third place.

The British Inter-Centre Championship was won by the East Midland team with a fine collective performance.

The final day's run was remarkable for the climbing of Alms Hill, near Henley, which, by its greasy surface, proved to be the most difficult ascent of the trial.

Only eighteen of the sixty-one survivors made clean ascents, among them being Miss Ball, whose ascent was a remarkable exhibition of riding. Miss Ball's machine skidded all over the road, but with great dexterity she retained control, and passed over the summit. There were many exciting failures. Riders skidded and fell in the mud, or charged the spectators, who scattered.

The only sidecar driver to make a clean ascent was F. Giles (A.J.S.), a member of the British international team; while of the solo riders the fastest was that. of R. D. Adams, on a Scott. One Norwegian, one German, and one Australian rider made successful climbs.
