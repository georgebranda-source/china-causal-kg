Simla, September 6.—An appeal has been addressed to Mahatma Gandhi over the signatures of several Hindu and Mussalman leaders requesting him to resume the leadership voluntarily surrendered. Fear is expressed that if the people's representatives are threatened with wholesale disintegration, as evidenced by the recent attempts at party mongering, the anti-Nationalist majority will be overwhelming in the next Assembly and the Councils. They contend that the country is even now Nationalist to the core, only needing a selfless leader.

During the year ended December 31, 1925, only one passenger was killed in accidents to trains on the railways in Great Britain, according to the return issued by the Ministry of Transport. Seventy other passengers were killed in falling on platforms, crossing the line, falling out of carriages in motion or otherwise. The number of passengers injured was 3,380; In 1924 24 passengers were killed and 3,179 injured in accidents to trains.

"But what can I do when I see the Cantonese forces invading Kiangse, and when I hear that they have gone so far as to capture Ping-hsiang? Tokyo, September 16. Captain Sugita, a retired Army officer, who was arrested at Shanghai some months ago, charged with offering to sell secret documents, has been sentenced at Nagasaki to five years' imprisonment. The arrest of Captain Sugita caused a great sensation in Shanghai as it was reported, at the time of his arrest, that he was trying to sell important military documents to the Soviet Government. The nature of the documents was not revealed, and after a preliminary examination here he was sent to the Nagasaki district court.

Tundry Pond, which covers 20 acres, the property of Sir Gerald Mildmay, of Dogmersfield Park, Winchfield, Hampshire, was emptied during operations to discover a leakage. When the water had gone, thousands of fish, mainly pike, carp, and tench, were left, floundering on the muddy bottom. To prevent pollution these were given away and people went home carrying their unexpected spoils in such and all kinds of receptacles. More than a thousand carp, weighing on an average 8 lb., were counted. The giant of the pond was a pike some 25 lb., in weight. The pond was chiefly used for fishing, permits being issued by Sir Gerald Mildmay.

The arrest was made at the instance of the latter. The charge against the editor is not known. Hsiao was formerly a copyist in this news agency but resigned last autumn to form his own agency.

The death is announced of Mr. C. W. Andersen, Chief Government Marine Surveyor, Bangkok. The late Mr. Andersen, who was born in Denmark, was 61 years of age, and came out to Siam with the Redang as Chief Engineer in 1901. He later joined the firm of Mackay, McArthur and Company, and entered the Harbour Department on July 15, 1906, as assistant Government Marine Surveyor. On April 1, 1920, he was appointed Chief Government Marine Surveyor, on the retirement of Mr. Mackay.

Stockholm, August 6.—Professor Voronoff, of Paris, lecturing here to-day on rejuvenation before the International Physiological Congress, described a method of transplantation from a human being to an ape. His audience was shocked and he was criticised by Professor Carlson, of Chicago; Professor Biedl, of Prague; and Professor Wiesner, of Berlin. Professor Carlson afterwards told a reporter that Professor Voronoff and his ape had made the Congress ridiculous. Others who attended said that Professor Voronoff's statements must result in a scientific scandal.

Adelaide, August 19.—In consequence of the pending resignation of the South Australian (Labour) Premier, Mr. Gunn, the Parliamentary caucus have selected Mr. Hill, the Minister of Education, to succeed him as Premier. It is thought that in addition to retaining his post of Education Minister he will become State Treasurer. The Speaker, Mr. McInnes will join the Cabinet as Minister for Works, Industry, and Railways. No other alterations in the Ministry are likely. Mr. F. W. Birrell will be elected Speaker. The Labour Party enthusiastically approves the choice of Mr. Hill, who is 45.

There are the latest despatches, giving vivid details of how 110 British Naval men fought 20,000 Chinese at Wanhai. The whole action and rescue is described at length.

Particulars about additions to the China Fleet are mentioned and the British Minister's Note to Peking nicely rounds off the narrative.

### BOYCOTT “SETTLEMENT.”

Mr. Eugene Chen's important despatch on the decision to settle the boycott and to impose new taxes is reproduced.

As with the case in all matters not readily understood, the "China Mail" has made it a practice to add footnotes or comment where required. Such will be found in profusion through the "Overland China Mail."

Several messages throw much light on anti-British propaganda.
