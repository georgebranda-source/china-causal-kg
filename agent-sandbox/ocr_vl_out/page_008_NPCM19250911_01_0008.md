The return of notifiable diseases for the 21 hours ended Wednesday, shows three cases of enteric fever—one Indian and two Chinese.

The Broadensters gave another enjoyable entertainment in the Theatre Royal last night. They perform again this evening and to-morrow.

Rescission of all rules that women teachers must resign their posts on marriage must be made by the London County Council and other education authorities who have made them. In future there will be many marriages by women teachers. This, it was stated by an L.C.C. official, is an inevitable sequel to the result of the test case in which a woman teacher won her action against Poole Corporation.

Touring Canada to study farm methods, four English lads selected by the English Minister of Agriculture, have arrived at Toronto. They are winners of a travelling scholarship donated by E. W. Beatty, President of the Canadian Pacific Railway. Besides visiting the various Government demonstration farms in Canada, they will spend a month at the Canadian Pacific Railway demonstration farm in Alberta. They are also stopping off at typical farming districts, and visiting agricultural colleges. The lads are Jack Hitchings, 16 years old; Leslie Anstey, 15; Alfred Belchin, 16, and Roney Howe, 17 years of age.

A Chinese women's daily newspaper, one of the first of its kind in the country, has made its appearance its Shanghai. It is owned, edited, and published by Chinese and deals almost exclusively in news and articles of interest to women. The new daily is called the "Woman's Daily News." It is a two-sheet paper about half the size of the average Chinese newspaper issued in Shanghai. Its columns contained several feature articles on household economy, education, fine arts, ethics, women's professions, and news stories covering women's activities in the city, outports, and abroad.

An heroic bronze monument to Mr. John F. Stevens, engineer who is well known throughout the Far East and in Siberia in connection with his railway building, was recently unveiled on the crest of the Continental Divide in the United States. Mr. Stevens, who is now 72 years old, built a railroad through the northern Rocky Mountains at an altitude lower than any other. He was formerly chief engineer of the Panama Canal.

Tommy Milligan, the European boxing champion, whose reported intention to become a member of the Jesuit Order has caused widespread interest, would neither deny nor confirm the report when questioned.

The blind students of Worcester College for the sons of gentlemen dug the excavations for their swimming bath, which was opened by Viscount Cobham. There was not a single accident, and when a wheelbarrow load of soil got off the track and tipped it only added to the enjoyment of the boys, who found great fun in doing laborious work to keep down the cost of the swimming bath.

Sir Alfred Fripp, the famous senior surgeon at Guy's, who is retiring about the end of this month, made his first step to fame by a lucky chance. For he was in practice at York, still quite unknown, when the late Duke of Clarence, elder brother of the King, was stationed there. "Prince Eddy" injured his ankle, and Fripp was called in. He made so good an impression, as much by his confident and reassuring manner as his skill—at school they called him "Jehovah Junior"—that the Duke mentioned him to King Edward, to whom he was subsequently appointed Surgeon-in-Ordinary, and his future was assured. Such Coupets are honorary, but the recommendation and introduction are naturally invaluable.
