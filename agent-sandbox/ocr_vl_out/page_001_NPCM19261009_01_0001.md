## MIDNIGHT TO-NIGHT!

Warning to Big Shipping.

TRACK OF THE STORM: A STEAMER'S

Hong Kong, it seems, will have to be resigned to a typhoon during October bank-holiday week-end. The experts say that the cyclone is coming this way and have issued a warning to shipping.

Midnight to-night is the time preached when cyclone is expected to pass to the south of Gap Rock, or roughly 50 miles from the Colony.

The typhoon passed a few miles south of Pratas at 6 a.m. on a west-north-west track.

It is most likely to pass south of Gap Rock at about midnight or a little later.

The wind at Gap Rock is north-north-west, forre 8 (which is ash gule" according to the aufort scale), for the last 12 hours, and is likely to increase as the typhoon approaches.

The typhoon passed over the Java "Lijin" s.s. "Tjiliwong" at 8 p.m. last night, in Lat. 19N., Long. 118½E. Her barometer was falling at 28.55.

The barometer at the Royal Observatory, Kowloon, is at present 29.69, falling slowly.

A strong gale is also reported in the Formosa Channel.

At Hong Kong the gale will probably commence from the north and veer to north-east and east.

About 11 a.m. the Observatory issued the following warning:—It is very dangerous for ocean-going vessels to leave Hong Kong at present.

The position of the typhoon at present is 150 miles south of Hong Kong.

Yesterday evening, No. 1 signal gave place to No. 2 (gale expected from the north).

Small craft went to shelter behind the Causeway Bay and Yaumati breakwaters.

The Government rescue lug

Ocean-going steamers left the Kowloon wharves last night and went to typhoon moorings.

A few of the river steamboats also took shelter but services this morning to Macao and Canton left as usual.

It was also noticed that although there was a blow in the harbour, quite a number of the smaller coasting boats were still at their buoys.

As the day progressed, a good many left for Kowloon Bay and the seclusion of behind Stone cutters Island.

In town the blow was not particularly noticeable. But on the Peak and on the Praya, a stiff breeze, approaching a moderate gale, was blowing.

It is thought that the typhoon—unless it swerves—will be felt at its height to-nigh and that the storm will be over by tomorrow night.

## POSTAL INSURANCE

IMPORTANT REGULATIONS

AMENDED.

The regulations made by the Governor in Council under section 3 of the Post Office Ordinance, 1926, on June 24, 1926, have been amended by the rescission of regulation 26 and the substitution of the following regulation therefor:—

"Registered letters and parcels may be insured up to the limits stated in the Postal Guide for letters and in the Appendix to these Regulations for parcels. The fee for insurance of letters shall be 20 cents for each 300 francs (or £12 or $120) or part thereof. The fee for insurance of parcels shall be 25 cents for each 300 francs (or £12 or $120) or part thereof. The right to insure and the right to compensate in case of loss or damage shall be subject in all respects to the provisions of the Postal Guide. In this regulation 'Postal Guide' means the Postal Guide issued by the Postmaster General and current at the time of insuring or claiming as the case may be."
