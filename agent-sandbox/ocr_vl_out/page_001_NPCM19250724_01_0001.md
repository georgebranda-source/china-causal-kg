## “COFFIN SAMPAN” YUEN REBELS

REBUFF TO CANTON.

His Connection With Chan Kwing-ming.



Preferring the life of the hills, a bandit who is best known by the name of "Coffin Sampan" Yuen, has rebelled against Canton, his action possibly giving rise to rumours of a Chan Kwing-ming rising.

THE TRUE VERSION.

(From Our Own Correspondent.)

Those who are not in love with the present Kuomintang Government of Canton have heard rumours during the last few days that military movements have taken place at Sheklung to reestablish General Chan Kwingming as head of Kwangtung province. Better-informed sources were at first chary of the reports, and it now seems as if the true version has come through.

General Chan, his allies, and subordinates were defeated by the Kuomintang a few months ago. One of his lieutenants is a former bandit chieftain who is known throughout the province as Yuen Ha Kau. Yuen is his surname. Ha Kau is a nickname but nobody seems to know what his real name is.

Ha Kau by itself, I am told, indicates a kind of small, decrepit sampan, used mostly for conveying coffins and corpses across the many inland waterways. Yuen earned his surname by the fact that when an outlaw he brought death to many and thereby carried many lives on his head—hence the similarity to the function of the sampan referred to. To live up to his name, so the story goes, he once agreed to hand back a kidnapped victim. This man had already died, but the bandit had kept the fact a secret and thereby obtained the ransom. At the rendezvous he turned up with a "Ha Kau" sampan which contained the body of his captive!

In recent years his influence increased as did the number of his band and those of his “allies,” i.e., associates in other parts of the delta. One such by name of Wong Cho, better known as “Munficent Cho,” now holds sway in the Pun U district after having found sanctuary in Macao when a price was placed on his head. To strengthen his hands after being ousted from Canton, Chan Kwing-ming enlisted the aid of several bandits. These outlaws were invariably made Generals or Commanders and their men of the hills made excellent guerrilla material. Another advantage was their knowledge of difficult country, and they were also adepts at fighting in uneven country. As regulars they had little value.

MUITFUL RAID.

Since the last report, a number of dens occupied by idlers have been raided by the Police and several arrests made.
