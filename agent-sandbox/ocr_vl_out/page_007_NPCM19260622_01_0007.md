## ANCIENT CANNON

### PENANG'S REMARKABLE "FIND."

A remarkable "find" has occurred here, a score of ancient muzzle-loading cannon being unearthed alongside the foundations of the old Central Station during the work of laying the foundations of the new Chartered Bank at the corner of Union Street and Beach Street.

They were all heaped together and though rusted are in a fair state of preservation. One bears the initials "F.M." over the touch-hole and all bear the Tudor crown, which gives some idea of their antiquity.

These cannon vary in size from a tiny ornamented affair, with a three-inch orifice, to a weightier piece of ordinance suggestive of a trench mortar.

It is suggested that they originally belonged to the old British East India Company, but their history has not yet been established, and how they came to be where they were is a mystery.—"S. T. Press"

## GENERAL WOOD

## VERY POPULAR WITH PHILIPPINES

"Governor General Wood is very popular with the Filipinos," said General McRae, formerly in command of the Philippine department, in an interview to day. General McRae will take command of the ninth district corps on Wednesday.

"The governor is especially popular," said General McRae, "with the older Filipinos who know that he has given the islands prosperity, schools and good roads."

He went on to say that American sovereignty was essential to the peace of the Far East and that the Philippines were essential to the maintenance of American prestige in the Pacific.-United Press

## BRAZIL OUT

## LEAGUE RESIGNATION NOW CONFIRMED

SPAIN MAY FOLLOW

Rugby, June 21.

Sir Austen Chamberlain, Secretary of State for Foreign Affairs, stated in the House of Commons, in reply to a question, that he had now received confirmation of the notification of Brazil's withdrawal from the League of Nations. He could only express "his sincere regret that the Brazilian Government was taking that step, and he hoped that it might yet reconsider its intention to cut itself adrift from this great and increasingly powerful international organisation to the success of which it had made great contributions in the past.

Sir Austen "Chamberlain added that, though he had no official information, he was afraid that the Spanish Government was going to take a similar step.—British Wireless Service.

## SUMATRA RUBBER

SEEN AS SOURCE OF U. S. SUPPLY

Akron, Ohio. June 17

The rubber Association of America has communicated with all leading consumers of raw rubber in the United States urging their cooperation in the establishment of American-owned rubber plantations in Sumatra.

A. L. Viles, the manager, said the entire scheme had been the subject of careful scientific and economic investigations. He said that the association planned no immediate action until assured of enough co-operation by manufacturers of rubber goods.

The Philippine Islands had been considered, he said, but it was his belief that the Sumatra project would most probably be accepted. He declined to estimate the amount of money necessary to carry out such an extensive development in the Philippines or in Sumatra. In another quarter it was learned that the association has planned tentatively to spend $50,000,000.

A project of this size and importance," said Viles, "must be thoroughly financed and a definite programme outlined before further steps can be taken. Prospects in the Philippines are still very unsettled because of the political and industrial condition existing to the investment of large sums of money there."—United Press.

## THE PHILIPPINES

VICE-GOVERNOR SOON TO RETURN.

Eugene A. Gilmore, Vice-Governor of Philippines, plans to sail from the United States for Manila on June 27 after spending some weeks at his home in Wisconsin.

Before leaving Washington Mr. Gilmore had a farewell talk with President Coolidge. He told "The Associated Press" that several conferences with the President had convinced him that Mr. Coolidge is keenly interested in conditions in the Philippines and well informed concerning them.

The Vice-Governor recently completed recruiting forty-five teachers for the Islands, and on his way to the coast he planned to stop at various educational centres to continue his programme of interesting experts in the vocational and agricultural activities of the school system of the Philippines.

Mrs. Gilmore will remain in Wisconsin until their children have returned to school after the vacation and will join her husband in the Philippines next October.—Associated Press.

"CONSTRUCTIVE ECONOMY."

Washington. June 21.

Speaking at the half-yearly Budget meeting of the Government President Coolidge advocated a programme of "constructive economy" in Government expenditure by a means, inter alia, of a gradual reduction in the number of Government employees by refraining from replacing those dropping out.

He predicted that the current fiscal year would close with a surplus of $180,000,000 and the following year with a surplus of $20,000,000.

The President declined to promise a further reduction of taxes on the ground that it would be unfortunate to raise such hopes until the state of the Government's finances justified it.—Router's American Service.

## PLEA FOR RUSSIA

MUST BE ADMITTED TO TO LEAGUE.

Chancellor Marx told the United Press in an interview that Russia must be admitted to the League of Nations and be allowed to resume her normal international status, or the world be plunged once more into a devastating war.

"One of the most fundamental facts in International relations" said Chancellor Marx "is that the powers must accept Russia once more into the family of nations if they hope for peace.

"Russia will not attempt to impose communism on the rest of the world but she is entitled to choose her own form of government and work out her own means of preserving law and order. Germany knew she had nothing to fear from Russian propaganda or she would never have signed the recent treaty of neutrality with the Soviet government."—United Press.

Washington, June 17.

American diplomatic officials today emphatically denied that they were in any way involved, even indirectly, in the League of Nations controversy, resulting in Brazil's withdrawal from the league. Rumours involving the United States in Brazil's withdrawal were circulated in Geneva and Rio Janeiro.
