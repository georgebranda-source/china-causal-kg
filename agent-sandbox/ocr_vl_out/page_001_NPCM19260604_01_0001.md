## CANTON ROW

POLITICIANS FORCED TO LEAVE?

"STRIKING TERROR."

H.K. University Honours Man

"On Leave."

(From Our Own Correspondent.)

This morning at dawn Mr. Foo Ping-sheung, Commissioner of Foreign Affairs for Kwanqtung, followed yesterday's example of Mayor C. C. Wu, and boarded the steamer for Hongkong. his presumed destination being the international settlement at Shanghai.

Mr. Foo is the second prominent official of the Kuomintang Government to depart from Canton since Sunday when General Chang Kai-shek struck terror into the hearts of the "moderates" by ordering the military arrest of General Wu Te-chen, former chief of police of the city.

[In May, 1916 Mr. Foo Ping-sheung graduated with 1st class honours as, a Bachelor of Science (Engineering); at the Hongkong University, being the first student to do so—Ed., "China Mail".]

Lam Chi-fung, an assistant in the Department of Foreign Affairs, has been named acting commissioner for "the time being, and, as in the case of Dr. C. C. Wu, when news of Mr. Foo's departure is made public it will be officially said that he has gone to Shanghai for a "month's vacation."

As yet Canton does not know of the departure of either official, but in Government circles and on Shameen in the consulates, there is great speculation as to whether or not Chang Kai-shek will fill their places with Communists.

Unquestionably the arrest of Wu Te-chen, and the imminent peril which made Dr. Wu ard Mr. Fogo to Shanghai is traceable to Communist influence. Even if the Communists do not get the jobs, they seem to have General Chang Kai-shek's ear, and to have his confidence in sufficient degree to enable them to influence his mind against the anti-Communist Kuomintang officials.

If Mr. Sun Fo should also leave, General Chang Kai-shek would probably find himself in an unenviable position, for if the leaders of "Sun Yat-senism" are forced out of office, the loyalty of the people to Chang Kai-shek will be questionable. More than that, it is considered certain that if the Kuomintang is entirely unseated, and if General Chang Kai-shek arrogates to himself the powers of military dictator, most of the Nationalist generals now commanding' Kwangtung troops against Wu Pei-fu's allies would revolt against the local warlord.

What Chang May Do.

In the latter event, General Chang Kai-shek would hold only Canton and the immediate suburbs, for his 40,000 loyal troops are practically all quartered here.

Additional manifestoes have been issued by the foreign office, following those concerning the ending of the Hongkong boycott and the abolition of the oil monopoly. One of the mandates appropriates $500,000 for bandit extermination, promises the people that the pirates will be cleared off from the rivers within a month, and that the land bandits will be killed or in hiding before the end of thirty days.

A fourth manifesto says:—“A Labourers 'and Capitalists' Arbitration Board shall be formed, composed of an equal number of delegates, from each side, with a Government delegate as chairman, and this board shall be vested with full power to settle all disputes.”

A fifth mandate urges the people to report cases of corrupt official-dom; a sixth demands reference to the Government of all disputes between organisations; a seventh orders the arrest and trial by martial law of all reactionaries and rumour mongers; and a seventh promises reforms in matters of education and the carrying out of plans for road and harbour improvement.
