BACK TO THE PARISH'PUMP!

With the passing of the boycott the parish pump will have a chance to come back into its own. Purely local affairs have been more or less under a cloud for sixteen months. The Colony has been content to "carry on" with the least possible attention to the local problems that matter—and many that do not!

The easiest thing in the day's life here is to find a complaint or a grievance. The Colony is simply swarming with them. But all eyes have been centred so much on Canton that may "handy annuals" have almost withered. Many, indeed, are sadly faded for lack of the necessary attention. It is a moot point whether this is all for the good. Goodness knows the community is not so articulate as it should be in years in which wars and strikes and boycotts play no part. Spasmodic efforts are made to create and sustain public interest in vital local problems; but the talk and the action are generally confined—like everything else here—to a select few who do not seem to mind shouldering other people's troubles and airing other people's grievances. The bulk of the community—and we write now only of the non-Chinese portion—are content to drift along in a happy-go-lucky fashion, hearing not and heeding not the turning of the parish pump handle. The call to real citizenship, sad to confess, apparently is heard by few.

Outside the Unofficial members of the Legislative Council and the Sanitary Board there is surely work for citizens of the Colony and citizens of the Empire—to make their influence felt; to stir up the dry bones in a too apathetic community; to arouse in them a sense of duty and of responsibility as residents of a British Colony and of the great Empire to whom they owe allegiance. That work need not necessarily be confined to grumbling and grousing and airing petty grievances. There is ample room in

ist as well as the destructionist. Why leave the entire settling of local destinies to the limited band of public men whose names figure in every scheme for the better

ment of the place in which we live?

If the passing of the boycott means a new innings for the parish pump, let our communal work be shared by many more than in the past. Good as is a Municipal Council for fostering the civic spirit the absence of such is no excuse for shunning and evading the duties of citizenship. A broader vision is required to cope successfully with local affairs. Mere denunciation of the parish pump because of its very parochialism will not lead to a solution of Hong Kong's problems. Parochialism, admittedly, can be carried to extremes, but there is always a happy mean that can easily be found by the individual desirous of leaving the Colony a better place than he found it.

## AUGMENT REPULSE BAY SERVICE

Two new and luxurious motor buses which have been added by the Hong Kong Hotel Garage to the Repulse Bay service will eliminate difficulty experienced in the past in maintaining a full service on all routes when any of the vehicles have been undergoing repairs.

The new cars are Miller built models on Studebaker chassis, the design and fittings making them the last word in comfortable travelling. Each passenger has a bucket seat, the cushions and backs being built on Barber double deck springs, amply padded and upholstered in brown leather, this together with the wide aisle and spacious windows making them ideal for local conditions.

Driven by Traffic Sergeant Mason and carrying seven passengers, one of the vehicles made a trial run to the town and back to the Central Police Station compound along Garden Road and Arbuthnot Road this morning

The sharp gradients on the route gave ample scope for testing the efficiency of the brakes which acted smoothly even no sudden application without causing the passengers any discomfort.

With its blue painted body and large luggage box at the rear, the bus attracted considerable attention in the course of the trial run.

Including the driver who sits by himself, the bus has basket seats for 14 persons.

## CONVOY SYSTEM

PICKETS' DEPARTURE SEQUEL.

Among other matters dealt with at yesterday's meeting of the Chinese General Chamber of Commerce was that of the armed convoy system for the protection of shipping by the Hong Kong Government.

Some time ago, the opinion of the Chamber as to the desirability of reinstituting the convoy service was asked for by the then Colonial Secretary, but as the picket nuisance which was then in being hampered the movement of vessels on any large scale, the Chamber had not hitherto replied:

At yesterday's meeting, however, it was announced that, after consultation with the "principal shipping interests," the Chamber was in favour of advising the restoration of the convoy system.

Mr. Li Yau-tsun presided over the meeting supported by Mr. J. M. Wong, vice-chairman, and Mr. Ip. Lan-chuen, general secretary.

The question of "the better supervision of the searching of luggage and other packages by the police on outgoing and incoming steamers again came up for discussion. The problem before the Chamber was how to advise the Police Authorities on this matter, as it could not recommend the abolition of the practice, and it was considered impractical to ask for higher officials to direct the search in all cases.

After some “discussion, the matter was referred back to the Hotel Guild, which made the complaint with the suggestion that possibly a joint private meeting of representatives of the Guild and the Chamber might be held.

The secretary read a number of communications addressed to the Chamber, including one from the family of the late Mr. Ho Fook, thanking the Chamber for its sympathy in the family's recent bereavement, and one from the Chinese Society in Cuba advising against further Chinese emigration to that country.

## EVADING FARE

## TWO EMPLOYEES USING ONE TICKET

TRAMWAY CASE.

Mr. A. Gillard, of the Hong Kong Tramway Company, prosecuted two Chinese youths before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning with misuse of a monthly ticket.

Defendants were both employees of the Kin Yuen firm, of No. 26, Des Voeux Road. It was stated that the first defendant was found using the ticket on Tuesday, and when questioned gave the name appearing on the ticket and the address of the shop. The following day the second defendant used the ticket while travelling in a car.

Some difficulty arose when the second defendant said that his name was 'not Wong Kin-yuen, the name appearing on the ticket. Mr. Gillard said that the defendant was pointed out by a shop employee as the man bearing that name.

The first defendant, who admitted that he used a ticket not in his name, was fined $10. The case against the other youth was adjourned for one week for the master of the shop to be called.

## TYPHOON TOLL

## JUNK WRECKED AT PO TOI ISLAND

ELEVEN CASUALTIES.

Further details have been received regarding the rescue of thirty-three Chinese who were ast ashore on Po Toi island during the typhoon.

These were the survivors of a trading junk which left Hoifung on Thursday carrying 39 passengers in addition to a crew of five.

The typhoon struck the vessel on the following day and broke it up on the rocks near the island.

On the receipt of the report at Stanley Police Station by the master of the grocery shop who attended to the needs of the survivors a launch proceeded to the island and brought them to Hong Kong yesterday.

Eleven persons perished in the disaster.

## ALLEGED PICKETS

## A MAN WHO WAS A PROPER SOLDIER

Two Chinese were charged at the Central Magistracy yesterday with the theft of a cargo of vegetables, valued at $426, from San Hui, Po On district, Chinese territory.

Defendants were arrested when they brought the cargo to Hong Kong, and it was alleged by the prosecution that they were in the company of strike pickets who fired on the boat and took possession.

Mr. D. McCallum, who appeared for the defence, suggested that the complainants lost their own cargo of vegetables and accused the defendants who had brought their cargo down to Hong Kong of having stolen it.

One of the complainants who gave evidence at the first hearing of the case last week was cross-examined at length by Mr. McCallum and gave "interesting details of village life.

Asked what occupation the first defendant followed, witness said he was formerly a farmer but now kept company with the pickets.

Mr. McCallum: You are a man of extraordinary little consequence in your village, aren't you?—I am a farmer in a small way.

During the whole of the time the strike and boycott has been operation has your village suffere at all?—No.

Have there ever been any pickets in your village at all?—I can't say. They might have been there while I was working in the fields. And throughout the strike you have been sending your produce to Hong Kong because it offers the best market open to you?—Yes.

It has been your habit to sell produce to farmers who have formed themselves into a group, to bring it down to Hong Kong?—Yes.

Who is Commander Wong Lok?—He is in the country now.

Is there a magistrate called Luk Kwok-hang?—Yes...

And are you a soldier under Wong Lok?—I am not a soldier now. I gave it up during the fifth moon this year and returned to the village to do farming again.

Why did you give up your soldiering?—Because I couldn't make a living for my family.

Not even by looting? (Witness did not reply).

Have you been going round the village and demanding money?—No.

Another complainant under cross-examination denied that he had had trouble with the defendants over ancestral funds and the suggestion that he lost his cargo and claimed that of the defendants as his own.

## BANK CLOSURE

## WINDING UP PETITION ADJOURNED

## RUSSO-ASIATIC BANK

In the Summary Court this morning (acting during the vacation for the Chief Justice in Bankruptcy), Mr. Justice Wood granted an adjournment in respect of an application for the winding up locally of the Russo-Asitic Bank.

Mr. H. G. Sheldon made the application, being instructed by Messrs. Johnson, Stokes and Master. He represented the petitioning creditor, Mr. Au Fung-chau.

The petition had been presented on September 28, Mr. Sheldon said, and the hearing fixed for to-day. The adjournment was asked for owing to certain documents not being yet available.

The Official Receiver, Mr. D. W. Tratman, had no objection and His Honour granted the adjournment, the necessary advertisement to be in the same form.

At Mr. Sheldon's "request," His Honour made a note that at this the first hearing the petition had "not been opposed.
