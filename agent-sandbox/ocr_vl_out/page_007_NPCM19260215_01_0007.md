## A DEADLOCK

## FULILE EFFORTS TO END WIRELESS STRIKE

### (Router's Service.)

## London, 10th, 14

The protracted negotiations between the representatives of the marine wireless operators on strike and the ship owners have ended in a deadlock with no immediate, prospect of settlement according to the Chairman of the Operators' Association, who said that the employers simply repeated their terms and gave the Association seven days to accept, threatening, in the event of refusal, to offer certain conditions to the men individually.

The failure to agree was confirmed to the questions of wages and reinstatement of the strikers, the employers insisting that the men resume at reduced rates and be reinstated only as the employers are able to offer them service.

The Chairman concluded that 1,300 ships are now going to sea without qualified wireless operators, of whom over 1,900 are on strike.

## Reductions Unjustified

The Wireless Telegraphists Association, in a statement, says that the operators are prepared to submit the whole dispute to the Industrial Court, but they are not prepared to prejudice the case before the Court by accepting a totally unjustified reduction.

It points out that the wireless operators accepted reductions in 1922 and 1924 additional to any reduction accepted by other sea-faring men.

## SWISS

FOR FAILURE TO SETTLE

SOVIET QUARREL.

FRENCH MEDIATION.

(Router's Service.)

London Feb. 14.

The Russo-Swiss reconciliation on which depends the Soviets' participation in the League's armament Conference, is making little headway, judging from the official statement from Moscow blaming the Swiss attitude for the failure to settle the dispute through the medium of France, declaring that Switzerland insisted on eliminating from the text of its declaration anything that could be interpreted as an unqualified expression of regret for the assassination of Vorovsky and refused to consent to any formula establishing an agreement to compensate Vorovsky's daughter.

The Soviet statement, however, ratefully, acknowledges the efforts of the French Government.

## Rounding on Council

M. Chicherin's reply to the League's invitation to participate in the preparatory meeting of the Disarmament Conference says that the Soviet cannot attend any conference on Swiss territory pending a settlement of its conflict with Switzerland. It hopes that the obstacles will be eliminated, otherwise, the Soviet will hold the Council of the League responsible for its exclusion.

## COAL COMMISSION

BOTH SIDES MORE PACIFIC

(British Wireless Service)

Importance is attached by one. Press to a meeting of mine owners which will be held this week to consider the coal situation.

It is stated that on both sides during recent weeks a growing feeling has been displayed in favour of making a further attempt to secure a peaceful solution of the problem.

Meanwhile, the members of the Coal Commission, in order that the Prime Minister shall have a report at the end of the month, have been working about thirteen hours a day for the past fortnight.

The rainfall for January at the Botanical Gardens was 31 inch on 4 days; at the Matilda Hospital, it was 35 inch on 3 days; and at the Taipo Police Station, it was a blank both in inches and days.

The lowest reading of the barometer (means a level) was 29.927 inches at 3 p.m. on the 18th.

The maximum squall velocity, as recorded by the Dines-Baxtendell anemograph, was at the rate of 44 miles per hour at 6.19 a.m. on the 3rd.

The mean temperature for the month was 61.1 degrees. The highest was 75.6 on the 18th and the lowest 51.5 on the 22nd.

There were 172.9 hours of sunshine during the month.
