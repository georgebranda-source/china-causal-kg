## TELEPATHY

We are all familiar with that sudden start which makes one turn the head in the, certain knowledge that one is an object of scrutiny. Few of us, probably, but have not focussed our eyes and endeavoured with varying success to concentrate on the process of "willing" that someone else should look in our direction. Such, undoubtedly, are elementary forms of telepathy, a scientific exponent of which we have in our midst in the person of Prof. Langsner. Involving considerably more complicated and, to the non-expert mind, almost inexplicable, mental processes are the not incommon forms of telepathy at a considerable distance between persons allied by common ties either of blood or of personal sympathy and affection. These sometimes "take the form of dreams—as in the case of the father of the murdered A.P.C. man, Mr. Philips, and sometimes a waking sense of uneasiness and apprehension regarding the safety of the other kindred spirit.

The existence of such forms of telepathic or teleplastic powers—to give the capacity of seeing things in a dream its scientific designation—cannot be denied although they are confined to isolated cases in which particularly susceptible natures are concerned. Whether the mental processes involved can be analysed sufficiently to allow of the evolution of theories capable of general application, is a problem in regard to which scientists may express opinions but which time alone can decide. The prospect of a world in which the capacity for speech is superseded by telepathic intercourse would, however, intrigue few and undoubtedly appall most rationally thinking persons:

## TARIFF CONFERENCE RESUMPTION

## IS PEKING COMPETENT?

On the important matter of the Tariff Conference, Mr. Eugene Chen (Acting Minister for Foreign Affairs, Canton), has authorised the publication of the following note, which was addressed to the American Consul-General (Mr. Douglas Jenkins) for transmission to the American Minister at Peking. Identical notes have been addressed to representatives of the other interested Powers:—

Canton, July 14.

Sir,—I have the honour to request you to communicate to the American Minister at Peking the protest of my Government against the resumption of the Special Tariff Conference which was lately suspended owing to the dispersal of the Chinese delegation. We are definitely and reliably informed that agents of Wu Pei-fu and Chang Tso-lin are now negotiating with the American and the other foreign delegates for the immediate reopening of the Conference.

My Government opposes and has opposed the Conference because it involves the consideration of issues which only a Central Government, representative and competent to speak and act in the name of the Chinese Nation, can negotiate in conference with the official representatives of the American and other interested Governments. Tuan Chi-jui's administration, admittedly, was not such a government, nor do the present servitors of Wu and Chang constitute the type of governing body which America and the Powers (assuming that considerations of political realism and international morality and decency still rule high foreign policies) can meet and treat with as a modern government.

None is so blind as to fail to see that the present phantom government in Peking is a creation of a brace of medieval militarists and a bunch of Mandarin states-boys and states-coolies whose obvious purpose is to grab the proceeds of whatever tariff doles and loans that America and the other Powers may be willing to grant in order to maintain a status quo that conflicts with every vital interest of Nationalist China.

Any payment of tariff moneys to Wu Pei-fu and Chang Tso-lin must necessarily mean that America and the other interested Powers—through the machinery of the unified, British-controlled Chinese Maritime Customs—will be (a) paying national revenues collected throughout the whole of China to two transient usurpers of detached pieces of Chinese territory, and (b) subsidising these two militarists to continue the prosecution of civil war against the Kuominchun and Canton who are the two modern arms of Nationalist China, and thus assist militarism to dominate and flourish in China. And specifically it will mean that America and the other Powers will be collecting the increased Customs revenues of Canton and hand over the same to the medieval Wu and the ex-bandit Chang in order to enable them the better to fight and attempt to destroy the greatest centre of Chinese Nationalist thought and activity, which is Canton.

I have to add that any loan or loans to be contracted by the agents of Wu and Chang on the security of the promised surtaxes shall not be recognised by the Nationalist Government. And I have the honour deliberately to warn America—and the other interested governments that Chinese repudiation of any such loan or loans may conceivably create a situation rendering it imperative for the principle of repudiation to be extended to other loans contracted in the interests of reaction and militarists and mandarin exploitation and plunder.

## STATE ORGANISATION NEEDED IN CALIFORNIA

500 ARRIVALS IN A WEEK.

San Francisco, July 9.—A statewide service organisation to care for the interests of Philipinos is considered a vital necessity as the result of the steady influx of young men from the islands.

An average of 90 men have arrived from the islands every two weeks throughout the spring and the Filipino community in the Bay District alone has grown until the Catholic Filipino clubs survey indicates a population exceeding 19,000.

More than 500 Filipinos arrived in San Francisco in one week recently, most of them from the islands but some from northern coast states who were going to or seeking new positions in the South.

## Much Help Given

All these men had to be housed temporarily, some had bills to be collected from employers and needed help, some needed medical treatment and many more needed work.

Filipino clubs in the district were faced with the task of performing these services and at the same time keeping the boys out of the clutches of sharpers.

A survey has shown that there is an organised effort in San Francisco and in most of the larger cities to fleece the Filipinos out of their earnings at the end of the summer resort season or after the crop season. These efforts also extend to new arrivals from the islands.

It has also been shown that the new arrivals are not properly equipped to go to some of the agricultural districts. There are now five Filipinos in Bay District hospitals dangerously ill from exposure. They went to the Watsonville district without warm clothing and contracted severe colds. Two had pneumonia and it is feared three of them may have tuberculosis.

While the various clubs are handling all such cases as are brought to them there is no central organisation that can apportion the cases and get quick action.

After the Filipinos have been here for a time, their condition is generally good. Most of the responsible Filipino contractors look after the welfare of their men. However when the boys take individual jobs they frequently meet with trouble. Some of the trouble has been due to lack of a knowledge and some to intentional imposition by American labour contractors or employers.

Committees Not Enough.

Committees like the one recent IV organised under the auspices of the Salinas Commercial club can not meet the situation without the aid of a central organisation.

In the cities the Filipinos are attending various schools and working part time. The problems are many and varied.

A paternal organisation with many branches could attend to these problems and also the problems of education. Filipinos are good students but in many cases there is a temptation to leave schools and earn more money.

The clubs want the boys to remain in school but if they remain there, entertainment and recreation must be provided and there must be constantly applied, the urge to finish the task.

Their own good nature and natural ambition has brought the temptation to leave school in hundreds of cases. Housewives find these boys take readily to cooking, consequently they teach them to cook, pay a little higher wage, and for a while everyone is happy.

However Filipino cooks in some San Francisco mansions command high salaries and cafe cooks are receiving $150 a month. The school boys want this higher wage but must work full time to get it.

Until the work of giving these boys the fullest service is organised club managers here say it is important for these contemplating leaving Manila to choose some club before sailing and see that they are taken there immediately on arrival—'United Press.'
