Berlin, July 25.—The revelation that General Ludendorff, the German National leader, attempted to send his personal fortune out of the country in 1922, when the export of capital was forbidden, followed an action which he brought against Herr Adolf Borchardt; his banker.

It appears that the general eager to save the small fortune which accrued from his post-war literary activities, instructed his banker to remit his money abroad, and to purchase for him gilded foreign securities.

The general's plan was detected after one or two remittances were made to a Swiss bank, which subsequently went bankrupt, and his fortune disappeared during the inflation.

"General Ludendorff sued" the banker for "the money which had thus been lost," and although the trial was held in camera the affair leaked out. "The former military idol of Germany is said to have now no other means than his pension.

## SCAVENGERS' STRIKE

CITY IN A DEPLORABLE CONDITION.

Bombay, August 27.

Following the unrest among the street scavengers owing to the Municipality's proposal to reduce the pay of new employees by Rs. 5 monthly the majority of the scavengers have struck and the streets of a large part of the city are unswept though the cut does not apply to old employees.

It is stated that legal action against the strikers has been launched. Bailable warrants have been issued against 40' strikers chosen from all the wards of the city.

### Bombay, August 28."

The sweepers and scavengers, who for two days past have held the city at the point of the broom, and who are demanding the restoration of the five-rupee cut in wages of newly-appointed hands, have been thwarted by the Corporation.

Twenty-one men were arrested and brought before the Magistrate to-day. Two were convicted and sentenced to two months' hard labour on a charge of striking without two months' notice as required by the Bombay Municipal Employees' Act.

Subsequently the men, as a result of consultations, agreed to resume to-morrow, upon which decision further hearing was postponed.

The accumulations of refuse in many parts of the city have been tackled by substitute sweepers from neighbouring parts.

To Cost Million Yen.

Local businessmen have long foreseen the further expansion of the Chinese port city and to meet the growing passenger trade which was accelerated with the acquisition of the Toyo Kisen Kaisha fleet, directors of the establishment unanimously agreed on the necessity of opening new and larger quarters in Shanghai, the general manager of the Nippon Yusen Kaisha explained.

The old Yokohama Specie Bank building will be temporarily renovated at a cost of Tls. 40,000 to house the booking office now quartered at the Palace Hotel. The general office is located at Hongkew.

## VON BISSING'S BAN

The will of Baron Walter Adolphus von Bissing, half-brother of the German Military Governor of Belgium, which was published recently, is notable for its strong anti-German feeling.

ENGLISH ESTATE

Baron von Bissing, a German by birth and a naturalised British subject, died domiciled in Italy, aged 70. Formerly he was for many years a prominent resident of Hove.

He left, exclusive of settled and foreign estate, unsettled property in England valued at £1,178, with net personality £1,136. Probate of the will, dated last April, has been granted to his widow, Baroness von Bissing, of 50. Tedworth Square, Chelsea, S.W. The Baron stated in his will:—

“I desire particularly to express in the most emphatic and precise terms that under no circumstances whatever is any German, whether relation of mine or otherwise,” to have any voice or right in or over the guardianship or upbringing of “my children.”

He left: £500 to his solicitor, Edward A. L. Smith, Gray's Inn Square, W.C., in appreciation of his services, and all his other property to his widow.
