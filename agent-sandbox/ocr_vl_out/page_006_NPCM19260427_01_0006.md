## Hongkong, Tuesday, April 27, 1926

Of all the dry-as-dust Gazettes published by any Government that issued locally must easily rank as first. Naturally there is no attempt at "frills" — nobody exactly desires these, — the ideal (?) being to make everything as stodgy and flat as possible. There is the usual list of appointments which His Excellency the Governor has been "pleased" to make the minutes of the previous Legislative Council; texts of Bills to be introduced into the Legislature; notices to mariners; quarantine measures; and so on. As a record of the present period it is hopeless. As a Government "organ" it is vastly out of tune save and except for the one fact that everything within its limited pages is strictly official. And there is the usual list of tenders for various works and goods, which, to be seen by the persons who ought to be concerned in submitting prices should rightfully be published as well in the daily newspapers. In comparison with the Hongkong Government Gazette the Municipal Gazette is the official

organ of the Council for the Foreign Settlement of Shanghai, is distinctly bright and readable. It publishes changes in the personnel of the Diplomatic Corps, Volunteer Corps Orders, and reports by the Watch Committee on hotels, taverns, and restaurants (which give the list of applications and the manner of dealing with them). Space is given to road extensions and widenings, sales of Municipal properties, excerpts from minutes of the Works Committee, the Public Utilities Committee, the Foreign Education Committee (Hongkong Board of Education, please note!) Municipal staff changes, extracts from the order books of each week relative to every phase of Municipal activity, and even a Municipal Band programme (on which, by the way, Mrs. Bowes-Smith, of Hongkong, figures prominently).

Nor does the up-to-date spirit end here, for there are very useful reports for March by the Commissioner of Police and the Acting Chief Officer of the Fire Brigade. The Police report in itself is valuable. It not only gives statistics of crime, but it reviews in an intelligent manner the main events concerning the Police. Forced during the month. It comments on the local agitation against the Central Government, anti-foreign agitation, lawlessness in Chinese territory, the awakening of the Chinese to the dangers of Bolshevik, Russian affairs, and political matters. There is even a small section devoted to dogs—the number in the kennies, the number seized by the Police, the number shot, and so forth. Next follows the report on the Fire Brigade for March.

It will be seen that there is no waiting in Shanghai for annual reports of Departments to be issued any time from six to eighteen months after the close of a year. Monthly reports are prepared and published as soon as possible in the Municipal Gazette. Every ratepayer within the Shanghai Municipality is apprised through the Gazette of everything that he ought to know.

Recently something was said of the need for a brighter Hongkong. Why not begin with a brighter Government Gazette? If it can be done in Shanghai by the Municipality, why not in Hongkong by the Government? The suggestion is thrown out in the hope that one of the enterprising Unofficial members of the Legislative Council will take it up with the authorities with a view at least to improving on-the-dry-as-dust organised by the Government at present.

MONEYLENDERS.

Hongkong has long since attended to the question of money-lenders. Money lending is an every day fact of business and social life. Without it many would find it hard to function business could not be carried on. Moneylenders, however, is generally speaking, a term to denote a class who lend money at a high rate of interest. In Hongkong, moneylenders are registered. They seem to belong to one class of the community and their operations seem to be done with another class. We are not certain if

the regulations permitting them to carry on business stipulates the maximum interest they may charge. Such does not seem to be the case, bearing in mind that it has happened that the Court has expressed itself clearly on the question of what is a reasonable interest. At Home, the crying scandal of unregistered money-lenders has long been in evidence. They operate chiefly amongst the poorer classes, and transactions, in number at any rate, are upon a colossal scale. Much of the misery in slum quarters can be traced to their work. Greed has killed all signs of benevolence or charity. Much would have much more—"full measure, pressed down, and running over." The House of Commons has given ear to a private Bill—which the Government will support—to control moneylenders. They are to be licensed and licences will only be given upon the submission of certificates fulfilling certain qualifications. There are other conditions which will lead to greater control. The one thing that surprises those interested in social questions is that forty-eight percent is laid down as the rate of interest for loans, "but this is not intended to be the maximum." There seems here to be an encouragement to all and sundry to indulge in a Wallingfordian effort to get rich quickly. Surely forty-eight per cent, as a seeming minimum is outrageous!

## DEPARTURES

MR. AND MRS. A. DENISON.

By the departure of Mr. and Mrs. A. Denison to-day old links with the Colony are broken. As an architect and civil engineer, Mr. Denison has played an important part in the rapid development of the Colony, his early work here including the design and construction of the original Taikco Sugar Refining Company, the No. 1 Dock at Kowloon, and the Kowloon Wharyes and Godowns.

Mr. Denison, who is able to look back long prior to the construction of the Peak tram, has thus witnessed practically the entire development of the Peak as a residential district. He was one of the pioneers at the Magazine Gap and Kennedy Roads districts.

Having first arrived here in 1881 and working in conjunction with the late Mr. W. Danby, he has been known in more recent years as the senior partner in the firm of Messrs. Denison. Ram and Gibbs. Mr. Denison entered into partnership with Mr. E. A. Ram in 1897 and they were joined in 1900 by Mr. L. Gibbs. Mr. Denison retired from the firm in March of last year.

Mr. Denison's favourite recreation has always been yachtin and he is one of the oldest members and a past Commodore

Mr. and Mrs. Denison were married in 1892, Mrs. Denison being the daughter of Mr. and Mrs. J. Grant Smith, two very old residents of the Colony, and a niece of the late Mr. Findlay Smith who was a resident here for nearly sixty years.

Mr. and Mrs. Denison leave today by the s.s. "President Hayes" and will carry with them the good wishes of a very large number of friends among whom they have spent the greater part of their lives.

## AMATEUR 'TEC

ALLEGED PLOT TO KILL HUSBAND.

TEA-HOUSE WHISPERS.

How an alleged plot in which a wife is concerned, to kill her husband, was revealed by an amateur detective who followed up a clue, is now being investigated by the police.

Yesterday, the Yaumati Police Court was crowded with spectators who wanted to see what the woman looked like but they were denied.

It is alleged that a young barber was in a tea-house with several bad characters. From snatches of their conversation, a Chinese suspected that something was amiss. He trailed them to different places in Yaumati and then informed the Police.

The husband in the case is the master of a ricsha depot. His wife is middle-aged and they have been married for about twenty years. The master of the house recently took a concubine. He is said to be in the habit of visiting a friend's house regularly every night to partake of opium. The amateur detective is believed to have seen the barber, and one of the gang of rowdies—a man with one eye—standing outside the friend's house to identify the husband. The barber and the one-eyed man are alleged to have conversed in the tea-house where the plot was hatched. Ten dollars "bargain money" is stated to have been paid over by the barber five dollars went for "expenses" and the balance of $85 out of a total of $100 promised for the job, was deposited with a shop. Half of the deposit receipt is said to have been found on the barber, who in turn is suspected of having been given the money by the woman.

The barber and the woman are now in custody, pending inquiries.

When the detective went to the rendezvous, he arrested—after a struggle—the one-eyed man, on whom was found a dagger. On a charge of being in possession with an unlawful motive, this man was yesterday sentenced to a year's hard labour.

## SUSPICIOUS

## CAUGHT A THIEF

While at Shaukiwan Road yesterday, Sanitary Inspector Taylor overheard a washerman's complaint to another that he lost a quantity of clothing put out to dry. Shortly after, the Inspector was proceeding on his motor cycle along the reclamation when he spied a man carrying a bundle in circumstances which appeared to be suspicious. A search brought to light the missing clothing, which was identified by the owner.

These facts were mentioned by Sub-Inspector Field when the man was charged with larceny before Mr. R. E. Lindseil at the Central Magistracy this morning and a plea of "guilty" was tendered.

The sentence was fourteen days hard labour.

## BOMB CASE

### LAST OF ARRESTED MEN DISCHARGED. NO EVIDENCE

The charge of possession of bomb material at No. 24, Yee Wo Street, Causeway Bay, against the last of the arrested men (two having already been discharged) was dropped at the Central Magistracy this morning.

Sub-Inspector Field said that about fourteen coolies lived at the house and it was hard to get evidence against one particular man. He was satisfied with the further investigations he had made and did not wish to proceed with the case.

The "Magistrate (Mr. Lindsell) accordingly discharged the man.

## SKIPPER'S LUCK

## STOLEN WATCH FOUND IN PAWN

## ARTICLE RESTORED

The $540 gold watch stolen from the cabin of Captain Inglis of the S.S. "Seistan" was traced to a pawnshop, but the person who pledged it failed to be located.

Det.-Inspector Pincott applied at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning for an order under section 24 of the Pawnbrokers' Ordinance for the surrender of the watch without payment.

The necessary order was made after proof of ownership and the evidence of the pawnbroker.

### HIT IN THE EYE. CHARGE AGAINST AMERICAN SEAMAN

Harold Little, a member of the "President Jefferson" crew, was sentenced to one week's imprisonment and was further ordered to pay $5 as compensation in an assault case heard at the Kowloon Magistracy this morning.

Evidence showed that while on the way to the ship words passed between him and a cargo coolie and the man was hit in the eye. Following this incident all the coolies wanted to go on strike, but they were persuaded not to do so.
