# AND GENERAL.

An Empire Day celebration is being planned by the Tokyo British League. The celebration in Tokyo will be held on the lawn of the British Embassy, which has been offered to the League by Sir Charles Eliot, the British Ambassador to Japan. As Empire Day (May 24) falls on Sunday this year, the gathering in Tokyo will be held on the afternoon of Saturday May 23. A committee of the League is now busy arranging the details of the programme of entertainment.

Alberta's population at the end of 1924 was approximately 640,000, according to an estimate made by the Provincial Government. This is an increase of 52,000 as compared with 1921, when the last official census was taken. It is also estimated that some 60 per cent of the province's population is of British origin.

In order to encourage the farmers of eastern Ontario in better breeding of live stock, the Canadian Pacific Railway is operating a Better Farming Special Train over its Ottawa-Pembroke branch. The train consists of fifteen cars specially built for the purpose, having exhibits of pure bred cattle, swine and poultry. Some of these animals are to be sold during the trip, but the majority are for exhibition and instruction. Attached to the train are demonstrators and lecturers. After the train concludes its tour in Ontario it will move west and be used for similar purposes out of Winnipeg.

There were 98,215 street accidents in Great Britain, caused by various types of vehicles, an increase of 15,114 over the previous year. Of these accidents 3,631 were fatal—652 more than in 1923. Of the fatal accidents, 258 were caused by horse-drawn vehicles, 459 by omnibuses and motor coaches, 113 by trancars and trackless trolley vehicles, 241 by pedal cycles, and 2,560 by other vehicles.

While visiting friends at Belper (near Derby) Horace Bent, of Luton, died in tragic circumstances. He was out for a walk with his financee, a girl from Luton, when he collapsed and died in her arms. The banns had been published at Luton, and all the arrangements for the wedding were in hand.

Preaching in St. Paul's Cathedral the Bishop of London referred to his own early scepticism. After taking part in a Lent mission, said the bishop, he felt how completely all our hopes for eternity hinged upon the fact of the Resurrection. If the Resurrection was not true, he idea of a life-long friendship with the Son of God was any empty dream. "The consciousness of this, declared the Bishop of London, kept me from taking Holy Orders until the age of 26."
