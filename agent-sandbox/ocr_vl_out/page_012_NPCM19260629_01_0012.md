## EUCHARISTIC CONGRESS

RELIGON ADDS TO NATIONAL

SECURITY

A HUGE SUCCESS.

Pierce Butler, associate justice of the supreme court of the United States, addressing the Eucharistic Congress last night, dwelt on the importance of religion as a force making for social stability and world peace.

"All that strengths religion, all that creates self respect, exalts the sanctity—of the home—and—the integrity of the family, adds to national security," said Justice Butler.

Men only filled the stands for the candle light benediction services last night which began at dusk. More than 150,000 men gathered on Soldiers Field in the most solemn and most impressive picture of the congress. A light rain had ceased and a brilliant rainbow arched the east colonnade of the great stadium. Then came the solemn procession of cardinals and bishops and the great roll of voices joined in "The Divine Praise."

Thousands who were unable to gain admittance to the stadium heard the ceremonies through loud speakers. The ecclesiastical procession was more than a mile long.

The men's meeting was addressed by Cardinal O'Donnell of Ireland, and Cardinal Mundelein of Chicago celebrated pontifical mass. Three messages were received today from the Pope through Cardinal Gaspari, papal secretary of state, expressing joyous gratification at the success of the Eucharistic Congress.

The priests of the Eucharistic League will hold their 1927 meeting at Cleveland, Ohio, Bishop Joseph Schrembs of Cleveland continues as president of the league.

Almost 200,000 men filled the gigantic open air cathedral in the Soldier's Field. Memorial Stadium for the third general assembly and mass of the Eucharistic Congress. The first was the opening ceremony attended by all pilgrims and church officials and as many of the general public as could approach the stadium. The second was a service for women and the third, Tuesday night, for men only.

While the Sacred Host was lifted in benediction over the congregation, all those present, which included the greatest to the poorest, princes of the church and prelates down to the humblest pilgrim, made the sign of the cross with lighted candles. The altar was brilliantly lighted and the service was regarded as extraordinarily impressive.

## CHAOS IN CHINA

STRONG HINT TO THE POWERS

TO ACT.

The “Times,” “commenting in an editorial headed ‘Ungoverned China.’” emphasises “the lack of Central Government as the root of the trouble, and doubts whether one will emerge from the deliberations of Chang and Wu, and adds that the defence of foreign interests is thus doubly difficult, but that it is an inadequate excuse for inaction. The plundering of revenue and of hypothecated foreign loans cannot be allowed to continue.

The attempt of the Shantung militarists to float a loan and secure the salt revenue is a flagrant violation of the 1913 Reorganisation Loan Agreement.

The "Times" lauds the personality and vigour of the British Consuls who often mitigated the harm done to their countrymen and secured redress, and says, referring to the Customs, that the foreign Powers are not dependent on moral force alone since their fleets ride at anchor in the Treaty Ports, and it does not doubt that if violent hands were laid on the one remaining source of income for the Peking Administration, the Powers would take combined action.

It concludes that whatever the fate of the transient figures composing the Peking Cabinet, the Government, as an institution, remains permanent, and each successive Ministry may properly be invited to assume the obligations of its predecessor; and in case of refusal there is no reason why commitments and promises to the Powers by China should not lapse. The promise of tariff autonomy by 1929 need not necessarily stand if all else fall.—Reuter.

## CA' CANNY

## CHINESE RICE FOKIS CATCH IT

SILKWORMS DIE

Fakkai, Kongmoon, June 23.

The Canton-Samshui Railway Co. proposes to build a new line from Fatshan to Kongmoon. Engineers have been sent to Kongmoon to survey the land and map out the route.

The silk cocoon market at Pakkai has opened again for the third time this year. Business is very dull. Owing to the recent violent fluctuations in the weather, many silkworms died just before the spinning season.

Rice has gone up 50 cents a picul. This is not due to any shortage in stocks. The "temporary employees" have brought about the "ca' canny" in order that they may find work—as they have been idle for some time—they have insisted that the "permanent employees" (who "are embodied in a guild") should stop work for a time, or, in other words, "reduce hours for their mutual benefit.

## Current Gossip

On June 14, the inaugural ceremony was held of the Association (at Canton) of Merchants, Labourers, Peasants and Educators. (Note: It was this body, which recently petitioned the Canton Government to open negotiations to settle the boycott).

Local public opinion is reflected in the following current street gossip. The people are very satisfied with the local militarists! No press gangs are tolerated: Soldiers are ruly and there is no open corruption. The officers are brave and honest. But the Merchants' Protective and Anti-smuggling Bureau is becoming very unpopular.
