It only remains for the Prince of Wales to be given a title in South America and then his Royal Highness will possess a special designation for every Continent and almost all parts of the world. In the United Kingdom, he has a choice out of eight titles, but the North American Indians, when he visited Canada for the first time in 1919, were not content with these and coined one. for themselves—Chief Morning Star. The Canadians had already become accustomed, through their returning troops, to knowing him as the Scout, while the Australians knew him as Dinkum Digger. Maoris, the natives of India, and the Japanese have all followed suit during his visits to them, and now the natives of South Africa have dubbed him Shipping Sun, a much more handy name than Langa Liyakanya, which is the official spelling.

The King has approved of Dr. W. Cruli as German Consul for Hongkong, and Herr Franz Winkler as Consul of Austria for Hongkong.

"India does one delightful thing which I never met with anywhere else," says Mr. E. V. Lucas. "It is an unwritten law in every Anglo-Indian household that an extra place be laid at meals on the chance of someone dropping" in.

The reason why Messrs. John Brown & Co. obtained the contracts for the two Australian cruisers has been divulged. Their tender was higher than the lowest by £12,000, but their designs were such that this difference would be saved in fuel within 18 months. This does look like a feather in the cap of Clydebank.

A “League for the Removal of the Tax on Marriage” is being founded by Dr. Marie Stopes. She claims that marriage is “taxed to the tune of more than £20,000,000 a year” in consequence of a husband and wife's separate incomes being reckoned as one for the purpose of income tax. This, she declares, is a directly anti-social and, immoral proceeding, and is against the best interests of the State.

A driver of a taxicab goes to the Chamber of Deputies in Paris, and when he arrives he places a black cover over the flag of his taximeter. This indicates that he is not soliciting fares. He puts his cwb on the rank and enters the Chamber. He takes his seat on the parliamentary benches, listens to the discussions and sometimes intervenes. When the sitting is over, or when he considers that his presence is not required, he leaves, returns to his cab, takes off the black cover, and plies for hire. For the taxi driver is a Deputy, but because he is a Deputy he does not therefore cease to follow his occupation. For a number of hours every day he drives for a living. The cab is his own property and he is therefore under no restrictions as to hours. His name is Monsieur Adam, and he sits as a Communist in the Department of the Seine.

One rather extraordinary fea- ture of the lawn tennis boom in London is the number of people in the suburbs who have had tennis courts laid out in their gardens, not for their own use, but for that of other people. These ingenious suburban- dwellers let out the tennis court for the season, and they also provide teas; those who have space for two courts make a quite good financial result from it. It frequently happens that those who hire the courts bring along parties almost every day. This means several teas have to be provided, and each brings a respectable profit.

The Princess Royal is happily recovering from the sudden attack of haemorrhage which caused the Royal Family considerable anxiety. The King, who is a devoted brother, visited his sister every day. Her life was undoubtedly saved by blood transfusion. The title of Princess Royal is not inherited by the eldest daughter of the sovereign by virtue of her birth. It is a special creation. King Edward created his eldest daughter, then Duchess of Flife, Princess Royal shortly after his accession. The title is held for life no matter how many sovereigns may succeed during that lifetime. George III's eldest daughter, Charlotte, Queen of Wurtemberg, held it for sixty years. Queen Victoria's eldest daughter, the Empress Frederick, for nearly the same period. Both were intensely proud of their title, and on all State and formal documents signed themselves, after their foreign appellation. "Princess Royal of Great Britain and Ireland."

The King of Spain, who is 39, has, with rather grim humour, formed a collection of relics of the various attempts which have been made to cut short his career. The collection is a considerable one, and its contents range from the test of a feeding-bottle used in an attempt to poison him at the age of eight, to pieces of the Barcelona bomb, the skeleton of a horse killed by another bomb, and a fragment of the landau in which he was riding with President Loubet at the time of the attempted assassination in the Rue de Rivoli, Paris. Of late years, happily, the additions to, the museum have been few.

The members of the Royal Family are not, as a whole, attached to literature. The busy ones, of course, haven't time to read much; but nor, for that matter, have they the inclination. The King is perhaps the solitary exception, the only one who would read a book for pleasure and not merely because he had to, says a London writer. I once heard the Princess Royal say that if all the members of her family had to pass an examination in current literature in order to gain a livelihood, they would starve. The kind of books that find their way on to the royal shelves are official biographies and memoirs. Modern fiction is neither seen nor spoken of, and people like Chesterton, Wells, Bennett and Shaw are only known because their names will keep cropping up in the papers.
