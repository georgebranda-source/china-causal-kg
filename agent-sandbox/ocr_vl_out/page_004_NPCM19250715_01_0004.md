## BETTER CARS

If it is possible to get away from China's upheaval and Hongkong studies for a moment, there is a plethora of subjects with which to deal, including the discussion in the House of Commons on the rise in rubber. This, again, serves as a reminder of the fact that the reimposition of what is known as

the McKenna dubies on the importation of automobiles and parts into Great Britain may, at first sight, appear to mean little or nothing in the life of the overseas motorist, but a little consideration will show that before long it should mean a great deal. It must be conceded that the foreign car is purchased as a rule for two reasons; in the case of continental manufactures, which compare in appearance and general design most closely with British, cheaper labour and differences in exchange produce a cheaper article power for power, and in the case of American cars the manufacturer provides a bigger and more powerful, but not as efficient a car, for a given price than the British manufacturer who believes in high efficiency and the most scrupulous: cara in fitting and furnishing.

It may be safely assumed that if he has to pay the same price for a continental car of any given horsepower as he is asked for a British car, for reasons of policy and service, and apart from any question of sentiment, the British car will get the verdict, for the most pro-continental motorist must admit that nowadays the Continental produces nothing to beat the British manufacturer in automobiles construction. The argument against the American is similar, for while it will still be possible to obtain a large and roomy car at a moderate price, the price will not now be so low as to outweigh the other advantages of low upkeep, but and low petrol and tyre consumption which have such an appeal, and which have enabled the British manufacturer to go ahead in face of foreign competition.

creams in the output and sale of all British manufactures of reputation may safely be expected. The price of all manufactures is governed very largely by output, and while cost of materials increases in direct ratio to output, labour cost does not advance in the same manner. The value of labour-saving devices is more and more apparent, and overhead expenses are very much lower in proportion to the output. The latter saving is very considerable item in these days of high rates, taxes, etc.

It is anticipated that a year's working of the reimposed duties should result in a considerable saving in cost, which will practically all be applied to the benefit of the purchasing public. Britain's overseas trade, will, of course, share in these advantages and price reductions. There is no doubt that the renewal of the import duties will enable most British manufacturers to produce even better vehicles at lower prices.

## Mixed Labour

Chinese labour has had the Colony by the throat. Its methods have even been cut-throat. Would the Colony have been partially paralysed if the labour elements had been mixed and not wholly Chinese? Those who discuss the question say it would not. Why not then have cocktail labour—a sprinkling of various nationalities? It would not prevent the consolidation of labour or reduce even the potency of Labour Guilds; but it would reduce the possibility of dislocation caused by freaks of humour and childishness. It is reasonable to assume that non-Chinese would refuse to listen to the voice of intimidation, and refuse also to strike unless there were good grounds for so doing. Is the matter one of practical politics?

## Cash Payments

The conditions under which we exist have played havoc with the chit system. Those who have risen to the occasion and continued to cater for our creature comforts have rightly stipulated for cash payments: It has enabled them to dispense with clerical labour and helped that quick passing of money which means good trade and cheap prices. It has been usual to commence certain types of conversation with—“When the strike is over.” When it is over and becomes a set topic of dinner conversation, perhaps a part of the “settlement” will be some drastic review of the chit system. Its entire abolition does not seem possible or desirable. But there should be some diminution in the amounts for which it is possible to sign.

## LOCAL SHARES

### PROVISION FOR "CARRYING."

NEW ORDINANCE.

The text of an amended Bill providing for a postponement of the June share settlement was circulated last night. In addition to validating until July 21 contracts concerned with share dealings in which a settlement had not been arrived at on June 28 and providing for the payment of 8 per cent. interest during the period of postponement a clause is introduced which affords facilities to buyers of shares for the June and July settlement days for getting themselves carried till the September settlement day. Such carrying is made dependent on the buyer giving certain notice and making certain payments.

This clause reads as follows:—

6. (1) In the event of a buyer not taking delivery of shares on due date at the contract price such buyer may, if he thinks fit, make, on or before the 23rd day of July, 1925, a request in writing to the seller to carry such shares for such buyer until and including the 22nd day of September, 1925, or until such earlier date (if any), as such buyer shall redeem such shares by payment of the moneys due thereon together with interest on such moneys at the rate of 12 per cent. per annum. Upon any such request as aforesaid such shares shall become the property of the buyer as between the buyer and the seller and the seller shall carry the said shares at the rate of interest at 12 per cent. per annum. Provided always that such request shall not be effective unless concurrently with the making of it the buyer pays to the seller all interest, brokerage and stamp duty which is then due from the buyer and also (where the price at which the buyer has bought is greater than the flat rate) the difference between the flat rate and the contract price.

The next clause gives similar facilities in respect of contracts carried over from previous settlements, viz.: 6 (2) The provisions of the preceding sub-section shall apply also to any existing arrangement whereby shares are being carried for the June or July Settlement day, as though such arrangement were a contract for the purchase and sale of shares for the June or July Settlement as the case may be and shall be construed accordingly.

## THE DELUGE

## STREETS CLEARED THOUGH INUNDATED

The trite maxim that "Tis an ill wind" applies equally to rain in certain circumstances. and yesterday's certainly did more thoroughly and with considerably greater despatch scavenging work which hundreds of coolies might have tackled in vain.

The number of pedestrians caught unprepared in yesterday's downpour and detained under shelter for the greater part of an hour when there was a temporary cessation was surprising in view of the warning given by the ominous clouds. Whilst it lasted, it was as heavy as downpour as Hongkong has experienced for some considerable time and if it had continued for any length of time there would probably have been serious landslides to record.

As it is, however, although the wake of the atmospheric disturbance left by the typhoon which entered the coast near Swatow has not yet departed, the worst that it has given rise to is a little temporary inconvenience, in some cases a slight inundation of the lower regions of the house through drains not having been cleared so regularly since the strike and in many cases of street floods due to the same reason and the tremendous tax placed upon the drains by the enormous volume of water and the refuse carried down to the main streets.

## RELIGIOUS TRIAL

## SPANISH INQUISITION RECALLED

#### COURT ADJOURNS. (Reuter's Service.)

Dayton, (Tennessee), July 14. After counsel for the prosecution and defence had finished their arguments on the defence's application for quashing the indictment in the Monkeyville ("anti-Christ's") trial, Judge Raulston took a motion under advisement and the court adjourned.

Mr. Darrow argued that the law on which the indictment was based was the first step towards fanaticism. He pointed out the bitterness caused by differences of religion and denounced by law as a breeder of hatred and bigotry, recalling the days of the Spanish Inquisition.

Attorney - General Stewart argued that the Legislature was empowered to control the curricula of schools.

The trial is of the school teacher John Scopes on a charge of teaching evolution in violation of recent State legislation enacted at the instance of the Fundamentalists, who insist on pure Bible teaching. Mr. W. J. Bryan leads the prosecution.
