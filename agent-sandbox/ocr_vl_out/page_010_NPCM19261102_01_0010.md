## NEW TURF ERA

## Betting Tax at Home In Force

## VOLUME MUCH REDUCED

## How Bookies Are Coping With New Situation

A new era of the British turf has been inaugurated by the operation of the betting tax, in which there is far greater interest on the race, courses than in the actual racing. Everything has worked smoothly.

A rumoured bovett by book-makers has not materialised but many preferred the role of spectators.

The immediate effect of the tax was, as predicted, a considerable diminution in the volume of watering.

Half an hour before the start at Birmingham, not a single revenue ticket was sold.

Later an enterprising individual did a brisk trade selling them at a penny each after he had purchased them at fifty for a shilling but he was soon stopped by revenue officers.

## Police to be President

The betting duty imposed by this year's Finance Act comes into operation to-day. The Government's proposal to tax bets was announced in the Chancellor of the Exchequer's Budget statement in April and in the interval all arrangements for issuing certificates and granting licences to book-makers have been completed. Race meetings to-day at Birmingham and Wye will mark the inauguration of the new tax. Three police officers with experience of race-course meetings have been appointed to assist the Customs authorities in the administration of the new law.-British Wireless Service.

## TREVERSA" TROPHY:

The sixth bi-annual race for the "Trevessa" Trophy is to take place to-morrow afternoon under the auspices of the Royal Hong Kong Yacht Club.

Twelve boats are expected to enter the race, which consists of rowing and sailing by the crew of lifeboats of ships in port round Channel Rocks, to "Kowloon" Rocks, to the Yacht Club line and to Cust Rock Buoy, and back to the finishing "line at the Yacht Club, North Point.

Spectators are welcomed by the Royal Hong Kong Yacht Club and launches are being provided to take them out to see the race. These will leave Queen's Pier at 3.p.m. will proceed to the starting point and then follow the race round.

Competitors should be in position by 3.80 p.m. and the race starts at 4 p.m.

## HARRY WILL'S LOSES

Jack Sharkey defeated the negro, Harry Wills in a fifteen-round contest at Brooklyn, winning on a foul in the thirteenth round.

The fighting was one-sided from the start.

Sharkey, despite Wills's advantage in height, weight, and reach, out-fought and out-boxed the negro in nearly every round, finding his nose, eyes, and mouth, and staggering him in several rounds.

Wills was badly cut and battered when the match was suddenly ended by the referee, who disqualified Wills for hitting with the back of his head, and holding and punching.

## CONTRAST PEKING1

## Foreign Firms Going Out of Business?

Now that the Canton boycott have been called off, and that reliable news agencies are reporting the return of normal shipping conditions in Hong Kong, all foreign business men in Peking and Tientsin are casting envious eyes to the South.

For business here simply does not exist. Hong Kong merchants and importers may have considered themselves unfortunate during the months of the strike but in reality they sat in the lap of luxury when compared with the conditions of foreign firms in Peking and Tientsin during the last four years.

The status of affairs here has reached such a pass that many of the great international firms announce that they will forsake North China within the next twelve months unless there is a decided turn for the better.

Legations, Not Shroffs.

There are scores of firms here doing almost no business at all—firms which four years ago had a profitable monthly turn-over of $500,000 or more. In addition to stagnation they have, in the aggregate, more than $50,000,000 owing to them by the so-called Central Government which makes no pretence of paying either principal or interest.

These great firms have been operating for several years on a basis upon which their overhead and annual outlay far exceeded their gross income. In other words, they have been eating into their capital, and now in many cases they will quit China before their capital is entirely gone. The Legations give no aid, but any they are “not bill collectors.”

## IN RUSSIA

## MISSING JAPANESE EMPEROR

Two young Germans, Di Kindermann and a student named Wolcht, who on a trumped up charge of plotting against the Soviet regime was solemnly condemned to death while travelling in Russia at the invitation of the Russian Government some years ago, have now returned to Berlin. It will be remembered that these two men, together with a number of German Popular officials, who on equally himay grounds were accused of commercial espionage, were in reality hostages seized by Russia in its determination to enforce by fair means or foul the liberation of the notorious Russian known as Skobolevsky, who was sentenced first to death and subsequently to penal servitude by the Leipzig Supreme Court for organising the Hamburg Communist rising of 1922.

## GERMAN DOCTOR'S EXPERIENCE

The stratagem adopted by the Soviet Government has now led to the exchange of the prisoners referred to, and the desperado Skobolevsky, whose real name seems to be Kryloeff had the satisfaction of crossing the German frontier on his way to Russia.

Both Kindermann and Wolscht have visibly aged as the result of the ordeal through which they have passed. They are very reserved concerning their experiences during the two years' sojotrn in Mogcow prisons. When the death sentence was pronounced they confess that they would be shot, and they wrote farewell letters to their relatives in Germany. Kindermann washed a shirt for himself in prison in order that he might, as he says, die in clean linen. A black cloth was hug out of the little window of their cell as a sign that their execution was impending. After three days of suspense they were told that they had been for the present reprieved, and they were removed to another jail. The walls of their new cell they found scribbled over with thousands of depressing inscriptions, dating mainly from the time of the revolution. Many of these merely contained the laconic statement: "I am about to be shot." Many of the signatures showed that the writers were Cosack officers. Finally the two Germans were informed that their sentences had been commuted to ten years' imprisonment with hard labour. It was not until Sept. 14 that they learned they were to be liberated, an official from the German Embassy bringing the welcome news. That evening, under a strong escort, they were taken to the Moscow railway station and sent to Leningrad, where they were placed on board a German stormer bound for Swinemunde.

## DRUNKENNESS

## INCREASING DESPITE PROHIBITION

New York, September 30.—Drunkenness in the United States last year increased almost twice as fast as it did in 1924 and now has reached its pro-prohibition level, the Moderation League, Inc., reports, announcing the findings of a nation-wide survey of conditions in more than 1,500 cities. Reaching the conclusion that "the Volstead act has failed utterly to promote temperance and sobriety," the report expresses the belief that a greater degree of temperance would be attained by a wise restrictive law.

The survey was the second conducted by the League, a similar finding as to the effectiveness of enforcement under the Volstead Act, having been announced last year.
