## MISSING COAL

LAUNCH MOVEMENTS

AROUSE SUSPICION.

COURT SEQUEL:

The manoeuvres of two launches lying outside Bailey's shipyard in the early morning of September 8 aroused the suspicions of Mr. Edwards, an engineer in the employ of Messrs. W. S. Bailey and Co., and as a result of his observations four men appeared at the Criminal Sessions this morning, one charged with misappropriating coal and the others with aiding and abetting him.

The launches in question were the “Poseidon” and the “TaiYuk Fong,” the former being a Blue Funnel (Ocean Steamship Company) launch operating from Holt's wharf, and the latter the launch of Watson and Co., Ltd.

The coxswain of the “Poseidon” was charged with misappropriating five tons of coal, at the Criminal Sessions this morning, whilst the engineer and 2nd coxswain of the same launch, also the coxswain of the “Tai Yuk Fong,” were charged with complicity.

The respective systems under which the coxswains of the launches were engaged were referred to by Mr. G. K. Holmes, Crown Solicitor. The coxswain of the "Poseidon" had coal supplied to him direct by his employers and the coxswain of the "Tai Yuk Fong" was paid $360 a month out of which he had to pay the crew and find the coal.

The “Poseidon” coxswain had 12 tons of coal supplied him on September 6 at which time there was approximately 4 tons in the bunkers. Between that date and the morning of September 8, 2 or perhaps 3 tons, at a liberal estimate might have been used, said the Crown Solicitor.

The coxswain and others on the "Poseidon" were arrested as a result of police investigation when the "Poseidon" arrived at Holt's wharf, Mr. Fraser, of Bailey's, having, telephoned to Captain Griggs. The coxswain of Watson's launch was arrested later, the launch on investigation by the police having been found to contain her full coal capacity.

At about 7 o'clock on the morning of September 8, Mr. Edwards noticed the two launches together lying outside Bailey's shipyard. Baskets were passing from the "Poseidon" to the "Tai Yuk Fong" which when he went to the water's edge to see more clearly he noticed contained coal. The "Tai Yuk Fong" took a list towards the "Poseidon" as loading progressed and then went to the other side and when it eventually steamed away it was on an even keel.

The case for the Crown proceeded no further than the outlining of the case at the Court this morning, the judge, Mr. Justice Wood, counsel, the Hon. Mr. C. G. Alabaster, K.C., (instructed by Mr. D. McCallum) and Mr. Elsley Zeitlyn (instructed to defend Watson's coxswain by Mr. Remedios) all proceeding in the launches to the scene where the alleged misappropriation took place.

## EXPORTING MONEY

## TRAIN TRAVELLER'S EXCUSE

NOT CROHING THE BORDER.

An excuse that he was going in search of an employee who absccaded with $1,600 belonging to a foundry at Yaumati was tendered at the Nowloon Magistracy this morning when a Chinese was charged with attempting to export $15 from the Colony and thus contravening a strike emergency enactment which is still in force.

Sergeant Morton said he did not wish to press the charge as the defendant when arrested on the train yesterday had been quite straightforward and told the same story.

In answer to a question, defendant said that he did not intend to cross the border. Another object of his journey was to await the arrival of his wife, who was on the Chinese side.

The money was returned to the defendant and he was discharged with a caution.

## EAST RIVER WAR

TERNITÓico GAINED BY

RAILWAY REPORTED OLEAB.

Considered an indication that the Reds have advanced materially from their original base in the campaign against the Chan Kwingming party, is the announcement by the Chinese section that passengers will again be carried on that part of the Kowloon-Canton Railway from Canton to Samchun, as from tomorrow.

At the height of their success, General Chan's army reached positions from which they could strike at vulnerable points on the railway. Civil transport was interrupted by frantic moves on Canton's part to rush reinforcements to the front. With the full of Wu-how the position—from Canton's point of view—has been eased.

## Conflicting Reports

Conflicting reports have been received regarding the fall of Waichow, the Chan stronghold on the East River. Some maintain that only the district capital by that name was surrendered to the Cadets but later reports claim that General Cheung Kai-shek's men are actually in the capital of the refecture (also of the same name) as well.

In certain places the army have got to grips. Reports which must be accepted with reserve state that the essentialities on both sides were comparatively heavy.

Meanwhile, writes the “China Mail” Pukkai correspondent, the pro-Chan forces under General Tang Pun-yan in the Ko-Lui (southern) peninsula are preparing to demonstrate against the Sze Yap districts (in Red hands) and the local Red commander has organised defences.

## CANTON MINES

LIVES LOST THROUGH RIVER EXPLOSION.

JUNK'S PATAL CRUSE.

At one o'clock last Thursday night, a junk struck a mine near Boera Tigris (Canton River) and although strenuous efforts were made to save the crew several of them were drowned.

This unfortunate accident took place through the junk men ignoring the repeated signals, reports the “Canton Gazette,” from the gunboat stationed there, for it to stop. The junk was evidently carrying contraband goods.

[Note: A warning by the Harbour Office, Hongkong, anent the mining of the passage near the Bogue Forts, has already been published.]

## CRUELTY CHARGE

## STRIPPING LIVE QUAILS OF FEATHERS

## CHINESE MINED

A Chinese, who was caught yesterday while in the act of plucking the feathers from live quails, was brought before Mr. S. B. B. McFlderry at the Central Magistracy this morning and charged with cruelty.

"How would you like to have your hair plucked?" asked the Magistrate. Defendant made no reply, but said later that he was a nowcomer and therefore not conversant with local customs. A fine of $10 was imposed.

## THE BOYCOTT

LATEST UP-COUNTRY NEWS.

(From Other Own Correspondent.)

Pakkai, Kongmoon, Oct. 8. All firms here, with the exception of British, have resumed trade. The Marion Barclay Hospital (Candjan) and schools are still closed. The situation generally is normal. Business is dull. The harvest has been good. H.M.S. "Moorhen" and H.M.'s armed launch "Kwong Lee" were here during the greater part of

There is a shortage of kerosine and liquid fuel. Merchants have attempted to smuggle the commodity in but their junkshares seldom got through the atrike pickets' cordons. All seized cargoes are confiscated.

The s.s. "Leung Kwong" (formerly on the Hongkong-Kong-moon run) is now plying between Canton and Wuchow. The s.s. "Kung On" was removed from Pakkai to Kong Mei, the owners' village, for fear of being "requisitioned" by strikers or the military.

## Pirates Dispersed:

Some of the men of the former commander at this port (Li Yatngok who fled to Hongkong after the first Red coup) have turned pirates, and established themselves at Pa Chung. A combined force of village guards and Kongmoon (Kuomintar) soldiers attacked the outlaws and dislodged them. Three pirates were captured. On his following being disbanded, Li Yatngok retired to Yanping, joining Leung Hungkal's remnants.

Pirates have often been active in the neighbourhood of this port. The Merchants' Volunteers (now known as the "M.F.T.en") have authority to patrol as before the Saikwan Massacre of a year ago. At present Commander Leung Hung-lam (a brother of Leung Hung-kai, the alleged anti-Red general detained at Whampoa) is at Chekhom and Yanping with his troops. General Li Chici-sum, the Red nominee, holds Summing, Kungvick and Sanwui but he himself is absent from the sphere of operations. Kongmoon is in the hands of Brigadier-General Chan Ming-shu.

Many junks have been commandeered to transport troops to Canton and communication with other river ports has been suspended for over a week but is now re-commencing.

During the present trooping season, there have been no pressgangs at work. Coolie volunteers are asked for. Even at $16 a month, there are very few coming forward. Accordingly the magistrate had ordered the prisoners on small offences to be put on to military transport.

## Miscellaneous Notes

The Commissioner of Police (Cheung Cho-wing) has been dismissed and Li Poon is in his place. The Commissioner of Customs is leaving for Home this month.

A Chinese who graduated in an American course of engineering has proposed establishing a waterworks at the top of Kwei Fung hill, outside Sanwui city. He is asking for subscriptions for shares and it is hoped that a modern supply of water will soon be available.

Saltfish is the only-import permitted from Macao but boats are now running here from Shekki.

Rice-mill workers are striking for higher pay. Negotiations have been going on and rice is a little higher. Premium on Hongkong banknotes (in exchange for Canton subsidiary silver coin) is 29 per cent.

It transpires that a Portuguese watchman was killed in the “Kwok Ning” piracy (reported previously in the “China Mail”).

## CANTON ITEMS

## STRIKE PICKETS ASSIST

FURTHER WEAKENING.

The action of the strike pickets in actually supervising the landing arrangements at Canton for passengers carried by the a.s. "Honam" on her last trip illustrates the general weakening of the strike directed against Hong Kong. The prices changed by the junka and sampans operating under their supervision are considerable and it is doubtless their need of such revenue that has led to the change of front, but the fact remains that no difficulty was experienced by passengers desirous of landing.

The “Honam” left again this morning with 400 passengers, nearly all of whom were Chinese. The “Fatahan” will leave tomorrow. No arrangements have to be made by the Steamboat Company for the “special tugs” which were deemed necessary to navigate that portion of the river which the Canton Government has stated is mined; what actually occurs is that the signal to stop is given from the hillside off Bocca Tigris forts, whereupon a boat puts out, questions about cargo and passengers carried are asked and a pilot boat proceeds ahead, deviating but slightly from the usual route.

## TO DISARM STRIKERS?

The strikers' executive have acquired considerable power in the absence of troops in the neighbourhood of the East River. The seizure of Annamite clerks in the employ of the Banque de l'Indo Chine is typical of their high handed actions. These clerks had walked through the French gate and presumably had the passes from the French Consul without which they are not allowed to pass through into Canton.

The disarming of the strikers upon the return of the troops from Wichow is looked upon as a certainty, as the Canton authorities are stated to have regarded the strikers' growing power with considerable uneasiness. but they have been powerless as yet to act in the matter.

## TRADE LOAN

WEDNESDAY NOT FINAL

CAN APPLY LATER.

Application for the provisions of the Trade loan were asked to be made by Wednesday, October 22, but the “China Mail” understands that this date was not looked upon as final, the idea in fixing a day being to have indicated the measure of the demand.

Many applications have been made, some of which are not thought to come under the category of needy relief and will therefore have to stand down. The sums applied for do not amount in aggregate to anything like £3,000,000.

The credit placed with the Colony in this manner will, however, be kept for the purpose for which it was asked and firms wishing to avail themselves of a portion of it may make application at any time.

'N.E. winds, fresh; fair,' is the weather forecast till noon tomorrow, for Hongkong. For the Formosa Channel it is 'N. winds, fresh to strong.' The monsoon will freshen along the coast and over the northern China Sea.

A meeting of the Sanitary Board is to be held at 4.15 p.m. tomorrow.

"Tijisondari" without permission were let off with a caution. ## AT AMOY

AVIATOR'S GOOD

FLYING.

HERE AT 5 P.M.T

The local offices of the Asiatic Petroleum Company, in whose hands are arrangements for his lapping, are in receipt of a cable announcing that Commander Di Finedo, the Italian aviator, arrived at Amoy at 11:45 this morning. He anticipated leaving Amoy and hour later and making Hongkong between four and five o'clock today.

Tokyo, October 17.—The Italian airman. Commander Di Pinedo hopped off for Kagoshima this morning.—Reuter.

Shanghai, October 18.—Commander Di Pinedo arrived here at 12.15 a.m. from Japan.—Reuter. Other world aviators have accomplished the journey from Hongkong to Amoy by air (roughly from 350 to 400 miles) in about five hours. Commander Pinedo's machine is similar to that of Major Maclaren, the British world flier, and is of the heavy flying boat type. The excellent flying time he has made since he left Japan speaks well for the new engine he had fitted there.

Commander Pinedo set out from Sesto Celende, near Rome, on April 22, his object being to fly to Tokio via Australia and the Philippines and to return by the route followed by other fliers.

## LOCARNO SUCCESS

A TURNING POINT IN HISTORY.

GERMANY'S SINCERITY.

Berlin. October 18.

Lord D'Abernon, the British Ambassador, in a greeting to the German Delegation on their return from Locarno, congratulated them on behalf of Mr. Austen Chamberlain who would always remember their spirit of sincerity and frankness, and added that the honour in taking the initial course of action resulting in the Treaty belonged to the German Government.

The Locarno agreements mark a turning point in the history of Europe.

A PREMIER'S TRIBUTE.

In a speech at the closing banquet of the Radical Congress, M. Painleve, the Premier, declared the Locarno agreement were the outcome of Cartel's policy and inaugurated a new era.

FRANCE'S PROBLEMS.

Nice, October 19.

At the conclusion of the Radical Congress, M. Painlevé promised that he and M. Herriot, would discuss the most efficacious means of dealing with the formidable difficulties confronting the country.

He pointed to Locarno, as evidence of the Cartel's policy of international peace and added that all danger in Morocco was now past... France desired neither conquest nor oppression, and was only looking for peace.
