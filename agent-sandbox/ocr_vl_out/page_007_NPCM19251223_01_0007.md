## BEATEN BACK

BIG ATTACK AGAINST WHOLE LINE.

THIS MORNING'S OPERATIONS.

(Router's Surgeon.)

A wireless message from Tientsin reports that after heavy artillery fire from the Kuominchun lines this morning at three o'clock they attacked in force the whole line from Hankou village to Meichang, driving Li Ching-lin's forces back to their reserve lines between Peitsang and Hsittow.

The Chihli troops were driven back four or five kilometres, and there are indications that the Chihli defence is breaking down as they are outnumbered by three to one. Feng's troops are expected to reach Tientsin to-morrow or even to-night. However, Li Ching-lin went to the front yesterday afternoon to organise a last stand.

The Japanese Government is sending immediately from Port Arthur two hundred troops to Tientsin, making a total garrison of 550.

It is stated that the situation is rather precarious in the Tientsin district.

## SOVIET'S NEW ALLY

TERMS OF TREATY WITH TURKEY.

(Reuter's Service.)

Moscow, Dec. 22.

The Press publishes the text of the Turco-Soviet Treaty concluded in Paris last Thursday between Mr. Chicherin and Tewfik Rushdid "tending to strengthen the exceptional friendship uniting Turkey and the Soviet," stipulating that if one were attacked, the other will remain neutral in relation to the first.

Each nation undertakes to refrain from attacking the other, and not to participate in any political union or agreement with one or several third parties directed against the other contracting party nor any similar union or agreement directed against the military or naval safety of the other party.

The agreement becomes valid on the ratification for three years of three protocols, which lay down:—

First: Each reserves full freedom of action in relation to other Power's outside the limits and obligations stipulated in the present agreement.

Second: Sets forth the “political nature” of the union or agreement aforementioned which comprises financial or economic agreements between any countries directed against either signatory.

Third: Lays down that the parties agree to arrange parleys to fix the method of arranging possible mutual differences that cannot be adjusted by diplomatic means.

## GREAT STORM

VERY HEAVY LOSSES IN JAPAN.

MANY SHIPWRECKS.

(Reuter's Service.)

Tokyo, Dec. 23

Northern Japan was swept by a storm yesterday, the losses reported including twenty fishing boats which capsized. 150 fishermen being missing in the Fukushima Prefecture.

Numbers of houses were destroyed and twenty people are missing at Aomori.

Forty vessels were shipwrecked, with ten casualties, at Muroran.

Ten men and six houses were carried away by a tidal wave at Oshima and Hokkaido, respectively.

Several deaths, casualties, and damage are reported from other parts.

## DRUNK AND UNFIT

EUROPEAN SERGEANT CHARGED.

FINED ON TWO CHARGES.

Before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning, Lance Sergeant C.E. Taylor of the Hongkong Police was charged on two counts of being drunk and unfit for duty and absenting himself from his station at West Point at 8 p.m. on Monday.

Mr. D. Burlingham (D.S.P.) said that the defendant had served since March 1924 and had been reported by the doctor to be suffering from a disease in the eyes. He was given the option of resigning or going before a medical board. No resignation had been sent in, and apparently failing to get a billet elsewhere, he took to drink and absented himself from duty.

Defendant pleaded guilty to both charges and had no explanation to offer.

Remarking that defendant had a bad record, His Worship imposed a fine of $50, or three weeks' hard labour, on each charge.

## DISHONEST COOLIE

RICSHA PULLER WHO

BOLTED

JAILED ON TWO CHARGES

A riccha coolie was charged at the Central Magistracy this morning with the theft of a letter case and alternatively with falling to report the find to the police.

The evidence was that a Chinese merchant inadvertently left the letter case containing banking documents in the ricaha at the Bank of Canton building. On discovering his loss the merchant ran back to find the ricaha coolie, but he had disappeared. He was arrested later by the police and reluctantly surrendered the missing article.

Defendant was sentenced to undergo one month's hard labour on the first charge and fourteen days on the other, the sentences to run concurrently
