The vernacular Press in Peking is unanimous in expressing approval of the step taken by the Chinese Government, and states that China has passed the test whether she would be successful or not as regards the revision of the Sino-Belgium Treaty.

The proposal of the Japanese Education Department to create an office of superintendent in the principal Government Universities in order to control the thought of students, was approved of at the Budget Conference of the Cabinet on November 7.

Premier Wakatsuki was plied with questions at his official residence on November 7 by Judge Sumami, of the Osaka District Court, in connection with the Matsushima Scandal case, Mr. Kawasaki, Vice-Minister of Home Affairs, also was tried on November 1 by Judge Sumami concerning the same case.

The Harbin correspondent of the "Shanghai Mercury" wrote on October 31: Three distinct shocks of earthquake were felt in the district of Chalantun one morning this week, one of them being so severe that a number of houses had their walls cracked. This part of the country was at some remote date a volcanic one and in 1901 there was an earthquake of considerably more violence than the one felt this week.

According to Bulletin Quarantenaire published by the Conseil Sanitaire Maritime d'Egypt, there were during the month of August last in Indo China 1,661 cases of cholera and 1,322 deaths from the same disease. Of this number 410 cases and 293 deaths were in Tonkin, 240 cases and 199 deaths in Annam, 272 cases and 226 deaths in Cochin China, 241 cases and 241 deaths in Cambodge, including the death of one European, 145 cases and 116 deaths in Laos, and 344 cases and 247 deaths in Kwang-chow-wan. There were also 11 cases of plague and nine deaths from the same disease.

A very dense fog enshrouded Shanghai on the 9th inst. Pedes-trians in Hongkew say that it was so heavy that they could not see more than 10 feet in front of them.

Four persons arrested in connection with the recent Kuomintang attempt to capture Shanghai were released by the native authorities on the 9th instant. Six others, however, are still detained at the Alladi headquarters.

A plaguo has broken out among the war refugees at Nankow and some twenty doctors have been despatched to cure the sufferers under the auspices of the Swastika Association, a local philanthropic society formed by buddhists.

No. 56 Hollywood Road was sold at the China Auction Rooms yesterday by order of the mortgagee Mrs Ho Shi becoming the purchaser at $30,000. At Messra Lammert Bros salesroom, No. 6, Prince's Terrace was also put up for sale at the order of the mortgagee yesterday. The property was withdrawn from sale at $28,000.

Hong Kong residents who are due back by the “Macedonia” which sailed from London on October 22 include Mrs. G. F. Brearley, Mr. and Mrs. Martin Blake and their two children, Mr. F. Cullan, Mr. R. C. Wallace of Taikoo and Mrs. and Miss Wallace.

Miss Grace Hutchins and Miss Anne Rochester, members of the Associated Press of America, with head offices in Chicago, have arrived in Shanghai, from a visit to North and Central China. They were guests on the 10th inst., at a reception given by the Chinese Journalists Club.

Another police mid was conducted on the Chinese Students' Union premises, 26 Chunk Wah Road, Shanghai, on the 9th instant. A quantity of inflammatory, comunistic literature was found, and two men who were attempting to escape were arrested and taken into custody. They are being detined by the Chinese police pending further investigation.

Since the destruction by the Japanese earthquake of the State carriages, motor-cars have been chiefly employed for the Imperial cortege. Since the beginning of this year carriages have been in the course of construction and seven of them are to be completed before the year-end, for the Prince Regent's use in the New Year ceremonies. The carriages are built of home materials, and are to be drawn by six horses each.

A certain foreign resident of Hankow was returning to town from the Rifle Range on the 3rd inst. and on his way was met by a native who asked him for money. On replying that he had no money on his person the foreigner was attacked and received a brick on the head which the Chinese had ready in his hand. The foreigner attacked his assailant and handed him over to the police.

An Interesting development in railway executive organisation is indicated by the announcement of the London, Midland and Scottish Railway, the longest railway group in Great Britain, that when the General Managership is vacated by the Rt. Hon. H. G. Burgess, on March 81 next, his office will be replaced by an Executive Committee composed of Sir Josiah Stamp, President, and four Vice-Presidents each of the latter to act as General Manager in relation to the group of departments which he will represent on the Executive Committee.

Police in the Chihli province of China recently committed a faux pas when they carried out the instructions of Governor Chu Yu-pu to the letter. "The edict had gone forth that bobbed hair was startlingly contrary to old custom, and females with shorn locks were promptly hauled off to the police station and fined anything from $25 to $250 gold. One day, however, the Governor's wife decided to try the prevailing mode, and she in turn was taken to the police station. And now ladies' fashions are no longer a matter for Governmental regulations in Chilli province.

Two Italian sailors were attacked by a crowd of rowdies on the Hwnchinkai, S.A.D., Hankow, on the 3rd inst. Seeing them in distress two American sailors went to their help; but on the mob increasing in size and violence, all had to run for it and fled to the Racecourse Ground. From there they were able to get a message through to the Italian gunboat, the commander of which promptly landed a squad of men since an Italian team was playing football on the Race Club Ground. The armed squad safely escorted the men back to their ships.

The trial of the men accused of taking part in the sensational Palace robbery at Bangkok some months ago has at last come to an end and judgment was delivered on the G3th retent. All the seven accrued were found guilty, one was sentenced to twenty years imprisonment, two to twelve years, one to nine, one to eight, two to five years. Pines were also imposed mounting to over $217,000 which was approximately the value of the jewellery stolen.

About 12.30 p.m. on the 3rd inst., whilst a "seaman named Heudrikson," of the U.S.A. Destroyer "John E. Ford," was getting into a sampan at the jetty in front of the San Pen. Steam Navigation Co.'s property, Hankow, he missed his footing and fell into the river, being carried down by the rapid current. "Naval officers made attempts to rescue him but their efforts were unavailing.

In its attempt to avoid a motor-bus a truck hired by the 22nd Infantry Regiment, fully laden with munitions, fell over a precipice and was smashed to pieces near Mishimamura, Ehimeken, Japan on the 4th instant. A sergeant, a private, and a purveyor were crished to death, while five others were badly hurt.

News has reached China of the resignation of Miss Hilda C. Bowser from the position of organising secretary for the Christian Literature Society for China. Lady Bucknill writing to a friend in Singapore says that the late Sir John Bucknill, formerly Chief Justice of the Straits Settlements, died quite suddenly and peacefully in his sleep. Sir John Bucknill, previous to going to the Straits was Attorney-General at Hong Kong.
