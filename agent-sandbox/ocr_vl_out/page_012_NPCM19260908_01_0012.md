## UNIQUE WEDDING

SATIN BRIDAL CHAIR AT ST. JOHN'S.

CATHEDRAL DECORATED.

A very pretty and interesting Chinese wedding was solemnised this afternoon at St. John's Cathedral, the "parties being Miss Alice Bernice Li, second daughter of Mrs. and the late Mr. Li Po of Heungshan, and Mr. Chow Chenglam, B.A., only son of Mr. and Mrs. Chow's Yee-on of Taiping, Perak, F.M.S.

Mrs. Li comes of a well-known Heungshan family, originally of the North, which founded its home in the southern district nineteen generations ago.

The Rev. H. Copley Moyle, M.A., officiated at the service which was attended by a very large gathering of friends. The Cathedral was tastefully decorated with ferns, flowers and palms which made a perfect setting for the ceremony.

## The Bride's Dress

The bride arrived at the Cathedral in a red satin bridal chair borne by eight bearers attended by her chief bridesmaids. She carried a sheaf of white flowers and Honolulu creeper. She wore with distinction a beautiful gown of white supple satin heavily beaded with seed pearls and crystal, the vest-shaped waist line of which was in-let with silver lacesa godes; with the long flowing skirt she presented a most graceful ensemble. The filmy bell shaped sleeves of silver net were caught at the wrists with orange blossoms. The veil was of embroidered tulle held in place by a pearl coronet; while from the shoulders hung a long satin train, edged with silver lace and orange blossoms.

The matron of honour, Mrs. Chow Yat-kwong, sister of the bride and daughter-in-law of Sir and Lady Shou-son Chow, wore a striking Chinese costume with long flowing sleeves and bodice of green and apricot in richly brocaded satin. The full "georgettes skirt was of rainbow hue trimmed with gold beads.

## Choral Service

The bridesmaids, the Missie Alice Chan, and Hing-tong Lau also wore Chinese costumes. Th vests were of gold lace over sati and the skirts of jade green, pin and mauve georgette trimmed with tiny gold rosettes. The carried bouquets of roses and maiden hair fern.

Miss Billie Seu and Maste Mark Wei were the train bearers and Miss Billie Mayes prettily filled the role of flower girl.

The bridegroom was attended by Mr. H. S. Yung of the Chartered Bank compradore department, as “best man.”

M

Messrs. Chau "Kwan-lam; Andrew Cheung, Cheung Wing-kue and Chan Leung-sze acted as ushers.

The bride was escorted up the aisle by her brother, Mr. Li Chorchi, who gave her away.

The service was fully choral with Mr. F. Mason at the organ. As the register was signed, Mr. Li Chor-chi sang Franck's beautiful "Panis Angelicus" (Heavenly Bread).

A reception was held immediately after the ceremony at "Dunheved," No. 33 Robinson Road, the residence of the bride's family, where a seven-tier wedding cake, a veritable work of art, and the numerous presents were admired by a large gathering.

Notable among the gifts were an elaborate silver tea service and an autograph congratulatory scroll in Chinese from General Chang Kai-shek (commander-in-chief of the Nationalist armies) and Madame Chang Kai-shek.

The toast to the health and happiness of the newly married couple was proposed in characteristically genial fashion by the Hon. Sir Shou-son Chow, to which the bridegroom suitably respond-ed.

In the evening a dinner and dance are to be held in the Kam Ling Hotel, West Point, after which the happy couple leave for Repulse Bay where they will stay for some time preparatory to their departure for Taiping, Perak.

### "WHITE GUARD."

PLOT TO OVERTHROW BOLSHEVISTS.

TRIAL OF RINGLEADERS.

The Soviet Press is giving great prominence to the trial of 27 members of the White Guard which began at Sevi:topol yesterday.

The. acceus... are charged with having attempted a counter-revolution.

Officers Bezemkow, Rapallsky, Detilot, and others of General Wrangel's Army founded a counter-revolutionary organisation in 1922 to carry on propaganda among the white officers of the Red Army.

It is alleged that ten members of this organisation, headed by Detilot, who were on their way to Odessa, aboard the steamer Irish, seized the ship and compelled the captain to land at Varna, where they linked up with foreign anti-Bolsheviks.

It is stated that the prisoners include Henrichson and his provisional Cabinet who were organised to take over the direction of affairs after the overthrow of the Bolsheviks.—Reuter.

## WAR NEWS

## CANTON'S THREAT TO KIANGSI

### Peking. September 7

In view of the dramatic turn of events at Hankow, increasing interest and importance attaches to the position of Sun Chuen-fang. Canton despatches suggest that he will become the next target of the Southern expedition, having been notified that unless he withdraws his troops from Kiangsi immediately, the Cantonese will begin an offensive against him.

The "above suggests that a telegram was probably despatched earlier and censored.—Reuter.

Earlier telegrams from Hankow show that on the morning of the 5th instant, the Northerners were still holding Wuchang in face of determined attacks by the Southerners who, according to Chinese despatches, have suffered severe casualties.

Meantime, a Southern force, stated to consist of a division and a half, is moving towards Heishan, twenty h² westward of Hanyang.

Shells from Southern guns began to fall on the Hankow concessions, by way of reply to the bombardment of the Northern gunboats, and fires broke out at Wuchang at several points.

At dusk on the evening of the 5th, the Southerners reopened their attacks against Wuchang, but still unsuccessfully, except, possibly, that they prevented the Northern reinforcements being sent to Hanyang, which is being heavily attacked.—Reuter.

## MUTINY ENDS

## DISAFFECTED ARTILLERY SURRENDERS

An official communique confirms the view that the artillery mutiny is ended, and that all the disaffected units have surrendered. The ringleaders will be court-martialled. The movement did not necessarily imply a plot for a military and political revolution. It was a rebellion against Government orders by a clique of high officers unsupported by the rank and file.—Reuter.

The Prime Minister's Office has issued an official note to the Press stating that there does not remain a single Artillery Corps which has not submitted to the Government.

The note declares that the law will be rigorously carried out, but not vindictively, in order to make an example.

It forushadows the withdrawal of martial law shortly.—Reuter.

## BANK ACCOUNTANT

## COMMITTED FOR TRIAL IN

SECURITIES RECOVERED

Evans has been committed for trial.

The prosecution stated that all the missing securities had been recovered.—Reuter.

The case against Llewellyn Evans, ex-Accountant, "Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation, on a charge of criminal breach of trust in respect of Government Securities valued at over 92 Rrs. 5½ lakhs came on for hearing on August 26.

Applying for a remand till that date, Inspector Hurst of the Bombay C.I.D., who is in charge of the case, stated before the Chief Presidency Magistrate that so far they had succeeded in recovering nearly Rs. 4 Lakhs worth of bonds of the property alleged to be misappropriated and some of the missing bonds were in Rangoon. Besides, the auditor's report on the Bank's accounts had not yet been received.

On Evans's counsel protesting against the undue delay of eight weeks in placing a charge sheet before the Magistrate, Inspector Hurst undertook not to apply for any, further remand.

## COAL STRUGGLE

## ATTITUDE OF THE OWNERS

The Mife Owners' Committee, after a three-hours meeting, after a three-hours meeting, endorsed Mr. Evan Williams's standpoint. "They communicated with Mr. Churchill yesterday evening, but in deference to the Chancellor's wishes, they decided to ask the districts whether they desired to reconstitute the Mining Association as a national negotiating body.

## Ministers Satisfied

The Central Committee of the coal owners met to-day.

Afterward it was announced that the Committee endorsed every word that Mr. Evan Williams, President of the Coal Owners, had said to Mr. Churchill and other members of the Government yesterday in maintaining the refusal to negotiate with the miners on a national basis. But in deference to the wishes of Mr. Churchill, it was decided to refer to the constituent District Associations of the coal owners the question whether they desired to reconstitute the Coal Owners' Associations as a national negotiating body.

The Committee decided to meet again next Monday to receive reports from the districts, it being considered unlikely that any decision could be reached by the districts before Friday or Saturday of this week.

The Coal Committee of the Cabinet met again this afternoon, and it is understood that they had before them these decisions of the coal owners' Committee.

It is stated that the Ministers expressed satisfaction at the line taken by the coal owners.—British American Service.
