# LOCAL AND GENERAL

An extraordinary general meeting of the Hongkong Philharmonic Society will take place at the Cathedral Hall, on Monday. June 7 at 5.30 pm.

Fines of $5 were imposed at the Central Magistracy yesterday on several Chinese for firing crackers in the vicinity of the Government Civil Hospital.

Captain Lorign (the Spanish aviator) is unlikely to fly his machine from Macao to Manila, because of the uncertainty of when his new motor will arrive in Macao from California.

A police report states that a Chinese escaped from the charge room at the Taipo police station while under arrest. The man's name is Lee Yuen, 21 years old and he was dressed in blue cotton clothes.

Evidence was taken at the Kowloon Magistracy yesterday in a charge of theft of about $400 in money and jewellery against a Chinese servant girl from a house at Woo Sung Street. The case was adjourned.

Three property sales are advertised to take place at the auction rooms of Messrs. Lammert Bros. on June 3 at 3 p.m. The properties that are being put up are: The Ming-Yuen Gardens, Leasehold Property, situate at 40, Bonham Road and Leasehold Property situate at Nos. 21, 22, 23 and 24 Hill Road.

For allowing his dog to be abroad without being muzzled or on a lead, Mr. Reid, of Broadwood Road, was fined $10 at the Magistracy yesterday. The caretaker of the Japanese Crematorium, Sookumpoo, was fined $40 for keeping four dogs without licences. Fines of from $5 to $10 were imposed on other offenders.

There were 314 deaths in Singapore during the week ending May 8, according to the statement of the Registrar of Births and Deaths. Pneumonia accounted for 44 of these, convulsion of 38, phthisis for 33, fever not specified for 21, and malnial fever for 21. Under nationalities there were one European two Eurasian 249 Chinese, 45 Malay, and 16 Indian deaths One death from bubon'e plague occurred. A ratio per mille of the population of 32.0 was returned.

Shareholders are reminded of the annual meeting to tomorrow, at Union-Building, of the Union Insurance Society of Canton, Ltd. (11 a.m.); British Traders' Insurance Co., Ltd. (11.15 a.m.); and China Fibre Insurance Co., Ltd. (11.20 a.m.).

The tenth Congress of the International Women's Suffrage Alliance is to be held in Paris, May 30 to June 6. Although Chinese Woman Suffrage has not yet affiliated itself with this international woman's organisation, it has decided to send a fraternal delegate to the conference to carry greetings from the women of the Orient.

"Mr. Chow" Shing-ting, the chairman of the Chinese Chamber of Commerce, Hankow, has sent in a long petition to General Headquarters complaining that in spite of the transport companies' advancing $300,000 to the railway company as transportation fees the military along the line are still freely detaining cars. Mr. Chow voices the hope that Headquarters will take the necessary measures towards protecting the cars in future.

Peking, May 13.—The Panshen Lama has circulated a telegram joining those in the demand that Dr. W. W. Yen assume office without further loss of time as Regency Premiers. The Tibetan dignitary dwelt upon the 'dangers which would ensure to the country if the present state of affairs in Peking were allowed to continue and voiced the hope that all civilian and military leaders throughout the country would co-operate with one another so that the country may settle down at an early date.

Billards enthusiasts are reminded of the exhibition by Mr. Claud Falkiner at 9 p.m. tomorrow at the South China A.A. headquarters in China Building. The famous British professional will meet Mr. S. C. Kwok, one of the best of Chinese cueists in the Colony. Mr. Kwok has been a finalist in the Hongkong championships. When not playing for any trophy, as he will be tomorrow night, he is likely to give of his best, his style being of the free and easy, and three figure breaks are not out of his reach.

Lt.-Com. G. W. Hoare Smith has been appointed to H.M.S. "Moth," in command.

# SOCIAL AND PERSONAL.

The appointment of Mr. E. T. Williams as a police magistrate at Penang, and also a member of the Board of Examiners in Malay, has been notified.

The long reported issuance of funds to the employees of the Peking-Hankow Railway Administration materialised recently, when all the officials received their pay for April, and were promised the arrears for January/February and March by instalments within ten months. It is said that Mr. Lao Chif-chang, the new Director of the Bureau is preparing to pay all the arrears of salaries to the dismissed employees shortly.

Peking, May 8.—It is announced that the Italian Minister will soon be transferred, and will return to his country at the beginning of June.

The Annual Ball of the Russian Benevolent Society took place at the Majestic Hotel on May 18, and proved an unqualified success. The ball room was thronged with dancers, close on seven hundred at least being present.

The engagement is announced of Joseph W. Brouwer, the Singapore Y.M.C.A. cricketer, and Edith Joan, younger daughter of Mr. E. V. Xavier, retired Kuala Lumpur postmaster, and Mrs. Xavier—M. M.

Peking, May 8.—The British Minister called on Dr. W. W. Yen this morning in connection with the liquidation of loans owing Great Britain by China. At the request of Sir Ronald, it is reported that Dr. Yen promised to present the question to the authorities, when a discussion of the liquidation of domestic and foreign loans as regard the use of the increased Customs revenues came up.

On arrival here from Haiphong, Madame. Herriot (wife of the ex-French Premier and President of the Chamber of Deputies) was entertained at the French Consulate. Madame. Herriot was accompanied by Madame Wilden (wife of the French diplomat).

It is reported that arrangements are being made by a number of leading Shanghai educators and merchants for the establishment of a commercial college in Hankow. Mr. Y. S. Wei, Mr. L. S. Tong and several other prominent businessmen are reported to have made contribution of funds and the college is expected to be ready to open sometime this September.

By the terms of a resolution adopted at the recent annual general meeting of the Shanghai Medical Society no longer will any account be taken of the nationality of those who may join the organisation. This means that after a lapse of years German and Australian physicians again will be admitted to the membership of the Society.

The Netherland Indian Government has declared Amoy to be an infected place or account of plague.

Ipon, May 18.—A mail bag from Ipon to Malacca was lost in transit to the local station last night and is still untraced.

Peking, May 8.—Evacuation of troops from Peking's historical centres is said to have been effected by General Wang Hual-ching. The places from which the military are being moved include the Confucian Temple, Lama Temple, Coal Hill Temple, Temple of Heaven and Temple of Agriculture.

On May 18 a fire broke out in a shop in Kampong Djawa at Padang. The flames soon spread to the adjacent buildings and it was not long before the whole of the market was one sheet of flame. All houses and shops were a prey to the flames. Lack of water was the reason for the rapid spreading of the fire. No casualties are reported. More than 100 shops have been burnt down. The damage is estimated at roughly 200 to 300 thousand guilders of which only a small amount is covered by insurance.

A telegram from Moscow states that Marshal Feng has been busy since his arrival there attending official functions arranged in his honour. He is quoted as planning to go to Germany very shortly to visit industrial centres.

At a recent meeting the Board of Directors of the Shanghai Chinese Y.M.C.A. accepted with regret the resignation of Mr. C. G. Hoh, for several years the General Secretary of this Association, who has been detained in Honolulu by family responsibilities. Mr. Z. H. Tong succeeds him.

Tokyo, May 21.—Seventeen Chinese connected with "electric enterprises" arrived from Peking, this morning, at the invitation of Baron Den, President of the Japan Electric Association. The party is remaining till May 27 inspecting the general condition of electric enterprises in Japan.

Nearly 400 people representing all the Volunteer organisations in the French Concession Shanghai were present at the dinner given on May 21 at the French Club by M. Naggar, French Consul-General, and members of the French Municipal Council, when high tributes to their services to the municipality were paid.

San Francisco, April 21, Liu Yu-ching 8½ feet high, arrived here to day from China. He is the tallest Chinese that ever came out of the Far East. A special bunk had to be arranged for him aboard the "Dollar Liner." President Lincoln. His weight is near 400 pounds. He is 38 years old and at one time was a member of the Imperial Guard at Peking. He expects to go into the movies.

Mr. Sllas H. Strawn, the American Commissioner and Chairman of the Extrateritorial Commission, is remaining in Peking to finish up what can be done at this time in the work of the Customs Conference. He hopes to make the trip north with the other Commissioner and to get away from Peking for the summer in time to meet his wife and daughter.

Madrid, May 20.—Archbishop Ventura Martin Garcia, the last Spanish Archbishop of the Philippines, is seriously ill and physicians feel that his recovery is doubtful.

John R. Freeman, Jr., of the Bureau of Standards of the United States Government, arrived in Shanghai recently accompanied by Mrs. Freeman. Mr. Freeman is the son of J.R. Freeman, Sr., who was formerly connected with the Whangpoo Conservancy Board here.

Mr. J. Merle Davis, general secretary of the Institute of Pacific Relations, Honolulu, was the guest of honour at a tiffin given by the Pan-Pacific Association of Shanghai at the Union Club on Monday, May 17. Mr. S. U. Zau presided and in addition to the address by Mr. Davis there was a talk by Mr. L. T. Chen of Shanghai.

Washington, May 20—Vice Governor Gilmore called at the White House to day for a final conference with the president before leaving Washington. He plans to sail for the Philippines on June 27. Subjects touched upon at their conference to day included the auditor bill educational and health questions.

Rev. Father Jose das Neves, Vicnr-General of Portuguese Timor, and Father Alberto Goncalves, arrived in Malacca on May 11, en route to Portugal. Father Jose has laboured in Timor as a missionary for 29 years without a break, and has held the office of vicar-general for the 1st 25 years. He is now going home on leave. The fathers left Malacca on May 14.
