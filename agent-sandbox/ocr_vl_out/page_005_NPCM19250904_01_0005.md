## NEAR THE END

SEAMEN'S STRIKE FIZZLING OUT.

FULL CREWS FOR ALL SHIP

(Reuter's Service.)

London, Sept. 3.

In spite of "the splendid strike position," claimed by Mr. Shin-well, all ships are sailing from Liverpool where there is no lack of men.

Full crews were obtained at Hull for all ships due to leave, including the "Port Napier" for Australia and elsewhere.

The strikers' places are being filled without difficulty and the dispute is apparently nearing the end.

## CAPETOWN SITUATION

### (Reuter's Service.) Clanetown, Sen

The strike situation remains unchanged.

The crews of twelve steamers at South African ports, totalling over 2,300 men, are now definitely on strike and other ships are being held up.

The cross-Channel service from Southampton was partially resumed to-day, The Royal Mail Tiner Glamorganshire has all-ed.

Two steamers have left Southampton, one for Jersey.

## EARLIER CABLES

The Steamers Piako and Bristol City both "sailed on scheduled time. The Piako obtained nearly a full crew from Bristol, and picked up the remainder in Barry Roads. The Bristol City similarly shipped the remainder of her crew in Walton Bay.

The latest move of the Liverpool strikers is for pickets to sign on Liverpool liners and when aboard to threaten the union men who refused to join the strikers.

The Cunard liners Carmania and Aurania and the Canadian Pacific Liner Montcalm are held up, firemen and seamen having struck, but officials are confident that new crews will be forthcoming. Men are arriving at Liverpool from all over the country in the hope of finding employment.—Reuter.

HOLIDAY MAKERS STRANDED.

At a mass meeting 150 seamen of Middlesborough decided to join the strike and appointed a strike committee and pickets. Holiday-makers are stranded at Jersey where no mail arrived today.—Reuter.

(Router's American Service.)

Detroit, Sept. 3.

Mr. Kellogg, Secretary of State, in his speech yesterday evening, intimated that Mr. Silas Strawn had been appointed special commissioner to the proposed commission on extraterritoriality, treaty revision and other special privilege questions for which the date had not yet been fixed.

Mr. Silas Hardy Strawn is a lawyer who began his career at the Ottawa bar and later took up practice in Chicago. He is a director of the First National Bank of Chicago and several big commercial concerns. He has been President of the Chicago Bar Association and has held other similar distinguished honorary positions in connection with the legal profession.

## EARLNER NEWS

The Secretary of State, Mr. Kellogg, in a speech at the annual meeting of the American Bar Association at Detroit, described the American policy toward China. He said that it was "to respect the sovereignty and territorial integrity of China, to encourage the development of an effective stable government, to maintain an open door, to carry out scrupulously the obligations and promises made to China at the Washington Conference and to require China to perform the obligations of a sovereign state in the protection of foreign citizens and their property."

Regarding the Peking conference to be held on October, 26, Mr. Kellogg expressed the opinion that the Power would come to the conclusion that the conference would have to be broadened beyond the strict letter of the Washington Treaty and added that the United States Government was willing to consider with China the comprehensive revision of the Tariff Treaties. He believed the United States would be willing to give up territorial rights as soon as China could demonstrate her ability to protect foreign lives and property.

## £8,000,000 WARSHIP

THE BIGGEST BUILT AT THE TYNE.

A 35,000 TONS "NELSON.

(Reuter's Service.)

London, Sept. 3

The first post-war battleship has been launched at Armstrong Whitworth's on the Tyne by Mrs. Bridgeman, wife of the First Lord of the Admiralty.

The vessel, which is named "Nelson," is the biggest warship built at the Tyne. She has a displacement of 35,000 tons.

## FREE FROM SLUMS

## AUSTRALIA'S LIVING STANDARDS

"WHITE" POLICY UPHELD:

(Reuter's Service.)

Sydney, Sept. 3.

The speeches at a dinner given by the State Government in honour of the visiting presemen developed into discussion of the white Australia policy. The Governor presided. The Minister, Mr. Loughlan, opened the subject by explaining that the policy, on which Australia was unanimous, was not based on any idea of the racial superiority of the whites but on the well-known similarity of the whites and coloured in feeling, sent sent and other respects. Australia recognised the need for more people, but she did not want by wholesale immigration to introduce slum-dom and poverty into the country. She wanted to protect her standard of living which was at least unexcelled by any other nation in the world. (Cheers).
