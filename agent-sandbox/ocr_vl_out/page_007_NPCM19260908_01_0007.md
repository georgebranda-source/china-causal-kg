Following Yang Sen's seizure of the two Butterfield & Swire's steamers; Yang Sen refused to talk with the commander of H.M.S. Cockchafer, which was lying at Wan Hsien and insisted on discussing the matter with the Consul, who accordingly "proceeded to Wan Hsien from Chungking.

## ONE BIG UNION

LIVELY DISCUSSION AT "CONGRESS.

OVERNMENT CRITICISED.

A lively discussion at the Trade Union Congress centred on the resolution of the General Workers' Union demanding the discouragement of the formation of new organisations with "One Big Union" as their ultimate goal.

The Congress finally adopted an agreement asking the Council to call conferences of trade groups in order to promote the merging of separate unions.

Thereafter an emergency resolution was carried expressing concern at the tendency of the present Government to interfere needlessly with the liberty of the subject and to utilise methods of repression in industrial disputes, and protesting against the unwavering application of emergency powers notwithstanding "the peaceful atmosphere of the mining dispute."

The proposal of the General Council to impose a penny levy per member for the next three years in connection with the Easton Lodge labour education scheme, for which £50,000 is required for alterations and developments, was rejected by 2,441,000 votes against 1,481,000. Countess Warwick had donated Easton Lodge for labour education. Reuter.

## TRAIN OUTRAGE

## CONFESSION BY TWO CRIMENALS

HORRIFIED BY SPECFACE

Amested in a casual ward a musician named Otto Salesinger (21) and a merchanic, Willy Weber (22) confessed their responsibility for the railway smash at Lehrte on August 19.

They said that they were employed and penniless for months and decided to derail the train and rob the passengers and mail. They loosened the rails and hid in the bushes, but were so horrified at the resultant spectacle that they fled without waiting to rob.

The deed preyed on the mind of Weber, who confessed to his brother at his father's grave, after which the Police got on the trail.—Reuter.

LIQUOR AND BEER TAXES PAID

TREATY RIGHTS:

Foreign importers, including British, Germans, Russians and Japanese, have been paying the liquor and beer tax under protest since it was put into effect on August 23, and are obtaining receipts, hoping to recover their tax payments when the impost is abolished. Representations have been made to Peking by the British, American, German and Japanese consular officials protesting against the tax which is collected by a specially organised bureau called the Kiao-no Territory, Wine, Spirit and Tobacco Tax Bureau. As no tobacco has been moved since the tax became operative no taxes have been paid on that commodity.

### Tsingtao, Aug. 29

## The Cholera Scourge

Cholera has made serious inroads here and Tsingtao has been declared an infected port. Few foreigners have contracted the disease but the daily number of deaths among Chinese is from five to 30. The British Consul has warned his subjects against sea bathing, while the American squadron in port has issued orders to the officers and sailors to refrain from eating raw fruits and vegetables. The entire crew of the U.S. Seaver has been kept on that vessel for the past two days because of a case of cholera among the crew.
