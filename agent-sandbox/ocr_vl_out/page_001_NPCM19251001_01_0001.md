## PEACE MOVES

ASSURING NEWS FROM CANTON.

From the tone of a letter received yesterday by Mr. Wong Tong, it appears that some of the "points" raised at Tuesday night's conference in Canton will be modified.

Mr. Wong of the On Lok Yuen Co., was mainly responsible for sending the Chinese delegates from Hongkong to negotiate, with Canton, an end to the strike.

The letter to Mr. Wong stated that the military, civil and labour authorities of Canton were very friendly to the Hongkong representatives. Certain demands at first raised would be changed for submission to a second conference which was to have sat last night.

Mr. Wong, attributed the delay in news getting through to the temporary dislocation of the cable services. He was confident that excellent results would be forthcoming. His opinion was based on the warm welcome extended to the delegates, and the elaborate precautions regarding a private train with poured cars for prevention conveying troops. Mr. Wong felt that a much better tone would have prevailed at last night.

## MOONCAKE FEAST

FENNY'S GREAT CHINESE FENTIVAL

ENIERRES CONCUBINE.

Moin-eoke festival, or, Mid-Autumn Festival, or Chung-chin which to give its chiscal rendering, falls this year on Friday (to tomorrow the 15th day of the 8th moon.

Tradition places its origin about 2,150 B.C., when Wiedith famous which emperor Hao I (Hao Ngai in Cantonese). He shot nine of the ten suns illuminating the heavens when the sun-goet besought him not to make a butt's eye of the tenth. For this been he was given a motion which would make him immortal with a home in the sun. Before he could take the potion which had to be prepared in an turn at night with devout prayers meanwhile, a naughty concubine by name of Sheung Ngo-drinad, the last drop while her spouse slept through, the wake night service. Light as a fairy the woman fled to the moon to escape the archer's wrath. Every year she returns to mother earth and moon-cakes are eaten in her honour.

This episode has been commercialised into a favourite stage-play being one of the star numbers of Mei Lan-fung's repertoire. Mei will be remembered as the famous Northern actor who was down here in 1922. The legend has been slightly lengthened and ends up with Hao I being thrown out of the moon after a hot pursuit of his concubine.

It is a harvest festival in that the tea-houses reap their annual harvest. Moon-shaped cakes with wonderful ingredients are made months ahead of the festival and shipped to all parts of the world where Chinese are to be found. Chinese delight in the delicacies. All other aspect is the making and sale of beautiful lanterns with which Chimatown will be decorated with a night.

Actually the men-folk take little note of the religious side of the festival except to organise a feast at West Point. Schools and various working guilds make a holiday of it and it is also one of the four settlement days in Chinese business circles. The women, of course, must "chin-chin joss."

This business settlement, of course, will not be like that of other years, owing to financial stringency. With the announcement of the £3,000,000 loan, there is practically no possibility of a panic.

A special week of celebration is announced for the Lee Gardens, East Point.

Having secured control of the s.s. It will be recalled that some time after the vessel was taken to Canton, the authorities ordered that she be handed over to the Government, the pretext given being that she had military officers on board when she was seized, and that, moreover, political documents were found on the ship. The decision to transfer the steamer to the Canton Navy was taken at a meeting of the Central Executive Committee of the Government recently held, at which it was pointed out that the vessel was strongly built of steel and was capable of doing a good speed, thus making her a valuable addition to the naval transport fleet. The "Wah Shan" is a small coasting steamer of about 300 tons, and was employed on the run between Hongkong, Macao and Kwang-chow-wan. Her utterly illegal seizure whilst engaged in the transport of merchandise to Hongkong, says "Shipping and Engineering," constituted an set of hostility to Hongkong, in that it was part and parcel of the Canton scheme to cut off food supplies from the Colony, and the taking over of the vessel by the Canton Government for incorporation in the so-called Navy in our country equally irregular.
