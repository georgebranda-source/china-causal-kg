A friend of King Edward, discussing the latest and most ambitious effort of the Prime of Wales in addressing the Royal British Association, expressed the view almost testily that no one knew what that fellow would be up to next. "Did you ever notice," he asked, "that the Prince never does anything that his father and grandfather regularly did as Prince of Wales, and does so many things that neither of them ever did? When King Edward was Prince and King George was Prince, you could tell exactly every year what they would be doing at any specific time. There would be Newmarket, Epsom, Ascot, Goodwood, Cowes, Sandringham, foreign spas, Scotland, and so on. Their visits to Paris would be regular and the routine would be observed there. Now the present Prince does nothing regularly except fulfil his official engagements. He never goes to Newmarket and hardly ever to any flat races, except upon compulsion. He has actually been heard to describe the Derby as a great waste of time! He had never goes yachting, he has no box at the opera, and he only plays cards if he cannot get out of it."

He believes that the mixed fruit and cattle-breeding farms on irrigated land offer the best prospects of success.
