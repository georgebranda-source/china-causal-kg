## WORLD TOURISTS

"CARINTHIA" ARRIVES HERE.

1 INTERESTING EXPERIENCE

By reason of a late departure from Chin Wan Tao, the world cruiser s.s. "Carinthia" did not arrive at daybreak as per schedule and she thus had to tie up off Kowloon until this afternoon when she moved into the wharf.

The 379 passengers on board were landed by means of the ferry boat "Evening Star" and the "Poseidon" at about 11:30 a.m. The original arrangements for detailed tours were abandoned on account of the late arrival and to-day is to be spent in individual shopping. The dozen and more ears engaged for the tour which had been kept waiting at Blake Pier since early morning were dismissed and the tourist "invasion" was carried out on foot.

Before their landing the passengers were treated to a display of aeronautics by airmen from the "Hermes" which was close to the "Carinthia" as she lay at the buoy. The launching and landing of the seaplanes occasioned considerable interest and the airmen obligingly hovered around the tourist ship.

Although continuing American tourists it was interesting to observe the word "Liverpool" under the name at the stern. The "Carinthia" is the newest of the Cundard fleet of liners and was only commissioned in September. The Raymond and Whitecomb Co. have chartered the ship, which is an oil burning vessel of the latest type with some 20,000 tons displacement and has magnificent appointments throughout. The arrangements locally are in the hands of the Hongkong and Shanghai Hotels Co., Ltd.

The tourists on their arrival this morning were full of their experiences on the rail journey from Chin Wan Tao to Peking. Great difficulty was experienced in getting through the train to meet the boat at Ching Wan Tao. Mr. James Park, in charge of the department of communications, seems to have had the most trying time of all. His job was to get the train through from Peking and owing to the revolt of Chang's troops the line was blocked with troop trains and he was informed there was no right of way.

Persevering in his determination to get the train through, Mr. Park carried on very carefully which was just as well, for beyond Tientsin the rails had been torn up. Mr. Park seems to have had great difficulty in getting the train crew to repair the rails and their task was rendered all the harder as it was then midnight. The rails were eventually found in the river bed and the train continued with the aid of its searchlight.

## LEGATION ESCORT

For the journey from Chin Wan Tao back to Peking with the tourists, the Allied Legations provided the expedition with an escort in the shape of a specially manned train which went before them. Even then, however, great delay and difficulties were experienced as the tourists were moving against the traffic of troops. After numerous delays and the interchange of much chaff between the Americans and those of the troops whom they came into contact with who could speak English, the train arrived at Peking at 3 a.m. instead of 6 p.m. the day previous. Once there the whole party was delighted with Peking as indeed they are with the impressions they have already had of Hongkong. The ship is to stay at Hongkong until Friday when it is due to sail at 6 p.m. To-morrow and Thursday are to be occupied with conducted tours. No trip to Canton will be included in the itinerary.

While at sea there has been a continuous round of gaiety and entertainments for the pleasure of the passengers. During the past nine days there has been a series of talks and lectures on Japan by Mr. Donaldson, the Cruise lecturer, who lived for several years in Japan, where he was a teacher at various places. These lectures have proved a very popular features. Two costume balls have been held on the ship already.

Tournaments in all the deck sports are in full swing. There is dancing practically every night.

## TO-DAY'S WEDDING

PRETTY KOWLOON

CEREMONY

Union Church, Kowloon, was the scene of a pretty wedding this afternoon, the parties being Miss Ida Mary Webb, daughter of Capt. and Mrs. C. E. Webb, and Mr. Austen Spary, of the Electrical department, P.W.D., son of Mrs. Spary and the late Lieut. Spary, R.E.

The bride, who was given away by her uncle, Mr. H. Stainfield, was prettily attired in a dress of white satin and georgetta trimmed with beads and orange blossom. Her bouquet was of white chrysanthemums and roses.

Miss Nancy McEwen, the brides-maid, wore a charming dress of blue crepe de chine and georgette with hat to match and her bouquet was also of white chrysanthemums and roses.

Mrs. C. E. Webb, mother of the bride, was matron of honour and her dress was of champagne crepe de chine trimmed with beads.

The Rev. J. Horace. Johnston officiated, Mr. D. W. Waterton was best man and Mr. Charles E. Terry was groomsman.

After the ceremony a reception was held at the home of the bride's parents at 3 Ley's Buildings and the bride and bridegroom are to leave for their honeymoon at Macao, the bride's travelling dress being a grey costume with hat and wrap coat to match.

The bride's gift to the bride-groom was a silver cigarette case and that of the bridegroom to the bride a slave bangle. Numerous costly presents were received by the young couple.

## BOYCOTT MODIFIED

## CANTON'S NEW IMPORT SYSTEM

FOREIGN TRADE RULES

As quoted by the “Canton Gazette,” the Strike Executive has agreed that “the regulations restricting the importation of foreign goods formerly, were too strict for the merchants.”

The Executive says: "So as to express our friendly feelings between the labourers and merchants, we draw up a modified system of imports." The modifications published are:—

(1) Cargo of non-British manufacture that come directly from Shanghai, although they are marked with the word "Hongkong," are allowed to be imported.

(2) Goods of non-British manufacture which have been stored in Hongkong and transhipped to Shanghai for shipment to Canton by another steamer, are allowed to be imported, if they are proved to be in accordance with our regulations, by any bill of lading and customs permit.

(3) Goods of all countries, even of non-British manufacture, if inscribed with the word “Hongkong,” when transhipped to Canton from the coastal ports of Kwangtung, namely, Swatow, Hainan, Kwang Chow Wan, etc., are considered infringements of our rules.
