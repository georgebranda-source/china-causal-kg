There is a slight improvement in the floods situation in Bagdad to-day, but 5,000 men are still working all night protecting the embankment.

Ladies of the Royal Family departed for the King's cotton estate, 100 miles north of Bagdad, but King Faisal himself found temporary accommodation in the house of a wealthy Jewish merchant of Bagdad.

Great alarm was caused this morning by a strong northerly breeze which raised a choppy sea in the huge flooded area outside Bagdad. Waves breaking against the embankment threatened to destroy it within a few hours with the dire results for Bagdad, but luckily the wind fell before the damage became irretrievable. During the crisis, however, £2,000 were expended in sandbags, the prices of which soared up, in an endeavour to keep the waves at bay.

It is estimated that 800,000,000 tons of water have been spilt from the Tigris in the last three days with the result that a drive round the city walls instead of the usual arid desert, the vista is more reminiscent of Brighton promenade. Local feminine fashions are changing accordingly. Iraq ladies are taking to sailor hats.

## LABOUR LEADERS LEAVE

## CANTON SHORT OF CASH

In order to attend the Second Chinese National Labour Mass Meeting to be held in Canton for ten days, starting from May 1, 1926, representatives (including ten employees of the Japanese owned mills) from the local labour organisations "have left secretly during the past few days. When the first mass meeting was convened last year, each representative from various cities was paid his travelling expenses to and from Canton by the Communist Headquarters in that city. But this year, in view of the shortage of funds by Canton Communists, representatives from local labour organisations were given $30 each as travelling expenses by their respective Workers' Union. It is feared that after the representatives return here from Canton, they will cause agitation among local labourers. Apart from anything of a political nature, a new movement aiming at improving living conditions of workers in general is expected to be promoted by them, thus appealing to public sympathy, according to the "Shanghai Mainichi."

Chicago, March 18.

A bit of the sen—1,000,000 gallons of genuine ocean water—is to be transported to Chicago.

Mr. George F. Morse, director of the $2,000,000 Shedd Aquarium, to be erected in Grant Park, is making a preliminary survey of the Atlantic, Pacific and Gulf coasts in search of a favourable spot, from which to obtain the sea water for the salt-water fish of the aquarium.

Synthetic salt-water lacks the organic content and chemical constituents of real ocean water without which salt-water fish cannot live, Mr. Morse said.

The entire shipment will require 125 railroad tank cura having a capacity of $8,000 gallons each. The $1,000,000 gallons will be stored in a huge reservoir.

"Once we have our full supply of salt-water," Mr. Morse stated, "we will be equipped for an indefinite period since the water can be used over and over again by virtue of a process of filtration and aeration. Evaporation will take place but this affects only the water and not the salts. We will simply add filtered water."

Some difficulty surrounds the search for an ideal spot from which to take the supply from the ocean, said the aquarium director. Water from an unpolluted place along the shore line is wanted, and the ideal location calls for a pier with railroad trackage so that the water may be pumped direct into the tank cars. Otherwise an additional handling by boats would be necessary. "We will select the shortest and easiest route available, all other things being equal, and ship the water about 10 cars at a time," Mr. Morse said.
