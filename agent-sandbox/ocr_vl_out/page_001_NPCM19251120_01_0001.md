## MURDER TRIAL

BUILDING

X YOUTH ARRAIGNED

The disappearance of it building implement is alleged to be the cause of a dispute between two employees, one an old man and the other little more than a youth. After high words had passed it is alleged that the young man who suspected the other of taking the tool, struck the old man with a knife in the abdomen. As a result of his injuries the man in question died at the Government Civil Hospital and the other is arraigned at the Criminal Sessions on a charge of murder.

He was represented this morning by Mr. H.S. Fitzroy and the Attorney General, Sir Henry Pollock, K.C., prosecuted.

The only alleged eye-witness is the man to whom belonged the tool which was the source of the trouble and on whose behalf the prisoner had taken up the cudgels. This man said that he saw prisoner strike the old man, with a knife and he (witness) then ran away. Later at a house at East Point he saw the prisoner who admitted that he had stabbed the man.

This witness was cross-examined, minutes by Mr. Fitzroy. Asked to account for the fact that it was he (witness) and not the prisoner who stayed away from work for two days and seemed to have most apprehensions, whereas prisoner went on with his work the witness replied that he ran away and hid because he thought if the police could not get the prisoner they would come for him.

Witness maintained that he had heard the prisoner make the statement as to having stabbed the old man. This despite the fact that Mr. Fitzroy pointed out that another man who had heard the alleged confession stated in the witness box that he was the only person there at the time the confession was made.

Medical evidence of the terrible nature of the wounds from which the old man died was given and cross-examined by Mr. Fitzroy witnesses said that they did not think that the wound could have been self-inflicted through the old man falling on the weapon clenched in his own hand.

The case is proceeding.

## PASSPORT CHARGE

INDIAN'S UNLAWFUL MOVEMENTS.

A FINE AND A WARNING.

An Indian named Chanda Singh was brought before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning to answer a charge under the passport regulations to which he pleaded guilty.

Mr. T. H. King (Director of Criminal Intelligence) said that he would not press the charge, but he desired to see a conviction registered and a warning given to the man as His Worship thought fit. Under the regulations, Mr. King, said, it was necessary for people like the defendant to have a passport or certificate of identity before entering the Colony. Defendant had served as a ship's guard from October 1923 to September this year, and resigned shortly after he was brought up on a departmental charge for bringing a man into the Colony without any papers. It was therefore desirable to keep track of defendant's movements. The man had recently travelled to Shanghai and back without a permit.

His Worship told the defendant that he should be perfectly aware of the laws of the country. Under the offence, with which he was charged he was liable to a fine of $250, and six months' imprisonment. He would be let off lightly this time with a fine of $10, but a repetition of the offence would be met with heavy punishment.

RED OFFICERS EXECUTED.

## LOCAL BOXING

OPEN TOURNAMENT FOR NOVICES

FINALS NEXT MONTH

It is proposed to hold a novices tournament open to all, on November 30 and December 1 and 7. The preliminary rounds will be fought on the first two days at Murray Barracks (by kind permission of the East Surrey Regiment) and the finals at the City Hall on December 7. The competition will be under Imperial Services Boxing Association Rules for competitions with any variations made by the Association's Boxing Committee.

Competitions, if entries are sufficient, will be held in each of the following classes (with maximum weight stated):—

Bantam 118 lbs.; Feather 126 lbs.; Light 135 lbs.; Welter 147 lbs.; Middle 160 lbs.; Heavy over 160 lbs.

Entrants never to have won (at time of entry) an open or open novices competition or to have taken part in a contest in which a money prize was awarded.

Entrants must scale within weights. Failure to do so will render liable to disqualification or to be entered in the class for which eligible at the Association's discretion.

Four to enter for each class. If less than four enter in any class, the Association will not present cup for runner-up and reserve the right to eliminate that class from the competition.

Entries (with approximate weights) to reach the Hon. Secretary e/q Messrs. Johnson, Stakes & Master, Prince's Buildings, by November 25.

Contestants to be weighed in at the Ring.

The Association reserves the right to refuse any entry.

Contests to be of 3 rounds each: The first 2 rounds will be of 2 minutes each and the third round of three minutes. In the event of a draw an extra 2 minutes round will be fought.

Any competitor not ready to enter the ring when called upon by the M.C. to do so, will be disqualified.

The Association reserves the right to make such further rules and conditions as may seem expedient.

Order of Fights

Preliminary Fights.— Monday and Tuesday, November 30 and December 1 at Murray Barracks.

Finals Saturday, December 4, at Theatre Royal.

Cups and trophies will, subject as above, be presented to the winner and runner-up of each class. Also trophies to the best loser in each class, if sufficient entries are received in that class.

No Entrance Fee.

The date of the draw for opponents will be announced after entries close.

It will facilitate arrangements if each unit of the Services will send in all names of entries from that unit, instead of each individual contestant submitting an entry.

## BRITISH AVIATORS' EXPLOIT

### (Reuter's Service.)

Cairo, Nov. 19.

Squadron Leader, Comingham and Flight-Lieutenants Bowley and Baggs who left Helouan at 7 a.m. on October 27 on three D.H. 9a machines on a flight to Nigeria and back, have arrived at Helouan on their return from Kano.

The Air Ministry had made arrangements for the dispatch this autumn from Cairo to Kano, in Nigeria, of a flight of three Service aircraft, from No. 47 Squadron of the Royal Air Force stationed at Heluan, Egypt.
