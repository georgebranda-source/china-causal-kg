## SWATOW BOYCOTT

BRITAIN AND JAPAN NOT EXEMPT.

ANTI-RED MEASURES.

DECLINE OF FEELING AGAINST FOREIGNERS.

According to latest advices from Swatow the anti-Reids are taking measures to ensure non-interference with foreigners there and to encourage a certain amount of trade but the Seamen's and Firemen's Unions are still a power in the land and it is still impossible to land British or Japanese cargo in Swatow. This, a skipper who returned from Swatow yesterday was able to confirm

Our informant stated that there is no lack of evidence in Swatow of opposition to the Canton faction. Ships which put in with rice for Canton are allowed to discharge at Swatow but no clearance is granted. Threats were even held out to a Soviet ship from Canton on its way to Shanghai with cargo and vegetables that soldiers would be placed aboard her and the ship seized. Clearance was refused.

The boycott on British and Japanese goods is still enforced, said our informant, and the proclamation which has been made calling upon the people to encourage trade does not apparently apply to these. Another proclamation states that foreigners must not be interfered with and the anti-foreign feeling would certainly appear to be on the decrease. Chinese servants have not, however, returned in large numbers and the consul, for instance, is still servantless.

The landing of passengers which was such a difficult task during the Red régime is now comparatively simple and sampans are allowed to go alongside ships and tout for passengers. Some ricshas will carry a foreigner in the street; others will not, but it is only in the course of the last few days that even a few ricshas have been available for foreigners.

Our informant, brought up in a nautical school in which discipline is considered the first essential for efficiency, was most unfavourably impressed by the untidiness and lack of system on board the Soviet ships he had the opportunity of observing whilst he was in port.

An incident, our informant actually witnessed during a brief period ashore, was the procession of armed soldiers with Chinese in the middle with his hands tied and a large mob following. Rifle shots were heard shortly afterwards and the information was the executed man was one of the principal officials in the late Red régime at Swatow. The charge on which he was convicted was that of “squeezing” money for the Chinese and using it for his own purposes. Although the subject is hardly one to trifle with, it was stated naively to our informant that the late leader's heavy expenditure was due to the fact that he had eight wives!

"RED" OFFICIAL SHOT.

The procession which, as already reported, was nipped in the bud, was to have been for the purpose of protesting against the proclamation in favour of resumption of trade and allowing passengers ashore. The leaders were informed that the procession would be fired upon if it took place.

The general opinion in Swatow appears to be that the "Reds" have to many domestic problems on hand at present to concentrate at present on the recapture of Swatow.

LOCAL INCIDENTS.

An enlargement, which can be only described as a fake, of a photo of the Shameen shooting, was found on a passenger arriving this morning on the O.S.K. steamer "Kaijo Maru" from Swatow and ports.

The owner of the picture was a Chinese. The print depicted for sign troops, under cover, and lined up for action, firing as a crowd of Chinese women with many dead bodies lying on Shakea but no Chinese soldier in. The vengeance! The owner was dealt with by the Police searchers.

## ABSENT PICKETS

## CHINSHAN STRIKERS NOT ON DUTY

NEWS FROM MACAO.

Alleged to have been “seen off” by a detachment of Whampoa Cadets, the strike pickets are not now on duty at Chinshan, the Chinese point just outside “the Macao border and” on the main road from the Portuguese Colony into the interior.

Accordingly, people in the vicinity who wish to get away are taking advantage of the opportunity.

From small, up-country ports in the Sze Yap, batches of people are also reaching Macao, having chartered junks for the purpose. The fare is $5 a head, this high price being necessary as the junky have to pay heavily for "safe duct" through a pirate-infested area which is not on the usual route. Pickets are stationed along the usual routes, hence the necessity to trust pirates.

The small steamers from Kwong Hoi city to Macao are still reported to be sailing.

## BORDER LOOT

POLICE INTERCEPT

RAIDERS.

CHINENE OPEN FIRE.

Details of another border outrage were received from the New Territories this morning, the scene being in the Shattankok district, where the strikers made a raid last Wednesday and carried off a number of pigs valued at $2,000 and also kidnapped two grocery-skip employees.

Emboldened by their success on this occasion, a large body of strikers and coolies paid a visit to the same shop, the Yuan Tai Tsan, on Sunday, and removed practically everything in the shop, including some more pigs.

They were proceeding with their loot across the frontier near Shataukok when they were intercepted by a party of Police, who fired a shot at one of the strikers. The whole crowd then turned back, and shortly after the Police party, which included some Punjabi soldiers, were greeted with a fusilade of rifle fire from Chinese soldiers who had concealed themselves in some bushes in British territory.

It is estimated that about fifty shots were fired at the Police, who were fortunately not hit, and they were forced to take cover without returning the fire as the ambushed party of Chinese could not be located.

Advantage was taken by the men carrying the loot to get away while the Police were covered by the rifle fire of their confederates.

### “MORE SERIOUS.”

## CANTON REPORT ON THE STRIKE

NOT "ANTI-FOREIGN."

Highly lauded by the "Canton Gazette" is a long survey on the recent situation in Canton by the correspondent of "an important news agency in North China."

The strike, strange to say, is becoming more serious owing to the strengthening of the strike organisation. It is hoped, however, that with the departure of the delegation, new developments in the strike situation may be expected. Very much depends on the nature attitude of Hongkong. Unfortunately she still demands intervention in Canton instead of pursuing a policy of reconciliation and mutual understanding. There is not the slightest trace of anti-foreignism in Canton. I learn from reliable sources that the Whampoa incident in which a Japanese launch was shot at with casualties already reported, has been settled in an amicable manner by both parties. Evidently neither side cared to give prominence to the incident. The blockade seems to have affected Canton very little. The import-export business is deeply affected, while the silk of this season has found a way out directly to Shanghai, it cannot be said that the problem of the export of silk from Kwangtung has been completely solved.

## BRITISH CARGO

HUROPE TARIFF RATES.

## SNUB TO REDS

LABOUR CONFERENCE DEFEAT.

COMMUNISTS NOT WANTED.

London, Sept. 29.

The Labour Conference adopted the Executive's recommendations against affiliation with the Communists by 2,600,000 against 480,000 votes rejecting the third Communist attempt to refer the matter back.

Complaints of the Communists that the Standing Orders were invoked to burke discussion, evoked an emphatic denial from Mr. Arthur Henderson, the Secretary who said the Executive considered it was not desirable to waste further time on a question which the Labour movement had already decided.

Mr. Harry Pollitt, Communist, declared that whatever the Conference did it could not keep the Communists out, adding that the Labour Party should be wide enough to include not merely those who dined at Balmoral, like Mr. Ramsay MacDonald or stayed on a yacht at Cowes with Lord Inchcape, like Mr. J. H. Thomas.

Mr. T. Bromley, of the locomotive Union, said the decision of the conference meant the beginning of a heresy hunt in the ranks of Trade Unions.

Mr. Ramsay MacDonald was ringingly cheered when he told the Communists to come out of the Trade Unions and propagate their opinions honestly outside.

## SEIZED SHIP

PORTUGUESE BOAT AT CANTON.

NOW IN STRIKERS' HANDS.
