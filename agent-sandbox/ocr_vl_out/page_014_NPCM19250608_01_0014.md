## PEAK TRAMS

## COMPANY'S PLANS FOR THE FUTURE

## BIGGER CARS

NO POSSIBILITY OF SLIPPING.

Plans for electrifying the Peak tram and for the construction of new cars to carry more passengers were referred to at the annual meeting of the Peak Tramways Company, Ltd., this morning. In order to meet the capital expenditure involved it was announced that a call of $4 will be made on the $1 paid up shares.

Mr. Henry Humphreys presided over the meeting, and was supported by Mr. W. E. L. Shenton, the Hon. Mr. A. O. Lang, and Mr. A. S. Gubbay, directors: Mr. G. Rapp, secretary; and the following shareholders: Messrs. D. E. Clarke, J. A. Tarrant, A. A. Alves, J. P. Braga, D. Cox and W. Morley.

The Chairman said:—Gentlemen, I propose with your permission to take the report and statement of accounts and Auditors' report as read.

The traffic receipts for the year under review show an increase of $19,787,67 over the corresponding period of last year but the net profits are $1,756,92 less. This is accounted for by the fact that last year we made a non-recurrent profit of $16,765 on investments realized; the difference represents increased running expenses. The item unexpired season tickets no longer figures as a liability in the accounts as we ceased issuing these in June last. Under the heading of "of assets" "Mount Cameron Surrey" $3,000 appears for the first time. This survey cost $6,000 of which the Government agreed to pay half. If nothing eventuates from the projected scheme, this $3,000 will have to be written off the Com

I do not think there is anything else in the accounts which calls for any comment, but I think shareholders and the general public would like to know what improvements have been effected and what will shortly be undertaken.

The improvements to the upper terminus, virtually destroyed in the typhoon of August, 1923, are nearly completed. They comprise increased facilities for passengers in the shape of waiting room and cloak room accommodation also more platform space. They also comprise two up-to-date modern flats in lieu of one that was by no means up-to-date. One of these the Superintendent will occupy, the other will be let.

The Government has approved of the Company's scheme for electrifying the tram. The current will be obtained from the Hongkong Electric Company at a cost which it is estimated will not exceed that of our present steam power. This, of course, entails new machinery and an extension of the existing engine house to accommodate same. Plans of the extension have been approved and tender for same accepted. Our steam haulage plant is practically worn out and whether we continue with steam, or adopt electrical power, new machinery will have to be acquired shortly. Steam plant would cost more and has the disadvantage of being noisier and dirtier. The Government has consented to our having new cars to carry 55 passengers in lieu of 42 passengers as at present. This does not involve any material increase in the load is the cars, though larger and equally strong, are built of lighter material than the old. The Company desired to construct cars to seat 60 passengers but the extra 13 passengers allowed is a great advance and should relieve congestion for many years to come. The cars will be run on the double drum system, with two ropes, instead of by two drums and one endless rope as at present. This will do away with the possibility of slipping which has always been a source of trouble.
