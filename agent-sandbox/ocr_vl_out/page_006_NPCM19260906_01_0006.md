## OUR NAVY IN CANTON

As showing which way the wind is blowing in the relations between Canton and Hongkong regarding the boycott, British gunboats have taken action in Canton by landing marines at the wharves of the Hong Kong and Macao Steamship Company and clearing "therefrom all Chinese" This signalises the determination of the naval authorities that passengers and cargo going from these wharves to steamers bound for Hong Kong shall no longer be molested by the miscalled "strike" pickets.

It is clear now there is a complete end of pourparlers between the Governments of Hong Kong and Canton in respect to a settlement of the boycott by negotiation. Canton has been challenged to do its worst, but the British naval authorities have made it quite clear at the same time that that "worst" shall no longer include the molestation of passengers and cargo going to the Hong Kong steamers. The pickets may continue to be "cocks of the walk" on purely Chinese territory, but they will menace British lives and British property with impunity in future. That is the plain and very welcome message to be read into the action by the British gunboats at the British-owned wharves in Canton. The position is one that will be followed with interest. A period has been put to the policy of passive resistance to the annoying and illegal acts of the boycott pickets and the miscalled "Strike" Committee in Canton. They have had a long and a merry innings. They have now been told in the plain language of decisive action that that innings is at an end so far as it concerns passengers and cargo for the Hong Kong steamers. Any further illegalities in this direction have now been made impossible.

This is certainly the best piece of intelligence in regard to the continuance of the boycott that has reached us for many months. It is evidently recognis-

ed by the British authorities that mere talking over peaceful negotiations for a settlement is an utter waste of time. A sterner policy has at long last been initiated, which ought to have a deterrent effect on the pickets and the "Canton Government which "connives at brigandage and piracy on the part of the miscalled "Canton Strike Committee." If the long lane of the boycott is ever to be turned it is clear that it can only be through the medium of some such action as that taken by the British gunboats at Canton on Saturday. The challenge has been thrown down: Peaceful negotiations having failed through no fault on the part of the British, at whom the boycott is solely directed, resort has been had to naval intervention to ensure that there shall be no further use made of British wharves wherefrom to carry on grossly illegal acts against passengers and cargo proceeding to Hong Kong steamers. This may well prove to be the first step toward compelling the Canton Government to adopt the course it could and should have followed months ago, namely, sending the pickets about their business and ruling them instead of being ruled.

## Balance of Power in China

Much that is informative has passed through the wires during the week-end but as yet “conflicting reports” is the only apt description of news about the war in China. During the last seven days Wuchang has “fallen” and “held out” several times. There seems less doubt about Hankow and the Hanyang arsenal; these being also immediate objectives of the southern Nationalists. One factor which will be noted by those who are hoping for peace in China is that the struggle seems to be spreading to other parts of the country. From far-off Szechuan province comes a report of merchant vessels being seized to convey troops to Wu Pei-fu's aid. Marshal Sun Chuan-fang, ruler of the five provinces around Shanghai, seems to be moving at last, on the side of his professed superior. As his vanguard is rather far inland it will take time to discover whether he had actually entered the war on Wu Pei-fu's side. With the armies at his command, the supply of munitions and his strategic position, Sun Chuan-fang may become the deciding power. How much truth there is in his supposed gesture against the southerners remains to be seen. It is true that he can strike a vital blow and the rear flank of General Chang Kai-shek, by marching on Changsha in Hunan province. But, on the other hand, vernacular papers still give prominence to his emissary being in conference with Canton. Nearest to Hongkong is the possibility of hostilities on the Kwangtung-Fukien border. For a month, at least we have been told by private sources that the Fukien army was going to cross the frontier, having long cast covetous eyes on the richer territory of Kwangtung. It should be borne in mind that Chow Yam-yan, the Tupan of Fukien, is a subordinate of Marshal Sun Chuan-fang. Unless Chow (or his man on the spot) is acting without instructions, then it has to be assumed that Sun is helping Wu Pei-fu from a motive of loyalty, or he is out to preserve the balance of power.

## USE OF THE KNIFE

## MAGISTRATE'S ADVICE TO CHINESE

FIGHT FOR A GIRL.

"Use as many stools as you want, but don't use a knife, because you are liable to kill somebody," was the advice which Mr. J. H. B. Nihill, at the "Kowloon Magistracy this morning gave" to

a Chinese charged with assault.

The complainant, also a Chinese and an engineer in the employ of the Water Police, invited the defendant to a "square fight" over a girl whom both complainant and defendant were courting, according to the Police. Complainant seized a wooden stool and hit defendant, but the latter succeeded in evading it. Defendant, who is a butcher by profession, tried to avenge this by wounding complainant with a knife on the left knee, the latter being treated for three weeks at the Kowloon Hospital.

"In passing sentence, Mr. Nihill took into consideration the fact that it was the "complainant" who provoked the quarrel, and therefore did not convict the defendant, but ordered him to be bound over in $100 for twelve months, and to compensate the complainant with $10 or 14 days' imprisonment in case of default.

## DOUBLE MURDER

## SERIOUS AFFRAY AT WESTERN MARKET

## AN ARREST MADE

One arrest has been made in connection with a serious conflict at the Central Market early yesterday morning between a body of men employed as packers and members of a Triad Society, as a result of which two men were killed.

The arrested man was overcome at the scene of the conflict after a determined resistance, the police having to use bamboo poles to effect his detention. He was armed with a dagger.

The other members of the two gangs made their get-away before the police could effect their arrest. Efforts to trace them have been without success up to the present. A police notification that a reward of $250 will be paid for information leading to arrest has been issued.

The reasons which led to the fighting are not yet known and, the number participating is in doubt but it is thought that from 20 to 30 men were engaged. The trouble started some time between 7-30 and 8 a.m. and the conflict seems to have waged for a considerable time.

The two men killed in the fray are thought to have been members of opposing factions. One appears to have been killed outright, his body being found lying at the eastern end of New Market Street, where the fight actually took place. The other was found at the other side of the market at the Morrison Street entrance and it is presumed that he was endeavouring to leave the scene when he succumbed to his wounds.

Both men had sustained terrible injuries, showing signs of having been badly hacked about with choppers in addition to having nasty stab wounds. Neither has as yet been identified. They are thought to have been 27 and 32 years of age respectively, and were dressed in black pongee silk clothes.

The arrested man also sustained wounds apparently received in the course of the fight and, together with a constable who took part in his arrest, has been admitted to hospital. It is alleged that the constable was wounded by the arrested man. Neither wounds are thought to be serious.

## Latest Details

Further details of the array were gathered on enquiries being made this morning.

It appears that the night was between rival parties who met near the market. The two deceased men were accosted by the members of the opposing faction, who counted the larger number, and an attack imined it by the one of the men was done to death on the spot, while the other, although mortally wounded, kept behind his assistant for a considerable distance before he fell dead. Tightly clutched in his hand was a police whistle which he used during the chase.

"When the police arrived on the scene one of those engaged in the fray escaped into Morrison Street, through Cleverly Street, and on to the Praya, where he was brought to bay by a Chinese constable. The man, who had already been wounded in the back during the fight, offered desperate resistance and brandished a dagger. The constable watched for his opportunity and dealt a blow which disarmed the desperado.

This man was taken to hospital, as also the constable, who received a cut in the wrist during the encounter.

No further arrests had been made up to a late hour this morning.

## CYCLE MISHAP

## MACHINE PUSHED INTO A DITCH

## (1) 

An unusual experience befall Mr. Littlejohn, of the Port Development Department, while he was motor cycling in the New Territories yesterday afternoon.

As the machine was passing Char Hang village, in the Taipo district, on its way to Fanling, the cyclist observed two men being chased by a cow and coming in his direction. The animal suddenly charged into the side car of the machine, the impact sending the machine and the rider into a ditch. The cow then bolted away through a field.

The machine was slightly damaged, but the rider was none the worse for the mishap.

## ARMED ROBBERY

## FISHERMAN AND HIS WIFE INJURED

## HAUL OF $615

An armed robbery was committed by four men on a fishing junk lying at anchor near Kau Sai, in the Saikung district, early on Saturday morning.

The men were armed with a revolver and a knife. During the intimidation of the occupants of the boat, Ng Sai-fook, the master, and his wife, the gang inflicted several injuries in their efforts to keep them quiet.

Ransacking the boat, the gang took money, jewellery and clothing to the value of $615.

The junkmaster and his wife were both sent to hospital.

## 20 ,000-YEAR-OLD

## COMPLETE REMAINS OF A MAMMOTH

FLESH USED TO FEED DOGS.

Prague, July 21.—The Moscow Academy of Science has just appointed the members of an expedition which will proceed to the lower courses of the River Amur, in Eastern Siberia, for the purpose of bringing back to Moscow the remains of a mammoth which have been found there, in a wonderful state of preservation in ice. The discovery of the mammoth was only made possible as the result of a fissure in a huge glacier embodied in the river bed.

This, the first complete mammoth discovered, is claimed to be more than 20,000, years old, and was in such excellent condition when discovered, that owing to the lack of proper means of transportation and the difficulty of taking the proper measure for its preservation, much of its flesh was used for feeding the dogs of the expedition.
