IN THE COMMONS.

Warm Discussion on Emergency Regulations.

London, May 6:

Their Royal Highnesses the Prince of Wales and the Duke of York were again present in the Gallery of the House of Commons when the debate on the motion authorising the continuance of the Emergency Regulations was resumed.

"A warm discussion ensued over a Labourite objection to the regulation dealing with the protection of property."

Mr. J. Batey (Labourite, Spenny-moor) saw in the regulation an attempt to enable the coal owners to fill the pits with blacklegs. He warned the Government that this would lead to trouble.

Sir Douglas Hogg (Conservative, South Marylebone) in replying said that the suggestion that the people should not be allowed to carry on their business because a body of Trade Unionists had forbidden it was the best justification for this regulation. (Loud Ministerial cheers and angry Labour cries.)

Mr. T. I. Murdy Jones, (Labourite, Pontypridd) hotly said that the Miners' Federation took up Sir Douglas Hogg's challenge. He declared that every miners' member would do his utmost to prevent the mine owners working blackleg labour. The regulations were a scrap of paper as far as the miners were concerned.

Mr. Jack Jones (Labourite, Silvertown) likewise warmly condemned the regulation, saying "Do your damnedest, we are prepared to fight."

The Labourite amendment to the regulation was rejected on a division:—

For..... 307

Aguinst...... 95

## Obstruction Policy

The Labourites adhered to their policy of moving the rejection of successive emergency clauses, but the amendments were rejected by overwhelming majorities.

Following a short debate on each clause Mr. G. Lansbury (Labourite, Bow and Bromley) moved the deletion of 'the regulation prohibiting acts likely to cause dissatisfaction among His Majesty's Force', Police, Fire Brigades, and the civilian population or to impede the distribution of food and other vital supplies, also the essential transit by locomotion. He provoked irational Ministerial laughter by declaring that already men were being interfered with because they were acting as pickets.

## Dark Threats

Mr. E. Thurtle (Labourite, Shoreditch) sought the erasure of Regulation 22 empowering the Home Secretary to prohibit meetings and processions where there was reason to apprehend that they would provoke disorder. Mr. Thurtle indulged in dark threats. He said that the West-end was now in the ascendant. When the East was in the ascendant "for every turn of the thumb-screw you give to-day we will give ten"

Sir Douglas Hogg paid a tribute to the exemplary patience and tact of the Police in difficult circumstances. It would be unfair unavoidably to increase their burdens. Nobody could doubt that scenes of disorder could easily be produced by irresponsible processions.

## STERN WARNING

Government and the Pickets.

London, May 8:

An official communique issued this evening says that abundant food and ample transport are available, but the strikers' pickets are interfering with their distribution. "This aspect will require increasing attention during the next few days, and all attempts to impede the free movement of the public or its food'shall be methodically and firmly repressed." The Navy has rendered prompt and effectual civil assistance at several seaports. No use for the display of armed force has been required.

It adds that the Government's policy to refuse co-operation from the organisers of the general strike should be maintained by all the authorities.

Insurance Agalnet Damage.

The British "Gazette" states that a considerable amount of business is doing at rates that do not show a panic tendency, though they are naturally higher than on May 3. At present they approximate from 2/6 to 4/2 per £100 for retail shops, according to the nature of the trade, whilst those dealing exclusively in foodstuffs and provisions are quoted at from 4/- to 7/0.

The market insurance against damage by rioters and strikers remains steady.

SAKEATVALA IN COURT

Intenced To Two Months.

London, May 6:

Mr. St. Saklatvala, Communist M.P. for North Battensen, against appeared in Bow Street Police Court. He refused to find two surities and was sentenced to two months' imprisonment.

His counsel told the Magistrate that accused had decided to conduct his own defence.

Saklatvala admitted the fairness and accuracy of the Police report of his speech. He said that his trial was intended for the public good. He did not regard it as a personal prosecution. He considered that in view of the present political outlook he should not be bound over any more than the Premier for the Premier's speech against a section of the community.

The Magistrate declared that no reasonable man could doubt that Saklatvalu's speech was most seditious.

## Effect on Currency

(Havas Agency.)

Paris, May 6

The papers unanimously consider the further decline in French, Belgian, Roumanian and Polish currencies as a result of heavy sales of depreciated moneys on the London Market in order to hold up and keep sterling on par with the dollar.

The “Petit Parisien” states reports compiled by the Finance Ministry distinctly reveal operations which affected in this sense the foreign markets.

M. Raoul Peret has had a long conference with the Governor of the Bank of France and financial personalities. The Council of the Cabinet are to consider the question this morning.

## Urgent Measures

Paris, May 6

The Cabinet has decided on urgent measures to check the fall in the franc.

The Minister of Finance M. Raoul Peret, after the meeting, said that the fall was undoubtedly due to the influence of foreign stock markets and the British banks seeking to obtain the largest possible quantity of sterling dollars. The fall was not due to the internal situation in France where the position of the Treasury was good and taxes were coming in regularly. The causes of the fall were only temporary.

## ARCTIC FLIGHT

MISHAP TO WILKINS'S

STRUCK A HUMMOCK

(Reuter's American Service.)

Fairbank, May 6

The single-engined aeroplane in which George Wilkins proposed to make his 1,000-mile Arctic flight had its right wing torn to shreds, the landing gear smashed, and the propeller shattered in striking a hummock of earth on attempting to rise.

This was the second attempt to hop off, the aeroplane's load having been lightened after the first unsuccessful run to field.

LEGISLATION AWAITED.

On inquity from official sources to day, the "China Mail" was informed that the Government had announced no decision regarding the holding of the Sanitary Board election for which Dr. F. M. Graca Ozoria and Dr. S. C. Ho are candidates.

As previously indicated in these columns, there will have to be legislation to get up a list of those who who are qualified to vote but who are exempted from jury service. Possibly, the exempted persons may be asked "to register their names if they wish to vote" but legislation—either by ordinance or by a regulation in the "Gazette"—will be necessary. So far the Government has not announced any decision as to what course will be followed.

## POLICE TRAP

## PRISON FOR ACCEPTING BRIBES

-

THE ONLY DETERRENT.

A trap set, to catch a Sikh Police constable who had been demanding bribes from a hawker with a threat to have him arrested for obstruction was described by Captain H. F. Bloxham before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning when the man was charged with accepting a bribe and misconduct as a police officer.

Three marked ten-cent pieces changed hands at Ice House Street and when searched before an inspector the coins, together with another 15 cents were found inside the pugarree.

The record produced by Captain Bloxham showed that the man had four year's service and twelve reports against him during this period.

Mr. Leo D'Almada, defending, said that accused had frankly admitted his guilt. He made a strong plea for a fine which would serve as a deterrent.

In imposing sentence of four months' hard labour, His Worship remarked that imprisonment was the only deterrent in cases of this kind.

## ARMED ROBBERY

## WEDNESDAY'S SHOOTING INCIDENT

FOKI IN COURT

The exciting early morning chase after an armed robbery committed at No. 35, Connaught Road West on Wednesday had a development at the Central Magistracy this morning when a foki of the firm arrested for alleged collusion with the robbers was brought before the Magistrate.

It will be recalled that one of the bound fokis extricated himself and gave chase to the man who was eventually shot in the neck by a detective at Hillier Street. ## QUI VIVE

CANTON TROOPS GOING TO

FRONTIER

NORTHERN INFLUENCE:

On the quil vive for developments extending into 'Kwantung province, Canton troops are now reported to be entraining daily (on the Canton-Hankow Railway) as far north as Shlukwan) to strengthen the garrison along the frontiers.

It is yet impossible to say what influence this will have on the situation in Kwangtung. The trend of events in Central China, and along the Yangtse Valley (writes a correspondent) is such that anything may happen.

Most of the troops sent North belong to General Li Chai-sum's army (or army corps). He controls the upper reaches (in the province) of the West River, and the lower stretches of the North River. Passenger traffic on the Railway has been disorganised for at least two days, owing to military transport monopolising the line.

## RUNNING DUEL

PICKETS DEFEAT STRIKE BREAKERS

"PUG" SAMPANS LOSE.

Another chapter has been recorded in the petty warfare waged continually on the 'Canton River' as a result of activities by the boycott pickets.

Native craft have got together crews who describe themselves as the "pugilistic sampana," their business being to convoy fast moving boats with passengers for the steamers bound for Hongkong.

Several fights between the strike breakers and the pickets have taken place recently. "Allies" of the "pugs" are the fish coolies who succeeded in beating off armed pickets and capturing even a number of firearms from the strikers.

On Wednesday night, two such sampans put off from Fongchuen, acting as guard to small passenger boats. When the pickets' motor boats have within range and gave an order to swing to, the sampans answered with a volley. After a running duel, the "pugs" were captured, together with their passengers, who numbered between ten and twenty. These, it is presumed, will have to undergo trial at the strike headquarters.
