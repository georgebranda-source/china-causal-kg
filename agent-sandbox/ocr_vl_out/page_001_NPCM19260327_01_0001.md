## BANK SUED:

A FORMER HONGKONG MANAGERS CLAIM.

FURTHER DEVELOPMENT

(Reuter's Service)

Paris: March 26.

There has been a further development in the suit brought by M. Durmarin, formerly of Hankong against the Banque Industrielle de Chine.

The contention of the Bank de Paris et Pays Bas—that it was acting for the Government—is supported by a letter from M. Robineau, Governor of the Bank of France to the Directors of the Bank de Paris et Pays Bas confirming the request of the Bank of France to various other Banks to furnish foreign currencies for the purpose of increasing the reserve which was being amassed to combat the speculation against the franc.

M. Robineau recalls that the Bank de Paris et Pays Bas suggested the Far Eastern market as being less susceptible to sudden fluctuations than the European and American markets; and, consequently, suitable for such operations.

The suggestion was accepted, and the Bank de Paris et Phys. Bas with other Banks subsequently placed at the disposal of the Bank of France, a certain reserve which contributed to reduce speculation.

M. Robineau's letter concluded by paying a tribute to the promptitude and competence of the Bank de Paris et Pays Bas.

[A Paris. cable of March, 2020 stated: M. Durnegin, till 1924 manager of the Hongkong branch of the Banque Industrielle de Chine, has brought an action in the Paris Court of Appeal against the Banque de wrongful dismissal, and says he was dismissed for refusing to carry out instructions which it was impossible to execute, and which, moreover, were calculated to lower the value of the franc. He says on May 7, 1924, he received a telegram from the Head Office ordering him to buy dollars or sterling on account of the Banque de Paris et Pays Bas to the extent of £100,000 daily. He refused to carry out the order because it was calculated to start a panic in Far Eastern markets. The Banque de Paris et Pays Bas statement declares that the orders mentioned were placed on Government account to defend the franc. ## AFTER 30 YEARS

NO "KING EDWARD"

Interesting reminiscences are recalled by Sanitary Inspector Horace Julian Knight, who is retiring from the Hongkong Government service and leaving for Home by the "Glenamoy" on April 7.

In the course of thirty years' residence, Mr. Knight has seen many changes. He maintains that he has witnessed nothing out of the ordinary but admitted, with a smile, that he could talk for hours about the old days.

It was on February 26, 1896 that Mr. Knight arrived here to join the Police force. This was the year after the first great plague outbreak when the authorities were particularly perturbed as the disease was not so well known as it is now. Those houses in Taipingshan (where Blake Garden is now situated) were still standing, not having been condemned and demolished as a centre of the dread epidemic.

## HALF—ICE HOUSE STREET

Within three years, Mr. Knight was transferred to the Sanitary Department. He has therefore been actually connected with the health work for 27 years, but in the time of the plague years in the nineties, the Police also did a great deal. Curiously, Mr. Knight has had very little service outside of the island.

When he first arrived, the Praya reclamation had not been completed. The great job was nearly done, but the sea water still reached that part of Des Vœux Road Central where Alexandra Building now stands. One side of Ice House Street was in existence as were Prince's and Queen's Buildings, but there was no King Edward Hotel. Mr. Knight has certainly seen the city grow. He remembers the old Harbour Office (next to the Western Market, now used as a branch post office, bye for dust-cart oxen and sanitary coolies' quarters).

At that time the reclamation of the Praya on the western part of the town had not been put in hand. Beyond where the Steamboat Co.'s Macao (Wing Lok), wharf now stands, there was a bay (trans run over it now), where the P and O boats tie up.

From the back of the Ko Shing Theatre site to the mall steamers was a very close call, the ships going there because of the proximity to the Harbour Office! This "bay" extended to Ko Shing Street, an offshoot of which is known as Kom U (Gold Fish). Street because of its association with something maritime. Today, of course, the "bay" is Des Vœux Road West, Connaught Road West, and the two rows of houses (back to back).

When that nullah came to be filled in, it was found that almost as fast as the material was dumped in; there was an accountable sagging somewhere which lowered the street level.
