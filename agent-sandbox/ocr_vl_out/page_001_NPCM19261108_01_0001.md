## NEW BOYCOTT

TRANSPORTERS' LICENCES

TO BE CANCELLED?

PROTEST OVER SEARCH.

Canton's "new" boycott comes into force to-day, Nov. 8, but it is not known if the threat to British ships will be carried out until a few days later.

The Transport Union (cargo toolies and curgo boats) in Canton has refused to handle shipments on the fleets of the Steamböat Co., the Indo-China S.N. Co. and the China Navigation Co.

Nevertheless, the s.s. "Liang-chow" (China Navigation) has brought down some cargo, en route for Shanghai.

From a reliable source the "China Mail" learns that the Maritime Customs authorities in Canton will cancel the licences of those concerned should cargo not be worked.

An association has been formed to clamour for the abrogation of Treaties and this is very active at the moment.

With regard to Canton's inspection corps to search all shipping, the "Cantor" Gazette reported that a number of Consuls had acquiesced. Such is not the case.

On October 23, the Consular Body in Canton pointed out that the Customs were the only body authorised to carry out searches on vessels.

Later on, the Portuguese Consul (as doyen), on behalf of the Consular Body, protested against the examination, in reply to Mr. Eugene Chen's despatch of Oct. 20.

Damage to the extent of about $100,000 was done on Saturday when the Strike headquarters at East Garden were gutted in a fire lasting two hours.

## WINDING UP

## KWONG CHOW HOTEL CO'S AFFAIRS

APPLICATION GRANTED.

An application was made in the Supreme Court this morning for the winding-up of the Kwong Chow Hotel Co., Ltd., of 578, Queen's Road, West.

Mr. C. G. Alabaster K.O., (instead by G. K. Hall Brutton and Co.) made the application, which was on the debtor company's own petition.

The petition, said Mr. Alabaster, stated that the Company was incorporated in May, 1922 with its registered office at 578, Queen's Road West and a capital of $100,000 divided into 1,000 shares. In the course of carrying on restaurant and hotel business debts had been contracted to the extent of $26,500, all of which were overdue and the Company was unable to meet them.

Two meetings of shareholders had been called but owing to insufficiency of numbers it had been found impossible to pass a resolution in favour of a voluntary winding-up order being made. The Company was unable to continue business and although an offer had been made for the purchase of the business as a going concern which would amount to considerably more than that of the debts, this offer would be withdrawn if the goods were sold in execution. The bailiff was already in possession in connection with a judgment debt for $7,000.

The winding-up application order was granted with the continuance of the special manager already provisionally appointed.

## STILL HOPE

COAL PARLEY'S AGAIN TO-DAY

OPTIMISM RESTRAINED

Rugby, Nov.: 7.

No definite results emerged from yesterday's long conversations on the coal dispute at the Prime Minister's residence, but the parleys are to be resumed tomorrow.

In view of past disappointments, optimism is restrained, but it is generally agreed that the situation has taken a more hopeful turn.

There was not a joint meeting between the parties to the dispute yesterday, each side meeting the Prime Minister and certain other members of the Cabinet separately.

An official statement issued later gave no indication of the trend of the conversations which were based on proposal submitted by the miners through the mediation, in the first instance, of the Trades, Union Council.

Some modification of these proposals was, it is understood, suggested by the owners, and was placed before the miners.

After consultation with the Trades Union Council, the miners decided to consider the owners' suggestions, which it is assumed have reference to the national principles which it is proposed should govern district agreements.

A "meeting of the Miners' Executive has been called to examine the proposals to-morrow morning, and in the early afternoon the miners' representatives will resume their conversations with the Prime Minister.

It seems probable that the discussion will extend over the next two or three days, and it is anticipated that the Miners' Executive will recall the delegates for a meeting in London this week, so that they may be kept in touch with the progress of the negotiations.—British Wireless Service.
