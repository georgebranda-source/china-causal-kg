## ANOTHER "TIGER

## N E W

Angry Growls And Never a Tree To Climb.

Following the stories of the presence in the New Territories of a wholly mythical monster, come reports of a tiger which a party comprising Europeans set out this week to shoot.

The story is being gleefully told in club circles of the all-night vigil of the party. The action of one bold spirit who squatted on a heap of stones armed with a rifle and with two kids as bait has come in for a great deal of amused praise, for there was no tree convenient to clamber up into safety and the tiger is said to have prowled about growling to himself all through the night.

The party were acting on information supplied by the villagers who have from time to time reported animals mailed and tracks found pointing pretty conclusively to the presence of a tiger in the neighbourhood. Although the mid-night sportsmen have nothing to show for the trouble taken in organising the expedition and running the risk, they are firmly convinced that there is a tiger in the neighbourhood to be had for the shooting.

There would appear to be a good deal of haziness in the minds of those who remember seeing the tiger which was actually shot in the New Territories as to what happened to it. Several statements were made most definitely to a "China Mail" representative this morning by people who should have known. "It's in the museum here" was one. "It was skinned and made into a rug and was either given to Sir Henry May, who was Governor at the time, or is in the museum," said another. "The skin was taken off badly and had to be thrown away," was a third version.

There is nothing improbable in their story of its presence there, for those residents who were in the Colony a few years ago will remember the body of the tiger shot by Mr. D. Burlingham in the New Territories being carried in the streets. A cast of the impression of another tiger's "pugs" was also exhibited in the window of Messrs. Lane, Crawford, Ltd.

Again, the presence of a tiger on the higher levels of the Peak was reported by a very well-known resident once when he was lying off the foot of Mt. Kellet in his yacht. About the same time "pugs" of a tiger or cheetah were reported in a garden in that locality and the explanation put forward was that the animal had swum across from the mainland at some particularly narrow spot. An explanation of its presence on the mainland is not difficult to give for there is jungle harbouring many such animals between here and Canton.

"From an official source the 'China Mail' learns that what actually happened was that, the skin was sent Home to be stuffed, the object being to have it placed in the museum here. CHINESE LAW POINT.

HOW MUCH LONGER.

(From Our Own Correspondent.)

Canton, May 7



Evidence as to the legal rights of a fourth wife under Chinese law was given by the fourth wife of a deceased Hongkong tailor in the Supreme Court this morning. The woman claims that to her care were handed two infant sons and that she is the fit and proper person to administer the estate.

It is the claim of a brother of the deceased that the fourth wife had no claim to administer the estate, even though the other wives were dead and that her application for letters of administration should not be granted. As the next of kin of mature age he claims that he is the natural guardian of the boys and the most fitting person to administer the estate.

The stepmother of the mother of the two sons in question gave evidence this morning. They said that the fourth wife had only acted as cook during the life time of the boys' mother and that it was impossible for the latter to have handed over the care of her sons to such a person (this being one of the claims of the fourth wife in asking for letters of administration).

Expert evidence was also called this morning according to which under Chinese law if the fourth wife could establish her claim to be the "compassionate" mother she would be entitled to administer the estate.

The amount involved is $189,000. Mr. C. G. Alabaster, K.C., with Mr. Eisley Zeitlyn (instructed by Mr. J. A. Gordon Leask) was for the plaintiff (brother of deceased) and Mr. Eldon Potter, K.C., with Mr. P. C. Jenkin (instructed by Mr. H. K. Woo) defended the fourth wife.

## JUMP INTO WATER

## THIEF FAILS TO ELUDE POLICE

Hemmed in between a European police officer and an Indian constable at the extreme ends of No. 5 wharf at Kowloon, a Chinese jumped into the water where he was ultimately fished out by the Indian.

This morning he was charged at the Marine Court with being a “reputed thief.” It seems that he is accused of having snatched a a fountain-peno from another Chinese. On cries of “gnatcher” being raised, the European chased him from the shore. end of the wharf and the Indian closed in from the outer end.

As no complainant was forthcoming, the charge was framed as stated. His excuse to the Magistrate (Lieut.-Com. G. F. Hole, R.N.) was that he had an altercation with a sampan owner about a fare and was pushed into the harbour.

A previous conviction for stealing was proved against him and he was sentenced to a month's hard labour without the option of a fine.

Inspector Bond of the Water Police prosecuted.
