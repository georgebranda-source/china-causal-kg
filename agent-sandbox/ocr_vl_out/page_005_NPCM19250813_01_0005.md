## COAL ENQUIRY

QUESTION OF PERSONNEL OF COMMISSION

OUTLOOK UNSATISFACTORY.

## London, August 12

Representatives of the miners and coal owners, separately and privately, discussed the proposed terms of reference and personnel of the Commission of Enquiry into the coal industry. The miners' executive sat for over four hours after which Mr. J. Cook (Miners' Secretary), officially made a statement declaring that the Court would command the confidence of all the parties concerned—the public, coal owners, and miners—only if these parties were fairly represented in the personnel. "A Court composed of great lawyers, captains of industry, with a few economists and politicians, would never satisfy the working miners if they themselves were excluded."

Mr. Cook also stated that the provisional terms of reference were not wide enough. The Miners' Federation intended that they should deal with every phase of coal production and distribution, including royalties. He did not doubt that the Government would agree fully to this, and added that the miners would see that the question of nationalisation came into the inquiry.

## Looking Grave

In a two hours' interview this afternoon, the miners presented the Mines Department with their views with regard to the personnel and terms of reference of the Coal Enquiry Commission.

The result of the interview has not been disclosed, but on leaving the members of the deputation looked very grave, Mr. Cook remarking that there was no agreement and no settlement.

The deputation will report to the executive of the Miners' Federation to-morrow morning.

## SHANGHAI TROUBLE

A SETTLEMENT REACHED

AT MILLS:

(Reuter's Service.)

The Japanese Consul-General, Mr. Yadu, and the Chinese Commissioner of Foreign Affairs, Hsu Yuan, on behalf of the mill owners and strikers, signed an agreement at 6.12 this evening, after three weeks' negotiations, which provides for the resumption of work in the Japanese cotton mills, involving nine companies owning 40 factories and employing over 10,000 people.

The Naigutwati mill, at which the riots started in the middle of May, has offered the wounded and the relatives of the deceased $10,000, which the Japanese Consul-Genry will retain until the trouble is completely settled.

The terms of the agreement are: — Firstly, the mill owners recognise the operating unions when organised according to Labour Union laws which the Chinese Government shall promulgate; secondly, no strike pay will be given, but as the reluctant strikers suffered through the loss of wages during a long strike the mill owners will compassionately extend reasonable help to the workers; thirdly, the mills will increase wages according to merit and also investigates the workers' living conditions and make necessary adjustments after consulting other mills.

The fourth clause solves the question of payment in depreciated subsidiary coins.

The fifth clause provides that Japanese foremen orderily will not be allowed to carry arms and the sixth that the mill will be dismiss workers if reasonably and will give due regard to their good treatment.
