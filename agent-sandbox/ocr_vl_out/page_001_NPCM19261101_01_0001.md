## Alleged Spiritualist Loses Her

The case in which evidence figured as to "magic ten" propitiatory to the "spirits" and an alleged midnight scene in the course of which it is alleged that a spirit took promotion of a woman dictating to the later of the house in which she should male a woman in question, came to a conclusion this morning, final evidence being given and judgment entered.

Mr. M. K. Lo addressed the court for the defendant (a Chinese na-countant who was alleged to have given the woman plaintiff $1,000 as a wedding dowry and then to have taken it to seek as a loan and not repaid it.)

The plaintiff, who was the former wife of the defendant, claimed for the return of the $1,000 and it was alleged against her by the defence that she had by pretence to supernatural powers, intimidated the defendant into marrying her. It was also alleged that she received the $1,000 marriage dowry and kept a promissory note for its payment on which she was suing.

### A "Pai Sun Poh."

Mr. Lo, addressing the court said that the only question in the case was as to whether or not the plaintiff did pay to the defendant the sum of $1,000 under an acknowledgment note as a loan, having already received it as a wedding dowry. This was denied by the defence which had put up a different story and he (Mr. Lo) submitted that the evidence bore out the defendant's story and that the plaintiff had failed to prove the alleged repayment in the form of a loan.

Mr. Lo asked His Honour to consider who and what the plaintiff was. As against her story as to being engaged in the harmless and innocuous occupation of a herbalist he submitted that from the evidence of the defendants His Honour could not but come irresistibly to the conclusion that she had held herself out to be something more than an ordinary person. The evidence pointed conclusively to her having held herself out to be a "pul sun poh" (one practising in the supernatural).

Mr. Lo, dealing with the evidence called for the plaintiffs submitted that certain matters went to prove that the statements made as to the money having been paid over by the plaintiff to the defendant were rehearsed. In support of this, he drew attention to the fact that certain of the witnesses were relatives of the plaintiff and referred to the evidence of the servant girl. Was it likely, he asked, that her mistress would have taken a servant girl so fully into her confidence as to explain that her husband or his firm was in financial difficulties and that she was lending him to tide over bad times the very money he had paid as a marriage dowry?

"Unadulterated Perjury."

Dealing with the denial of the plaintiff that she had pretended to supernatural powers by which she had influenced the defendant and the "kit fat" wife she had replaced, Mr. Lo said that it was unadulterated perjury. He submitted that the evidence of the Chinese reporter who called at the house and wrote a report in the Chinese press went to show this. As an unbiased witness, merely recording something unusual, in that a young man, already with a "kit fat" wife, was taking to himself a woman many years his senior, the reporter had correctly reported the facts of the case, said Mr. Lo. The report clearly proved that the woman had claimed to exercise supernatural powers.

Mr. Lo termed her an "idiotic story" the plaintiff's contention that the defendant and his former "kit fat" wife had conspired to a fendish and diabolic plot against her and that it was subsequent to this that the defendant and his former "kit fat" wife had prevailed on her to marry the defendant. Mr. Lo said that the defendant would have had to have been a "senior wrangler in rasseldom" to have conceived the plot and to have fabricated the subsequent story as the plaintiff alleged.
