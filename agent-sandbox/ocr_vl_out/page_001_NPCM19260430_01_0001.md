Over eighty passengers are alleged, in the "Kung Sheung Yat Fo," to have fallen into the hands of boycott pickets in Chinese territory near Macao, for whom ransoms were demanded.

## REFORMATORY FOR STREET

With a number of Clubs in the place, why not a Hongkong Street Urchins Club?

The idea has been furnished by the receipt of a report from the Ceylon Police who have instituted such a movement in Colombo with satisfactory results. "As an experiment a Club on similar lines has been started by the Hongkong Police and barely two weeks old; it promises to be a permanent one in making useful citizens of the waifs and strays in the Colony.

Well might the boys say "We are Seven", for that is the present membership of the Club. Details gathered show that their ages vary from 14 to 16 years, while residence in Hongkong since leaving the various Chinese districts from which they had all count from one to seven years. All but one lad who states he has a father in the country are without parents. During this period they have lived in the streets and carries on a miserable existence on the proceeds of pilfering and hawking.

Clad in navy blue suits provided by the Poor Box funds in place of the rags in which they were found in the streets, the boys attract considerable attention while engaged in their new occupation of selling Chinese newspapers. With the confidence born of their new environment, they are happy and keen on their work.

At present the boys are provided with a room above the Central Magistracy. They are supplied with soap and towels and one of the stipulations is that they have a bath every day. Facilities are given to take part in games while otherwise free from their work of selling newspapers in the mornings and afternoons. Already a football has been supplied and the boys often engage in a little game on the compound. Arrangements have also been made with a school at Cochrane Street to teach them English and Chinese during their leisure hours at moderate fees to be borne by the boys themselves.

Although the wages earned at present make the boys self-supporting, it is hoped to find more remunerative employment for them later. They provide their own meals and pay 25 cents each a month as rent, this sum being placed to a fund for their benefit. Two have already started to save money, their savings bank books shewing sums of $1 and 40 cents.

Membership is secured on expressing a desire to join, but candidates must be physically fit and prove that they are bona fide waifors strays.

Sergeant Nolloth, in charge of the hawkers' department, presides over the Club and when more roomy quarters have been secured for the members they will be looked after by a responsible officer who would reside in the place.

The main idea is to eliminate petty crime among juveniles and this seems to be gradually achieved by the formation of the Club.

## Central Cases

Cruelty to a bullfinch was alleged against a Chinese youth by a European Sergeant who in his evidence at the Central Magistracy this morning stated that he saw the accused making the bird go round in circles to the delight of a crowd round him. The bird and a wing and a leg wrenched off. Fine $20.

A Shantung constable was charged at the Magistracy yesterday with misconduct. He was caught seated in an opium divan at Peal Street at a time when he should have been on duty in the street. His excuse that he went there to have a cup of tea did not satisfy the Magistrate, as Captain Bloxham produced a bad record. He was sent to prison for three months with hard labour.
