## SATURDAY, SEPTEMBER 4, 1926

## VICEROY'S ADDRESS

GOVERNMENT DETERMINED

TO DO ITS DUTY.

COMMUNAL RIOTS

Simla: August 17.

Striking observations on matters constitutional and communal were made this morning by Lord Irwin in his first address to the Central Legislature.

The Viceroy said that they could no more deny the fundamental duty of Parliament to assist India and judge of the progress made than they could deny the ultimate claim which India made and for the satisfaction of which they worked. But before this claim could be fully realised many obstacles imposed by history, circumstance and nature would need to be surmounted. While the past achievements of progress had been the happy fruits of joint Indian and British effort, so now in the solution of the present difficulties, each race had an indispensable part to take. He was the last to ask those who criticised reform to abandon the principles which they revered. The British did not seek to force India into a mould unfriendly to the main features of Indian life and character.

The Viceroy emphasised that events between now and the Commission's statutory enquiry could not fail to exert a great influence upon the conclusions of that body regarding the efficiency, or otherwise with which the policy of 1910 had proceeded.

Unhappily, 'the burning question remained. of the feeling between the communities. He admitted that time was required to heal the wound.' Meanwhile the Government had obligations to law-abiding citizens. Although the matters were the primary concern of the provincial Governments, the form in which they were now emerging made them of all-India interest, intenible claims of rights and exaggerated opposition from time to time had caused anxiety to the authorities. The Government had no intention of allowing any unjust or "unreasonable claims, still less any violence, or threat of violence, to deter them from their duty of maintaining the public peace, and, so far as was compatible with the rights of others, to assure the right of the individual citizen to pursue unhampered-his lawful avocations. India had an abundance of ability but some of it in the past had been directed along lines that could at best lead to no useful result. 'n the future the whole of India's resources were needed for one of the most constructive tasks which had ever enlisted human energies and hopes.

## Imperial Affair

Referring to foreign and imperial matters, the Viceroy touched on the friendly relations with Afghanistan and Nepal, and his satisfaction at the solution of the Mosul problem.

As regards the South Africa, a question, there would be a delegation to the forthcoming Conference shortly, which would satisfy the Indian public on the question of Indians in South Africa. He declared that the Government was not susceptible to an easy decision by coerive force. The solution must be based upon mutual accommodation and carry the free assent of both communities.

The Viceroy next advised the Assembly to examine the Currency Bill, not in the light of local interests, but with reference to its ultimate reaction upon the economic and commercial prosperity of the whole country.

Alluding to the policy of the development of railway traffic in rural areas with the view to stimulating agriculture, the Viceroy mentioned that the railways had planned to add 6,000 miles of railway, which would be remunerative and a boon to the country. He also referred to the changes in public services based on the Lee Report and declared that shortly all action on the Lee recommendations would be complete and the re-organisation of the services of a very striking nature will have been accomplished.

The public galleries were packed to overflow, and the House was f., and the Viceroy on concluding was loudly cheered and applauded.

## COCAINE CHARGE

## NINE MONTHS' HARD LABOUR

Henricus Augustus Boom, a Dutchman, aged 28, describing himself as independent, was brought up on remand at Westminster Police Court and charged with being in possession of a large quantity of cocaine. Boom was arrested at Victoria Station on July 21, when, according to the police evidence, a brown paper parcel alleged to contain cocaine was found in his pocket. Mr. Muskelt prosecuted for the Crown and Mr. F. W. Perkins appeared for the accused.

Mr. Muskett said the authorities agreed to the case being dealt with summarily, and Mr. Perkins announced that Boom would plead guilty.

Mr. Muskett stated that the accused was a drug suspect, and had been watched by the police. Analysis of the contents of the parcel found in his possession showed that they consisted of 89% per cent. pure cocaine. The parcel was an extremely large one.

The Magistrate: I have never known a case with so large a quantity.

Mr. Muskett: Now nor have I, and we know that when this pure cocaine is diluted, as it is, for sale to women of a certain character and others, it fetches enormous prices. The man is a trafficker in the drug. An order for his deportation has already been made by the Home Office.

Detective-inspector Burnby said Boom had been in this country about twelve months, and had made frequent journeys between here and Holland and France. The police information was that the journeys had been made for bringing cocaine to this country and for arranging to bring foreign women of ill-repute here.

The Magistrate (to accused). You will go to prison with hard labour for nine months, and I shall recommend you for deportation.

## AGRICULTURE

## REPORTED TO BE GENERALLY SATISFACTORY

OUTLOOK IN INDIA

Agricultural statistics at the end of July with reference to the progress of the Monsoon, as summed up officially are:—“The Monsoon is sufficient” in North and East Bengal, Bihar, the Central Provinces, Western India States, Lower and Upper Burma and the majority of the Punjab States. In Assam and parts of Madras the Monsoon is fairly satisfactory. The rainfall is below normal elsewhere and more is needed. The standing crops are generally fair and food supplies are adequate except in parts of Burma. Fodder and water are sufficient except in Sind and parts of Madras, Lhopal, Indore and Rajputana. The condition of the cattle is good except in desert tracts of Sind and parts of Bhopal, Indore, Rajputana and two districts in Burma. Severe cattle disease prevails in the Chittagong district of Bengal. Some cattle disease also exists in Assam, the Central Provinces and three States in Rajputana.

..Simla, August 17

In Assam the prospects of tea, jute and cotton generally are good. In Bengal there is an increase of about 16 per cent. in the area under jute, the condition of which is generally good. The gradual rise of rivers in East Bengal is expected to facilitate retting operations in Bengal. The price of jute continues to fall, in anticipation of large supplies, but the price of rice is rising slowly in the Punjab.

The price of wheat is lower than last year in the North West Frontier Province, grains being exported to Afghanistan from Kurram. But there is no appreciable change in prices in Burma. The prices of rice and paddy, and other food grains are generally higher than those at the corresponding period of the previous year. The rice may be due to heavy buying by China and the Far East and to the last harvest being poorer than 1924-1925. The prices are higher than last year in Bihar and Orissa and generally lower than last year in paris of Madras, but are steady in Bombay, and the tendency to fall in Western India States and elsewhere is fairly normal with a tendency to rise.

The economic conditions and the agricultural outlook are generally satisfactory except in Sind where there has been generally a decrease in the area under cultivation, one telsil in Ajmer-Merwara, and in the scarcity of works opened owing to the failure of crops.

## FRANCE & SIAM

VISIT OF INDOCHINA'S GOVERNOR-GENERAL.

CONVENTION SIGNED IN B'KOK

The Governor-General of French Indo-China, M. Varenne, arrived this evening at 6.45.

The original programme was that he should arrive at eleven this morning, but the steamer encountered bad weather in the Gulf and was considerably delayed at the bar.

The Governor-General transferred to a Siamese warship and was brought up river to the Royal landing, where he was received by representatives of the King and the Foreign Minister and by the French Minister.

The Governor-General immediately proceeded to the Foreign Office where the convention between Siam and Indo-China was signed at seven o'clock by Prince Traidos on behalf of Siam and M. Varenne on behalf of the French Republic.

## Convention Signed

The convention “delimits the boundary between the two countries on the Mekong river and also relates to their commercial interests.”

M. Varenne will stay until Sunday afternoon, and during that time will have a full programme of dinners,'receptions, etc.—'Straits Times."

## GALLANT RESCUE

##### DROWNING SWIMMER SAVED BY DR. A. H. SWAN

AN UNKNOWN MAN

Shanghai, August 30. — At Tsingtao during the past week, Dr. A. H. Swan was instrumental in rescuing a Mr. Eames, according to word received in Shanghai yesterday, from drowning and a buffeting to death on the wild coast at Itis Hook, Mr. Eames went swimming off the point during the heavy sea subsequent to the typhoon and became exhausted, being dragged away from the coast. His condition was noticed and many people rushed to the beach. Women became frantic.

The men seemed powerless to do anything.

The drowning man was swept against the rocky projections but managed to keep afloat although the waves dashed him time after time against the rocks. Dr. Swan

came on to the beach and seemed to grasp the situation immediately. He rushed for a life preserver and, despite the heavy seas, dashed through the surf into the swells beyond. Towing the life preserver, he gradually made his way toward Mr. Eames, who was weakening fast. It was after he had put the life preservor around Mr. Eames that the real struggle began. The rescuer had not been a physical director in vain, and his past experience stood him in good stead. Struggling with the waves, he gradually neared the beach, where he was assisted by the others present in dragging the victim of the waves on to dry land. First aid measures were applied and it is understood that Mr. Eames is getting along nicely.

We have been informed that another foreign gentleman, name unknown, either partially or wholly assisted Dr. Swan in the long swim and rescue.—“N. C. Daily News.”

[To the Editor of the "China Mail"]

Sir,—Some months ago we first heard of the Police action, in taking care of some stray lads, giving them clothes and lodging, finding them jobs, and educating them.
