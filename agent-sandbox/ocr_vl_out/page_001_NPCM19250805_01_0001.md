## SHANGHAI INQUIRY

LEADING POWERS

AGREED.

CHINESE AS PRESIDENT?

(Reuter's Service.)

London, Aug. 4.

In the House of Commons, in answer to questions, Mr. Ronald McNeill, Under Secretary for Foreign Affairs, said that the principal Powers concerned had now agreed in principle regarding the judicial commission of inquiry into Shanghai disturbances. The precise procedure to be followed was still being discussed, also the composition of the committee, but presumably the commission itself would decide which of its members would preside.

Captain Wedgwood Benn asked would "the British representative on the commission" favour "the appointment of a Chinese as president.

Mr. McNeill said that the Government desired the commission to have the advantage of a Chinese judge thereon but the matter had not been definitely decided.

### “AU REVOIR.”

## PRINCE AND SOUTH AFRICA

The Prince of Wales has sent a wireless message to the Governor-General expressing his gratitude for the wonderful hospitality of South Africa and declaring that he will always cherish the happiest recollections of the country.

He says that one of the most encouraging memories of the tour will certainly be the large numbers of happy, healthy children who greeted him everywhere and who will, he knows, realise their good fortune and grow up to be good citizens, in a land endowed with an unrivalled climate and boundless possibilities of development, with confident hope in their own future.

The Prince concludes: "I was very sad at leaving you all and can only say au'revoir."

### MR. SUN FO

## SUDDEN MISSION TO PEKING

WHAT HAS HE GONE FOR?

(Router's Service.)

Peking, Aug. 4.

Sun Fo arrived at Peking this afternoon accompanied by the Canton Foreign Commissioner, Mr. Fu Ping-chang. Governmental departments sent representatives to meet him.

Mr. Sun Fo is reported to have come here to make arrangements for his father's funeral, but it is stated that he will also probably discuss the Shameen affair with the Government.

## POLITICAL SIGNIFICANCE?

Mr. Sun Fo, as most people will know, is the son of the late Dr. Sun Yat-sen. He was at his father's bedside when the latter died in Peking but returned to Canton subsequently.

Prior to Dr. Sun's departure for the North, Mr. Sun Fo had been Mayor of Canton for some time, being the first to hold that post when municipal administration was mooted.

In the subsequent re-organisation of the Kuomintang Government at Canton, after the Yunnanese and Kwangsi mercenaries had been expelled, Mr. Sun Fo was given the post of Minister of Communications.

## OUR VOLUNTEERS

[As reported in the “China Mail,” Mr. Fu Ping-chang (or Fu Ping-sheung, in Cantonese) is the Commissioner for Foreign Affairs in Canton. He is fairly young and belongs to Mr. Sun Fo's clique. Educated at St. Stephen's College, he graduated from the Hongkong University. Last year he took part in the negotiations regarding the Sha-meen strike.]

According to rumours current at the time of Mr. Sun Fo's departure from Caxton, he was going either to Shanghai, Peking or to interview Marshal Chang Tso-lin. Mr. Sun Fo is not considered to be one of the Canton radicals. When party strife was being waged in Canton, Mr. Sun's name was connected with the "Prince" party which did not see eye to eye with the allegedly Communistic party. His present mission to Peking may have even more significance than hinted in Reuter's cable, perhaps discussion with either Feng Yu-hsiang (the Christian general), Tuan Chi-jul (the Chief Executive) or Chang Tso-lin (the Mukden warlord).

## ARMS CASE

EXEMPLARY SENTENCE AT THE MAGISTRACY.

A. VAGABOND JAILED.

The hearing of an arms case against two Chinese, adjourned from last Saturday, was continued at the Central Magistracy yesterday with Mr. S. B. B. McElderry and Mr. R. A. D. Forrest sitting together as Magistrates.

Only one man was charged with unlawful possession of a loaded revolver, and he pleaded not guilty. The evidence was that the accused was challenged by Sergeant Gough and a Chinese constable who had noticed him acting in a suspicious manner near the trum terrainus at Shaukiwan. The accused was seen to throw the revolver into a nullah. The weapon was subsequently recovered by the police.

The accused was found guilty, and under the powers vested in them by the emergency regulations their Worships imposed a sentence of five years' hard labour.

The other accused was sentenced by Mr. McElderry, to six months' hard labour this morning on a charge of being a rogue and a vagabond.

## LIGHTNING TOLL

## FATE OF TWO VILLAGE CHILDREN

A sad accident befell two children from the village of Lok-machau, in the New Territories, when they were returning to their home early on Monday morning after completing an errand

The children were forced to seek refuge under a tree from the rain, which later developed into a violent thunderstorm.

NATIVE REPORTER AS COMPLAINANT.



An application made by Mr. J. A. Gordon Leask for bail was refused by the Magistrate (Mr. E. W. Hamilton) who characterised the offence as a serious one and running on a par with those perpetrated in Peking.

A clap of thunder which shook the village caused uneasiness in the minds of the relatives, and as soon as the storm had abated they set out in search of the children. They found only the charred remains of the unfortunate children, as the lightning had struck both the trees and those who sought shelter at the foot.

## STRIKER'S PAY

WHAT ARE THE NAVAL YARD DOING?

A PECULIAR REPORT.

Reports still continue to be received of strikers returning on the one hand and others being employed to take the place of strikers on the other.

The Dairy Farm has had a number of workers return and about 40 per cent. of old Naval Dockyard employees are now at work. The Government Civil Hospital has taken on a large number of men to replace strikers.

The China man learns from a reliable source that the attitude that the Naval Yard authorities are taking to returning strikers is that they should be placed on the lowest grade payment of their particular class of work only for a week or two after rejoining, the opinion being that this is sufficient to bring home to them the unlawfulness of their action in leaving. Many strikers are now reported to be earning pre-strike pay.

The Naval authorities refused to confirm or deny this, however, a “China Mail” representative being told that they had nothing to impart to the Press as to the attitude they were adopting.

## NO NOTICE

AMAH WARNED BY MAGISTRATE.

A Chinese amish until recently in the employment of Mrs. Gibbieson, of No. 3, Torres Buildings, was charged before the Kowloon Magistrate this morning for leaving without notice.

It was stated by the complainant that the accused first left the house on June 3. She came back on July 1, worked five days and left again.

Questionsed by the Magistrate as to the date on which the accused was stated to have first left, which was long before the strike commenced, the complainant stated that there had been no mistake. She said that she did not desire to press the charge, but only wished that the accused would not molest a new family since engaged by her.

His Worship discharged the accused with a warning.

## MACAO PICKET

## STRIKERS' BRANCH AT CHINSHAN

LEVIES FROM TRAVELLERS.

(An Occasional Correspondent.)

Macao, Aug. 4.

Because workers from Hong Kong have managed to get here by the Shekki tow boat, a striker's branch depot has been started at Chinshan, which is in Chinese territory, just outside the Macao boundary.

Instead of coming direct to Macao, the Shekki tow boat now calls at Chinshan where passengers landed. All those who want to pass the Chinese border are interrogated by a picket. It is stated that workers are not allowed to go and that others are mulcted in “fines” of $20 or $25 for the privilege of going through.

## ANTI-STRIKE ORATOR

## ALLEGED THROWING OF STONES

## TWO CHINESE CHARGED

A case of much interest to the Chinese community was again adjourned by Mr. R. A. D. Forrest when informed this morning that the proceedings would last several hours owing to there being a number of witnesses on each side.

Sam Choi, an accountant of the Kwong Sang Hong Company, defended by Mr. C. A. S. Russ, and Wong Chuen, defended by Mr. C. H. Lyson, were the accused persons in the prosecution which is being conducted by Mr. T. H. King, Director of Criminal Intelligence.

The two accused were taken into custody for alleged stone throwing at a street lecturer, who is stated to have been delivering an oration against the strike under the auspices of the Labour Maintenance Association, and in addition to disorderly conduct they are charged with intimidation.

Bail for the second accused was fixed at $500. Mr. Lyson's application for a reduction was unsuccessful.

The case was fixed for hearing at 12 noon, to-morrow, and during the afternoon.

## LOCAL TRADE

MERCHANTS AND FUTURE PROSPECTS.

Seen this morning in connection with the departure yesterday of the Wosang for Tientsin and the Hangsang for Bangkok, Mr. Ho Leung, compradore of Messrs. Jardine, Matheson & Co., Ltd., in reply to the question whether such movements could be taken as representing a return to anything like normal trade conditions, said that the merchants were doing their best and would continue to do so. It was hoped for instance, to get another boat away for Tientsin on Thursday or Friday, but it was impossible to forecast regarding prospects of future general movements of goods, so much hinged on Canton. The Wosang, with a cargo of rice and sugar, was the second boat that the Indo-China Steam Navigation Company had got away to Tientsin since the strike, and in all eleven had been cleared.
