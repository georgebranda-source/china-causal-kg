### TOMB OF ALEXANDER I. IS DISCOVERED EMPTY

ANOTHER MYSTERY.

## (1) 

Revelations have been made in the Russian journal "Slovo" that the Soviet Government in its hupt for jewels and documents recently ransacked the crypt in the church of the St. Paul fortress, in Loningrad, in which the remains of Peter the Great, the Great Catherine, Alexander I., and the later Romanoffs he embalmed.

Special interest was attached to the opening of the coffin of Tsar Alexander. According to the historians he died at Taganrog, in South Russia, on December 1, 1825, but the popular belief has always been that Alexander I, did not die at this time, and that the body of some unknown person was buried in his stead, while he escaped to disguise to live for many years as a hermit. In a remote monastery, He is said to have been recognised long afterwards in the person of a grey-bearded monk who was making a pilgrimage through Russia. Strange to say, states the "Slovo," the coffin of Alexander I when opened was found by the Soviet workmen to be amused.

When the crypt was opened an old woman among the crowd of loiterers we had gathered round the gates, the fortress cried jeeringly to workmen as they passed in, "rave a care; children. Catherine is a dangerous one to middle with!"

Catherine was seen as she had last lain in state dressed in a gown of heavy pale blue silk and wearing pink silk stockings and dainty white satin slippers. An order studded with rubies, emeralds, and diamonds was fixed to the broad silk ribbon she wore across her breast, and below it a cross of sapphires. The dead Empress wore a diadem set with pearls and diamonds, surmounting the high white periwig.

An inventory was made by the officials present, and the coffin was carefully closed, since a telephone message had come from the Soviet authorities directing that the jewels should be allowed to remain

A few days later the sarco phagus of Peter the Great was opened, and the figure of the founder of St. Petersburg was disclosed, still awe-inspiring even after all these years.

The superstitious workmen engaged in the task of opening the coffin involuntarily made the sign of the Cross at the sight of the once mighty Tsar. Despite the twinkred years that had passed since his death the thick grizzled half of the Emperor was still unchanged.

The body was clad in a dark green military tunic, and on the feet were black leather cavalry boots.
