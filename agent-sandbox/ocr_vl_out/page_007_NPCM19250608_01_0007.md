Berlin, June 6.—Authoritative political circles are of opinion that the Allied Powers display complete misconception of the extent of Germany's disarmament. It is stated that the conception that Germany has formed an army in proportion to her population is entirely opposed to facts. Nevertheless the Government is prepared immediately to carefully examine and note the remedy for any failures to carry out her obligations. It is emphasised, however, that Germany cannot go beyond the loyal interpretation of the Treaty of Versailles and the Government's one aim is lasting peace.—Reuter.

## COTTON NEEDS

## VIENNA CONGRESS VIEWS

### (Reuter's Service.)

VIENNA, June 7.

The international "cotton congress has passed a resolution expressing the opinion that the world's statistical position in cotton is such as to warrant an extension of cotton growing wherever feasible on commercial bases; urging the governments of all countries where cotton can be produced advantageously to stimulate development of cotton growing by increasing the cultivated area; creating efficient transport; recogning the construction of irrigation and drainage works such as are being undertaken in the Sudan and India which are the proper means of bringing about an increased supply of cotton; and fervently hoping that all governments will decide on similar works where it is shown the cotton crop will be considerably increased by means of irrigation.

At the final sitting of the cotton conference the chairman, M. Buhler, of Switzerland, referred to irrigation proceeding in India and Sudan and declared that was the best way the Governments could extend cotton growing. He added that the high price of the American product, which was likely to continue, was injuring the welfare of the industry.
