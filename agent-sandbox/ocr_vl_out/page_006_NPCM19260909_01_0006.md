ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་རྒྱལ་ཁབ་ཀྱི་ར��

Events in China are moving rapidly, and public interest is not centred merely on one phase of the recent dramatic and startling developments. Before the Canton Generalissimo began his long and fatal trek to the North the prolonged period of calm was nothing if not irritating to the advocates of methods more forceful than the emission of harmless vapour—miscalled negotiations for the settlement of the boycott. It was felt that with that question out of the way the public of this Colony at least could regard with equanimity the clash of arms in the North. It is one thing to advance on an enemy with rapidity and effect a series of coups. It is another thing to consolidate these successes and pave the way for the eventual linking up of the whole of China with the Canton regime and its Bolshevist advisers and Generals. The Southern troops have succeeded in taking Wuchang and Hankow. But it is very problematical if they will ever enter Peking in the role of victors, and still more problematical whether their beloved Canton will ever hear the tramp of their feet any more. Still, in Chinese civil warfare we have to be prepared for anything, and particularly treachery of the kind that led to the capture of Han-yung through the defection of one of Wu Pei-fu's Generals. Even so, it is difficult to believe that the Chinese as a nation would ever permit the Bolshevist-ridden Cantonese to rule the roost and subordinate the whole country to the wishes of Moscow. It is unthinkable, to say the least of it.

### PICKET "PIRATES."

## EVENTS AFTER OUR NAVAL MOVES

### "THINGS MOVING."

"Things are moving as expected," remarked one who should know, when approached for information as to the latest activities of the pickets "following the decision against them.

In addition to the landing at the Steambont Co.'s two wharves on the West Bund, and the "removal" of the anti-British examination shed, it appears that a move was made elsewhere.

Near Macao Fort, on the back reach of the approach to Canton harbour, a picket boat was "captured." The Chinese name of the boat is the "Asia." One of the four of H.M.'s armed launches in Canton (formerly used for anti-piracy convoys from Hongkong) was sent to this boat.

## Arms Left By Pickets

About fifteen bluejackets, armed and with a machine-gun it is stated, went over to the boat and met with no opposition. The pickets on the "Asia", immediately evacuated, making a report its headquarters. In a search of their patrol boat it is rumoured that arms were found, presumably left behind by the pickets.

On Tuesday one of H.M.'s gun-boats, anchored off the Bund, went out into the stream, cruised round Fong Chuen (the jumping off point for passengers and cargo for Hongkong). A small boat was lowered, going inside the creek. The motor-boats of the pickets were nowhere to be found.

Naval guards are still reported to be on duty at the Stenmbont Co.'s wharf but as yet no step seems to have been taken towards ejecting the pickets said to be in the Stenmbont Co.'s godown on the Bund, just across the roadway.

## Still Seizing Boats?

The name of the picket bout taken at Swatow is given as the "Shansi"

There are now no pickets on the waterways in either port. Intimidation ashore is confined mostly to pickets in plain clothes, and in some cases without arms. The Swatow strike executive has also asked Canton to protest.

At other places like Kongmoon, there is no "report as to whether similar measures have been adopted.

From Sha-u-chung, the small port on the boundary of the New Territories, half of which is on Chinese soil, comes an unconfirmed report of the seizure of two cargo boats by ruffians. "Unreliable" native troops are also in that vicinity so that it is not possible to say whether the culprits were guerrillas or pickets.

## How Cargo is Moving

It is incorrect to say that there has been an immediate effect on trade. On Tuesday, as reported in yesterday's "China Mail," the "Lungshan" brought down a large number of passengers and plenty of cargo.

Yesterday the "Fatshan" was not so crowded. Fish and produce coolies seem to be the first to benefit from the British Naval patrols. Knowing where the warships and their auxiliaries are at a precise moment, the traders bring all their goods in comparative safety instead of running the gauntlet as hitherto.

On the other hand there has been an improvement in the cargo going to Canton from Hongkong. Piece-goods and manufactures are being shipped away and the presumption is that there are now opportunities to take delivery.
