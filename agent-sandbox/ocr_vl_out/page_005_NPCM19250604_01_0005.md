ACTRESS'S CARROT

PERJURED CASE AGAINST

Miss Lilian Gish, recently appeared in an American court nibbling a carrot, which she continued to munch at intervals.

"Carrots," said Miss Gish, to an interviewer, "are good for nerves, and this one helped to soothe mine."

Miss Gish, who was followed into court by troops of adoring New York typists, was defendant to an action for breach of contract brought against her by Mr. Charles H. Duell, her business man and recent fiancé, for whom she left Mr. D. W. Griffith and appeared under the banner of Inspiration Pictures, founded by Mr. Duell.

They went to Italy and produced “The White Sister” and “Romola,” after which Miss Gish promised to visit London, but the promise was mysteriously cancelled and she returned to New York, where she said that she would like to “put a hemisphere between herself and Mr. Duell.”



Miss Gish explained her counsel, "has a horror of legal documents and an aversion to reading them. She placed absolute confidence in Duell, and even allowed him to handle most of her financial transactions. She named him executor under will, and made him trustee of her securities. Similar documents in favour of Duell were executed by her mother and sister, Dorothy Gish."

Counsel for Mr. Duell said that Miss Gish had reached her thirtieth birthday, and “should therefore be sensible enough to know what she was doing in sign-ing a contract.”

The judge dismissed the action against Miss Gish, and detained Mr. Duell on a charge of perjury.

"With one exception," he said, "it has never been my misfortune to deal with a case of more flagrant and more outrageous breach of trust. There has never been more perjury committed before me than by Mr. Duell."

## ARCTIC TRAGEDY

## youn

Mrs. S. G. Clay, the wife of Staff-Sergeant Clay, of the Royal Canadian Mounted Police, has died after "being attacked by savage dogs at a lonely police post at Chesterfield, an inlet on Hudson's Bay. The dogs lacerated one leg and made amputation necessary.

There was no surgeon within 1,000 miles, and the operation was performed by two of the Mounted Police and a missionary. The operation relieved the pain, but Mrs. Clay died on the following day, showing splendid co-rage to the last. The tragedy occurred in September last, but news of it has only just reached the police headquarters in the spring mail.

Sergeant Clay, who is a veteran of the Arctic, was absent on a long patrol at the time, and attempts to reach him were frustrated by storms. Mrs. Clay was familiar with Arctic life. Such attacks by dogs have not been known by hitherto. — “Central News.”
