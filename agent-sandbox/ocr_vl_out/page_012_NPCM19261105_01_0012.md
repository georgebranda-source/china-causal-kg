## BIG ARMS HAUL

MAUSERS WORTH $20,000

SEIZURE ON "COBLENTZ."

One of the largest seizures of arms and ammunition made in recent months took place on Wednesday evening, when officers of the Re-

Norddeutscher-Lloyd s.s.,"Coblentz" 5,393 tons (Capt. W. Heher), upon her arrival in port from Continental porta via Singapore.

Acting upon information received, Revenue Officers went straight to the vessel's forecast where twenty racks, all now, were found in apparent readiness to be moved over board.

These sacks were forthwith seized by the Revenue Officers participating in the search and, upon examination, were found to contain 140 Mauser pistols and no less than 18,000 rounds of ammunition. ## CANTON'S BOYCOTT

Transport “Severance”

CORPS OF LADY SEARCHES.

Views on British Goods Being

Hong Kong's steamboat companies have not been officially informed to do cargo will not be handled at Canton as from Monday, Nov. 8.

As reported in yesterday's "China Mail," the Transport Union passed a resolution to the effect that British goods would be boycotted from the date mentioned.

Local Chinese firms, however, have been instructed by connections in Canton, not to ship up, any cargo after to-morrow as there will be no "sampans or coolies" to land merchandise from Hong Kong.

The boats will continue to carry cargo if forthcoming and it remains to be seen if the threat will be carried out on Monday.

Blind Girl Pickets?

## KIUKIANG TAKEN

ALLIED BASE GONE!

British Naval Party Land on

Concessions

The greatest surprise of the present China War has been caused by the sudden capture of Kiukang by Canton's forces.

Two cables received to-day leave little room for doubt. They read:—

### Shanghai, Nov. 5

Southerners occupied Kiuklang yesterday.—Reuter.

At Kliukiang there was heavy firing around the foreign Concessions.

British Naval men were landed and everything is quiet at present.—Reuter.

How the Southerners accomplished the feat is at present a complete mystery.

Three or four weeks ago, they came very near to entering this most important base, the headquarters in the field of Marshal Sun Chuan-fang.

Other Powers Also Land.

Trenches were dug, on the heights guarding the key to the river port and several determined Cantonese onslaughts were repulsed.

Although they had been in Te'an and Nanchang (both on the railway running inland from Kiukiang), the Nationalists had never indicated that Kiukiang was their goal.

A wireless report states that Naval detachments from the American and Japanese warships at Kiukiang were also landed on the Concession to keep order.

Serious Northern Debacle.

Evidently, the Northerners were taken by surprise. Bearing in mind the incidents at Nanchang (which twice "fell" to the Southerners through "revolt"), it can only be surmised that treachery was partly, if not wholly, responsible for this most serious Northern debacle.

Lying as it does at a strategic point on the lower course of the Yangtse, Kiukiang bars the way of the Cantonese towards Shanghai and the Central China coast. The Southerners can now move on Anking (capital of Anhui) which is so close to Nanking (capital of Kiangsu and Marshal Sun's seat of government) as to predict great danger. Nanking is about 33 hours by rail from Shanghai! Earlier war news will be found on page 6 of this issue.
