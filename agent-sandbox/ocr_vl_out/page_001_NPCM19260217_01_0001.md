## CITY BURGLARY

AUBERT CAFE THE LATEST

VICTIM.

$1,500 LOOT TAKEN

Thieves Help Themselves

A haul of $500 in money and property valued at $1,000 was taken by burglars from the Albert Cafe in Queen's Road Central after the establishment had closed at about midnight on Monday. In addition the thieves helped themselves liberally to roast fowl milk, and the other French delicacies with which the Cafe is stocked.

to food.

Marks of the gang's work during the night were apparent in the disarranged condition of the contents of the glass cases when the proprietor, M. Albert Guetat, opened the main door from the outside yesterday morning.

The thieves slid down some bamboo scaffolding running the full length of the wall of the two-stoneyed building at the rear and over the flat roof of the kitchen. A ladder placed here helped them to alight in the yard outside the kitchen. A thin wire netting barrier placed over the iron barred windows of the kitchen was cut through and the gang got through this hole into the kitchen which gave them free access to the premises.

The door leading into the lavatory from the yard was also forced open. Another door from this room gives access to the alley way between the Cafe and the Asiatic Building. This door was found locked from the inside when the theft was discovered in the morning, but it is not unlikely that the stolen property or a part of it was passed on to a confederate outside.

The money was taken by breaking open the drawers of a wooden table used by the proprietor's wife as a cash desk. Two mall leather cases containing passports, the Cafe licence and other documents were also taken from the drawers, but the lady's marriage licence was fortunately left behind. Property stolen comprised silver crockery articles, an electric iron, a weighing balance, linen and other goods.

As in the majority of city burglaries, the gang possessed an intimate knowledge of the building and its contents and took everything of value which they could conveniently carry away with them. Suspicion rests with several coolies, who were in the employment of the Cafe and had to be discharged recently.

A report of the theft was made to the police yesterday, but so far no arrests have resulted.

## DUTCH POLITICS

AMUSING REFERENCES IN

A SPEECH

POLICE COURT SCENE

A Chinese medicine vendor was charged before Mr. R. E. Lindsell at the Central Magistracy this morning with making a seditious speech calculated to lead to a breach of the peace at Mercer Street on the night of February 9. Two detectives stated in evidence that defendant attracted a crowd of over 100 people by singing a comic song and then taxed his countrymen on their lack of common sense in working under "foreign devils" when they could easily get appointments in Canton under Chang Kai-shek who was stated to have plenty of money and ammunition and in a position to attack any place. Among other references defendant was alleged to have delivered himself thus: "We are so numerous that we can drown those with curly hair and hooked noses (meaning Europeans) and if you are not patriotic you will be treated like the Indians who are not allowed to marry until the age of 40 and the race is exterminated."

After the cross-examination of the witnesses by Mr. D. McCallum (for the defence) and evidence given by the defendant from the witness box, he submitted this was a case of the average medicine seller who sings a comic song to draw a crowd, then talks on sundry subjects, and lastly comes to his medicine. He said that defendant had not used any seditious language, and even if the term "foreign devils" had been used it had become a common expression with the Chinese as to be almost of no value.

His Worship held that some ill-

advised remarks had been made

by the defendant, but did not

agree that he deliberately tried to

create trouble. Defendant was

fined $20 and ordered to sign a

## AFRICAN FLIGHT

FOUR. BIPLANES TO LEAVE CAIRO.

12,000.MILES' JOURNEY

(British Wireless Service.)

—Rugby Feb:16

On March 1 the flight of the Royal Air Force, consisting of four Fairey Napier biplanes, will leave Cairo in an attempt to fly to Capetown and back. Wing Commander Pulford will be in command.

The outward journey will occupy about six weeks, as it has been decided to remain for a couple of days or more at each stopping place. If the return flight to Cairo is successful the machines will be fitted with floats, and the journey will be continued to Britain. The distance thus covered would be about 12,000 miles.

The route to be followed will be almost the same as that followed by Mr. Alan Cobham, and it will be interesting to see how the performance of the water-cooled Napier engine compares with the air-cooled Jaguar, of Mr. Cobham's machine.

There may be a meeting between the Royal Air Force flight outward bound and Mr. Cobham homeward bound.

Particular interest will also be taken in the way that the Royal Air Force machines take off from the Bloemfontein Aerodrome. This aerodrome is 6,000 feet above sea level, and the rarefied air and heat caused Colonel Van Ryneveld to come to grief here whilst taking off in 1920, and nearly ended Mr. Cobham's flight in spite of the fact that his machine...was given an engine of 200 horse-power more than that normally fitted to overcome this very problem.

Incidentally, it is learned that when Mr. Cobham makes his return journey to Cairo he will do so at the utmost possible speed in the hope of making a record flight.

## NATIONAL DEBT

BURDEN ON TAXPAYERS.

Mr. Winston Churchill, Chancellor of the Exchequer, stated in the House of Commons that, taking the pound sterling at par, the British net payments to the United States Government during 1926 would amount to £33,620,000 in capital and interest. Against this we should receive about £14,000,000 from German preparations and inter-Allied war debts already funded.

Mr. Churchill said that the total amount of the National Debt at December 31 last was £7,738,000,000. The estimated charge for interest, sinking fund, and management for the financial year ending on March 31 next was £305,000,000. The amount due per head of population of these figures was £172 and £615a. respectively.

To Mr. Churchill's statement it may be added that the National Debt on March 81, 1914, just prior to the outbreak of the war, was 2661,000,000 or about one-twelfth of the present amount.
