#### Counter-Movements. Rugby. August 22

Interest in the coal dispute is now centred in the Nottinghamshire and Derbyshire coalfields, where yesterday there was a further increase in the number of men presenting themselves for work.

Reliable figures will not be available until the pits open to-morrow, but it is clear that the response to the owners' new wages offer for a 7½-hours' day has been large.

In Mansfield yesterday there was at one time a queue 100 yards long of miners waiting to sign on for works and the offices of certain collories were busy throughout day accepting men. At two pits 20 per cent. of the normal strength has already been engaged, and some colliery owners anticipate that the pits in the Nottinghamshire area generally will next week be working up to 75 per cent. of the normal.

Whilst the colliery yards in this area were filled with men eager to register themselves, however, large meetings were being held in localities at which those miners who refuse to break away from the Federation's national policy attended. At these meetings resolutions were unanimously passed calling on the miners to stop away from the pits until all have returned together.

The movement for a return to work is still confined mainly to Nottinghamshire, Derbyshire, Warwickshire, and Leicestershire.

The areas apparently unaffected by the "movement include "Lancashire, Cheshire, Durham, and Northumberland.

## VATICAN AND MEXICAN TROUBLE

Rome, August 21.

The Vatican has issued a semi-official statement regarding the Mexican situation, to the effect that President Calles's refusal to accept the demands of the Mexican Bishops regarding the suspension of the anti-Catholic laws and the attainment of a fair separation and arrangement between Church and State has created no surprise at the Vatican, where the refusal was foreseen.

Mexico City, August 21.

Following President Calles's reply to the Archbishop's petition, the Episcopate announces that an appeal will be made next Congress to amend the religious clauses of the Constitution. Catholic laymen will handle the campaign, as clergy are forbidden to take part in political activities. The National Chamber of Commerce, which has been considering the economic situation, has adopted a resolution advising Mexican business people to reduce their purchases of foreign commodities and suggesting a substantial increase in import duties, particularly of luxuries. Also the formation of a Government Commission to supervise the issue of foreign drafts to combat the sending of Mexican money abroad.--

Renter's American Service.

Mexico City, August 22.

For the first time in Mexican history, since the period of the War of Reform sixty years ago, the President has officially conferred with high Catholic dignitaries. Yesterday President Calles conferred for ninety minutes with Archbishop Ruiz and Bishop Diaz on the religious question. The proceedings were cordial, but there is still a deadlock, as the prelates pointed out that the Pope had forbidden the Church to recognise the principle regarding the Churches' public property, upon which President Calles insists.—Reuter.

#### MR. S. BALDWIN

HOLIDAY.

London, Aug. 22.

Mr. S. Baldwin, the Prime Minister, has left for Aix-les Baings on holiday.—Reuter.

## In Need of Rest

No immediate prospect of a national settlement of the coal dispute is in view, and the Prime Minister, who had remained in London for the past two days in case his services should be required in connection with the negotiations, decided late last night to carry out his original intention of spending a holiday at Aix les Bains. Accordingly he left this morning with Mrs. Baldwin for the Continent.

Although his health has not suffered, the Prime Minister, who has worked under a great strain for many months past, is in need of a rest. Quite apart from the present anxieties of the coal dispute, recuperation is necessary; for not only is the coming Parliamentary session likely to be heavy, but the meeting of the Imperial Conference, which has been summoned for mid-October, will require his constant attention.—British Wireless Service.

## LEAGUE COUNCIL

## AN OUTLINE OF GERMAN POLICY

Berlin, August 22.

In a speech at Breslau, Dr. Marx declared that Germany's expectations from the Treaty of Locarno had not yet been fulfilled to the extent desired. Germans were painfully disappointed since the evacuation of the Cologne zone at the number of troops in the second and third zones, which lately had increased by thousands of men.

Dr. Marx said that they must be certain when they went to Geneva that Germany's entrance into the League would proceed without friction to a successful conclusion. He explained that Germany was ready to co-operate and work with the Examining Committee and in the interests of the League as a whole. We shall strive for the maintenance of the Council and for not posing as the opponent of any candidate. We will only ask, in a final settlement, that the interests of all members of the League be taken into account."—Reuter.
