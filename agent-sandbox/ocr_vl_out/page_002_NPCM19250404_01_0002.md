## COMMERCE AND FINANCE

## THE LINEN MARKET

LATEST BELFAST REPORT.

Flax.—Irish markets still remain small and all Flax marketed has been bought up. Demand is better, but there is practically no change in value, the tendency being against buyers. On the Continent French Spinners are buying more freely, which has stopped any further fall in value.

Latvian Flax.—There has been no change in quotations during the last fortnight; in fact the exchange has rather stiffened them. Thers are reports of big negotiation pending at present which would steady this market if the business comes off.

Esthonian Flax—Little going prices quietly steady.

Weavers.—There seems to be a rather steadier feeling in the market, but there is not much business being placed. There are a good many small orders being distributed over the various qualities. Damask in the low end shows no sign of improvement, but in the fine end more business has been placed, and there is a much healthier feeling in this section. Manufacturers are busy completing old contracts for Dress. Linen, most of which require to be completed by the end of next month. Merchants.—Housekeeping Goods. In actual business placed there is not much to say since last report Conditions at present need careful attention on the part both of buyers and sellers. In the general line of Housekeeping goods values offered are based on bare manufacturing costs, and do not represent anything in the way of margin to Merchants. These prices can hardly be repeated, if orders come forward in the usual quantity for Spring business. In Cambrics and Sheers there is likely to be a revision which will reduce the low numbers and bring them into line for more attractive selling prices in Domestic Handerchiefs. Medium and better numbers are to-day selling at closest margin possible, and are perfectly safe for forward buying.

Soviet Flax.--The Continent is taking more interest but as these prices have been reduced very little from the height, business is difficult to arrange.

Dress Linens - Important shipments for U.S.A., and other markets have been made during the past fortnight and some moderate sized orders have been arranged. A general improvement in business is expected during March.

## THE COTTON MARKET

## LATEST REPORT

## MANCHESTER, March 4

Trading in the cotton markets has been more active and American values have advanced steadily. A sound undertone has been in general evidence owing to the fact that there has been no decrease in the larger absorption of cotton by the world's consumers. American advices report a continued strong demand for spot; Liverpool sales have been fairly good; and steadily advancing premiums have been paid for deliveries before the end of the season. New crop months have also been well supported owing to the lateness of preparations for want of rain. There is still time to retrieve this loss of time but reports have been much more insistent and serious in tone, and a certain amount of nervousness has been created as to the possibility of another inadequate crop. Mainly in consequence of this heavy buying has taken place and in all the advance has totalled ½d per lb. The Egyptian section has been quiet and prices are practically unchanged. In the Manchester market hopes have not been fulfilled. There has been a fair volume of enquiry but although the smaller outlets have again made a moderate showing they have not made up for the lack of business from the large Far Eastern markets which is now unusually late. The low selling prices ruling in these markets and the unsatisfactory, offtake is still apparently absorbing more attention than new business. There are purchasers who appear to be looking ahead more but they are few. It is very likely, however, that these few will prove to have bought well. American yarns have been in active demand but spinners have been barely able to obtain the full advance in the raw material. Medium Egyptians have been in better request but super grades are practically neglected and prices are unaltered. India has been quiet beyond a fair enquiry for dhootics, light whites, prints and fancies. China has enquired for fancies and dyed goods with poor results. Singapore and Java have been fairly prominent for miscellaneous styles. The Continent, Africa and South America have also done a small trade.

## HAMBURG

German hotel and restaurant proprietors are considering the possibilities of moving pictures on the premises as producers of trade. They argue that as there are no free passes to the movies, a condition that prevails largely among the theatres, the man who goes to the movies probably has money to spend for refreshments. Hence, if they could once get him on the lot, they would stand a fair chance of selling him something more than his movie ticket.
