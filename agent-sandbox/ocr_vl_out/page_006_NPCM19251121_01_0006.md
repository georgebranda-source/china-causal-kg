## THE QUEEN MOTHER

Queen Alexandra has passed away, and a whole people and Empire will grieve as if for a personal loss. The Queen-Mother was loved not only in herself but in the legend of beauty, grace, and all womanly fulfilment with which her life endowed and adorned over sixty years of English history. There is no romance dearer to British hearts than that of “the sea-kings’ daughter” for it is one that they have seen lived with their own eyes.

All goodness was foretold of her, and all goodness came true. To win the devotion of a strange and proud people, to reign over a society critical of foreign 'Consorts, and to discharge many of the delegated duties of an august and secluded Queen—these were hard matters to try the mind and courage of a maid. Queen Alexandra achieved them all, and in achieving them grew into the nation's own life and thought, becoming part of its happiness and one of the pillars of its faith in those things which are true and lovely and of good report. She kept alight about the Throne those lamps which are its real lustre. Her own disposition found for her the closest affinities with a race of deep generosity. If the Queen-Mother had had one distinguishing impulse, it was an unbounded, charity in all the sweeter uses of that word. Nothing could measure what her gracious leadership and example did for the relief of human suffering and the promotion of good works. None ever obeyed more faithfully and humbly the injunction upon the "greatest" that they must serve all. It is in the abodes of affliction that the name of Queen Alexandra has won the sincerest benediction and reverence, and to no sovereign could a worthier crown than that be offered.

Speaking at the annual meeting yesterday of the Ex-Active Service Men's Association, Mr. S. C. Feltham, Chairman, referring to the activities of the Association during the past year said that there was not a great deal to say about the report and balance sheet, although the latter did not show a very good balance. This was partly due to the fact that a large number of members had not paid their subscriptions of $5 per year and had not supported the Club as they should have done. This was much to be regretted. The late Mr. H. W. Chaney at one time made an eloquent appeal on behalf of the Association as opposed to the Club. This year, he thought, an eloquent appeal should be made for support of the Club, upon which the existence of the Association depended. If they really wanted a Club and Association they must support the Club, more than they had done. Without the Club, the Association would cease to exist and would become dormant, like the British Legion which did not do a great deal. The Ex-Service Men's Association did a great deal which he considered should be done by the British Legion.

Referring to their Hon. Secretary, the Chairman said their Secretary had had nothing but praise, praise, praise all the time he had been in office. He had deserved every single word of it—(applause)—and nothing he could say would be adequate to express their appreciation of Mr. MacReynolds' work for the Club and Association. As a slight token of their esteem and respect for their Hon. Secretary and what he had done for the Club and Association, he proposed that they elect Mr. MacReynolds a life member of Association. (Loud applause.)

Mr: Pearce seconded and the motion was carried unanimously.

Mr. MacReynolds, who was informed that he was the first life member of the Association to be elected, suitably acknowledged the compliment and gave interesting details regarding the formation of the Ex-Active Service Men's Association. He added that he was disappointed in the attendance of members of the Association at the recent ceremony at the Cenotuph on Armistice Day. The attendance was not so good as it should have been. If the day was going to be remembered it was the members of the Ex-Active Service Men's Association who had to remember it. The younger men in the army and the navy did not like it and it was for the older men, who knew what they had gone through, the comrades they had lost and, how they had nearly lost their own lives, to commemorate the Day in a fitting manner. So long as he was in the Association he was going to see that there was a good parade at the Cenotuph on Armistice Day.

A lengthy discussion ensured regarding the Eassma batling beach at Stonecutter's, which it was shown was kept going up a loss each year. The general opinion of the meeting was that it must be placed upon a self-supporting basis and eventually, after numerous suggestions had been put forward, it was decided that in future all members of the Club and members of their families over fifteen years of age should pay 20 cents for the use of the beach, visitors 50 cents and that the latter also be charged 30 cents in addition for taxes.

A suggestion brought forward to run an annual dance or series of dances and whist drives and concerts was also discussed at considerable length and eventually left to the incoming committee to deal with.

(From Our Own Correspondent.)



Parkkai, Kongmoon, Nov. 16. Four seaplanes arrived here today from Canton or Whampoa, to assist the Reds in clearing out their opponents from the southern part of the province.

It is believed that foreigners are acting as pilots, observers, and bombers but the mechanics are mostly natives of Sze Yap. Another rumour is that the flying base will be established in this locality. Two of the planes are going on to Cheung Sha, which is near the Yupping border.

NBW NAVAL BASH?

Pakkai has now the appearance of a small but busy naval port. Yesterday: H.M.S. "Robin," the Canton men-of-war "Chung Shan," "Kong Tai," "Kong Koo," "Kong Hon" and a number of armed launches were here at the same time.

Shekki having reverted to the control of General Cheing Kuihe's troops, river communication has been resumed and two boats are running to Canton, etc.

## MILITARY MOVEMENT9

By November 15 all anti-Red troops had been expelled from the Sze Yap districts and the Canton Reds are pressing hard on their heels, having advanced as far as Youngkong.

As Brigadier Chan Ming-shu penetrated too far to preserve his alignment he ordered reinforcements to move up by forced marches so as to maintain pressure. The Red offensive stopped for a day or two till help arrived.

Blowing their bugles and shoulder-ing banners like soldiers on the march, bandits entered Tongha (25 miles from Palkai on November 15. After plundering for several hours and burning down a number of houses the gang took away over a hundred persons for ransom.

## OBITUARY

Received just after the) “China Muil” had gone to press yesterday was the notification that Mr. Sutherland Stewart of the Taikoo Sugar Refinery had passed away at the Government Civil Hospital. It was then thought that he had succinbed to acute malaria which had affected his brain.

Mr. Stewart leaves a wife and three unmarried daughters to mourn his loss and the sympathy of all will go out to the bereaved family. STRIKE INCIDENT ON CANTON RIVER.



In a race against time the Kowloon (Chinese) Customs has been asked to rescue a dozen souls murooned on a rock, outside British waters, in the Canton River, others having already perished.

The night before last a junk put out from Su Cheng to pick up a ferry to Hongkong by evading the cordon drawn by the strike pickets. In the dark the junk crashed against the well-known river landmark of Tai Chaam and foundered.

Two people are known to have drowned at the moment. About a dozen were carried, clinging to wreckage, into British waters (near Deep Bay) and saved by the s.l. "San Hang On." The remainder were last seen off a precarious foot, hold with a strong wind blowing, hence the necessity for urgent rescue.

## HOCKEY MATCH

SURREYS' REVENGE FROM THE CLUB

ANOTHER PENALTY “BULLY.”

The Club "A" team had bad luck in losing to the Surreys "A" yesterday afternoon.

The game was of a very fast order, and in the second half the Club had far the best of the play. The Club started with only ten players and had the misfortune to lose three goals before the arrival of the last man, who was ten minutes late. The rest of the first half the Surreys tried hard to increase their lead with the Club making some fine dashes to get on terms.

In the second half the Club eleven combined far better. Soon after the interval a good movement on the left led to Sanderson scoring for the Club. This set the Club going in earnest and soon after their efforts were rewarded by Plummer scoring, leaving them only one down. The Club pressed hard and the Surrey goals was pulled up for obstruction, a bully taking place within the circle, Lloyd having bad luck in not scoring from same. The whistle blew soon after giving the Surreys the win by the odd goal in five.
