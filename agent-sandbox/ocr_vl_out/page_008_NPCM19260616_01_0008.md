The anniversary of the signing of the Declaration of Independence (July 4) will be appropriately served this year by the American community in Manila. Preparations have been started for the celebration and a sum of Pesos 6,000 will be raised.

The attention of owners of motor vehicles is drawn to an announcement by the Police Department, in this issue. Owners are requested to send their vehicles to Police Headquarters to be weighed on the weighbridge (which has been specially constructed for the purpose). The licensing season commences on July 1 when the fee will be by weight. Full details are given in the advertisement on page 12.

In Hongkong in 1898, as representative of the Sun Life Assurance Co., Mr. H. H. Tibbey left Shanghai for Home on June 13. In 1902, he joined the Glen Line (then McGregor Bros. and Gow) and went to Shanghai in 1904. The "N. C. Daily News" says that he "has done outstanding work. It is with great regret that Shanghai will take its farewell of him, for his connection with the port has been of invaluable assistance to merchants, while in other spheres he has played a part making him universally popular."

Manila, June 10.—The municipal board yesterday unanimously adopted the motion of the chairman of the law committee of the board, Ruperto del Castillo, to suspend indefinitely the public dance hall ordinance authorising establishment of cabarets half a kilometer inside the city limits. Councillor Castillo was directly responsible for the passage of the cabaret ordinance. The auctioning of the privilege to operate cabarets at Santa Cruz and San Nicolas also will be postponed indefinitely as a result of the city council's action, Mayor Romualdez and City Treasurer Victor Alfonso announced. There are two applications filed by Chinese merchants with the city authorities, asking permits to operate cabarets inside the city limits.

A new, modern fire engine has been purchased by the Chamber of Commerce at Kongmoon, the mercantile interests having taken into their own hands the question of protection from fire.—Our own correspondent.



On the occasion of a mass ceremonial at Sanwui city, for the purpose of an interchange of greetings between the military and the civilian population, the public were ordered to fly the (Canton Government) national flag for two days, over shop premises.—Our own correspondent.

Instead of being run over, an old woman carrying a child, was tossed some distance by the cowcatcher of a train, at Lin Tong station, on the Sunning Railway, Eye-witnesses were astounded as it seemed certain that she would be caught by the train. She was knocked out of the way and only suffered minor injuries. Our own correspondent.

Students at the Sanwul middle school went on strike, following the dismissal of seven of their number who were dismissed because they were absent on May 30, when they are alleged to have taken "French leave" to participate in the procession, on the first anniversary of the Shanghai shooting.—Our own correspondent.

Honolulu, May 5.—Amateur radio station Hu-6CIJ here claims another record—a 57-minute two way conversation with station O-A3D, South Africa, with a 50-watt tube. The conversation was carried on between 6.18 p.m. and 7.15 p.m. Honolulu time. It was already daylight of the next day on the other end of the circuit. Station Hu-6CLJ is operated by Masayaki Hisamoto, radio operator for the Nippu Jiji, local Japanese daily newspaper.

America and China were linked by radio broadcasting on the evening of May 21, when the programme sent out by the "Post Enquirer" from station K.T.A.B. at Oakland, California, was heard very clearly by Peking radio sets. Both the musical and speaking portions of the two hours programme were clearly picked up in Peking. The programme was continued for two hours during which instrumental and vocal music was audible there in addition to the speeches.

Making the third motor car reported stolen or missing in Shanghai this month, a gang of Chinese stole a big hire car from a Chinese garage in the French Concession on June 4, and no trace of it has been found. The men hired the car, threw out the chauffeur on Hungjao Road, and decamped with it.

Captain and Mrs. S. Bridgeman, Mr. T. L. Durand, Mrs. Grimble, Mr. T. T. Gilmore, Mr. and Mrs. C. Stanton and Dr. W. Lupton arrived here yesterday by the "Empress of Canada" from Vancouver and ports.

Bangkok, June 4.—Owing to a difference between cattle dealers and butchers, Bangkok is without fresh meat to-day. The parties cannot agree as to the prices to be paid for live stock. This is a sequel to the recent outbreaks of rinderpest and anthrax.

San Francisco, June 4.—R. Stanley Dollar who returned to-day from Washington where he acquired from the Shipping Board the five Admiral-Oriental Line ships on the route between Seattle and Manila, announced that there would be no change in the personnel or the schedules of the ships.

Bombay, May 26.—The death took place in St. George's Hospital, Bombay, yesterday afternoon of Mr. William Mackenzie, Manager of the P. and Q. Banking Corporation, Bombay. Mr. Mackenzie was very well-known and popular in banking and business circles in the City. He came from Scotland to Calcutta in 1912 as an assistant in the Mercantile Bank of India, and in 1918 joined the Tate Industries Bank, Bombay as Assistant Manager. After that bank was amalgamated with the Central Bank he was appointed Manager of the P. and Q. Banking Corporation. Bombay. In December 1923, Mr. Mackenzie who leaves a widow and a child was 35 years of age.

Moscow, May 22.—Not only have the "Shell" and the Standard Oil Companies failed to squeeze Soviet oil from the European market, but Soviet oil has become a severe competition to these great oil enterprises, according to Mr. G. Lomov, Chairman of the Neftsindikat, the Soviet oil selling enterprise, who has just returned from abroad.

Passengers who sailed for America (via usual ports) yesterday by the "Korea Maru" included Mrs. A. A. and Miss Joyce Claxton, Mr. C. Little, Mr. and Mrs. E. E. Roote, Mr. L. P. Rashussen and Mr. D. C. O'Dell.

New York, June 7.—Japan would refuse the Philippines even as a gift was the statement of Judge José Abad Santon, head of the special Filipino commission recently arrived here. Judge Santon gives as his authority statements gathered while he was in Japan from, he says, the great leaders of Japanese thought.

The Canberra Vigilance Committee urge the Commonwealth Government to open the Federal Parliament at Canberra in January of next year. It is proposed that a member of the Royal Family should formally open the new capital.

Peking, June 3.—Despite the repeated warnings of the authorities, the second-hand article dealers continue to deal in arms and sundry military equipments left behind by the Kuominchun secretly. With a view to preventing these from falling into the hands of the bad characters and evil doers, the authorities have been forced to adopt more drastic measures to suppress such unlawful transactions. A number of second-hand dealers in Peiyuan and Siyuan were punished during the last two days by having one of the two ears of the offender cut off as a warning for his ignoring orders.—"Kuo Wen."
