## TAX ANOMALIES

Canton Special Levy in Operation.

RIVER SHIPPING TRIALS.

"All" Vessels to Submit to Examination?

From Our Own Correspondent.J Samshui (West River), Nov. 24. The special tax bureau of the Nationalist Finance Ministry is now in full operation at Wuchow and there are many anomalies arising out of an impossible situation.

It is well known that the bureau levies 2½ per cent. on ordinary imports and 5 per cent. on luxuries. But for convenience, or some other reason, the staff of the bureau merely asks for half the amount collected by the Chinese Maritime Customs in Hai-kwan taels.

If a part of any shipment comes under the category of "luxuries," the bureau does not trouble to work out its own assessment, merely accepting the figures of the Customs.

Should the Maritime Customs make a seizure of goods not on the manifest, the bureau also puts in a claim for seizure, but the merchandise is only handled in these cases by the Customs. There is no harm in making a formal claim but the question of custody does not arise.

The staff of the bureau now wear a uniform much similar to that of the Chinese Maritime Customs. The outstanding exception is in the badge.

Instead of the dragon in the middle of the emblem, the Nationalist bureau men have a blue medallion on which is superimposed the white-rayed sun.

Native exporters at Wuchow have been notified by the bureau that the penalty for a false declaration is three years' imprisonment. The staff board the pai's (pontoons serving for wharves), in harbour and scrutinise manifests.

Liquor Shipment Astray.

I hear that the Custom's Club at Wuchow has not received a shipment of'stores which was sent some' time ago.

Inquiries among the Nationalist Inspection Corps at Do-singled to a belief that the stores had gone astray—to Canton.

Subsequently some stores were delivered through a British steamboat from Hong Kong.

A Portuguese merchant in the liquor business lost altogether 115 cases of wine on one trip, 7 of which were for up-country Roman Catholic missions. This gentleman has now open to him the course of interviewing the Finance Ministry in Canton.

Conditions at Do-sing are different. There the Inspection Corps is only authorised to effect seizures of alleged contraband, but there is no special tax bureau to make collections.

Between Two Fires

The Chinese gunboat which previously left after an encounter with pirates has returned, and is anchored close to the tow, near the special tax bureau.

In this port (Samshui, further down river), merchants are literally between two fires.

Native junks and cargo-boats are told that they must stop for examination at the Nationalist depot, which is outside Samshui harbour limits.

When that examination is over, they proceed to enter port, going a distance of 100 yards or so. But because they have to submit to the bureau they render themselves liable to a fine by the Maritime Customs for “anchoring in transit and breaking bulk.”

What Compliance Means.

Not every Hong Kong merchant is aware that vessels going from Hong Kong to Vuchow must report to the Customs station at Samshui in case of any stoppage between Whangmoon and Samshui due to fog, breakdown or other unforeseen circumstance of navigation. Failure to do so can result in a smart fine.

But the special tax bureau intends to establish a depot three miles outside Samshul and traders have been informed that all vessels, of whatever nationality, will have to stop and submit to search, Compliance with the tax bureau can only mean running foul of the Customs.

## PARDONED

## JAMES EDWARD ATHERLEY RELEASED

NOW ON HIS WAY HOME.

The “China Mail” understands that a pardon has been granted James Edward Atherley, who, on January 17 last, was sentenced by the Chief Justice (Sir Henry C. Gollan) to two years' hard labour following a long and sensational trial at the Supreme Court for embezzlement of money the property of his employers, Messrs. Getz's Bros. (of the Orient), Ltd. Mr. Atherley is at present on his way to the United States.

The authorities concerned are reticent with regard to the matter, but as far as can be ascertained, Atherley, who had made several apparently unsuccessful attempts to have his sentence reduced, recently received intimidation that His Excellency the Governor had reviewed the case and that it had been decided that he be released from custody and given a passage to the United States. This was "accordingly done, Atherley being escorted one evening to a trans-Pacific liner by which he proceeded to his native country.

## COAL TERMS

SCOTLAND FALLING INTO LINE.

NOW 428,000 MEN BACK.

London; Nov. 26.

Satisfactory progress is being made in Scotland towards settlement of the coal dispute.

Matters have been left in the hands of a small joint. committee which will submit the terms to each side for ratification.

Officials of the Lancashire and Cheshire Miners' Federation have discussed terms with the coal owners. These will be submitted to the miners' conference in London to-morrow.—Reuter.

428,000 miners were working today.—Reuter.

## Eight Hour Day

The Scottish coalowners' and miners' representatives have arrived at an agreement which will be submitted to both sides.

It is understood that the agreement provides for an eight hour day and a nominal minimum wage of $98.4d.

The Northumberland Miners' Council has decided to recommend acceptance of the owners' terms, including the 7½ hour shift for hewers and 8 hours for other workers.

After a lengthy conference at Cannock Chase the coalowners and miners at Birmingham failed to reach agreement, but the great majority of the men are working. —Reuter.
